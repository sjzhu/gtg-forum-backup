Greetings and salutations, comics fans!

This is the second thread of my increasingly convoluted and wild project, The History of Venture Comics! About two and a half years ago, I started designing an entire fictional publishing company set in the world of the Sentinel Comics RPG, Venture Comics. For those who aren’t aware of it, SCRPG is a fast-paced, action-oriented superhero RPG that uses the setting of the card game “Sentinels of the Multiverse”, which has been dormant for the past few years but might be spooling up again in the near future. Fingers crossed.

Using a series of randomizers and a lot of work, I put together a timeline of the comics running from the Golden Age of Comics all the way through to 2019, through the creation of the setting’s biggest heroes and villains.

Then I added a bunch of D-Listers to fill the series out. Then I went back and added even more heroes and villains, along with a number of key supporting characters and minor lieutenants.

Now I’m moving into the second phase of the project. Over the next two and a half years, I’m putting together three more supplements for Venture Comics: a large collection of environments, fleshed-out information on various supervillain teams and organizations, and finally a supplement fleshing out the mid-tier characters and events of the setting.

(Some people will note that I had mentioned a possible Diamond Age mini-supplement in the past; it’s been absorbed into the other projects in order to let them be a bit more fleshed out.)

The Penitent’s first few appearances took place strictly in the mundane world. While he battled against demonic cultists and dark magicians, these battles took place in small towns and hidden alleys, not in stranger realms. But it wasn’t long before his creators, emboldened by the popular support for his character, decided to start trying stranger things. The first such place were the Hellish Halls of Zabaranth.

In Campfire Terrors #17, the Penitent arrived at a college where a group of researchers were intended to use a collection of ancient relics to pierce the veil between worlds. They held the Hand of Zabaranth, which they believed would allow them to freeze space itself and open a pathway. Unfortunately, they were right. As the Penitent arrived to stop them, their ritual was complete and they were pulled into a frozen mirror of the college, its towers twisted and tucked within each other. Icy demons stalked the halls, sniffing the warmth of human blood.

The Penitent quickly entered the Halls of Zabaranth behind the would-be explorers, defeating the first demons who threatened them. But his efforts to lead them back to safety were quickly complicated when he discovered that he had been followed. In Campfire Terrors #16, he had saved a young newsboy named Reggie from being sacrificed to the Cult of the Blood Tide. Unbeknownst to the Penitent, Reggie was a clever and alert young lad, who successfully tracked his saviour and came to thank him and offer his aid. Instead, he was sucked into the portal behind the Penitent and lost in the Halls himself.

Over the course of the issue, the Penitent was forced to shepherd the academics to safety while also tracking down and saving Reggie, who in turn saved the Penitent when a particularly powerful ice demon ambushed him and knocked away the protective amulet he had been using to ward off the region’s bitter chill. Reggie risked himself to recover it, and soon enough everyone was back in their own world. The Penitent claimed the Hand of Zabaranth from the chastened explorers, and made them swear never to seek out dangerous magic again.

He tried to extract the same promise from Reggie, who cheerfully told him that he would see the Penitent the next time he was in the area. Reggie would go on to appear in roughly a quarter of the Penitent’s stories, staying carefully out of the darker or more brutal tales in favour of the ones in which the Penitent was able to save everyone.

Like many of the Penitent’s early stories, Zabaranth wasn’t intended to re-appear. It was just a strange demonic place that allowed for both complicated art and haphazard layout. But the storyline was popular enough for the frozen realm to appear a few more times over the course of the 1940s, and the Hand of Zabaranth became one of the relics that the Penitent drew on when he needed to cast spells of cold.

In 1954, the realm vanished along with Campfire Terrors, but it returned in Vanguards #156 as part of the return of Ignition. Here, the realm’s nature was retconned; it had once been an ordinary dimension until it was invaded by the Empress of Ash, and the inhabitants had worked a vast magic to expel all flame from their realm to defeat her. They were successful, but the cost was their civilization and their natures, turning into frosted creatures that hunted for heat to remove from their home. When the Vanguards successfully lured Ignition into Zabaranth, her magical flame was quickly extinguished, forcing her to flee. After that, the realm would appear periodically, usually when someone needed some ice demons in a hurry.

As the United States entered World War II, businesses scrambled to show their support for the cause, putting out comics loudly declaring their patriotism and support. Venture Comics had been actively supporting the Allied Powers for over a year already, first through their Covert Tactics stories and then with the development of Madame Liberty, but there was always room for more, and in 1942 Skybreaker began to face off against Nazi plots and Fomorian tricks designed to weaken the morale of the brave American soldier and the populace that supported him. These storylines were neither particularly nuanced nor, in many cases, particularly well-written. They were mostly chances for Skybreaker to investigate a mysterious situation, discover a dark creature at the heart of it, and then throw his spear at it.

Occasionally, however, Skybreaker was given a chance to use his charisma and drive instead of just fighting, and one of the most famous of these stories occurred in “The Rationing Riots”, the main story for Cryptic Trails #42. In this story, Lewis Lamont returned home to discover simmering discontent over recently-applied rationing for the war effort, with even longtime friends that he believed would support the cause of fighting fascism talking about how annoyed they were not to have enough sugar for their tea, or rubber for their tires. Immediately suspecting Fomorian activity, Lamont set to work investigating.

It wasn’t long before Skybreaker’s efforts uncovered a two-pronged assault on American morale. Nazi spies were stealing rations, artificially limiting supply even further than expected and leading to people being unable to turn in their ration coupons, while Sluagh lurked at the rationing centres and subtly pushed the minds of the citizens to focus only on what they were missing, and not on what it represented. As the discontent began to explode into violent altercations, Skybreaker was forced to confront a riot on the waterfront, fighting to keep innocent people touched by Fomorian power from hurting each other and themselves while he rounded up the Nazi perpetrators.

Ultimately, Skybreaker threw his spear into the clouds, creating a thunderstorm that washed away Sluagh’s hold over the people, and then he faced off against the Nazi spy that Sluagh had empowered and killed him with a single blow of his spear. Sluagh managed to escape, mocking her Nazi master for falling so easily and swearing that she would have another chance to show Skybreaker that he was no better than any other human.

The story wasn’t particularly groundbreaking, but the underlying message would help to set the tone for Skybreaker through the war era – that people who were scared or angry were likely to be manipulated by a few dangerous individuals, rather than being cruel as a group. It helped to cement Sluagh’s relationship to Skybreaker and established the disdain for her would-be masters that kept her from becoming a Nazi villain and fading away, and it provided a classic example of Skybreaker simply creating storms, something that he rarely remembered he was able to do even in much more dangerous situations. And, of course, it expressed public support for rationing and sacrifice on behalf of a greater purpose, without delving into pure hatred of the enemy.

Behind The Scenes:

Thinking about what the superheroes were up to during the war, and of course a lot of it was ‘fighting Nazis’, but I wanted something a little bit unusual for Skybreaker. This served the purpose. Rationing was really kicking off in the US in late 1942, so it would seem like a topical thing that readers would look at and say, “Oh, yeah, I’m also annoyed about this but I’m not going to let myself be tricked into complaining!”

Pure propaganda, but not necessarily in a bad way.

The launch of Flatfoot Adventures in 1942 led to a growth of more brightly lit, technologically-fancy Ferristown locations, many of which were later retconned into being part of Ferrisville. One of the more enduring was Electric Bay, a glittering amusement park on the edge of the lake fueled by the latest in electrical technologies.

Electric Bay first appeared in a storyline featuring the Company. In Flatfoot Adventures #7, the amusement park opened its doors for the first time, showcasing the latest in Ferris Industries technology with a collection of brand new rollercoasters and rides. Ferris hired the best technicians and carnival staff from around the country, but the true purpose of Electric Bay was to cover large-scale smuggling operations from the lake. The crowds and constant trucks of supplies for the midway served as a distraction that would allow Ferris to smuggle in weapons, alcohol, and machinery for his operations.

Flatfoot, on the trail of guns being brought into the city, soon found his way to Electric Bay, but when he discovered a shipment in progress the smugglers immediately opened fire, causing the park’s advanced technology to go a bit haywire and turning the situation into a near-disaster. Fortunately for Flatfoot and unfortunately for the Company, Ferris had failed to understand the carnie mindset. The workers that Ferris had assumed would rally to his troops cared more about the park than they did about their paycheques, and after a brief tussle with the robot lawman (who they instinctively distrusted) the staff of Electric Bay ended up rounding up the smugglers who had damaged their equipment and helped Flatfoot turn them in.

This became a trend for Electric Bay over the next several decades. Re-introduced in the 1960s under new management, the amusement park was a frequent setting for dates, covert meetings with informants, and running battles with supervillains.

Led by new manager Carmine Galbadi, a low-grade crook with aspirations of going straight, the park started to take in other crooks and hoodlums who were tired of the life but didn’t have a way to reintegrate into society easily. The staff of the park carved out a neutral space, fending off attempts by the Company to take over while also holding off the law from inspecting their rides and shipments. Sometimes they would take money to let the Company move goods through their turf, as long as it wasn’t anything too dangerous or liable to bring superheroic eyes on them, and sometimes they would acquire a bit of dangerously advanced technology and immediately integrate it into a new ride to draw in the crowds, but most of the time Electric Bay was more of a backdrop than a faction. Carmine himself was constantly frustrated by Flatfoot, alternating between reluctantly lending the robot a hand and actively getting in the way of his work.

In the Iron Age, Electric Bay would become an ally across the lake for the Rogue Agents, helping them to keep the Company and the Table from spreading back into Ferrisville. Over the course of the 1950s, Flatfoot settled into his role as a ‘special deputy’ of the Ferrisville Police Department. Despite being a problem initially, the feud between Company Town and Flatfoot Adventures had proven to be the character’s salvation; by the time the Comics Code came into effect in 1954, Flatfoot had already almost completely abandoned any stories about corrupt officers or politicians, letting those writers work on the Revenant while Flatfoot dealt with more colourful opposition. As a result, the Silver Age version of the character was a member of a thriving and honest police force. The officers were sometimes too zealous, and there were stories where some beat cop or detective clashed with Flatfoot over their belief that he was no more than a mobile tool that shouldn’t be operating unsupervised, but they always ended with Flatfoot bringing them around to his point of view.

Operating out of the FPD’s Central Precinct building, Flatfoot reported directly to Police Commissioner Whitby, and largely had complete autonomy for patrols and investigations, focusing on the field of super-crime and organized criminal activity. Whitby would give Flatfoot tips when reports of super-powered criminals came in, and he would request Flatfoot’s support for major crime busts and make sure that the superhero’s investigations didn’t run afoul of active police investigations, but for most of the 1950s the only part of the Central Precinct that writers bothered to show was Whitby’s office. Launched in 1961, the bi-monthly Champions of Truth had two major objectives. The early 1960s were largely an excuse for Neutrino to fly to whatever strange and mysterious space things his writers could imagine, do some super-heroing there, and then fly away. No one particularly cared about making them scientifically accurate and most of them never appeared again, but occasionally something would pop up that was strange enough that it would leave a mark on the pages of Venture Comics.

The Zlytaran Nexus was such a place. It first appeared in a simple story in 1961, in which it was not particularly interrogated or discussed. It was simply a mine where ‘Time was mined’, with a vague implication that this was a fuel used by interstellar travel. Neutrino saved the miners from a space beast that devoured Time to grow incredibly quickly, and moved on.

In Celestial Travels #298 and #299, though, the Nexus would become a much bigger part of Neutrino’s story. The Interstellar Trading Guild had just made overtures to the Nexus and its workers, but as they sought to bring it into the fold, the region was attacked by Subjugator. She was in search of the powerful time crystals that formed in the nebula’s unusual radiations, and which had the power to speed up or slow down the flow of time. Rumours said that particularly pure crystals could allow for actual time travel, and Subjugator intended to travel back in time and steal rare artifacts before they became rare, so that she wouldn’t have to deal with any of the security that now surrounded them.

Neutrino responded to a distress call from his reluctant ally Lieutenant Heerah, using the ITG’s gates to quickly skip back to the Nexus. After a brief dogfight that saw the Aurora Borealis nearly destroyed, he slipped into the mines themselves, tracking down Heerah and saving her from the Starbooters before challenging Subjugator directly. Her reckless operation was threatening to unleash the temporal energies of the nebula, dooming everyone present, and Neutrino tried and failed to convince her that her goal was just too dangerous.

In the ensuing fight, the Aurora Borealis was destroyed – but at the start of Celestial Travels #299 a stray time eddy *un-*destroyed it, allowing Neutrino to pursue Subjugator as she navigated the Nexus towards the pure time crystals that she had discovered. Neutrino defeated Subjugator and freed the Nexus, but in the battle his own radioactive energies merged with the energies of the Nexus, leading him to spend Celestial Travels #300 hopping through his own timeline, attempting to change history to prevent himself from leaving Earth and ultimately only managing to create the conditions that had allowed him to survive his transformation.

The Zlytaran Nexus remained a popular part of Venture’s galactic network, however. Zlytaran crystals could be used as a shorthand to empower various science fiction technologies, allowing for rapid cloning devices, specialized healing chambers, and stasis fields that could be deployed by individual supervillains but whose extremely rare power source kept them from being something that had to be reckoned with as a common part of galactic life. Over the years the Nexus would be attacked by the Uranians, the Kliz’Exth, and various smaller groups, but without the generations of knowledge that the local miners had developed to manage and live in harmony with the local temporal landscape, none of them were able to hold it for long.

The Riddlemaster immediately perked up and began creating obstacles and puzzles for Mr. Infinity. It quickly became obvious that the entire fabric of the dimension was theirs to command, able to create illusions of life and even rewrite the Vanguards’ powers and abilities on a whim. If they stayed for too long, they would begin to break down existence and absorb it into their own lonely realm, which was why their visits were so short.

When Doctor Cosmos left the Vanguards to travel with the Celestial Travellers, the Riddlemaster followed her, becoming a recurring character in Celestial Travels while also popping in to Earth to offer new challenges to the Vanguards. Both teams were fond of but frustrated by the godling, and did their best to guide it to better life choices while surviving the nonsense it threw their way. This helped it to stay relevant while the Vanguards were out of the picture in the 1990s, and they remained a minor fan favorite through to the present.

In the early years of the Riddlemaster’s appearances, it would pop up as a man, woman, both, or neither according to its whim; the 2010s codified them as a being that had been non-binary even before developing infinite cosmic power, and who used that power to maintain whatever gender was most comfortable in the moment.

In the late 1960s, beginning with Edwin Sherman and continuing with new writer Matt Solares in 1698, a series of Covert Tactics stories began to wrestle lightly with the question of interventionism and how to best to handle the team’s fights against mad scientists and autocratic powers. Solares frequently used these stories to flesh out the competing drives of Kid Liberty, who favoured an aggressive approach, with the much more cautious attitudes of the Steward, who had centuries of experience in the potential for meddling in other societies to go wrong. Pulsejet tended to side with Kid Liberty, Irogane was more in line with the Steward, and Big Brain would shift back and forth depending on the situation, creating a complex team dynamic that was highly popular. New supervillains introduced in this period were driven by arrogance, greed, or beliefs in their own cultural superiority, forcing the team to protect innocent civilians from the machinations of powerful villains.

The Kurkani Civil War, however, took all of this to a new level. The storyline began in Covert Tactics #82, an issue that barely included the team at all, with the discovery that the small South Asian nation of Kurkan had been invaded and conquered almost overnight by an unlikely enemy – Jonah Drake, the Human Torpedo! The aquatic hero had taken it upon himself to organize a coup against the Kurkani Crown, seeking to dismantle the ‘malignant societal conditions’ that he saw as responsible for decades of violence by instilling strict moral and cultural regimens of his own devising. He believed that he could transform Kurkan into a gleaming utopia within two generations, and had just enough military support to undertake the effort.

Drake had had a run of antagonistic encounters with other heroes throughout the late 1960s. Raised under euthenicist principles, Drake firmly believed that with the proper upbringing and environment anyone could be physically, mentally, and morally upstanding, but he also believed that many of his fellow heroes lacked the moral and environmental training required to handle the problems that they faced. He was particularly harsh to heroes with criminal or difficult pasts such as Madame Liberty or Big Brain; while he applauded their drive to become better people, he believed that their pasts put a limit on how far they could improve and had clashed with several teams already over differing beliefs in how best to resolve dangerous situations.

But even for the Human Torpedo, overthrowing a country and trying to dismantle their culture was a massive escalation. Hoping that Drake’s old ally the Steward (one of the few heroes that he trusted and respected) might be able to talk sense into him, Covert Tactics deployed to Kurkan just in time to discover that the situation had already gotten immeasurably worse. Loyalists to the autocrat king, backed by a mixture of old guard believers and citizens who had been opposed to the crown but were now furious at having their way of life turned upside-down, had appointed Dr. Roach as the leader of their military efforts. With the Kurkani treasury at his disposal, Dr. Roach was using wildly dangerous experimental ionic technology to attack Drake’s military forces, as the would-be hero and the mercenary villain clashed across the country.

The effects of the Sovereign of Secrets and their meddling in the timelines of the world weren’t limited to the heroes of Venture Comics. Through the mid-1980s, writers took advantage of the adjustments to revise, retcon, or otherwise alter the backstories and histories of several of Venture Comics’ villains. Mr. Ferris became a younger man, back in control of his Ferristown empire. White Mantis became two generations of supervillain, both drawing on the history of the original. Poison Pen’s stint as a small-time mercenary vanished along with much of her Silver Age history, moving her back into the shadows as a villain known to only a handful of heroes.

Not all of these changes were so direct, however. Because the situation with the Sovereign was a soft reset with an in-universe reason to exist, some writers chose to play with the situation, exploring how the adjusted world might affect characters more directly. Jordan Marks, taking over Champions of Truth, decided to have some fun by doing this with the Head Master.

In the “Head in the Clouds” two-parter in mid-1985, Marks revealed that Head Master’s predictive abilities were in absolute chaos. His powers were based on subconsciously gathering information from the past and present to predict likely outcomes with near-perfect accuracy, but the past was no longer a reliable source of information. A mess of stacked timelines had left the villain with crippling migraines and an inability to track what was happening or would happen, constantly reacting to situations that weren’t happening or preparing for eventualities that the Sovereign’s interference had wiped clean from existence. In a desperate attempt to repair his capabilities, Head Master reached out to a Dread Power. He opened a portal to the Empress of Ash, seeking to burn away the false timelines in his head and restore his power.

It didn’t take a master foreteller to know what would happen next. The Empress tried to seize control of the ritual, hoping to transform the villain into her latest minion for her assault on Earth. With limited understanding of magic and dimensional powers, Head Master found himself locked in a mental battle with a fundamental force of devastation as prophecy and fate began to burn around him. The new Champions of Truth arrived, but were quickly assaulted by both the forces of the Empress and the minions of Head Master trying to keep the heroes from their boss.

The resulting battle was fierce. Wonderer and Reverie repaired the damaged ley lines, Greenheart and Skybreaker took the battle to the streets and cleared away both the demons and gangsters that opposed them, and the Penitent sought out Head Master directly, severing the connection between him and his would-be rescuer and delivering a very impassioned speech about the absolutely insane danger that he’d put himself and everyone else in.

At which point the Head Master smirked, told the Penitent that actually, everything had gone according to plan, and triggered his contingency plan, escaping into a secret passage warded against magical intrusion and leaving the Champions to clean up his mess. He’d always known that they would respond to a sufficiently chaotic situation, and had deliberately planned for them to save him from a situation that he knew he couldn’t handle alone. The battle had cleared his powers, aligning him to the new timelines, and he was ready to become a menace once again.

“Head in the Clouds” served to re-establish the threat of the Head Master without undermining the Champions, while also helping to play with the complex situation of the post-Sovereign world and touching on the potential return of the other Dread Powers. Vortex’s first major foray in the Iron Age was the “Like a Hurricane” storyline. Working with a small terrorist cell that called themselves the Red Wind, and who wished to return the world to its agricultural roots, Vortex connected herself to a dangerously untested hurricane generator, using it to boost her powers to a point where she could wipe an entire city off the map. The incident helped to cement Covert Tactics’ distrust of AEGIS, develop an ideology for Vortex that worked alongside her more common mercenary work without making her particularly sympathetic, and made it clear just how dangerous the new world order was becoming. The popularity of the comic led to the growth of AEGIS as an active threat rather than an obstacle, with readers agreeing that AEGIS’s willingness to kill thousands to ‘protect’ the rest of the city showing that they were one of the villains that needed to be dealt with.

Flat and sleek, with ‘solar sails’ on the sides and a shimmering cockpit in the back, the ship held enough room for a crew of six, although for most of the 1970s only three people occupied it for any length of time and Wavelength and Doctor Cosmos occupied the same crew cabin to ‘save space’. It also featured an integrated artificial intelligence called “Aurora” – although not technically sentient, Aurora had a rudimentary ability to respond to threats and anticipate the needs of the crew, and most of them often interacted with it like one would a particularly smart pet.

When the Celestial Travelers grew to five members after Peacemonger’s invasion, the first comics issue was dedicated to their ship and the complicated relationship of three people who had been journeying together for some time suddenly needing to open themselves to a new romantic couple joining the team, a pair who were still reeling from the dissolution of their own team and their need to not be on Earth for a while. Half-Life and Ember were grateful to join the Celestial Travelers; Half-Life in particular was glad to be with his old partner Wavelength again, and to help to recover his still-fragmented memories, and Ember was glad to have a chance to prove herself. Half of Celestial Travels #601 was dedicated to the five heroes working around each other, figuring out where they stood, and deciding whether their new arrangement was going to be temporary or ongoing.

The other half was about Subjugator and the Starbooters trying to hijack the ship, having carefully set up a team designed to hit Neutrino, Wavelength, and Reckoner where they were weakest.

The pirates overloaded Aurora, confusing the AI and shorting out the ship’s internal sensors, and then invaded armed with cosmic bafflers and nuclear-absorbing technology. Reckoner was still wrestling with her new powers and was quickly taken down, and Wavelength was caught mid-teleport and knocked out. Half-Life caught the raiders off-guard, but his nuclear powers were similar enough to Neutrino’s to be vulnerable – but Ember’s command of flame was not, and in fact the technology the Starbooters were using proved to be particularly vulnerable to overheating. Despite gravity failures, several failed attempts by Aurora to assist with the situation, and Subjugator’s raw power, the Starbooters were quickly forced back, their ship entering a warp path while damaged and vanishing into the depths of space.

The Celestial Travelers were quickly established after that. While they might bicker and need to figure out their space, they’d been through a confusing, complicated mess and come out the other side, and their further adventures across the Iron Age would reinforce their connections and gradually help them become a family.

By the early 1990s, Venture was using its ‘Annual’ issues primarily for big summer and winter crossover events. Most of these events featured three or four annual issues, along with one or two standard issues or one-shots linked to the main event taking place. In the summer of 1990, the decision was made for the main annual crossover to be between Knightgrave and the Champions of Truth. Greenheart was in Japan on her ill-considered ‘ninja’ arc, although she was returning to the Champions periodically for their own battles, and it seemed like a good time for a few of the Champions to come to England for a devastating battle against a foe that had lost some of his shine over the past few years.

X-Caliber had become a bit of a joke in the late 1980s; after a strong start in the first two years of Knightgrave, he’d faded into the background as Knightgrave dealt with the Ravenmaster, Sleight, and the six demon princes seeking Merlin’s head. His most recent appearance, the year before, had featured an attempt to join forces with the Dread Dynasty that ended with him mocked and spurned by the power-supremacist aristocrats. In the hopes of revitalizing the villain and moving him back into position as Knightgrave’s greatest enemy, the Venture editorial room collaborated on a new attempt to bring ruin to England.

The story kicked off in Knightgrave Annual #3, with Baron Carmichael discovering the grave of Mordred Pendragon. His men were able to unearth remnants of Mordred’s endless battles during his centuries of death, coalescing into blood crystals that they were able to use to exsanguinate captive civilians. The resulting crystals held phenomenal power, and X-Caliber was able to use them to construct his ultimate flying fortress, Neo-Avalon! Powered by ancient blood magic mixed with modern technology, the fortress was able to project an energy shield that guarded it from missiles and assault. With his forces arrayed behind him, X-Caliber flew Neo-Avalon into the heart of London, threatening to rain death upon the city unless the House of Windsor abdicated and declared him the rightful King of England. His knights began kidnapping government officials and civil servants, holding them as hostages that could be used to bolster the blood crystals powering his ship and his followers.

Knightgrave tried to handle Neo-Avalon himself, but the fortress’s magic was turned on him, weakening his own blood magic and nearly ending his immortal life. X-Caliber stabbed him through the heart and threw him from the platform to drown in the Thames. At the last moment, Knightgrave was rescued by Wonderer, Penitent, and Skybreaker, who had arrived in the nick of time to deal with what was rapidly becoming an international incident.

Over the course of Skybreaker Annual #18 and Champions of Truth Annual #12, the four heroes set themselves against Neo-Avalon, using a mixture of force, guile, and persuasion to find and turn the members of Carmichael’s household who were horrified at the murders taking place against him, weaken the blood-driven engine, and ultimately direct the entire fortress into the English Channel. The final battle was fierce, with Knightgrave returning the blade Carmichael had driven into him back into X-Caliber’s chest. The fortress sank, and the synthesized blood crystals were lost, preventing another from being created.

Carmichael survived, of course. He successfully argued in court that he had been corrupted by exposure to an undead knight, and that Mordred Pendragon had been the one truly responsible for Neo-Avalon. Most people didn’t believe him, but just enough did to keep Knightgrave in an uncomfortable position with the law and to keep X-Caliber out of jail. He would go on to be a persistent thorn in Knightgrave’s side, wielding power and the law against him.
