My normal activity of making heroes has for the moment slowed down, and I'm curious to hear other people's thoughts on a red zone ability.

The social quality list contains Lead By Example, which I am just now taking a really good look at.

Lead By Example: Make a basic action using [Quality]. Use your Max die. All other heroes who take the same basic action on their turn against the same target receive a Boost from your Mid+Min dice.

First, an interpretation qestion: When another hero uses that basic action, does the Mid+Min boost apply to their effect die, or is it just a bonus they can use anytime after?

Second: Has anyone else seen this ability in play? It looks potentially absurd, like most red zone abilities, but because of it's utility instead of raw power. The ability to Overcome, Attack, Defend, Boost, or Hinder using your max die using a social quality feels like something that could easily be used even if your allies don't play along and use basic actions. So yeah, looking for thoughts on this.

Given the wording, that the other heroes have to take the same basic action against the same target, would seem to suggest that the boost will be applied to that action.  It seems the most logical interpretation.

As for how absurd it is, that is a rather subjective opinion.  Can't really say how useful it would be overall, but I am sure that players of more subtle or support characters might choose it because it better fits their hero's concept than a more direct (and arguably powerful) ability.  One hero I could definitely see possessing such an ability would be Captain America, though I can't think of a specific incident that fits such an ability that he has had (though if you give me a few days, I am sure I can find something... maybe from the Earth's Mightiest Heroes cartoon?).

Seems fine compared to other Red abilities.  Lots of them let you use all three dice for a specific type of action, so using your Max only but having the freedom to choose any action type is more versatile but less powerful.  And any allies who want to take advantage of the Boost have to use a basic action, which means they aren't using their own abilities (and most likely they also have access to the powerful Red ones), not even their Principles.  Plus there's a limit to how useful it is to do exactly the same thing--at some point that enemy will go down or that challenge will be overcome.

Unless they're cheesing it with combos--a whole team with Lead By Example could get nuts fast, and it would also be great with Mutable Form or Inspiring Totem--I don't see a problem.

I see it as a Social Quality Red Zone Ability because it's not strictly multitasking.

Banter? Oh yeah, you're doing your thing telling witty jokes. But that witty joke doesn't come on it's own; You set up an ally along with it, or use it to distract a foe at an oppurtune moment. Maybe it comes in alongside a knuckle sandwich for the punchline, or you bring up your guard when they strike before delivering the punchline.

Imposing? Your ally knows that the foe is paying attention to you, or you make a foe mess up on their next action. You can shrug off a punch from someone without as much presence, or maybe you just outright scare them in surrendering. All of that could happen just by doing things being imposing.

Insight? You can tell that someone isn't being forthright, or read their body language for an advantage. Or, you read the crowd of minions and just naturally discern a weak point in their formation for an ally to exploit. Body language can reveal an incoming attack, or just reveal their guard being lowered for an oppurtune strike.

Leadership? Your allies are waiting for encouragement, or your foes backing off from a shout of defiance. You kick down a locked door to clear the way forward, punching a minion to get them out of the way of your group, or put up your guard as they begin to open fire.

Persuasion? Call out to a foe who's wavering to further shake their commitment, or suggest a nearby cop help out your friend who's helping a cat out of a tree. A precisely worded offer could make someone's brain hurt to grasp, or maybe you convince the BBEG to give you just a moment to drop your weapons.

 

None of those are just multitasking. They are doing extra things, by being social.

If it’s a normal villain, you could be insulting or demoralizing them–remember, “damage” doesn’t necessarily mean physical damage. One of the examples of an attack in the rulebook is a taunt.

For other types of enemies, like machines, it’s a little tougher, because most enemies don’t have immunities. You can attack an automated turret with telepathy just fine, for instance. It could just be a follow-up with a quip–an extra panel where the turret slumps pathetically and collapses and the hero makes a snide comment.
