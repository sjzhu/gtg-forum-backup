The OP mentioned using Metal and Creativity (rather than Metal and Technology), but the same issue applies.  Flinging a shard of metal at a target doesn't particularly represent a manifestation of either Creativity or Technology.

The word "creativity" implies using abilities in a novel or unorthodox manner, something new or beyond the norm.  Simply throwing metal shards at something-- even sharpened ones-- isn't either novel or unorthodox.  Nor does it represent any particular level of technology, beyond that of a sharpened shard.

A potential Creativity + Metal attack could easily exist, perhaps as something like,  "I'm going to take those two pieces of sheet metal and tekekinetically snap them shut around the creature like a bear trap" or "I'm going to swing that giant wrecking ball around like a flail and slam it into the creature."  But as it's described above, I'd probably rule the same as your GM: Metal + Ranged Combat (or Thrown Weapons, or Telekinesis, or some other attacking power/quality).

The case can surely be made for "Describe cool things, and then find dice for them," but what's being described in the original post seems more to be along the lines of "I want to use my best dice, regardless of whether they apply to what I want to do."  Not accusing or judging, here--there are surely ways to use the combo described above, but doing so seems like it should take more than "I throw metal at it. I'd like to add some things as well;

1) The question comes down to what counts as 'creative'. The game would allow a character to use their 'creative' quality for any action that is 'creative'. However, the ultimate qualifier for whether or not the action is 'creative' comes from the GM. The game might include descriptions in the full release for people who prefer a concrete definition or basis, but it wouldn't change the fact that the GM is the arbitor for it.
2) Picking the GM and RPG group that is right for you should always be a concern, but on the GM's side of things, they should probably not say "no" to their players, and instead say "yes/no, and..." In this sense, the GM made a mistake not allowing 'creative' to work for the action.(because they should've given alternative options, penalties, or explanations for the action. But GMs aren't immune to making mistakes)
3) From there, the question becomes "how important is this to me that this works?" Many people would laugh at the idea that you are complaining about 'making a worse attack', when the GM presumably took much more time and/or thought to run the scenario. But a significant difference, one that might mean the difference between a close success and horrible failure, might be one a player should confront the GM about.(I know there have been a few times in my RPG sessions where I felt the GM ruled 'stupidly', and it ruined the session.)
4) Which comes to the final point; what is the statistical difference between rolling a d4 and a d10 in that scenario, and this system in general? The answer is actually not much. I could do the math, but it should be clear that with the extra dice messing with the average, the difference is less than +3 to the roll. That, combined with the fact that getting a TPK(total party kill) probably didn't hinge on the roll, meant that letting it slide is probably the more mature and fun way to continue the game.

TL;DR The GM probably could've ruled in a way that was more fun for you and the group, but it might be more fun to just roll with the lower rolls. And when you do have a situation that is more serious, make sure to be clear to the GM when you aren't having fun with the way they are GM-ing, and hopefully they can show they deserve your time by making a responsible decision so that everyone can have fun.
