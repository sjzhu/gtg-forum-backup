From what we’ve seen on the livestreams, it’s exactly the opposite of “labyrinthine.”  It’s very linear and holds your hand the whole way.  I’m not sure how much it gives up in the way of flexibility, though–Christopher’s players all used the random approach and just went wherever the system took them, and Paul’s players were talking over each other the whole time and it was hard to follow.

As far as I could tell, the guided approach limits players to about 3 options when they make a decision. It seems good for players new to the system, or to roleplay in general, since fewer options make choices quicker and easier. Restrictions like that can also help with creativity.

The free approach is apparently the same creation, but choosing the dice rolls. Way more flexibility, but potentially overwhelming. It seems like a good system for players with a character concept in mind (which probably includes everyone who’s been following the game this much).

This is from memory so I may be miss-remembering the details. 

With the dice method, I think for each step, you roll 3 dice (d10s, maybe) and you can pick the sum of any 2, and even then you can re-roll if you don't like what you got. 

Or with the guided method, you just pick something off the list at each step. 

It may seem like you have to use the dice because the 1st mini-campaign, everyone used dice and some folks used dice in the 2nd.

But if you sit down to make a character and say "I want to play a Rook City accountant named Steve Fiddler who was feeding info on the Organization to Mr Fixer becase when Steve was a kid, he took classes at the dojo, and then the Organization burned his house down, but the experimental chemicals used to set the fire gave Steve fire powers like the Human Torch, and now Steve fights crime as 'Dragonfire," then you can just do that, picking the appropriate items off the lists. 

You may have gotten that impression because I think none of the players in either game sat down with that firm a concept, so they used dice to sort of help them along.
