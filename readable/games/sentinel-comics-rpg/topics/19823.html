Just a few friendly suggestions on the way I tend to handle things like this:

- The bomb goes through the portal, but nothing happens. The team learns that the bomb definitely ended up in Russia, definitely should have exploded, but they can't find any evidence that it did. They realize that someone stopped the bomb, and now that Someone is very interested in who dropped a bomb in Russia. This could involve the team Perestroika, or just a random Russian villain you invent.

- The bomb goes off in a remote part of Russia. However, since there's no clear source of the bomb - no plane, no missile, etc - the world blames something/someone else - aliens, Oblivaeon, the Citizens of the Sun, etc. The team has to find a way to either keep the world ignorant or inform the world that it was their fault.

- The bomb goes off in a remote part of Russia, creating a super-threat (kaiju!) that is now a new problem for the players. Also, possibly Russia/the world is kind of mad at them for that whole thing.

This isn't really about "the world of Sentinels comics" so much as just how you can avoid worrying about world politics and global nuclear war in a globe-spanning role-playing game involved super-powered people. ;-)

I know that it's probably way too late, but the way I would've handled it was to have them roll an Overcome for disposing of the bomb, with the mandate that any resulting Twist would be a story twist for later.  That way the players feel like they still have agency, and the players (though not the characters) get foreshadowing as far as how annoying the consequences should be.  Plus it gives you some kind of guideline for how annoying to make said consequences!

I think the already mentioned suggestions of having the blast just so happen to further destabalize the russian power plant (again, to what degree being based on Twist severity) is the best way to go here.
