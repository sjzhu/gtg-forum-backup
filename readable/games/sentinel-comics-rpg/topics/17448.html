First time attempting a GM role with a group who is playing this as their first tabletop RPG. We played our first session last night and I struggled a bit, as would be expected.

One of the things that was hard for me was keeping track of the many minions, in the first scene there will be 3 x Heroes minions. With 3 PCs that's 9 minions. I had a hard time keeping tack of which minions were boosted and which minions were hindered at any given time. Also there is no board position to worry about but I split the minions up (3 are attacking Legacy and 3 more are attacking Bunker) so now I have to keep track of which ones are attacking who.

I was trying to keep track of it in notes but it was pretty unorganized with lots of numbers and crossed-out numbers.

 

So does anyone have some good advice on how to keep track of which minions are hindered and boosted and what they are doing from round to round? Or am I just overcomplicating this? Or maybe I'm making it too simple and I should have a note card for every minion instead of minon groups?

I find it helpful to have a notebook to write out notes in whenever I GM a game, it helps keep my thoughts straight. I also keep an extra set of dice about just in case I want something I can use to represent arbitrary numbers.

One thing you could do in that particular situation is have a die to represent each minion. First divide them up into groups of three(one per hero) then raise the die number for each time a minion gets a boost. That way you can know at a glance how many boosts a minion has. :)

What I tend to do is a blend of what you are already doing and some of Phantom5613's suggestions.

I write down minion groups, plus individual Lieutenants and Villains, on note cards. For Lieutenants and Minion Groups, each individual NPC is represented by its die, which I stack on the notecard. That way, if I have a group of 6 d6 Spider Bots, I have 6d6 stacked on a notecard that says "Spider Bots". If any bot is downgraded, I swap out a d6 for a d4. If any bot is Boosted or Hindered, I write out another note card for that Boost/Hinder (e.g. "Broken Legs -2"), and put it underneath that specific bot's die, overlapping the "Spider Bots" minion group card.

Does that all make sense?

Colored paperclips on the notecard. Can get 100s for cheap and have them indicate different statuses. Dice are a must for visuals, but if you don't have a collection of dice yet you could just write "d6 d6 d6" on the card and downgrade them as they go.

I wouldn't worry about keeping track of specific penalties or bonuses to specific minions. Groups of minions facing that hero, yes, but down to the individual minion? Too tedious to track IMO. I would have the penalty apply to the entire group of minions so that math on each roll is minimal. I'd keep the action flowing for minions and leave specific tracking for lieutenants and higher.

I only had 6 spider bots to deal with when running Scene 1 of Issue 1, but I simply just wrote them out vertically on a piece of paper. Something like:

Spider Bots:
D6
D6
D6

D6
D6
D6

I always had the first one vertically in each group use webs, the 3rd one attack, and the middle one boost. I dealt with the bots one group at a time, the middle acting first, followed by the bit receiving the boost (so it was easy to remember), and then whichever not was left.

It worked well enough for me, and when the heroes wanted to attack “the one closest to Unity shooting webs”, I knew which one was receiving the attack. (And if the bit survived the attack, I just crossed out D6 and wrote D4 beside it).

To help keep track of which squads have taken turns, and which squads are "nearby" (for purposes of hypersonic assault, etc.) I actually break up by squads into half-index cards or post-its. I labeled the top with the hero they would initially target, and then place my 3d6 in a little triangle that I draw on the cards, where the front d6 is always the attacker, the back left is always boosting, and the bottom right is always hindering. This helps me with the boosting and hindering as well, since the boosts are "always" to the attacking bot, I just slot in a half-index card under the squad card that tells what that bot's boost is.
Thinking of your low die counts, you could actually erase or cross off points of the triangle as bots get punched, freeing up the actual dice for the necessary rolls.

This also helped me re-consolidate squads as they got picked off, as the heroes may be split into two(or more!) groups, which meant that lone bots from one "area" wouldn't attempt to re-squad with bots from the other "area".

Bonus use: roaming spider bots in Iss. 1, sc. 2 can be split into smaller groups that are threatening individual heroes by re-using the half-cards that have the heroes name up top. Really helps to limit an overeager Tachyon player.(maybe letting that player try some overcomes to find that network =])
