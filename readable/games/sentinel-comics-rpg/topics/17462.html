@MindWanderer & @Jeysie

You are absolutely right! Generally when starting a scene, I have some NPCs act first, or let a specific Hero start, depending on what makes the most narrative sense. Then, each player gets to choose who follows them, as long as they haven't already gone in the round. Once every single PC and NPC has gone in one round, the last player gets to choose which PC or NPC begins the next round.

When running demos, players will generally have all of the heroes go and forget to give initiative to the NPCs. I then use this as a teaching moment; after all of the NPCs go once, (say, Minion Group 1 -> Lieutenant), I then explain that I could begin the next round by going "Minion Group 1 -> Lieutenant" again. They immediately see how terrible that would be. I then pass initiative to a hero instead, and let them know that next time I won't be so nice. After that illustration, all of the groups I've ever demod for have immediately grasped the strategy of deciding when to pass things off to the NPCs for the round.

You have the Attack action right, but not Defend. Defend is a proactive normal action that reduces the amount of damage taken before your next action. You can choose yourself or someone else to Defend (at the time you take the action).

You can also “hit the deck” as a Reaction to Defend yourself only, but doing so inflicts a Minor Twist–it’s effectively already a Risky Action. I’d personally rule that extending that to use “hit the deck” on an ally should be used sparingly, if at all, would require a vivid description, and would incur a Major Twist instead of a minor one, but that’s just me.
