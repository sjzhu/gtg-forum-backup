I use the books as a framework, but ultimately, anything can change.  For instance, in the Void, I seperated the whole party and they were each tested for their worthiness to reach the void crystal.  Legacy had to save he daughter (pre powers) from Baron Blade. Wraith had to learn the lesson of relying on her friends. I re-enacted Spite in the bell tower, except she was able to save Sara and Eduardo with the help of the Freedom Five.  Absolute Zero had to face a fire breathing dragon in a lava pit to save Tachyon. Tachyon had to outrun Friction and save her from herself. Unity, was faced with a choice between machines and humanity as her best friend, Omnitron X, went rogue and was trying to kill the Freedom Five. 

Anyway, the books are just a guide.  Espicially issues like Tomb of the Bizzare.  Just some room descriptions, a scene tracker, and some ideas for obstacles.  It's all up to the GM to create the connective tissue, or just start from scratch and make it your own.  

Hope that helps! :)

I think the key is to not pitch the game as an RPG.  When I talk about it I say that this group is a team of writers at a comic book company.  They are primary writers for their hero and I am the editor in chief.  We are going to write a series of stories together.  Each issue is part of a mini story arc (collection), and the dice are going to help us guide the story in exciting directions.  In terms of each character succeeding or failing a particular mission; it may or may not affect how the story is told next issue, but it's still your issue.  All the experience is the same either way, and once we finish the story, you'll have a collection to draw upon the next time we write another story.

There is no winning or losing this game. A failure to defeat the villain at the end makes your heros even more interesting, because they now have the potential to regroup, retrain, rebound, and defeat the villain the next time.  Spiderman wasn't able to save Gwen Stacey. He wouldn't even be Spiderman if he was able to save Uncle Ben that one night. Superman got killed by Doomsday only to return a year later even stronger. The most intersting stories in comics are the ones where the heros are forced to overcome the greatest odds, and they are often beaten and bloodied, forever changed by the trauma. 

Metagaming this system will just make for a boring story.  Maybe turn it around on them.  Create your own metagame where each of the comic writers are competing for issue sales.  The hero with the best role playing, most creative use of powers, and overcomming to hardest failure us able to sell the most issues that month. Kind of a game outside the game. :)
