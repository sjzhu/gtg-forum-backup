1) Bonuses vs. Multiple Effects.

For me, this is a GM ruling issue, which keys primarily off of what the player wants to come from it.  Let's say, for example, that The Wraith wants to find some cover to hide behind during a pitched combat.  Given that, I'd likely say that, were she rolling Strike From the Shadows, the bonus would apply to the Defense (Min die), rather than the actual attack.  However, if she wanted to sharpen her Grappling Hooks, I'd say that the bonus would apply to the initial Attack.

The text of the Gameplay Guide says (p. 7)--"...a mod applies to the effect die rolled on a later die roll, either adding to or subtracting from the result.  You, as the creator of the mod, can usually decide who uses that bonus or penalty as long as it makes sense in the fiction of the scene..."  

2) Legacy vs. Scene Tracker

I'd probably rule that, since he succeeded the Overcome action, that the T-Rex's actions would not be able to accelerate the Scene Tracker, but the end of the round would still escalate the scene as normal.  He just would prevent it from going farther.

3) Legacy vs. Fortitude

Yeah, the T-Rex would deal physical damage, so he should reduce it by 1.

4) Tachyon vs. her Principles

I'd probably rule that only one Principle could be invoked on a given Overcome roll.  As such, Tachyon could gain two Hero Points in quick succession by zipping up the side of the building where Blade has set up his gravity generator (Principle of Speed), then using her scientific knowledge to shut down his device safely (Principle of Science).  Even though they're close together, they'd require two separate rolls (which might invoke twists or the like, on either roll).  Principle of Speed seems to be more along the lines of overcoming physical obstacles:  "I need to beat Baron Blade across the city:  I'm going to roll Speed + Finesse + Status to make my way through traffic.  By the time Blade gets there, I'll already be waiting for him..."

Thank you everyone for the responses.

 

A couple of follow-up questions:

 

The concensus seems to be that Tachyon's Principle of Speed cannot stack with her other principle.  This seems reasonable to me, but leaves me wondering what the purpose of making the principle inherent was.  Movement in this game seems to be mostly dramatic (in the play example: Legacy was able to arrive at the scene, fly over to talk to the cops, then fly over and punch the dinosaur all in his first turn).  Characters have no movement-specific stats and there wasn't a discussion of zones or anything similar; the intent seems to be that movement and location are handled narratively (fast and loose) like in Feng Shui or Cortex (a system that Cam Banks has previously worked on).  This leaves only a couple possible interpretations for what Tachyon's prinicple of speed actually does. 

 

The first (and the one I favored before reading these responses) is that all heroes can move freely until they take their action, but their turn normally ends after their action.  Overcoming and then darting out before an opponent can react would be a risky action (taking a minor twist for something extra).  Tachyon has an exception, when she successfully Overcomes (though not when she uses any of the other actions and not if she fails), she can always move elsewhere afterwards (and generates a hero point when she does so).  But it seems that if this was intended to be an exclusive ability, it would have been easier to make it an action.

 

An alternative interpretation (and the one that, if I'm understanding correctly, is favored here) is that Tachyon's principle of speed works basically like all the other principles we see and is just oddly phrased.  In this interpretation, the only real difference between Tachyon's speed and Legacy's leadership is that instead of using her max die to overcome, Tachyon gets to move afterwards if she succeeds.

 

In either case, this principle's rules text is clearly unusual.  It's the only prinicple in any of the included character booklets that is marked as an inherent ability instead of an action.  It's the only one that doesn't allow the hero to use their max die.  It's the only principle that requires success in order to generate a hero point.  And it's the only ability that explicitly mentions movement.

 

 

As a completely unrelated question: what type of action is Unity's golem creation?  Most Fate flavors (Fate is clearly one of the inspirations for this system) avoid one-off actions, using Overcome or Create an Advantage (the Fate equivalent of Boost/Hinder) as a catch-all action for things like this.  The specific reason this matters is that Legacy's Galvanize ability specifically affects Attack and Overcome actions by his allies.  I'd like to know if Unity can benefit from Galvanize when creating golems.

Re: Tachyon’s movement. The ability to move around after overcoming will show itself to be more useful as you get to to later issues.

Principle of Speed applies to every successful overcome, so even if you can’t trigger it off of your Principle of Science overcome, you’ll have plenty of other opportunities to use it.

And for what it’s worth, on Saturday I allowed one of my players to trigger Principle of Speed after succeeding on her Principle of Science overcome. However there was only 1 hero point. (The extra movement wasn’t really useful, but the player did use it to add some flair).
