I went to my pictures of the character book that I took at the demo, and here are all the references to defensive/healing type mechanics that I could see:

Defend

Your hero is always taking steps to protect themselves, but there are times when they want to focus on protecting themselves or someone else. In a Defend action, you describe what you hero is doing to avoid or reduce an Attack. Assemble your dice pool, roll those dice, and note your result. When you or the target you are Defending would next lose Health before your next turn, reduce the amount taken by your Defend result. If you or the target you're Defending are not Attacked before your next turn, the effect is wasted.

If you are under the effect of multiple Defend actions, choose the higher result to use; do not combine them.

Risky Actions

You can choose to add an extra effect to your basic action by taking a risky action. There will definitely be fallout--hence the "risky" part--so choose an appropriate minor twist when you decide to take a risky action.

A risky action only works for a basic action, and should only be as circumstances warrant it. If you're doing something you've practiced, it's an ability

Hit the Deck!

A common risky action is to take a Defend action when facing an enemy's massive attack. A basic Defend action can be done out of turn as a reaction by activating a minor twist. You may only Defend yourself this way and not others.

Reactions

Reactions (labeled with an R) are specific actions you can do in response to something happening. No matter how many abilities you have that grant reactions, you can only perform one reaction per round. This resets at the start of your turn.

Recover

Some abilities or other circumstances let you use a die from your roll to Recover as part of your action. Regain lost Health equal to that die's result, up to the maximum of your Green zone range. For recovering outside of action scenes, see Montage Scenes.

Montage Scenes

When moving between action scenes, the heroes might be in a montage scene to cover what happens in the downtime. Heroes can also use montage scenes to Recover lost Health if there's time to do so. Each hero describes how they recover from the events of the previous action scene. They recover Health up to the maximum of the next zone up. They may choose to take a minor twist (that lasts through the next scene as well) to Recover two zones, or if they were in dire straights, they may take a major twist to recover all of their Health at once.

 

There is also the fact that as you move from one zone to the next from taking damage, more abilities get unlocked and are available for use. So while you might consider taking damage again and again to be ridiculous, it does result in your character becoming more powerful and having more options to fight back or react to incoming damage. And even when you are Out, you still provide a unique ability for anyone on your team to use, just like in the card game.

I've not had the chance to play the game, having missed out on the con demos and picked up the Starter Kit just a week ago as a birthday gift to myself, but having re-read the rules several times, one thing mentioned here stood out to me.

The mechanics seem bult to specifically emulate the card game. The heroes are on a timer to achieve their goals set not just by the environment (i.e. the Scene Tracker), but also by the inevitable attrition of their health. In Sentinels, invulnerability is non-existent, and victory in battle is defined mainly by endurance (who outlasts their opponent).

As such, that is a major paradigm shift from other RPGs, especially superhero ones, and one that may be difficult for many players to achieve, never mind find satisfactory.
