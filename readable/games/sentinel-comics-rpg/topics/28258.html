Well, Earth already pretty much exists in the Stone (Materials) Power, so that one’s covered.

Weather (Element/Energy) can easily be used for Air, and perhaps Water.

Generally, when builing Heroes, I find it helpful to think in Power classes rather than individual Powers.
This also helps to realize that sometimes, the Power you have in mind is maybe a bit to broad, and should in fact be several Powers.

Example:
I made a reality shaper type scientist Hero, who would generate macroscopic quantum effects to bend the laws of spacetime around himself. There is no Power for ‘Spacetime meddling’, obviously. Adding that as a hallmark power (or even shoehorning it into the ‘energy’ class, as some sort of a natural force) seems the straightforward route, but I realized that would basically create a catch-all megapower.

So I split it up into its self-control aspect (Intangibility, renamed superposition), the tech-equipment part (Inventions) and the spatial uncertainty (Teleportation) as well as the slight temporal advantage (Awareness).

I realize that this is ‘using up’ a lot of powers, but feels so much more rounded, and in the spirit of how the game works.

#15

Our games have mostly ignored the book definition of Infernal and just used it as generic “unpleasant magic” effects, which has included hellfire, life-draining/pain and shadow/darkness effects depending on who was using it. Similarly, Radiant gets used for mundane lasers at least as often as “good magic” functions.

The principle danger with adding a bunch of new element/energy categories instead of broadening existing ones is that each new type reduces the many abilities that let you ignore, invert, or boost off of a single E/E type. It’s already hard to benefit from those with any kind of reliability (barring combos with your own powers) and it’s worse the more E/E types there are to choose from.

All-new powers and qualities also cause some minor complication when picking Red abilities. You really can’t shove all the new stuff in the Hallmark category for that - that Red chart is written so that three of the four options are restricted to Signature Weapon or Vehicle, and the one other choice isn’t particularly appropriate for many things you might add as new powers, like (say) Tunneling or actual Shadow Manipulation as its own thing.

I think that Telekinesis covers the majority of the potential applications of a “Gravity Manipulation” power.

I would ask the GM before the game starts if they’ll let you use Telekinesis to create “pure force” that directly “crushes”/“smashes”/etc. your target. If the GM says “No, you can only use Telekinesis to move/throw/lift/drop stuff”, then I would take another power for those direct applications of “pure gravitic force”, most likely Cosmic.

Either way, supplementing Telekinesis with Metal or with any other Materials power seems redundant to me.

For gravity-powered personal movement, I would take Flying, Leaping, and/or Wall-Crawling. Those are all easy to justify with “Gravity Manipulation” as a descriptor.

Either Density Control or a combination of Strength and Vitality would work if you want the character to use the same pseudo-scientific explanation of “accustomed to higher gravity” for enhanced durability and strength as characters like John Carter of Mars or Superman.

Sidebar: I once created a character for a superhero RPG who was basically a mash-up of the titular characters from Doctor Who and House, MD, a cranky super-intelligent alien from a “low-gravity” world. They were physically disabled while on Earth, suffering the same mechanical penalties that Earth’s natives would suffer on “high-gravity” worlds, so they had a debilitating addiction to painkillers, and they walked with a cane that they had souped-up into a sci-fi multi-tool and arsenal, like a sonic screwdriver crossed with a Star Trek phaser.

Most darkness/shadow controllers in superhero comics draw their power from a single extraplanar source. In the DCU, it’s the Shadowlands, and in the Marvel Universe, it’s the DarkForce Dimension.
In most Dungeons & Dragons cosmologies, The Negative Energy Plane and The Shadowfel are similarly described and utilized.

The Darkness (Image Comics)
Grue (Worm)
The King In Black (Marvel Comics)
Kirigan (Shadow & Bone)
Clan Lasombra (Vampire: The Masquerade)
Nightshade (DC Comics)
Raven (Teen Titans cartoon adaptation, not the original comics version)

I played a darkness/shadow controller in my first Sentinel Comics campaign. I took Telekinesis so that I could create mobile force constructs out of darkness the same way that Green Lantern rings create them out of light. I took Infernal to represent attacking people with dark “anti-life, anti-light” energy in a more “direct” way, though I seriously considered using Cold for that instead. Teleportation was my travel power, jumping in and out of shadows and using a “darkness dimension” as a shortcut through space. I took Invisibility to vanish into shadows and to extend shadows to obscure myself. I couldn’t actually become a shadow, or else I would’ve taken Intangibility, and I would’ve considered Wall-Crawling. One could also justify creating ropes/chains from the shadows for Swinging. I know that I at least considered Imposing + Presence to represent intimidating people by making the darkness show them their worst fears, but I don’t remember if I actually wound up doing that or not. I know that I didn’t wind up taking Remote Viewing to represent spying on people through shadows, but I did consider that option as well.

AmeliaHealYa:

Minor Nitpick: James Robinson’s (excellent) 1990s Starman series and subsequent The Shade miniseries changed The Shade into more of an antihero type.

Sure, but the bulk of his career (and it’s a long one) has cast him as a villain pure and simple, and even as an antihero he’s still on the (ahem) shady side by comic book hero standards. He didn’t start out as a shadow-wielding hero, or even antihero.

Similarly, while Cloak is clearly a shadow user (which I wasn’t questioning), his status as a hero was pretty shaky early on. I haven’t read much of him (or Dagger) since the first year or two of their existence and they’ve probably changed a lot since, but both were more on the “fairly ruthless vigilantes hunting drug dealers” side of things in their origin than squeaky clean moral paragons.

AmeliaHealYa:

Shadow Lass, AKA “Umbra”, depending on the era.

Still the only shadow-wielding hero-from-day-one I’ve read (The Darkness from Image definitely does not qualify as a hero, and only one iteration of Raven really leans that way over “dark magic user”), but I think it’s worth noting that her shadow powers are just that - she makes darkness. Unlike the majority of “shadow supers” her power isn’t generally associated with things beyond darkness, no cold or life draining or necromancy or other dark magic stuff, and certainly no portals to other dimensions or teleporting or any of the other tricks that are often part of a package “shadow powers” deal. It’s a pretty notable difference between her and the rest.

Whether that’s solely due to many LoSH members having rather narrow, specialized power sets is debatable, but it does keep her from having the kind of creep factor many shadow supers do. Most of the time she’s clearly heroic without the baggage of (say) Raven or Cloak and his fellow DarkForce users. The couple of times I’ve seen writers try to tie some dubious mystical stuff to her it hasn’t worked well or stuck around long.
