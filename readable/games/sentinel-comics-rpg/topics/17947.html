It's very difficult to play tabletop RPGs on your own, and although there will occasionally be written modules that do allow someone to sit down and play through an adventure a la a choose your own adventure book, those tend to lack a lot of what makes a tabletop RPG fun, because they essentially railroad you into playing in a certain way. However, I would totally recommend you pick up the RPG anyway, since, in this golden age of the internet, there seems to be a general interest in getting online groups together to play it. If online roleplaying groups aren't your thing, there's Sentinels of Earth Prime, a SotM style card game being designed by Christopher for the folks at Green Ronin and the world of Mutants and Masterminds, and there's Sentinels of Freedom, a tactics video game written for the Sentinels Universe that is on Kickstarter right now.

I hope that helps!

If I were making dice goodie bags for players, I'd say they need something like: 2d4, 4d6, 3d8, 2d10, 2d12. 

A GM doesn't need a lot of dice, but it helps if they do. I've been gaming for a ridiculously long time, so I've got lots and lots of them. 

Since minions and lieutenants are represented by dice, I put down dry-erase cards (but index cards or post-its work too) with the type of villain, and just put a pile of appropriate dice on it.  

So there might be a card with "Blade Battalion Squad" written on it, with 6d6 (one for each member of the squad). In a bigger fight, there might be multiple cards for multiple squads.  Then I might have another card with "Mobile Defense Platform" on it, with a d10 sitting on it and another with "Omniblade" and a d8.  It's really handy, because when you want that whole Blade Battalion to attack at once, you just pick up all their dice and roll them at the same time. 

And since damage to a minion or lieutenant is represented by shrinking the die type, you'll need plenty of d4s and d6s. If I didn't have lots of dice, I'd use different color poker chips, or pennies and nickels or something like that.
