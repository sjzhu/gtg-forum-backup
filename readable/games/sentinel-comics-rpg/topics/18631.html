I had this idea listening to the live play. I like the way initiative passes works except that it seems to boil down to: all of the heroes but one go, then the villains go, then the last hero. 

What if one side couldn’t take more sequential turns than the other side?

For example, the Freedom Five are fighting Citizen Dawn and 4 citizens. 

The heroes go first.  If they do the standard tactic then it’s 4 of them, then all of the bad guys plus the environment, then the last hero.

But suppose Legacy goes, then Wraith then passes to Dawn. So at most, 2 villains can act before she has to pass back to the heroes. That feels like it might allow for a little more back and forth in combat.  

Thoughts?

Cool idea. I'm not sure how well it'll work, but it's still neat.

Personally, if I gm a game and I find my players doing the "all but one" technique, I'll just counter by doing the same thing in reverse order.

So, say there's four heroes, a set of minions, a villain, and an environment turn, and the three of the four heroes went and then passed it to the minions. I'd have the minions pass to the remaining hero, forcing the villain and environment to go last, then on the start of the next round pass it back to each other giving me that (mostly) double villain turn they were trying to avoid.

Reposting from another thread, re: the Initiative system:

To me, that's an easy fix:

1) The environment/scene tracker always goes last in initiative order.  

2) The environment does not get to 'choose' who goes next; the last player (or GM) to take a turn chooses who starts the next round (following the Environment/Scene Tracker).

3) The GM may not pass initiative to themselves, even if they have multiple actors within the scene, unless all players have already gone.

4) Any player who chooses to pass initiative to the GM may, on their next turn, reroll a single die of their choice.

Yes, this doesn't "eliminate" the possibility of the players all going before the villain, but it does disincentivize doing so, as a player can reap a tangible, in-game benefit by passing play to the GM.  Further, it makes GM tracking easier, by keeping the scene tracker's place in initiative static.
