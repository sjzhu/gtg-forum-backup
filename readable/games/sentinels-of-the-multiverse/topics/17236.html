I would definitely include those two, although with caveats.

I'm not sure I'd put Mr. Fixer at a 2 anyway, he's a very straightforward character who just has to carefully evaluate which style and tool he wants (when he even has a choice).  If some of the variant 1's deserve to stay 1's, normal Mr. Fixer should be a 1, too.  DW Fixer is a high 2 or a low 3, since he has to make continuous sacrifices to do anything at all, especially in rounds 2-3 or so (or after a wipe).  He's very hard to build up, and has to play a continuous balancing game between damage now and options later.  And he has to have solid awareness of what every other hero is doing to know whether they can spare something to fuel his attack.

Hunted Naturalist should be a 3 for the same reason The Sentinels and Setback are: more stuff to keep track of.  I break out some small tokens or toys to mark what form he's currently emulating, and any time you have to do that I think a 3 is merited.  His strategy isn't any more complex, but his mechanic is.  It's very similar to Sky-Scraper in many ways, who is also a well-deserved 3.

Completionist Guise is also easily a 3.  You have to know what variants are in the box and the best circumstances to switch your whole team between all of them.  Although with all of Guise's bizarre interactions, he may deserve to be a 3 anyway.  Completionist might just be the most complicated hero in the game.

Xtreme Captain Cosmic is pushing it.  He can suicide very, very easily.  Fortunately he does have a couple of alternate powers he can give himself.  But he might again deserve to be a low 2 in his normal form and a high 2 in Xtreme form.

I haven't played with Setting Sun Ra yet, but he reads like a lighter version of DW Mr. Fixer.  He has a much more complex resource management game to play.  He's defninitely not a 3, and he can always put out cards that give him alternate powers, but I don't think he's a 1 either.

Most of the others whose variants are much more complex than their base card–Termi-Nation Unity, BoT Chrono-Ranger, Rogue KNYFE, Xtreme Haka, PW Fanatic, Termi-Nation Bunker, F5 Bunker, F6 Tempest, PW Tempest–do tend to transition to using powers from cards as soon as they can get them, and their powers don't make it any harder to reach that point, so Christopher's logic is good here.  But for heroes that depend heavily on their base power throughout the game, and for whom that power is a core mechanic, I think he's wrong.

You do make an interesting point in that "complexity" is not clearly defined.  For instance, in some cases of 3's (The Sentinels, Nightmist, Setback, Sky-Scraper), it's just a slightly odd mechanic that doesn't appear anywhere else in the game but isn't hard to understand or manage.  In some (AZ, Scholar) it's because they have resource management that don't work the same way as most other heroes (and when did Scholar get bumped up to a 3?) and which sometimes results in multi-step math.  In one (Argent Adept) it's mostly because he easily gets flooded with options and has a lot to consider every round, and plan for future rounds.  It's not consistent and it's often hard to compare.
