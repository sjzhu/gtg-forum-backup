I'd just like to refer to Urban Dictionary's definition of broken, which myself and other players usually associate with:

  • Something/Someone that is so good in a particular context that it eclipses second place.
  • A game object or facility that is too good to exist. It is so powerful that it is unbalancing and hence breaks the game. Every winning player has to use this to be competitive.

Now, the reason why I prefer this distinction is because while having an overpowered or "broken" hero can infringe on the enjoyment of a game, it doesn't make it unplayable; it just removes part of the challenge. I'd apply "unplayable" to a hero with complicated essential setup to yield underwhelming benefits, or a villain which modified the win condition in a silly way, or hindered the players' moves to a nigh-unwinnable situation. But that's taking it to an extreme; sometimes "broken" can refer to a mechanic that breaks the rules by utilizing a silly and unintentional/unintuitive combo.

I find TLT does none of these things; I agree. But in many cases she "eclipses" other powers, like Bunker's Initialize. It all just depends on what your team is trying to accomplish. If you need an extra power from AA's songs to help with AoE, you might be better off with a Squall or one of Expat's guns.

As an alternative to the randomizer, you could also set up an extended challenge for your group---to play against a whole suite of villains, with each hero only able to be used once.  So if the first team you all use in the challenge is Nightmist, Scholar, Ex-Patriette, and Tachyon, then next game you can use anyone except those heroes.  Over the course of things, the available pool of heroes will get smaller, so you'll have to be creative about who to use up front vs. who to save for later, which heroes pair well with which teammates and opponents, and how to give support to characters you haven't used as much.  Depending on how you want it to go, you can either include all the toughest villains, or a mix of tougher and easier---if everyone's tough, you'll probably want to try for more balanced teams that all have a shot, but if it's a mix, then you'll probably try to choose your best characters in the hardest fights and use some "weaker" characters against the easier villains.  You have a lot of options for how you want to approach things.

Like others have suggested, framing it as a challenge may make it more palatable to these players, and this way you're guaranteed to see every character in action at least once, which may lead to people finding new favorites.  If the team likes the challenge the first time, you can easily run it again against a new set of villains after you're done and test out even more playstyles---you could even add an additional requirement that no one can re-play a character they used during the first run-through, which'll spice things up more.  One nice thing about this is that you're specifically still letting the player still use Scholar once during the challenge, not banning him---he can just only use him once, just like every other player and hero, so he's not being singled out and still does get the satisfaction of a Scholar win even if some other fights are tougher.  If your team wants to strategize the teams ahead of time, you can plan with him about which villain battle really needs his Scholarly help and which heroes he can help out, to hammer home that even while you're setting up the challenge, you still do respect how good he is with the deck and you're not trying to shut him down.  Could help soften the required change for him.
