#9

I avoid it mostly because it reminds me of jargon, and has basically the same effect of alienating people who aren't part of our little fanperson club.

Your average player hasn't read the bios and even if they have, the haven't memorised everyone's real names. I have to think for a second to recall them myself. And even in a G+ hangout full of experienced players a few weeks ago we had to look up Parse's name (I was 80% sure Parse was Kim but we didn't know her surname) and I was the only one who knew KNYFE's.

I worry that this "jargon" effect can make newer people especially feel confused and left out because they don't know what we're talking about. This is why I don't like unexplained acronyms/abbreviations either. If you don't know who "Drake" is or what "EPE" means you either have to ask and risk looking stupid, or keep quiet and not understand and feel stupid, or work it out from context if it's possible. And that makes us look like an exclusive and mildly unwelcoming little club, and we're very much not.

Similarly, descriptions that only reference card art and/or flavour text exclude people who don't have them all memorised. And Magic the Gathering-specific slang like "mill" and "tutor" are totally untransparent to people who haven't played that game extensively.

So yeah. If I abbreviate or use nicknames or real names, I only do it when I'm pretty damned sure everyone else knows what I mean or can work it out easily from context, because I want to keep things as accessible as possible. (No problem with things like Dawn, or Expat, or OX, or Time Cat, or whatever, because they are trivially simple to figure out).
