tl;dr i liek Guise.

The way I play him, Guise is very team-oriented. Knowing the other hero decks actually helps alot, I feel, when deciding to play him or not (You should pick him anyway). Guise generally wants to go last in turn order to let other heroes play things he may want to copy. Some heroes don't offer much at all to Guise in terms of useful equipment or ongoings, like Unity or Captain Cosmic, or they may have a useless base power, like Naturalist. On this note, I haven't seen a ruling yet on whether Guise is actually allowed to have an Unlucky Pool to even use Setback's base power... oh well. In addition, Guise can function like a second Argent Adept if you copy his power or use one of his instruments, as you don't need to copy AA's songs with Uhh, Yeah to use their effects. You can be Legacy too, with Uh, Yeah and I Can Do That Too. to give +4 damage to your team (Galvanize and Inspiring Presence) for a turn. Well, 2 from you and 2 from Legacy. May as well let him think he's helping.

Guise's ongoings are all slightly situational, but all very powerful. If set up with Barbarian or other +1s, Sellout and Super Ultra Kawaii!! allow for a crazy number of damaging plays. SUK (hehe) is reaaally good at letting you play approximately a billion extra cards, especially if you have only 3 heroes in your game. But that means there are less options for you to copy, so be aware.  

Otherwise, hold onto a heavy damage card like Blatant Referance until You have +1's from somewhere, such as Guise the Barbarian or Legacy. Or, if he isn't helping, just do it yourself, because You Can Do That Too! Selling Out has some really interesting mechanics behind it, because it happens at the end of the envirmonment turn. So, for example, if Obsidian Field is out, or you know it's going to come out, and you have the cards for it, you can play Selling Out on your turn and do some damage at the end of the environment, then, play Retcon as your last one-shot to stop the Villian from getting Obsidian bonus, and draw(or play) a card!  

Ummm... Gritty Reboot pairs well with Legacy's Lead from the Front to absorb alot of damage and draw cards. I'm not a huge fan of Gimmicky Character. Probably becasue CC didn't turn over a construct that I needed to survive the Villian turn.

Also, you're Guise.  If your hand is crap, yell at your cards. Read the italicized text. Make your turn a show.

Powerhound pretty well described how Guise gets played in my group. Accumulate cards, then bust out Super Ultra Kawaii to drop a bunch of ongoings and Selling Out to set up a Best Card Ever/Say Cheese/Look What I Found chain. While waiting to set up, he bides his time with Guise’s base power, and Gimmicky Character if he starts getting low on health.

A few other things about Guise:

  • Guise really needs friends. (Take that any way you want…) He does best in 4- and 5-hero games, since it gives him more room to work with on Super Ultra Kawaii.
  • Anyone who can feed Guise a steady stream of cards helps a lot. (Granted, this is true of most heroes.)
  • Some heroes are better than others at providing Guise good ongoings or equipment to copy. Legacy is particularly good here.

Guise’s biggest weakness is that he really needs time to set up. If you’re dealing with a villain that can clear the board easily like Apostate or Citizen Dawn, you’re going to want someone who can protect him from the villain deck, such as Parse or Wraith. It’s also an issue when fighting villains where you need to bring a lot of damage in a hurry, like Mad Bomber Blade.

We’ve also had a couple games where he shows up late to the party - by the time he’s got his big combo set up, the other heroes have already taken care of everything.

He's been used in two recent PBF games and seemed effective in both, if you want to look at them for possible ideas.  Out-of-turn activity is a strength of his.  I would actually disagree entirely with the statement that he needs time to set up or has issues with villains that clear hero setup, because his deck is one-shots and limited ongoings that go away at the start of his next turn anyway.  His abilities and choices are only really relevant for a round at a time, and then something new becomes available.  Because he can either play two or draw two cards on his turn, he can either recover quickly or unload pretty easily.  He's got a lot of flexibility inherently, and will have capabilities that vary depending on his teammates.
