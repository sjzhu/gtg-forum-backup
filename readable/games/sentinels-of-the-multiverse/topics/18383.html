Here’s my two cents:

I don’t think that Sentinels is ever going to fade.  I expect that Sentinels of Earth-Prime is going to be the first of many spin-offs.  With GtG’s acquisition of Cheapass making the board game community news, the Sentinels engine may attract the attention of other licenses.  Maybe even some Licenses.  With a capital ‘L.’  And since we already know that early planning has begun on Earth-Prime sequels (based on some wording in the Kickstarter posts as well as a homebrew/adapter’s instinct), we’re already guaranteed to stir up the content pot for most of the coming decade.  With there being so, so many off-brand superhero continuities in the field of games alone, I think we can count on a couple more licensed products.  And the number of possible licensed mini-expansions is endless.  The limiting factor is literally Christopher’s willpower and love of the Sentinels system, and those will be around for a while yet.

Suppose I’m wrong, though, and Earth-Prime is a complete disaster, preventing all other possible licenses.  Christopher and Adam quit GtG, become mad scientists, and propel themselves into the moon due to a tragic mass miscalculation in their doomsday device.  There is no more official Sentinels content.  Ever.  Fortunately, there’s a thriving homebrew community already, and even when Christoper’s suddenly-cosmonautical brain runs out of new ideas, that homebrew community will pick up where the official content left off, probably to slow-claps.  Maybe not all the homebrew stuff is as good, but a lot of homebrewers pride themselves on throughly testing their content (myself included, which is why none of you knew about my secret until now), and the Sentinels engine will be able to coast for at least a decade.

Either way, Sentinels will have officially made it to The Next Generation, and if there’s one thing I’ve learned, it’s that any game that can survive that long has a good shot at becoming a Timeless Classic.  After all, the rich, continuous lore, exciting setting, and rock-solid, unique gameplay of Sentinels is something to be remembered.  I have never played a fully cooperative game that has been this consistently fun and fresh.  But from the moment I first realized how Absolute Zero worked, I knew that I had found a game that was neither mindless nor esoteric, that felt fresh and exciting every time, built on a system that was made to ignite the imagination and forge new friendships.

TL;DR- Yeah, I think it’s worth the investment.  That said, Adam would probably appreciate if you waited for the new art version.

I see you already got the Core Set, which is obviously the best place to start. Not sure if you're still looking for advice on this, but my recommended order for buying expansions would be as such:

1) Your first expansion should either be the Rook City/Infernal Relics compilation or the Shattered Timelines/Wrath of the Cosmos compilation. Each of these is a combination of two "standard" expansions that I don't believe are sold separately anymore (I could be wrong on this). Each contains a total of 4 heroes, 8 villains, and 4 environments. Personally, I prefer Shattered Timelines/Wrath of the Cosmos, though you really can't go wrong with either.

  1. After that, it's a question of what you want- do you want more of the same? Get the other compilation expansion. Do you want to try team villains? Go with Vengeance- the team villain format is somewhat divisive, so I suggest this over Villains of the Multiverse as Vengeance comes with 5 heroes and 2 environments that can still be used for standard play if you end up being one of those that doesn't enjoy the team villain format. The downside to this is that Vengeance only comes with five villain decks, so if you use a full party of five heroes, you're always going to be fighting the same team. Meanwhile, Villains has 10 team villain decks and 4 environments.

  2. Once you have around 20 heroes or so, I strongly recommend getting the alternate heroes pack- they really add a lot of options to the game, and effect the way heroes play more than you might think at first glance (some are only subtle, but others completely change how you effectively use a hero).

  3. You should have a fairly sizeable collection before you consider Oblivaeon- you don't need everything, but the more you have, the better it works as it actually plays off being able to bring numerous heroes in rather than just H heroes. That being said, even if you're ignoring the Oblivaeon mode completely, it still has 5 heroes (all of which are villains from previous sets- some redeemed, others forced to team up with those they know they'll eventually be fighting once more) and 5 environments which can be used in any mode.

  4. Oversized Villain set. If you don't care about asethetics, this is probably one of the last things you'll want- the only thing it adds gameplay-wise is six variant villains- two for the core set villains, and one each for the four standard expansions- they certainly add some interesting options, but value-wise I'd think you'd be better off putting the money toward a mini-expansion or two. That being said, if you're in it for the artwork, you may want to bump this up higher- some of the earlier villains really show how much Adam has improved his art since they first began, especially the core set villains. Though I repeat: If you don't care about the art, it really is the last thing I suggest picking up.

Miniexpansions… There's 11 of these that are one deck each, and then there's Void Guard which adds four new heroes (a separate deck for each character that was part of The Sentinels hero deck in Vengeance). I'm not putting them at the bottom because I suggest getting them last, but really because I don't see a particularly good spot to place them in the list.
