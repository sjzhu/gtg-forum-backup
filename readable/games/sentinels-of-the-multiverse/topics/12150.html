If what you want is to give players a rough idea of what it feels like to play a given hero, I think you should not use a "classic" distinction of roles. I would suggest trying to describe hero by the way they feel, by what they can be expected to do most of the time (keeping in mind that nearly all heroes have a lot of uncommon tricks) with one or two descripters.

I'd suggest :

Front Liner : you can expect this hero to regularly do damage, from the start of the game.

Finisher : these heroes can do very good damage, but it takes time - you will often spend a few turns before the damage magic begins.

Helper : these heroes often help others, one way or another. It can be by protecting them, healing them, giving them bonuses, etc, depending on the hero.

Survivor : these heroes often are very good at surviving damage, either through DR, healing, or more uncommon methods.

Manipulator : these heroes can often modify the the environment or villain plays : deck control, ongoind destruction, etc.

Martyr : for the heroes who often use their own HPs and/or the damage they take to become more powerful.

Demanding : for the heroes who often will do "bad" things to other heroes to fuel their power, like Unity's base power, Setback, etc.

Convoluted : for heroes who are often hard to set-up, or ask their player to know them well before they are really effective (Fixer, AZ...)

I think it's enough for most player to know if the "style" of a hero fits them, or if they want to stay away from Convoluted Martyrs :)

I generally divide them into damage, tank/healing, support, control, and shenanigans, and individual heroes will be a combination of them.

Damage, obviously, excels at dealing damage. Pure damage types (or mostly, since “pure” archetypes are few and far between) include Ra, Chrono-Ranger, and KNYFE.

Tank includes The Scholar, and Naturalist in Rhino form. Not many of them, as they aren’t fun to play unless they are REALLY unique.

Support is Captain Cosmic, mainly.

Control is Visionary and Gazelle Naturalist.

There aren’t any PURE shenanigans characters, but Setback, Nightmist, and Guise come the closest. Things like redirection, imitation, discarding, and otherwise hard-to-categorize effects go here.

Damage/Tank is Bunker, Haka, The Sentinels, The Naturalist overall, and Fanatic.

Damage/Support is usually skewed in one direction or another. Fixer and Tachyon are mostly control, with some damage and support. Legacy CAN do this, although he is usually Tank and Support. Setback is mostly damage, with some support in there.

Damage/control is Tachyon, as mentioned, as well as Wraith and Fixer. ExPatriette and Chrono-Ranger have shades of this as well. Sky-scraper is this, but she alternates between them, rather than blending them. Tempest is this as well, with some mild support and shenanigans.

Damage/Shenanigans is where Guise falls. He has some tanking in there. Setback also qualifies, with some support thrown in there.

Tank/Support is Legacy, The Scholar, and Omnitron-X.

Tank/Control is Nightmist, with some shenanigans in there as well.

Support/control is Argent Adept and Parse.

bshugg:

I'm putting together a list for new players that breaks down the heroes into different categories.

I would highly suggest separating the heroes by complexity first. Then separate by only a few catagories (Damage Dealer, Support, Tank). A new player that wants to do damage and mistakenly picks Absolute Zero is going to be highly disappointed. A new player is going to have little understanding about how the game works and even less understanding of which cards in the deck are important. Separating by complexity allows you to ease people into the game. If you give them way too many choices with no real reference point they might get overwhelmed and just opt to not care what they play or not play at all. Additionally, separating by complexity allows people who are familiar with the game to run their brain in low power mode if they want to by selecting one of 5 or 6 characters all listed in one place (no need to go looking all over the place).

Catagories, four catagories that reflect the primary playstyle of a hero.

Beater- Primarily deals in straight forward tactics or direct damage; Haka, KNIFE, Expatriette, etc

Controler- Deals with the board, usually through deck manipulation or Ongoing/Enviornmental destruction; Visionary, Argent Adept, etc

Buffer- Adds to the team as a whole, through Damage Reduction, Increases to Damage dealt by Heroes or Letting heroes play or draw extra cards; Legacy, Parse, Omnitron X, ect

Renegade- Deals primarily with themself using their own cards, powers and often health as a means to an end; Absolute Zero, Fanatic, Nightmist, Setback, etc

 

Subcatagories, six (for now) subcatagories that represent individual utility that heroes can bring to the table.

Widespread-Able to handle multiple targets at a time; Tempest, Sky-Scrapper, Bunker

Alpha Striker-Able to do a tremendous amount of damage after a bit of build up, usually a one-shot type thing; Bunker, Tachyeon, Haka

Healer-Able to heal themselves or other heros; The Sentinels(Dr. Medico), Tempest, Argent Adept

Tank-Able to soak up damage from other players; The Scholar, Haka, Legacy, The Sentinels(Mainstay)

Horder-Someone that plays with a lot of cards on the field, building up a small army or a large Arsenal; Unity, Expatriette, Captain Cosmic

Workhorse-Someone that can play multiple things a turn, through repeated use of powers or card effects; Tachyon, The Sentinels, Argent Adept

Some characters fit into two or more catagories but ultimetly I feel that they usually fit one role the best. The Naturalist's catagory depends on the form he's in and Sky-Scrapper is only a Controler until she's a Beater. Also, yes, Guise is a Renegade. He is dependant on other heroes but ultimately it's still just for himself. Characters can have multiple subcatogories and some make their mark on it, Argent Adept, for example, is a controler that specilizes in utility and dips his toes into quite a few of the subcatagories.

I would go for a few things:

I generally give new players a choice between Haka (“A Maori brawler”), Ra (Indiana Jones turned into the sun god, aka kill it with fire), Tachyon (female Flash), the Wraith (female Batman), Legacy (Superman), and sometimes Chrono-Ranger (time traveling cowboy).

And of course, that’s just for the first game. After that it really depends on the player.
