I'm a teacher (5th Grade) and as the ARG was happening, I was freaking out at my desk as more and more revelations were taking place (both in puzzle solving awesomeness and in actual story content). The students were, of course, perplexed by the random faces I was making as my laptop lit up with more and more goodies, so a few of them stayed in at recess to figure out what on earth was going on. I kind of told them how the card game works and, of course, they were enthralled. So...I bought another copy of the core game. I figured, why not? I brought it today and the kids pored over every card! "I wanna be Legacy!" "The Wraith is so awesome!" "Ra?! Like the Egyptian god?!" We haven't started playing yet, but they are extremely excited to beat up the nefarious Baron Blade. Just thought the community would want to know how the game, and really the ARG, has impacted the rest of my recess times for the year. =) Happy times!

I've run SotM with my now-5th-grader & some of his friends on several occasions. If there are only 2 or 3 kids, I'll play. Otherwise, I'll run the villain deck & moderate. Some advice, based off these sessions:

1. If you can arrange it beforehand, give the kids a chance to look through the decks (which it sounds like you've done).

2. To the extent their attention spans permit, I'll read the flavor text on the villain & environment cards as those cards are played.

3. I find it really useful to keep the game moving with statements like "Legacy, you get to play a card."   "Legacy, choose one power to use."  "Legacy, you get to draw a card."

4. I will explain for them what a card does if they ask, but I will not advise which card is "better" if I'm not playing the game. I have certain play styles (so does the Grasshopper), but every hero has more than one valid way to play. For example, I think of the Utility Belt & Infrared Eyepiece as the Wraith's two key cards, but other people may want to go Throwing Knives/Microtargeting Computer.

5. First scenario - Freedom Four vs. Baron Blade in Insula Primalis. Swap Ra for either Tachyon or Bunker if you insist. There's a reason that's the official convention demo scenario.

It's slightly different than 5th grade, but I teach at the university level, teaching literature, creative writing, and general composition classes.

Being a graduate student, I haven't had the opportunity to implement it yet, but I have an idea for a creative writing/technical writing class uses SotM, where they would create their own characters, write bios and full-length stories, and also research technological advances, cultural themes, etc, for their own character. Then, using technical writing skills, write cards and rules, etc. It would be a good way to talk both about character, setting (environment!), and the particulars of language.

 

I have a Facebook friend/colleague I've met through facebook groups that teaches fiction writing exclusively through various RPG settings, including Fan-Fiction stuff for Game of Thrones, in his college classroomes. He's had lots of great success.

There is also a professional conference for Game-Based learning (which I learned about through him). I am sure they'd have some ideas for how to implement games like SotM into classrooms at all ages.
