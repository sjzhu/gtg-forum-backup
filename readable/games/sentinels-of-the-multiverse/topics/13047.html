Nope, Patrick Rothfuss.  Rothfuss suffers from slow-writing syndrome like Martin, but fortunately doesn't seem to have caught Martin's trilogy-creep disease.

 

(On a related note, my friend and I have a standing bet about which will be finished first, the Song of Ice and Fire, or the third Mistborn trilogy*.  Anyone want to choose a side?)

 

*Sanderson's long term series plan is rather complex.  Mistborn is a trilogy of trilogies (first trilogy is fantasy, second will be urban fantasy, and the third will be Sci-fi), although there is now a series of interquels being release which take place in between the first and second trilogy.  The 2nd trilogy is (I believe) projected to be written between the first 5 and the last 5 books of the Stormlight Archive, and I think there are another series or two in between the completion of the Stormlight Archive and the 3rd Mistborn Trilogy.  So there are at least 11 books to be written (but easily as many as 20) before the 3rd trilogy can get started.  On the other hand, Brandon releases books much, much faster than Martin does.  Since A Dance with Dragons was published, Brandon has released 3 major books, 3 YA books, 5 novellas, and a few short stories, with two more books to be released by next January.

From the 2014 State of the Sanderson

There will be one more Wax and Wayne (early 1900s-era) Mistborn book. Back after I finished The Alloy of Law, I sat down and plotted out a trilogy with the same characters. The Alloy of Law was more of a happy, improvised accident. The follow-up trilogy is meant to be more intentional. So in the end, we’ll have four total. (The final one is tentatively called The Lost Metal.) From there, I might jump to the second “big” trilogy, which is 1980s tech. Or I might dally a little more in something 1940s-era instead. We’ll see.
