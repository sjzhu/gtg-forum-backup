About 95% of my Sentinels gaming consists of my wife and I taking two heroes apiece and setting out to defeat one of the classic-style villains. Although we got Vengeance as soon as it was regularly available, we rarely ever play a Vengeance-style game. (In fact, from our win/loss tally sheet, it’s clear that we’ve only played Vengeance style about three times.) I think that’s because we typically have two players, and the heroes’ “planning horizon” goes down from one round in classic style to one hero turn in Vengeance style. Neither of us really likes that format. We did play Vengeance with four human players one time, and that was more enjoyable - though still not as fun as just taking on Citizen Dawn, in my opinion.

Anyway, I preordered Villains mostly for the environments, mini-expansions, and oversized villain cards. But it really would be a shame to let all those villain decks go unplayed! I’m thinking about trying out some games with the Vengeance-style villains, but putting (H) - 1 or (H) - 2 of them in the classic villain’s position, one after another following the environment. I think that would address my general preference against the Vengeance format, at least in the two-player style I usually play.

Have any of you thought about how the difficulty and playability might work in such a format? I don’t see any fundamental reason why it wouldn’t work. What would be the optimal number of villains to pick?

We play 2-player Vengeance/Villains format (I'm going to start calling it "V-format") by having one of us control the first two villains, the first hero, and the environment and the other person control the last villain and two heroes. V-format -- for us -- really shines with H of 3, and this makes it a very fun way to play (as long as one person is willing to only have one hero they are controlling).

To directly answer the question, though, no I haven't considered that. We'd definitely have to try it out, as it seems like it could be hard to recover from some of the hits that would come from that. But it's worth trying!

Making the Villains go one after the other sounds like a good way to streamline the gameplay with fewer players, it will feel more like the Ennead. H-1 Villains also sounds good, but I feel you do need at least 3 Villains (which is what you said).

 

The modification I did to play Vengeance was that when a Nemesis target enters play, it needs it's Hero Nemesis to be in play, otherwise it is discarded.

Thematically, the goon is fighting his Nemesis somewhere else. Mechanically, you take away some of the Villains plays (they still have their End of turn effect) and reduce the number of overall HP Vengeance has. Both things make the game go faster and makes Vengeance not as hard.

The variant Medic-Tank suggests is a popular one to pump up weaker villains.  You just add a Vengeance style Villain to a regular game.  It does really ramp up the difficulty, so it is better for 5 player groups.

More to your speed though, have you tried 2 heroes vs. 2 Villains?  it is less balanced, but it does work.  The balance issues are largely based on team design, making sure you can take out ongoings and deal with damage immunities is the biggest part.  It should work better with the new expansion than it does with just Vengeance.

As for the difficulty and bookkeeping, We put a lot of work into this expansion to make it less work to run, and added quite a few lower difficulty heroes.  It is at its best at 3 players, but I played several 2 player matches during playtesting and they went fine.

See, I've always got the opposite problem -- my game night regularly has at least 6-7 people in it. So has anyone tried going the other direction, doing six or seven heroes against six or seven villains from the Vengeance and VOTM sets?

So far, we've done 6 or 7 against the regular villains -- it's an interesting challenge. On the one hand, we have a lot of time to do damage between villain turns and there's much more chance of having good utility/deck control when you need it. On the other hand, when the villain turn comes around, we get clobbered. This week we did six heroes against Iron Legacy, and if it wasn't for throat jabbing and hypersonic assaulting, he'd have trashed us in two rounds, easy -- as it is, his first turn took several of us down to half health.

Then we did La Capitan with seven heroes and it makes most of her crew members terrifying -- if we hadn't avoided the card that takes them out of the trash, it would've been it for us.
