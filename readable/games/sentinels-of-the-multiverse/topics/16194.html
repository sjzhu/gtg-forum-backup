Johnny Rocket: Another career superhero with no background or personality besides "fast" and the fact that he's gay.  Yawn.  He does seem to have some abilities centered around creating wind flow (e.g. tornadoes) with his speed, which will be a nice hook for Christopher.

Lady Liberty: Was in pre-law.  Other than being very patriotic, that's pretty much all there is to know about her as a person.  Power-wise, though, she has some potential: in addition to the usual flight and strength, she has healing powers and apparently some firework/explosion stuff.  I guess she's probably the one Christopher mentioned in the podcast as having a shield that isn't documented anywhere.

Pseudo has a pretty thorough background, too.  Good stuff on the Atomic page.  Christopher will have both some espionage (mind-reading and becoming insubstanial) and shapechanging abilities to work with.

Siren is basically like Ra.  Lots to work with here.

Star Knight: A former cop.  Backstory is essentially a cross between the Green Lantern and agent J from Men in Black.  I don't have a good feel for her abilities other than armor, flight, and an energy sword.

The Raven: Yet another career superhero with not much else in the way of background.  She's basically the daughter of Batman, trained to be just like him.  Making her different from Wraith is going to be a serious design challenge.

 

So to sum up, we have 5 heroes who are plain-vanilla and have not much background or life outside of being a superhero, two or three that are OK, and two or three that are pretty good.  Mechanically, we have four or five that will be tough to make interesting, four or five that have some clear potential, and one that will undoubtedly be interesting but will be hard to reconcile with the lore in a way that makes sense and is fun.

To answer questions about pricing, things like mini expansions with fewer cards are much more sensitive to price increases in materials. That is because 1) a mini-expansion uses less paper (about 1/14th as much paper as the core game), so you can't get as much of a bulk discount in order to help reduce the cost, and 2) assembling the core game is more labor intensive, so a higher percentage of the cost of the core game is labor.

To give you some data from our perspective, since we started printing mini-expansions, they have gone from a product with one of the best profit margins that we produce to a product with one of the worst.
