For your first game with her, I recommend giving new players a choice between Ra, Haka, Tempest, the Wraith, or Tachyon.  Give short bios for each (e.g. "Wraith is Batman, Tachyon is the Flash, Tempest is an alien who controls thunderstorms, Haka is a Maori who smashes things, and Ra kills things with fire"), and go into more detail as players show their interest.  Choose either Baron Blade or Omnitron as the villain.  I recommend starting in Insula Primalis.  That said, I've found that it's fairly safe to let the new players choose the environment - nine times out of ten they will choose the environment described as "Dinosaurs!" on their own.  Definitely avoid Megaopolis (too many cards that say "players can't" on them).

 

Try and choose your hero(s) based on what characters/mechanics you think that your wife might find interesting (e.g. if she likes complicated puzzles play AZ.  If she likes support and tanking, play Legacy)

 

Be careful not to play the game for her - give advice about the things she wouldn't be aware of (Lightspeed Barrage, Haka of Battle, etc) and comment if she makes any drastic mistakes, but otherwise avoid providing unsolicited help (if she asks, then feel free of course).  Let her handle her own health.

The big reasoning behind the conventional wisdom of "don't use Megalopolis for a first game" is that players are still learning what they CAN do. Megalopolis has cards (like Hostage Situation, Traffic Pileup, and Papparazzi on the Scene) that specifically PREVENT players from doing things that are otherwise a normal part of their turn. It interferes with the learning dynamic.

I'm going to third the suggestion of using Baron Blade as your introductory villain. He's the most straightforward villain in that he only flips once per game, introduces the flip mechanic, and introduces the nemesis mechanic if Legacy is also in the game.

For a 2-player game of rookies against Baron Blade, I'd suggest the following:

Baron Blade

Player 1: Play Legacy

Player 2: Play one hero (pick from Ra, Wraith, Haka, Tempest, Bunker. KNYFE is also good if you have her, but I'm assuming you don't)

Player 1: Play one hero (pick from same list as above)

Insula Primalis

 

If one of you focuses on running Baron Blade & one of you focuses on running Insula Primalis, that spreads the work out pretty evenly.

Hey there, welcome to the forum :).

For a first game, I shall join all those who said to fight Baron Blade in Insula Primalis, with Legacy and any of Ra, Wraith, Tempest, and the other straightforward heroes who were mentioned :).

In terms of which expansion to get, they all have replayability - every deck does, I would say. That's how come we've all been playing it for so long…well, that plus it's co-operativeness. I'm not even particularly intereted in superheroes :D.

Rook City is probably the hardest in terms of villains and environments. All the villains are varying types of brutal (though one or two can become relative pushovers if you bring certain heroes against them). Of the two environments, one is generally considered the harshest upon the heroes out of all environments in the game, while the other has some annoyances and can be painful but on the whole I would say isn't nearly as bad (especially if you have environment destruction). The heroes are a martial artist mechanic guy and a woman who uses many many guns.

Infernal Relics is my favourite out of all the expansions, possibly because of the magic theme but also partially because the decks are just cool anyway. The villains are generally not too bad (although the Ennead, a group of nine Egyptian-gods-who-used-to-be-archaeologists, like Ra, are reportedly one of the most difficult fights in the game if you play them on Advanced). The environments are similarly so and I find quite interesting. The two heroes are rather toward the more complicated side of things, one in particular but he also happens to be my favourite in the game - he's kind of like a DnD (or EQ) Bard, using music and song to support his teammates and let them do All of the Things. The other hero is an occultist investigator who investigated a bit too much occult and is partly made of mist - she summons relics and casts spells and involves a lot of self-harm and self-healing to do a wide variety of stuff.

Shattered Timelines is probably my second-favourite expansion. The villains tend to vary between not-too-bad and omg-that-was-a-massacre depending on card luck, and one in particular is probably about hte toughest in the game. Certainly games against hium are unlikely to last longer than about fifteen minutes, whether you win or not (and generally speaking, you probably won't because he really hurts). The environments aren't too bad. The heroes are two that I really enjoy playing - a time-travelling cowboy sheriff guy who, once he gets set up with a few cards (which he can do within a couple of turns if he gets luck yon the draw) is epic at focusing down single targets, even without external damage buffs. The other is a diferent kind of time-traveller, a future version of Omnitron who installed an empathy drive into himself to try and understand how the various heroes help each other in order to keep defeating him (so he can in turn defeat them) and promptly realised "oh crap, I've murdered so many innocent people…this must never happen", then time-travelled back to try and destroy the first incarnation of himself. Anyway, his cards are basically heroic (and therefore somewhat toned-down) versions of the villain Omnitron's cards, and I find him pretty versatile and pretty good fun :).

What's next…oh yeah, Vengeance…that one changes things up a bit in terms of villains. Instead of getting four individual new villain decks, there are five…but they're all a team. Fed up of getting his arsed repeatedly kicked by the Freedom Five (and others), Baron Blade has put together his own team, the Vengeance Five, to try and get…well, vengeance ;). Each of the five is nemesis to one of the Freedom Five and has other nemeses (to other heroes) within their decks. You don't necessarily fight all five of these guys at once, though - when you set up a game, you fight H of them. So you'll only fight against all five decks if you have five heroes present. Then play alternates between villains and heroes (and the environment on the end, as usual). The heroes in this expansion are a good mix, I think. My probably-second-or-third favourite is in this deck, a guy who can shapeshift into any of three different African animals which provide him with different effects. There's also an archer, a Scottish lady who stabs things with energy blades, a team of four heroes (who have 11-14hp each and have one of their number capable of rezzing the others if they go down, but if they all go down then they're officially Incapped), and a guy whose power seems to be bad luck…as in, literally, he has really bad luck and it ends up in various people (usually himself or the bad guys) getting hurt :D.

Then there's the most recent expansion, Wrath of the Cosmos, which has some really difficult villains, one of which is pretty interesting in that the set-up is that you're in a giant arena thingy and you can earn Titles for doing certain things, and you don't win by defeating the villain but by earning Favour Points for basically beating up enough bad guys that the audience decides you're the best :D. I haven't had as much time to play Sentinels this last few months as I have previously so I haven't played this expansion so much. The heroes are pretty cool - one can change her size and has three character cards to show that (you switch between them as you change size). The other summons up energy construct things and plays them on his allies to give them a variety of bonuses and extra abilities and stuff :).

Upcoming in a few months or so is Villains of the Multiverse, which contains no new heroes but has a bunch of new villains who have the same grouping mechanic as the Vengeance ones (and can be mixed and matched with them). These ones aren't part of a team like the Vengeance Five, they're all separate in terms of story.

If you end up liking the game, you'll probably end up getting all of this stuff anyway :D.

All cards in the environment deck are environment cards (same with hero cards in hero decks and villain cards in villain decks). A target is anything with hp. Some environment cards are targets because they have hp. Anything that affects an environment card will affect any card in that deck. Anything that specifies an environment target can only work on stuff that has hp. The same applies to hero and villain decks, with the slight addition that the main hero and villain cards (the ones with their name and hp total and stuff) are character cards, which are still targets, they're just special ones - you can recognise character cards as they have a different card back to the others in their deck. Some things can't affect character cards, while some things can only affect character cards.

I'd suggest getting Wraith's Infrared Eyepiece out ASAP -- it'll keep Blade from playing the really nasty cards like Devious Disruption, The Price of Victory or Hasten Doom. It also helps you with card draw in getting the rest of her stuff out quickly. One of the nice things about Wraith is she can do control and damage if you get utility belt out, so do so as soon as you can.

You'll soon learn that Infrared Eyepiece is the second best card Wraith has (the first being Impromptu Invention). Deciding what card the villain plays next (and which cards he won't play for a long, long time) is often worth more than even hugely damaging powers.

For Tempest, it depends more on what you have in your hand. The good thing is, he can damage everything, so he should be good at keeping the minions and such in check, especially if you can get something like Grievous Hail Storm or Localized Hurricane out -- with Legacy's buff(s), they'll do some serious damage. He's also good for healing if you can get Cleansing Downpour out.

Between Tempest and Wraith, you should have environment and ongoing destruction more or less covered, as they both have a few cards for it.

Legacy should focus on buffing and supporting the others. Drawing cards, boosting damage, stopping the villain from playing cards or buying a round of invincibility will do wonders against Baron Blade, especially on those rounds when Infrared Eyepiece reveals two really bad cards. Alternately, you can focus him on tanking -- Lead from the Front, Superhuman Durability, Fortitude and Next Evolution are amazing for that kind of thing.

I know I am kind of late to this, but hey:

jvirtue, I just got into this game a few months ago - I purchased the digital game to see how I'd like it and then bought the entire physical game. While the digital game is good and I like it (but haven't played it much lately), I like many of the additional options in the card game. What I found when playing the card game was that it was stressful to keep track of everything by myself and while I had fun, I had approximately 10x as much fun when I finally played the game with other players (which is why I haven't played a lot of the digital game much - it didn't have multiplayer until the multiplayer beta started yesterday). A lot of the issues that come up playing alone are resolved by having multiple players, simply because 2-5 brains are better than one. :)

A lot of the fun also comes from variety - exploring the different hero, villain, and environment decks. 

Anyway, I don't regret a single dime I've spent on this game. It's definitely become one of my favorites.
