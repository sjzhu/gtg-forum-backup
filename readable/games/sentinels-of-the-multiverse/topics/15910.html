#73

Some specifics can't be shared, but there really aren't a lot of stories to tell.  Mostly it is arguing wordings and whether a hero innate power is too weak or too strong, or if some edge case is enough to warrant changing an otherwise really cool effect.  We argue a lot about variant powers.  A lot.

There are a lot of playtester rebuilds, from single cards to entire rebuilds of decks.  Because everyone sees characters differently and have their own ways they would love to see a character go.  We argue and playtest them and eventually Christopher shows up, grabs what he likes, ignores the rest and we get a new version and start the process over again.

I like to think of the playtesters as a population of thought bunnies, nurturing our little bunny idea children and sending them out in the field in the hopes that Christopher the majestic and beautiful Bird of Prey will swoop down and carry our little idea off into the sky where he will eviscerate and repurpose our perfect little wordings.  Having your little thought bunny chosen is a great feeling.

There are really exciting points, like when we get a deck for the first time, and when we get to see the proofs (and art) for the first time.  Even better if you happen to be online and get to read and respond before other playtesters.  Also a lot of boring parts like waiting for a new version when business and outside things slow down the process.

Those are the times when the playtesters get restless and angry.  We don't get much advance notice of delays, setbacks, or changes, but we can tell things aren't going fast enough, and we notice when business interferes and we get silence for longer than expected.  It isn't fun for us fanboys and fangirls seeing things not going the way GtG wanted and not knowing why.

And when we find things out through public releases we get upset about that too.  We get used to finding things out first from the comfort of our little playtester bubble, and we don't like it when that doesn't happen.

There's a lot of feelings involved in playtesting, we are emotional little thought bunnies.  Also spellchecking, a lot of spellchecking.
