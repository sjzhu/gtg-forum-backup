So, I've searched around on this, and found bits and pieces of speculation on this, but can't find anything definitive. Has anyone come up with a generally-accepted theory on the Fanatic/Apostate First Appearance quandary?

Just to be clear, I'm talking about the following:

Apostate's First Appearance is in Fanatic #25.

Fanatic's First Appearance is in Mystery Comics #368.

Apostate has quotes on Holy Nova, Tome of the Unknowable, Final Absolution and Reactive Plating from Mystery Comics #367, 368, 369 and 370, respectively. The art from several of these issues appears to show a conflict with Fanatic and Apostate.

Fanatic is quoted on Brutal Censure, Sacrosanct Martyr, Illusory Demon, and Aegis of Resurrection in Fanatic #2, 14 (twice), and 19, respectively. The art from several of these issues appears to show a conflict with Fanatic and Apostate.

Apostate also is quoted on Condemnation, Corrupted Effigy, Remorseless Provocation and Gauntlet of Perdition in Fanatic #25 (twice) and 27 (twice).

We also know that "After Fanatic was confronted by the deceiver, Apostate, she had to regroup. She spent three entire days sequestered in the bell tower of a monastery in prayer and meditation, not stopping to eat or sleep. Finally, she emerged from her requiescence, refreshed in the knowledge of her steadfast righteousness." (Redeemer Fanatic section).

Now, there's several little sub-problems in here as well, and I'll list those, too, but the main problem is:

How does Fanatic have a first appearance in Mystery Comics #368, which by all appearances seems to be part of a "confrontation" with Apostate, given the frequency of Apostate quotes and some art (though not Smite the Transgressor, which is stated as being from a different issue), but then Apostate does not have a first appearance until Fanatic #25, which, theoretically, must be after Mystery Comics #368, since it is Fanatic's eponymous book?

Here's a couple of theories:

1. (The thought behind this first sentence is not mine, though I couldn't easily put my finger on it to attribute it) Apostate is quoted in MC 367, 368, 369 and 370, but doesn't actually "appear" in any of these. (I think this starts to be me from here on) What this ACTUALLY is is Fanatic starting to deal with some of the relics and demons associated with Apostate, but hasn't actually run into Apostate yet. Apostate is only sort of a mysterious voice-over at this point, almost like a narrator. This could, theoretically, appear to be its own story arc, and could happen substantially previous to the events of even Fanatic #1, but the "mysterious voice" isn't resolved until Fanatic #25.

2. The first 24 issues or so of Fanatic's eponymous book tell the story of the origin of Fanatic. Her young life, etc. Therefore, while Helena appears, Fanatic does not appear until she officially takes up the mantle in MC #368, which immediately crosses over into her book on issue #25.

And the second problem is that I don't actually like either of these. Here's why:

1. I find this one to be hard to believe from a business perspective. With the voice-over of Apostate, you are clearly setting up that this story is not done - there's the unexplained outside agency left lurking. However, assuming standard publication rates, readers are expected to hang around for the payoff of the story for at least two years in another book. Also, this requires that you have the idea for a nemesis for Fanatic, but decide to hold back from giving it to her during the period when she most needs to grab excitement and readers - when her title is first starting up. It is possible that there have been little teasers from Apostate occurring in Fanatic #1-24, or Fanatic trying to figure out who the agency was behind the scenes, but there is no evidence of this on the cards.

2. You give Fanatic her own book, named after her. Fine. You have it not start out with the named character actually appearing in it. A bit avant-garde, but also fine. You then have the payoff of the first appearance of the Fanatic in another book? Not fine. Completely unbelievable. All of these people are reading along, waiting for poor little Helena to really do something fanatical to justify the title, and then it happens in Mystery Comics? I just have trouble believing it, and I thought it up! ;P

So, who has a theory?

2. The theory must explain that Apostate is quoted before Fanatic's first appearance, but his first appearance is in Fanatic's book, which is generally assumed to be later.

I always thought of Mystery Comics as the equivalent of Weird Mistery Tales and other comics of the kind - horror/crime comics from the time before the rebirth of interest for super-heroes.

In the history of Comics, many super-heroes (and villains) appeared first in the pages of these kind of Comics, when the craze came back, as "one-shot" characters, before gaining their own comics and story line later, when the audience reacted favorably.

My guess is that Fanatic and Apostate were first part of some kind of horror story in MC.

Then "later" the character was developed has a super-hero with her own storyline and comic, and Apostate as her big bad.

There are many explanations possible. Maybe (it happened in true comics) a one-shot story in MC was later retconned and rewritten in a different way as an adventure in Fanatic's comic.  Maybe Fanatic's comics were published later, but began the story before the events of MC. Maybe Apostate was not really a "character", or at least a "villain" in the super heroic sens of the term, in MC, but just a narrator of dark stories - or the main character of the Fanatic story.

Maybe the Fanatic/Apostate conforntation was meant to be a one-shot mistery tale at first - but then inspired an author. Like what happened for Man-Thing and other monsters-turned-recurring characters.

"First Appearance" may very well mean "as a super-villain". If I remember correctly, there are demon-like characters in Marvel and DC who first were characters in horror stories, before being used a villains in later super-hero comics, with a few modifications.

Like, for instance, it seems Chrono Ranger was first a character in Western stories comic books before entering horror stories and finally becoming a recurrent super-hero in the SH comics :D

And, yes, I think about SotM as if the publication history wasn't fictional - I am convinced Christopher and Adam even thought about explaining some events in the stories and changes in characters as a change of the writer or artist working on the book at the time :D
