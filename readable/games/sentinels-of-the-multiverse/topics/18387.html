To me the key to Harpy is managing and setting up swings between Arcane and Avian tokens.  Don't just sit on one side until you run out of cards for that style and then not be able to swing to the other side.  Your cards that flip tokens are huge, use them wisely and often holding onto them until it is time to switch sides is key.

Pay attention to when thigs happen.  Conjured Aura checks for destruction at the start of your turn, you need 3 arcane tokens at the start of your turn.  At the end of your turn H&M can set up your nest turn with a flip.  Uncontrolled Flock needs at least 1 Arcane token at the start of your turn or you lose it.

Darkwatch Harpy is much faster at swinging between Avian and Arcane.  You can flip a lot of tokens with that power and a second card play.  With DW Harpy you really need to strategically use your cards that flip tokens.  To get the most out of DW Harpy you need to plan out several potential turns that allow you to do different things and then when your turn comes up do the one that fits best.

Original Harpy is amazing with Applied Numerology, and your main power drives your deck.  With less token flipping you will often just sit on one side of the token spectrum, and won't bounce back and forth quite as much.  This one is less complex than DW, simply because Harpy will often feel like you don't have anything to do even though you have a ton of cards, if you aren't thinking ahead and setting up big swings and turns.

> I did some testing!

 

20 four-hero games vs Advanced Baron Blade in Insula Primalis, 10 games with just heroes from the base set and then 10 games with OblivAeon & Mini-Expansion heroes (no variants for either set).

 

On average they both took 5 turns to win.

 

The Base heroes did a tiny bit more damage than the OblivAeon heroes, but you would expect them to do roughly the same damage if pitted against the same deck again and again.

 

The OblivAeon heroes played roughly 100 more cards than the base heroes, and drew about 100 more cards too (311/210 and 300/198 respectively).

The OblivAeon heroes handed out almost 3 times as many powers to their friends (71/24).

Base heroes lost 2 games, OblivAeon heroes lost 1.

 

Counting losses as 4 heroes at 0HP the base heroes lost 9 members of their team over the 10 games and the OblivAeon heroes lost 6 heroes but the base heroes generally ended on higher HP when they won.

 

What does this mean?

Well, it appears to me that the OblivAeon heroes are doing more things, but the things they are doing are not more effective than the smaller number of things the base heroes are doing. It would appear the OP-ness is all in the mind (or I'm misinterpreting the data or the sample size is too small to draw any conclusions).

 

Google Doc with raw data if you want to see actual numbers.

> I wanted to compare a "pure" base set untainted by possible power creep from other expansions.
