TakeWalker:

The same thing will happen if, say, you use Don’t Dismiss Anything and Scholar puts Know When to Hold Fast into play. He draws five, the rest of DDA resolves, and then he ends his turn.

That’s not quite correct. “End your turn” means “move to the end of turn step”. This happens before DDA finishes resolving.

The other notable interaction this causes is with Wager Master’s card which reverses turn order. Ending your turn moves you to the end of turn step, after which you proceed to the draw step, the power step, the play step, and the start of tirn step.

Also, this is how “end your turn” differs from “skip your turn” Skip skips all remaining phases in your turn, including the end step.

To be clear, "end your turn" means "go to your end-of-turn phase"; so your turn isn't done, but you may skip one or more of your previous phases.

"skip the rest of your turn", however stops everything and immediately goes to the next turn. You see this on Meteor Storm, for example.
