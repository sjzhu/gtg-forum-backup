Yesterday I ran into a fight against advanced Plague Rat which went absolutely completely out-of-control. I was playing with the complete F5 and it resulted in Plague Rat doing 85 damage to the heroes in just one turn!

Advanced Plage Rat (+1 damage) had Plague Locus (+1 damage) and Shadowy Ambush (Play a villain card when PR does +5 damage) in play. The fight was in the Realm of Discord with Imbued Frailty (+1 damage) in play. Because of +3 damage dealt by PG and H=5, every one-shot of PR resulted in an extra villain card play. So in PG's third turn, he played consecutively:

Vicious Onslaught -> Ravage -> Noxious Bite -> Vicious Onslaught -> Tooth & Claw -> Tooth & Claw -> Noxious Bite. Also, all infections cards, 2 Afflicted Frenzy cards and 1 Bestial Vitality.

Even with Legacy having played Heroic Interception his previous turn (!), at the end of PG's turn the result was:

Legacy: 21 -> 0 HP
Tachyon: 15 -> 0 HP
Bunker: 17 -> 6 HP
Wraith: 20 -> 2 HP
AZ: 23 -> 4 HP

With all the heroes infected and multiple ongoings of PG on the table, it's not necessary to mention the outcome of this battle.. I could only smile when I saw PG dominating me.

 

This led me thinking whether it is possible to play all of PG's cards in just 2 turns. Turns out, even without the aid of the environment and without any major DR to the heroes: yes, advanced PR is able to do that (even with H=3). For this, his turns has to be like this:

Turn 1: Shadowy Ambush
Turn 2: Tooth & Claw -> Shadowy Ambush. After that, PG plays 2 cards every time he deals +5 damage, and still has to do the second damage by Tooth & Claw (resulting in 2 card play). If always at least one of these 2 cards is a Tooth & Claw or Noxious Bite, it will be a train unstopapble. I calculated that for H=5, this will be 199 damage done by PR in just 2 turns.

Of course this is a very specific set-up, but it is crazy to realise a villain can play his complete deck in just 2 turns. Also, you can imagine that even with a less optimal set-up, a situation like this can be devastating, like my experience yesterday.  

 

So, it rests me to say: all hail the Plague Rat!
