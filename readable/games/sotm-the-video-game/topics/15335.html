Hahaha.  I know you're joking, but you're not all that far from the truth.  I've actually never liked the Weekly One-Shots.  I appreciate the work the team does on them and it's a great idea in theory, but I hate the way that it "punishes" (I admit that's an overly strong word, but bear with me) those who try to figure out the solutions for themselves and rewards those (like me) who wait for someone to figure it out and copy it.  I know in my heart that this is not something that should bother an adult, but I'm a lifelong comic collector and video gamer and "completionist" doesn't even begin to describe it.  

Maybe I can explain my feelings better by way of an analogy:  I love Zelda games.  I've beaten almost every Zelda game released to 100% completion.  My goal is to beat each game 100% without using a game guide, but when I've done as much as I can and I feel like I can't find anything else, I'll check a guide for the last few items that I missed.  When it no longer feels like fun and just feels like labor, I throw in the towel, but I still want that 100% on the front screen.  Now, although I just said I don't like using guides, the first thing I do when I get a new Zelda game is Google "[Zelda Game Name] Missable Items" and, although it can sometimes be a bit spoiler-y, this helps me be on the lookout for any items that one can miss if they don't get them the first time through, usually due to a glitch or a designer oversight.  Every Weekly One-Shot feels like a "missable item" to me, so I feel that my best chance for getting a Mint is to at least read some tips from a few others who have already done it.  

Awhile back, I posted a SotM puzzle on this forum.  It was a description of a set-up and I challenged forum-goers to try to determine the best way to win the scenario.  I had also written most of another one and never got around to posting it.   If I had more time, I would take pictures of the set-up instead of the description (with permission of course) and post that instead.  I really enjoyed the mental exercise of working backwards from the solution and I think the few people that checked it out enjoyed solving it as well.  In my perfect world, the Weekly One-Shot would be more like that.  Or something like "Win the following challenging scenario" and you would get a rating of Mint, Near Mint, etc. depending on certain restrictions like finishing with all heroes above or below a certain health or something like that.  You would be able to play it over and over to try to meet the criteria so that it was always possible to get a mint with logic, practice, and perseverance.  Of course I realize that wouldn't be everyone's cup of tea either.

I just realized that I got a little carried away explaining that lol.  To get back to my original topic, I heard back from Handelabra customer support today and there is indeed no way to restore my cover collection on my current device without restoring the full back up which I cannot do.  It's actually the only app I have that I was not able to restore from a Facebook back-up or through a game server.  I'm a little disappointed, but I'll get over it.  The silver lining is that I think I can now break the cycle of feeling compelled to do the Weekly One-Shot and instead try to find some enjoyment in it.  There's no point in sweating what rating I get on it, if it's just going to disappear when I get a new iPad in the future anyway.

One last thing I'd like to say:  I hope this post doesn't come across as overly critical.  I think that the Handelabra team does an amazing job in implementing an already amazing game and the Weekly One-Shot is a really cool addition that they didn't need to do, but that a lot of people appreciate.  I can even say that I appreciate the effort they put into it, even if it's just not my cup of tea.  I wish it was more to my liking and I'm bummed that I have nothing to show for the time and effort that I put into completing them since the beginning.  

Edit:  Fixed some bad grammar and altered a few word choices to underscore that I'm not mad at Handelabra
