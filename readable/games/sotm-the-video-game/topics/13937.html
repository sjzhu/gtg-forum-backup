Actually, the thing that really annoys me in online games is some of the etiquette. It's a little bit irritating when everybody is immediately suggesting what card they think should be played out of their turn instead of holding back for at least a few seconds to allow the other players to consider their options. I guess it's understandable when you realise people may have played a lot of solo games and are just used to clicking on the card they want to activate every turn, but then I've had a couple of games where people are also giving others a telling-off in the comments about making a choice against their wishes, or asking for turns to be rewound because they didn't like the outcome, which is just off.

On the subject of rewinding, I personally don't like to do it unless someone really borked their turn and wants to go back (I've done that myself before when I was away and rushed my turn in a panic when I got back), preferring to just roll with the punches instead of trying to craft the perfect game. I've thought about using the closed hands feature, but it's also important to know what cards the others have and without VOIP visible hands is the only effective way to communicate that.

I'm also annoyed with some of the brusqueness, when people are barking orders like "Advanced" or "Voss" or in the comments, and the head-on rush to get through games as quickly as possible. Playing SotM in person tends to be more relaxed, more chatting and discussion of strategy; even when we used to play on Google+ it was far more relaxed and sociable. It just seems that there are quite a few people in the online game circle who just want to rush through the games without any time for pleasantries, expecting everybody to play the game their way and without delay. It's not endemic or anything, but it's not the sort of table I'd join on a club night if you know what I mean, and yet I don't have much choice on whose game I get involved in.

I think the nature of how the game was made and released has created some interesting situations. When it was only a card game, you played with about the same people over and over. Those people have their own strategies and their groups have their own strategies. Over the years, those strategies become the "good play". Once multiplayer hit, and we are now seeing a bunch of different ways to play, some people are having a hard time differentiating between "good-bad plays" and "different approaches".
