Bachelor of Mathematics reporting for duty! Let's hope I don't screw this up.

At the start of the game, Wager Master puts H condition cards into play. There are 4 copies of Wagelings in a 25 card deck. So if H is 3 or 4, he can win by having H of them on top of the deck when it's initially shuffled.

There's a 4 in 25 chance he plays a Wageling first. P = (4/25) = 16% chance

There's a 3 in 24 chance he also plays a second one. P = (3/24) * (4/25) = 2% chance

There's a 2 in 23 chance he also plays a third one. P = (2/23) * (3/24) * (4/25) ~= 0.1739% chance

There's a 1 in 22 chance he also plays a fourth one. P = (1/22) * (2/23) * (3/24) * (4/25) ~= 0.007905% chance

So the odds are 1 in 575 for H=3, and 1 in 12,650 for H=4.

edit: Oh, I did screw it up, it's more complicated than that. "Cards are revealed from the top of the villain deck until H condition cards are revealed and put into play." That's more math, I'll make another post.

Due to what actually happens, we don't care about the non-condition cards. This makes the odds of it happening a lot higher!

Here are the condition cards:

An Unwise Wager (1)

Losing to the Odds (1)

Not All He Seems (1)

Playing Dice With the Cosmos (1)

The New Deal (1)

Wagelings (4)

Who Are You Fighting? (1)

So there are actually only 10 (TEN!) condition cards!

There's a 4 in 10 chance he plays a Wageling first. P = (4/10) = 40% chance

There's a 3 in 9 chance he also plays a second one. P = (3/9) * (4/10) ~= 13.33% chance

There's a 2 in 8 chance he also plays a third one. P = (2/8) * (3/9) * (4/10) ~= 3.33% chance

There's a 1 in 7 chance he also plays a fourth one. P = (1/7) * (2/8) * (3/9) * (4/10) ~= 0.476% chance

So the actual odds are 1 in 30 for H=3, and 1 in 210 for H=4. Not that rare at all!
