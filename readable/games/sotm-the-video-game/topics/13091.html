As someone who has tested games in the past, you honestly probably won't be missing much and you are probably saving yourself a lot of time and frustration.  The game will crash, it probably don't work as expected or should (maybe some weird Argent Adept interactions), text/art is missing or has place holders.

Network play is always a pain to debug.  You're sitting there in a chat with the developers (who's stayed up late), trying to get your iPad to connect to the server.  And "yes, I've double checked the stupid wireless router" that's sitting three feet away and it's working because we are are chatting through it right now.  And you can connect from your PC, but your iPad just sits there at the connecting screen saying "connecting..." in some saccharine font that you've grown to hate at this point.  And nothing you do seems to get it working, even the debug image the dev team sent you which spits the same message over and over. Connecting...Failed...Connecting...Failed...Connecting...Failed.  

And then you realize that somehow your fricken 8 y/o  connected your iPad to your new smart thermostats wireless network.  (╯°□°）╯︵ ┻━┻

So then you sink into a depression because two hours ago you had assured the developer that "yes, my iPad was connected to the Internet" because you saw that wireless icon in the corner of the iPad showing three bars.  And now you have figure out some way not saying you just wasted precious developer (sleep) time because you couldn't read the network name right next to the stupid wiress icon that said "Honeywell Thermostat".

Okay, maybe I have issues, but seriously it's that kind of frustration that you have to deal with when you beta test.

You may feel cheated, but really there's not a lot of pleasure in testing software.  The pleasure is in finding the issues, reproducing and documenting them (if you can), and submitting bug reports.  Yeah, it gives you early access to some new features or expansions, but that's a side-benefit at best.  Early betas are not about building company loyalty, it's about loyal fans helping the company.

Ben
