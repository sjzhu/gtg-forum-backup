well, the difference is that getting extra card plays is the difference between having your shots ping uselessly off his armor and damage getting through, or being able to set up and play something defensive while at the same time being able to also put out something to sacrifce to the drug.

In addition, there are a host of options for dealing woith large-scale damage.  It brings cards like shielding winds and superhuman durability to the forefront, as well as support cards like twist the ether and redirection cards like tachyon's synaptic interruption.  

Gaining +1 card play is huge for heroes that are heavily dependant on their gear/ongoings.  You can play AZ without having to have EVERY one of his turns being playing a module (especially since it goes back to hand, and not hte trash wher eit could be pulled into play by a modular re-alignment)

the damage is nasty, but you've got a much better shot at counteracting the drugs with extra plays.  If you don't have heroes who can counteract/mitigate damage, then AoG is probably better- though it will be a longer fight.  If you have ways of handling the damage, and a plan for doing so, then the broken vessel side can be really good times.  I'm not saying that flipping him is a good idea every time, but there is definitely a good reason to do it.

Why save the victims?

because he does damage to everyone if he kills them. And if he has his +1 damage drug out, he will deal 4 damage (in a 4 person game) at the end of the turn. 

Many environments have +1 damage effects, and spite gets extra damage if you still have people in the safe house. So it is very easy, particularly as the number of players climbs, for him to do enough damage to trigger "Collateral Damage".  If he has enough of a damage boost, it is quite easy for him to do 4 damage, trigger collateral damage, do 4 damage, trigger collateral damage, etc, etc, etc until all victims have been cleared. 

I generally find large single hits easier to deal with than multiple AOE hits. I'll admit that this is a personal/play style preference!

a lot is going to depend on the environment, modifiers, and team dynamics. If you've got ways to tank and reduce his damage, then keeping hostages out is less of a danger.  If you have people who are setup intensive, and Spite is undoing everything, then it makes more sense to keep stashing people to get more precious cards.  If you have no ways of killing ongoings, and Collateral Damage comes out, then you might not want to tempt fate!

I'm not saying that there isn't a good reason to just keep all the victims in play, but generally I prefer not to so that I (and the people in my group) can get ahead of his drug card-back-to-hand curve, and punch through his armor!
