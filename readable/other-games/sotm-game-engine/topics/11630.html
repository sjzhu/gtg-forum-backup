I saw this come up as a bug for the Video Game (although, getting stuck is a bug), so I wanted to mention the rule here. If a card grants "an additional power this turn", it allows you to use an extra power during your power phase. If that card is played outside of your turn, you don't get a power use, because you have no power phase. So, if a card like Smite the Transgressor or Flame Spike is played outside of your turn, you don't get to use a power.

This came up during development and we could not find any official ruling. It is working as we discussed with Christopher. Here is the logic, which also generalizes to "play an additional card" and "draw an additional card" text:

If the card granting an additional power use is played on a turn other than the card’s owner’s turn, then they immediately get to use a power. If they do not do it immediately, it's gone.

If the card granting an additional power use is played on the card's owner's turn, after the use power phase, then they immediately to use a power. If they do not do it immediately, it's gone.

If the card granting an additional power use is played on the card's owner's turn, before the use power phase, then must wait until their use power phase to use the additional power. (this is the typical use case, BTW)

From what we've been told the power phase is just the time in your turn you get to use a power by default, that is all.

The ruling from Spiff also shows the current standing on when those powers occur.  Since you play Overdrive in your play phase, and can use a different power, then use strike twice, the powers granted by card play don't have to be immediate to the card being played.  I remember that being discussed, and I believe there was an official ruling on it, but searching really old posts is a nightmare, so I can't be 100% sure myself.

As for being granted an additional power in a turn, The rules of Arithmetic apply, 0+1=1.  If you get 1 additional power that turn, and you normally would get 0, you now get 1.  Unload works like Overdrive, Expat gains a number of power uses that turn equal to the number of gun cards in play.  As Christopher has stated before, you aren't limited to using powers, drawing cards or playing cards to the specific phases of your turn, those are just the times that you get to do that action naturally.

These are older rulings, which is why they don't have their citations, but they are from official word.  Things are always subject to change, and I can definitely see extra powers and cards being granted occurring immediately, since otherwise it would lead to a complicated interface for the player.

 

The way you describe it above seems like the best method for doing things in the video game, and while it is a slight alteration from current official word (on these forums) things change like that a decent bit, and gameplay wouldn't be hurt by it.
