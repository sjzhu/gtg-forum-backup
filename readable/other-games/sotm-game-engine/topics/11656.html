I find that ranking is far too much of a generalisation and an over-simplification. You think Fixer is weak but he's extremely strong in certain match-ups and can almost trivialise some villains.

I've never heard anyone else claim Nightmist's unhelpful - I'd say she's pretty regularly near the top of the "my favourite hero" lists and she's arguably the most effective tank in the game, with redirection which can absolutely turn the tide of the game. I've seen her win matches pretty much single-handedly after the other heroes were taken out very early.

Setback can be a very heavy hitter - you have to know how to manipulate his Unlucky Pool, but he's another fan favourite. What better help is there than doing a ton of damage to a target that's hurting the team?

My argument is that you can't simply label one hero as objectively better than the other because how "good" they are depends so much on the villain, what environment cards come out, how much deck control your team has, who the other heroes are, whether there are any damage boosts or damage reductions, whether your teammates can feed you extra cards, and loads of other outside variables. No hero works in isolation.

Take Unity. She can absolutely own the game against a villain who tends to hit the highest HP targets all the time or focuses on ongoing destruction, but she really struggles with area attacks that kill off her bots. She's a lot better when she's being Timeshifted by Omni-X too. Chrono-Ranger, Fanatic and Tachyon can all be seriously weakened by even a little bit of damage reduction to the villain, especially early on. How good are they? It depends. That's kind of my point.

Sure, you can rank them in terms of how much you like to play them, but it's impossible to objectively assess heroes as better or worse than each other. It's just not that simple.

Wow man, you've got some really powerful heroes in your bottome 5

FYI for Parse's Critical Multiplier it only boosts the next instance of damage, using it on Exorcism or Squall will only grant the bonus damage to the first point of damage dealt.  Similarly Haka Of Battle will only boost the first hit of Taiaha or Rampage.

 

You seem to be valuing big shots of damage and healing, but not as much some of the other great ways to affect the game.

Nightmist is absolutely crazy strong, esp. when villains can do more than 2 damage in a hit.  She can take 2 damage to gain 2 cards, and then trade those cards to redirect a shot of damage.  Defensively that's a natural reduction of damage to 2, but it also deals damage to the enemy.

Cards like Mistbound or Take Down are incredibly powerful, setting back better cards a full round, lowering the total cards played by the villain and buying a free roundfor the heroes.  Similarly Grease Gun and Ground Pound can buy a respite from damage.

Combine cards like that with deck control cards liek Visionary has, or Nightmist Parse or Wraith and it gets even better.

Especially good is Suggestion, which can clear certain cards out of the trash, and also give the heroes a specific card on top of the deck, often causing a villain to waste a turn as effectively as Mistbound or Take Down.

Redirection is even better than reduction or healing, Fixer or Nightmist can do it every turn, and it not only preserves their health, but gives you damage as well.

To give one example - Grease Gun versus the Matriarch when she has all her birds out, then another hero uses something which damages everything (or all villain targets), such as Tempest with Squall, or Haka with a Rampage (if he's got Savage mana out, even better :D), or Nightmist with an Oblivion. Birdies all die and no-one gets hit by Lilian or any Carrion Fields :).

One thing about this game is that you can learn so many new things just by talking to other people - it seems clear that you value high damage and healing over pretty much everything else, but playing with other people or talking to them about how they play certain heroes can give you some new insight into things :). I initially played the Scholar mainly by using Flesh-to-Iron but if you can get enough card draw (Team Leader Tachyon is probably the best by far at achieving this for you, but of course stuff like Bring What You Need are helpful too :)) you can put out the other two forms instead (multiple copies of each if you can manage it). Then volunteer to get hit every time there's a choice over who takes damage (or let other heroes hit you when they can attack multiple targets and have some spare damage they'd like to use), because you'll need it - in between doing this, get healed whenever you can (Tempest and the Adept can be super-amazing at this), even if you're unable to use your base power to do so. Your Liquid Form will increase the healing so you get more hp back, then Energy Form will turn it into damage and if you have any buffs (eg from being Galvanised by Legacy) it'll increase again and you're basically hitting something for free every time you get any hp back :). I don't know if I'd've thought of playing the Scholar like that but someone posted about it on this forum somewhere and I thought I'd try it out - it was fun :D.

Anothiner thing you need to bear in mind, as Silverleaf has mentioned, is that how well a hero does is determined by more things than just which cards are in their deck - it depends on which cards actually come up, as well as which other heroes are present (it's a co-operative game, remember, so reliance on your allies is totally a thing :)) and the other their cards come up, and the same again for the villain and environment. Citizen Dawn starting a game with Truth, Sweat, and Tears is considerably worse than if she starts with Blood, Spring, and Assault. But this kind of stuff you'll start to see as you play more games, getting to know the various combos that can occur which are super-amazing or horrifyingly terrible ;).

An even easier way to insta-kill relics is to have Omni-10 Timeshift one - reveal the top card of a deck then put it into the trash or into play. If said top card is a relic, bye bye :).

On the Adept (my favourite character so I have played him a lot :)) I actually very rarely use Cedistic Dissonant. I tend to focus more on stuff that lets other people do stuff. My favourite combo is the Lyra, the Harp, Inspiring Supertonic and then anything else because you use the first instrument to activate Supertonic which activates the other instrument which activates Sueprtonic which can then activate a third instrument (or your base power if you don't have one, or don't have one that's particularly useful right now) to use on some other song which can do something cool. And then you rattle back down the line and finish off the other half of the previous instruments, usually netting yourself 2-4hp in the process (more if you have the Pipes and both copies of Rhapsody of Vigour), if you need it (in the latter stages of a long game, you usually don't).

That said, I don't think heroes should be played with an eye to get their "power combo" going. Yes, on some heroes certain set-ups are very nice (Jim putting Ultimate Target and By Any Means onto something and then getting his Bow out kicks plenty of arse even if doesn't get any external buffs, especialy if the target is one that deals damage on multiple turns, such as retaliation damage), but if you play a deck and only focus on doing that then you might miss all kinds of cool stuff that you could've done with whatever you had instead. There are a lot of cool combos, though - Omni-10 versus Rat is another one. Just get out Ablative Coating, your Reactive Plating Subroutine and for added longevity both copies of Innervation Ray. Then get Infected. At the start of your turn you have to hit yourself for a single point of toxic damage (assuming no buffs)…but it's irreducible so it goes straight through your plating, meaning you've taken toxic damage which that plating would normally protect against, which sets off your RPS and lets you hit Rat. And with an Innervation Ray out you've basically cancelled that self-damage (need both out if Rat still has his Locus or you've been buffed by, say, Legacy), or actually gained a hp if you have both and aren't buffed :).

Oh yeah, that's another thing - however cool things like Galvanise normally are, suddenly they become the bane of your life when fighting someone like Rat ;). Ra with Imbued Fire and Flesh of the Sun God in Insula Primalis when the volcano comes out is pretty epic, though (even more so if any or all of the three Obsidian Fields are in play) :D.

Mr. Fixer

Styles:

Driving Mantis is awesome, a phenomenal tanking card, esp. with Pipe Wrench.  It is better the fewer heroes you have, which is awesome.  A 2 damage redirectionfrom a villain back to a villain is a 4 damage swing, but it also lets allies like Expat or AZ use extra points of damage to hit the same villain an additional time.  With Pipe Wrench and Mantis you can redirect the third hit of Flying Smash, the second hit of Taiaha, etc.  It is really good.

Alternating Tiger:  This one is really good in advanced when villains are more likely to have reduction.  Reliable damage is often better than lots of damage in those matches.

Riveting Crane:  Trades Fixer doing damage for everyone doing it.  It can be tough to get over reduction, but when you can you can set up your whole team.  One of my favorite uses in when Apostate gets his relics out at -2 damage taken and the one redirecting all damage to itself.  Bloody Knuckles+Crane strips that reduction and a power like Squall just tears up.  Is really good with Crowbars when a lot of targets have -1 taken.

Grease Monkey Fist lets you change to any damage type in the game, one of very few cards with that ability.  The +1 damage is also solid.

Tools:

Dual Crowbars:  Fixer's minion beating weapon.  2 targets 2 damage is pretty good for 1 card in play.

Jack Handle:  All non-hero targets is strong, esp. when you can modify it to increase damage or make it irreducible.

Tire Iron:  Great Card.  I love this and Tiger vs. advanced Voss, when -3 damage dealt to villains is frequently going to come up and you can just crush a guard.  Also let's you avoid certain flip conditions that trigger on the villain having 0hp or less.

Hoist Chain:  A less good stun bolt (you have to deal damage) but it is still really great for crushing villains, can work with Mantis in a pinch to get that 3 damage shot down to redirectable levels.

Pipe Wrench: Solid, beastly with Mantis.

The best of the other cards:

Charge: phenomenal card, it is better than Overdrive because it is +1 damage.  Does everything Overdrive does 1 damage better.

Overdrive:  My favorite with Hoist Chain, dropping Apostate or Matriarch damage by 2 is crazy good.

Grease Gun:  It's great, high cost though.

Salvage Yard:  Phenomenal, I've gone into lists about great uses of this card, but to make it quick the recovery of equipment, the second power use if you've gotten an overdrive in the trash, it's amazing.  The best trick is to undo discard costs for heroes like Bunker, Omnitron-X, Haka, etc.  This card is the best one in Fixer's deck, no one else can match the cards to hand power it wields.

Harmony:  Great when you get to use it, not that big a deal though.

Bloody Knuckles:  Situational, when a certain target needs to go down, or to get over reduction with Crane.  It is awesome when someone like Legs or Naturalist are tanking for everyone.

Toolbox:  I like this card for one reason:  when limited destruction requires a sacrifice you can feed it your toolbox, also use it to feed Unity a Golem.

 

Combos:

These are going to get a lot better shortly, but we all have to wait for that.

Writhe:  Blackout.  Hit Fixer, Fixer gets to modify a hit to every non-hero target by his equip, with Jackhandle in play that's X hits of 2 damage to everything where X is the number of non-hero targets in play that can live long enough.  Crowbars/Monkey/BloodyKnuckles is the best I've pulled off.  Fixer took 4, then wiped the Chairman off the board from full Health.

Also with Crowbars and Tiger Writhe's signature lets him return the Driving Mantis favor.

Redirecting those extra hits from hero friends to double up on a target.

Adept:  Out of turn powers and card play are wicked with Fixer, esp when you play Grease Gun out of turn to avoid its cost.

KNYFE:  KNYFE and her destroy on use equipment love Fixer.

Nightmist:  Oblivion loves Driving Mantis, and Nightmist loves getting cards back in hand.  You can KO a villain with their own attacks pretty easily with these 2.

Haka loves getting cards back, and with Ground Pound + Grease Gun you can crush the enemies ability to hit you.  Taiaha double up is fun, and Rampage likes Driving Mantis a lot.

Expat:  Expat with speed loading and Fixer is awesome, use that ammo for free then return it all to hand for another run.

Unity: get your equip back, Fixer is great for loaning you equipment to turn into golems and Hasty Augmentation is amazing with Fixer.

Almost every hero has something awesome they do with Fixer, he is definitely more of a veteran player hero in that you need to know what combos you can do with the heroes, villain and environment decks in the game.
