Current thoughtwave on selecting targets:

If a card affects [select number] targets, or up to [select number] targets, dealing a quantity of damage not dependant on any quality (HP count for example) then targets are chosen one at a time and damage resolved one at a time; which is to say, targets are chosen one at a time as part of effect resolution

Example: Haka's Taiaha hits Target A; if hitting/destroying Target A causes Target B to come into play, Target B is a valid target for the second instance of damage

Example 2: Korrupton deals every target 1 damage. If somehow during resolution damage dealt / destruction of a target caused a new target to come into play, that new target would be affected by Korrupton just the same.

If a card affects any number of targets, applying differing effects based on target qualities (like HP count) then the first instance of damage will target and resolve, then the second instance of damage will target and resolve; effect resolution is interrupted to check for targets (and validity thereof) before any instance of damage

Example: Grand Warlord Voss deals H-1 damage to the Hero with the highest HP and H-2 damage to the lowest. Let's say there are 4 Heroes in play, one is at 13 HP, two are tied at 14 and one is at 15. The Hero at 15 will be brought down to 13, then he could be targeted again because he is now tied for lowest.

If a card, however, applies the same effect to a number of targets based on target qualities (like HP count)... Then we start having a problem. In this case you choose targets before resolving the effect and then deal damage to them, without rechecking for targets.

Example: Actually phantaskippy's example with Ambitious Racketeer works fine.

 

Why does it work like this? Beats me. This is as played by the rules as it gets, as confirmed by how it works on the videogame. It is also, in my opinion, terribly inelegant, inconsistent and counterintuitive. I can totally understand why it works like that. I still don't like it.

You can't just state that all effects first choose targets and then resolve - well you could at your game table, but be aware that things break under this, most notably Fanatic's End of Days. Personally I'd like to somewhat normalize situation number 1 and 2 to work similarly to number 3 - when the number of targets is finite (be that number and/or targeting method and/or the effect quantity variable or fixed) you target first and resolve second; you keep only the "All targets" condition as an edge case in which you resolve the effect then recheck for new targes.

It's a big mess imho. This game would benefit immensely from some mechanical rigor.

The first ones have to hit X targets.  That means they can't hit the same target twice, but they can hit more targets if they haven't reached X yet.  And they are not particular about which targets they're hitting.

I actually did come up with a house rule way to handle "Deal the 3 targets with the highest HP" and similar cases in a manner similar to the way "Deal up to 3 targets" without creating non-intuitive results.

 

Problem:  If each target is chosen seperately, then when the damage dealt changes the HP order of the targets, it becomes possible to skip dealing damage to the highest HP target.

Example:  "Deal the 3 targets with the highest HP 2 damage" when the targets are at A(22), B(19), C(18), & D(17).  Barring schenanigans caused by reacting triggers, A,B,&C should take damage, D should not.  But if targets are chosen sequentially, then if C is chosen as the first target, then it will drop below D, moving D into the "top 3".  You could then choose D and then B, and A would not take damage.  Clearly, this is flawed.

Solution:  I can't think of a good way to word this in general, so I'll word it for the specfic example shown above.  The first shot can target any of the first 3.  Now there are only two shots left, so the 2nd shot can target any of the first 2, excluding any previously targeted targets, and the 3rd shot must target the highest not-yet-previously targeted target.

Example:  Scenario above:  C is chosen first.  Now the order is A,B,D,C.  The second shot can only target A or B.  Whichever is targeted, the other must be the last target.  If A was chosen first, the order is now A,B,C,D, but A's been chosen, so the next target must be one of the top two of B,C,D.  Either way, it still will achieve the expected result.

 

I haven't heavily tested this method with schenanigans, but I do think that it will behave intuitively in most cases (i.e.  You won't be able to leave the highest HP  target untouched unless it takes damage from some other source in the middle of the damage or a new target replaces it as highest HP, etc)

 

It's obviously not the offical way that the targets are chosen, but if you prefer it to the offical version, feel free to use it as a house rule.
