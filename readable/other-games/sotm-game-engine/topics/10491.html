Yup, you can play any combination you like of hero, villain, and environment decks. It's just the Vengeance villains who are different, as they have a mechanic unique to them in which you fight H of them, as opposed to other villains where you just fight that villain. Okay, the Ennead are a bit different in that they're a villain group as well, but they only have the one deck between them and all act on the same turn (a Vengeance game has villain and hero turns alternating, so you normally have each player controlling the villain who predeces them), same as the Sentinels do.

It's also possible to tweak in vengeance villains with classic villains in game of 4 or 5 heroes. Like say you find ambuscade boring and wont to spice things up a bit by giving him a partner that helps with his weakness: ongoing and equipment destruction. What I did in a 4 hero game was play Ambuscade as H=3 and add Ermine to the party. Environnment H is still 4 though. And Ermine would play between the 2nd and third hero to make it as even as possible.

 

This is an unofficial use of vengeance and can make your game harder but when I tested it it works well especially against weak villains.
