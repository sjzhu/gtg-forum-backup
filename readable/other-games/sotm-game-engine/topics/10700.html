There are a few heroes that can do small things by being in a certain.  But it's really not that big of a deal.

One example off the top of my head is having someone with Ongoing destruction going first. THe can destroy Baron Blade's Ongoings, thus preventing anyone from having to take extra damage or have to overcome some DR.

Unity before Mr. Fixer, because of Mr. Fixer's Grease Gun, which is a card that will destroy itself at the start of his turn. Welcome to the site!

Some folks like to optimize situations, so they'll figure out interactions and put heroes in the appropriate locations based on the specific situation (heroes, villain, and environment). Because there are so many permutations, it's hard to say there's a solid set of rules you could follow. For example, some folks like to place Haka so he can take out as many targets as possible to put under Savage Mana. Against some villains, you might want him to be later in the rotation so other heroes can soften up the targets, but against the Matriarch he's better off being early so he can take out as many of her birds as possible.

For our group, we pick our seats, pick what we're playing (which is quasi-randon), and discover how that matters in the course of play. Generally, we don't worry about optimizing our situations - at least, if anyone does, they don't tell me about it!  For me, doing that results in a lot of time doing analysis, generally after a day of doing analysis (as that's my job), so I'm more interested in relaxing and enjoying a game.

That's just me, though!

What the others said, having someone that can deal with a nasty card early (Ongoing, Environment, or damage reduction) can be helpful. The same with heroes that buff other heroes.

Ignoring the optimizing issue, a lot of times I like to have heroes that do consistent damage earlier in the turn order. In SotM, overkill doesn;t do anything, and you really want to destroy something with "exact change." When I first started playing with Expat, I would put her last, since, by that time, I would know precisely what she needed to do. Needs some more damage to destroy things, play Incediary Rounds. However, if you don't need that extra damage, she can play another gun, or load another gun, to keep her ahead of the game.

In truth, nothing here can be predicated from the outset by asking everyone what they can or cannot do. Sometimes, though, it can be a pain to do the precise calcualtions, and sometimes we are talking about the difference of only a single point of damage. Anytime a character doesn't have to deal damage, they can setup for later.

Heroes who let others play cards outside of the normal turn order are the only ones with whom I usually pay attention to seating order. They're most effective after heroes with "Until the start of your next turn..." effects so they don't just expire right away. Putting them before heroes with cards that act on their turn and then stay in play, however, allows them an extra turn to act. There are also some hero cards (Hunter and Hunted et al.) that can both hurt and help, and their owners might want to destroy them before the villain turn rolls around.

Let me know who I missed; I'm at work and somewhat rushed:

      1. Heroes with effects that expire automatically on their turn (Tachyon, The Naturalist, The Sentinels, Legacy, Scholar, Parse*)
      2. Heroes that allow others to play cards out-of-turn (Parse, Omnitron X, Argent Adept)
      3c. Heroes that may want to destroy their "extra" cards before the villain turn (Mr. Fixer, Chrono-Ranger, Setback, The Sentinels)
      3b. Heroes with effects that act during their turn and then stay in play (Omnitron X*, Unity, Absolute Zero, Tempest, Ra, Expatriette, Chrono-Ranger)
      3c. Heroes with cards that [may] trigger during their turn and then blow themselves up (Parse*, Bunker, KNYFE, Unity)
       *When more than one out-of-turn enabler is in the game

This also applies to out-of-turn power usages for the handful of powers with Start of Turn expiry dates. Legacy prefers to go before The Scholar, for example, so his Next Evolution can stay active for the villain turn. GI Bunker's base power is the same way.
