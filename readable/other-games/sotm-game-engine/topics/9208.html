When I'm introducing SOTM to new people, I find that the hardest thing to do is to explain the heroes that they have to choose from in a way that will instantly make sense.  I tend to fumble through awkward introductions, of which one notable example was me finding myself able to identify Bunker only as "the guy who needs a gun" in slightly more words than this, and having someone choose to play him on that basis.  When RC/IR shipped, I was pleased to note that the four heroes in the set did not get only biographical info, but also a little "How to play" section.

I think it would be a good idea to make up little cards or sheets of paper with very brief introduction info for all of the characters, to facilitate these kinds of choices.  These would be a nice accessory that you could include in your set if you were big on enrolling new players, rather than just having a steady playgroup.  So I'm opening this thread as a brainstorming and copywriting arena for the project.  The goal will be brevity, clarity, and immediate grokkability - this will be a way to get people into the game who know nothing about it, who aren't necessarily big on comic books or even familiar with card games (generally they will be gamers of some sort, but very little should be taken for granted).

From what I remember reading when I first downloaded the charts to add to my own cards, the creator counted the cards that had each effect. Simple as that, they were intended to simply be an indication of the proportions of each deck that involve healing, or deck manipulation, or whatever.

I honestly don't see another even vaguely sensible way of quantifying such complicated and varied powers (which as we all know will have different "values" depending on lots of other variables like number of heroes, what other cards are out, whether the Scholar's in play, or whatever).

Say Legacy has an arbitrary attack value of 2. How do you compare Ra's attack value to this? Sure, we'd all agree it should be higher than Legacy's 2, but how much higher? We'd never reach a consensus because we'd be using a fuzzy definition of "attack value". "Number of enemy-damaging cards in the deck" is a concrete value that no-one can argue with.
