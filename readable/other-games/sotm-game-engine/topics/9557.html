Note also that you can use Omni-10's Timeshift to get around restrictions like Bunker's Mode cards, or the Swarm of Scarabs in the Tomb of Anubis which prevent you from drawing cards, or the Hostage Situation in Megalopolis which prevents you from playing them - with Timeshift, you aren't drawing a card or playing one - you're revealing it and then putting it into play. The only time this wouldn't work would be if your deck had no cards left, because revealing from a deck doesn't let you reshuffle it.

So really, any card which enters play is being "put into play". It's just that not every card that gets put into play is actually "played".

Depends on your character. Yeah, the Scholar and Nightmist would both like to get as many cards into their hands as possible. But the more Omni-10 Timeshifts himself, the faster all his lovely components enter play. By saying "Timeshift is kind of like drawing", I meant it as in "you get to take a card off the top of your deck, which you can normally only do when drawing". If you're a hero who doesn't need loads of cards in-hand and you get a card you don't want, you can just ditch it. If you're a hero who has cards that you might not want to play right now (Inventory Barrage, Self-Sabotage, etc) then fair enough if that was a card you might have wanted to use later but there'll be another copy of it somewhere and anyway you might have someone like the Adept, Fixer, or Tempest in your group who may be able to get it back for you ;).

At least you get a choice of what to do with a Timeshifted card - not so much with a DDA'd one, should you choose to play your top card, or with Setback's base power.
