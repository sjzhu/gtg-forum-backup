From the recent Fireside Chat update:

Dual Crowbars works somewhat like Isothermic Transducer. The final damage dealt from the first instance of damage forms the initial amount/type/irreducibility of the second instance of damage. That means you can benefit twice from damage increases.

I'm curious about the irreducibility part of that.  *Usually* irreducibility is granted by the original source of the damage, so inheriting it in the second instance makes sense.  But what if the irreducibility came from somewhere else?

Example: GI Bunker uses his Panzer-Buster power on, say, a Gene-Bound Guard.  Mr. Fixer (with Dual Crowbars out) then uses Strike against that Guard, and the damage becomes irreducible from Panzer-Buster.  Dual Crowbars kicks in, starting a new damage instance, whcih Mr. Fixer aims at Grand Warlord Voss.  Is the damage against Voss irredcucible?

By the ruling above, clearly yes.  But it doesn't seem obvious to me that this wrinkle was considered when making the ruling.  It's not a game-breaker either way, but I thought the issue was worth at least a brief discussion here.

While my gut says that it shouldn't be irreducible, the wording on the card is vague, and actually demands it is. Dual Crowbars does not specify damage type for the second instance, which means that if it is inheriting one quality of the damage (type). If it inherits one quality, it should inherit all qualities, which would include irreducible. While the irreducibility is attached to Panzer-Buster's target, "that damage" is now identified as "damage dealt" rather than the initial damage dealt before bonuses. This means the time to determine the second instance's initial damage is when you apply the first instance damage to the first target.

It seems arbitrary to place the damage amount determination after reduction, and somehow drop the quality irreducible, since the determination of whether the damage is irreducible must be done before the reduction itself is done. In cases where Panzer-Buster is not applied, the damage was deemed reducible before reduction was be applied, and the amount of damage to be dealt is determined after reduction.

The damage types are Cold, Fire, Toxic, Psychic, Melee, Projectile, Radiant, Infernal, Sonic, Lightning, and Energy. You can deal Irreducible Cold damage. You cannot deal Fire Toxic damage.

This is why I have been using the term Quality, and MigrantP uses the term Attribute for the same purpose. Quality is not a defined Term, but something indicating the various adjectives describing some aspect of the damage. We need a term like this for this discussion, because we have not been given one in the Rules. Type is just a Quality. Irreducible is another Quality. Pickle is also a Quality, though unused in the game (Guise points at his pants and winks). Any adjective that is be used to describe the damage would be a Quality or MigrantP's Attribute.

All damage must be one of the 11 types. Since Dual Crowbars does not give the damage a Type, the only place the Type could come from is the Type of the damage Dual Crowbars is duplicating. Irreducible is a Quality describing the damage, not a Quality describing the defences of the original target. Damage can be Irreducible, and you will find that term described in the Damage section of the rules, and that places the Quality Irreducible on the damage, not on the defences of the target. Since the second instance of damage already must copy the Quality Type, there is no reason it would not copy the Quality Irreducible. It would also copy the Quality Pickle, if it had it. What's confusing everyone is that when multiple targets are select, like with Taiaha, the second chosen target cannot be the same as the first chosen target.  Dual Crowbars doesn't work like that, because Dual Crowbars doesn't change the damage dealt to select two targets.  (That's how it used to work, more or less, but I digress).  Dual Crowbars is a second instance of damage that triggers off of the first instance of damage being dealt, which means that the target which is excluded is the target that was damaged by the first instance, not the target that was first targeted by the first instance.

Scenario 2: Fixer attacks A, but the damage gets redirected to B. Then Dual Crowbars lets him attack A. This is fine, because A did not take damage from the first attack, B did.

In the most common case (scenario 1), targetting for Dual Crowbars works the same as other multi-hit attacks. But once you throw redirection into the mix (scenarios 2 and 3), the rules are almost the opposite of the normal rules.
