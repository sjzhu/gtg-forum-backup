After some extensive searching, I figured out what I was remembering, although I could not find the thread in question. I definitely remember it happening, though.

We had a discussion on Iron Legacy, with both Redirections out, on his advanced flip side. The question was, how many hits would it take to break through those? The sample damages were 3 hits of 5, 5, and 4; they could be assigned in any order.

And the answer was that it depended on what order the hits were in. Since the redirections triggered in the order in which they were  taken, the first hit would redirect regardless of how much damage it was. So, if the first hit was a 4, then the second hit of 5 would trigger the first redirection, and the third hit of 5 would trigger the second.

 

In retrospect, I'm inclined to believe that this was erroneous; after all, if redirecting it means that he never took the damage -- which is what allows the second redirection to trigger on the "first" time he takes damage, even though it's not the first time he's been targeted -- then what is stopping the first redirection from just triggering infintely? Or Driving Mantis, for that matter?

On the other hand, this seems to conflict with the general idea that "being dealt" damage = "taking" damage. If it's the first time he's taking damage, then it's the first time he's being dealt damage, which means that -- according to our ruling on temporary damage reduction vs. redirection, wherein we determined that redirecting damage away from a target with a "next time this target is dealt damage" effect does NOT use up the DR, as it did not take damage -- the redirection itself cancels out the first instance of taking damage.

So, while I'm inclined to agree that Guise and Iron Legacy should only be able to use their "first time each turn" redirections once, it means that we will need to reconsider our position on redirections affecting whether or not something has, in fact, been dealt damage. If it hasn't, then the next damage it takes will trigger another redirect, since that will be the first time it is dealt damage. If it has, then that will use up any "next time it is dealt damage" or like effects, even if it is not the final target.

This is my attempt to understand what our discussion is about.  It will probably oversimplify a number of things but should hopefully achieve it's goal of highlighting how our opinions relate to each other.

 

There are currently in existence 4 distinct "temporary" damage affecting effects (I hope I got affect/effect right there) that I see:

  1. Increase/Reduce the next damage dealt to/by <Name> by X
  2. The first time <Name> would be dealt <> damage, redirect it to <>
  3. The first time <> damage would be dealt to <Name> each turn, prevent that damage.  (Currently only found on Deadline's Auto-Armor Caster)
  4. Increase/Reduce the first damage dealt to/by <Name> each turn by X.  (Currently found only on Fright Train's Engine of Destruction)

 

(You could split this into several subcategories by seperating increase/reduce, or to/by, but I think that these are the important categories for this discussion.)

 

I think that 2 and 3 can be considered the same for all analysis of the subject.  1 has gotten an official ruling that instances of 1 will not get used up unless HP is removed - unless they were necessary to achieve the end result.  I don't think 2 and 3 have been officially ruled, but at least one thread in the past has agreed that all instances of 2 and 3 will get used up at once.

 

I think that our discussion could be summed up as "what would happen to case 4."  If I understand Pydro's position correctly, he holds that the difference between 1 and 2/3 is "first" vs "next", and that 4 should be ruled like 2/3.  My position is that the difference is between "reduction/increasing" vs "redirection/prevention", and that 4 should therefore be ruled like 1.

 

My argument (which I think is shared in essence with Phantaskippy) is that prevention and redirection are replacement effects (signified by "would be dealt damage") as opposed to application effects (signified by "is dealt damage") and that the distinction allows for a different handling method (which is not a timing difference) for when they get used up.

Pydro's argument is that my "different handling method" is in fact a timing difference, and since it's been established that there is no timing difference between application effects and replacement effects, it cannot be used as the determiner of behavior.  Therefore, the difference must be that "next" has unique handling distinct from "first".

 

 

Pydro and Phantaskippy (but especially Pydro), do you feel that I have accurately summed up this discussion and your opinions, and do you have anything that you wish to correct or add?

Yes, but the next time need not be the first time in a turn. I agree with the consensus in this thread that "first time [text] each turn" is special. The examples listed a bit sloppy, because the omit that wording and just say first time.

The semantic parsing gets a bit ugly, but here's my opinion:

If you have two such first time each turn effect active, and you get damaged, only the first one triggers. This is important, because some future card might have side effects. You just resolve the first card, and ignore the second, because you weren't damaged.

If you have two next time effects, same thing.

If you get damaged a second time with two first time each turn effects, the second effect does not trigger, because you've already passed the "first time" condition. It's now really the second time, at least for purposes of the qualifying event.

If you have two next time effects, it's still "the next time" because the effect's still in play, so it triggers.

 

Now where it gets tricky is stacking redirects and reducing damage. My take on it would be that we're dealing with an event flow, and that if you triggered redirection, you can still trigger damage reduction later, because you never got to the "actually apply damage" step in the event flow. And in this particular flow, all of your first time / next times trigger (and get used up) when you would apply damage to a target. This is because the damage isn't sent away, so you keep reducing the damage until you run out of effects that reduce it.

In a nutshell, redirecting first damage in a turn would not prevent you from later reducing first damage in a turn.

If I am going to get on the next bus that stops, and then a bus stops and I don't get on, and then another pulls up and I get on I did not get on the "next" bus.

If I am going to get on the first bus that stops, and then a bus stops and I don't get on, and then another pulls up and I get on I did not get on the "first" bus.

If the first bus drives by and doesn't stop, and the next one does, then I got on the "Next" bus that stopped, as well as the "first" bus that stops.

 

If something would deal you damage, and it is the first time that turn that you would have been dealt damage, and the first time since the "next" effect was activated that you would have been dealt damage, then it is both.

Any damage after that can not qualify for either, because the next and first already occurred.

If after the first time you would have been dealt damage, you re-activate the "Next" effect then that second damage will be the next damage.

 

Next does not self-reapply, the Next bus is not "every bus that stops until I get on," it is the first one after you activate "next."

I think that the best way to word something that would trigger twice (due to having two sources, like synaptic interruption), it should say "Whenever X" instead of "the next X" or "the first X".  Then, if the first instance that would trigger, does, it uses the trigger the second would have used.  And the second can then trigger when the event occurs again.  But those usually require destruction of the card or some other way to signify that particular instance has triggered.  If it is a card that stays in play but should redirect if X damage makes it through (only once each turn, though), how should it be worded so that two instances of that card can redirect two instances of that damage and no more?

Then, once we figure out how they should be worded, we can see if the current wording on any of these cards seems like they were aiming for that goal or for a single-shot regardless of instances effect.

Personally, I think the cards that say "the first" are trying to be multi-card, single instance (no matter how many cards of that type you have out, they only trigger once) and the ones that say "the next" are trying to be multi-card, multi-instance (each card stops one instance).  In which case the flow order goes like this:  Damage is trying to be dealt.  Check the first card that says it can stop it.  It triggers and is spent.  The next time damage is dealt, we skip the first card and check the next.  If it is unspent, it becomes spent and stops it.  Otherwise, the wording difference between "first" and "next" has no point and they should all say first or all say next.

"The next time" is always the first time after a given point in time. But what point is that exactly? Is it ever stricktly defined? Why can't that point in time fluxuate under certain cercumstances? In fact, we already know that it does. Look at Haka of Shielding. Les say Haka uses Shielding on himself and two instances of 2 damage comes his way. The first gets redirected by Wrest the Mind. We know that Haka of Shielding in this case is  still considered active and will reduce that second instance of damage. Why? Because since Haka of Shielding wasn't used, it is still active. We are still technically reducing the "next" instance of damage that would be dealt since the card is still active.

Card is active, trigger clause is literally met, so effect will trigger.

However, "the first time each turn" is always the first time after a specific point in time, the start of each turn. That specific point can't fluxuate due to its very definition. So say those two instances of 2 damage get dealt to Fixer with Mantis in play. The first gets redirected by Wrest the Mind. Now we have a case were Mantis might still be active because it wasn't used, but quite litterally it is now the 2nd time this turn he would be dealt an instance of 2 or less damage, and the trigger clause on Mantis fundamentally can't be triggered. Whether Mantis was used before or not makes no difference.

Card is active, trigger clause can not be met, so effect doesn't trigger.

First time and Next time are not terms you can whimsically interchange as you please. They have different connotations and apply differently under certain contexts.

This is the classic case of Square = Rectangle but Rectangle =/= Square. 

Square = First time

Rectangle = Next time
