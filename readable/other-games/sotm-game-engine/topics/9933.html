Medic-Tank:

Even then not a lot of cards allow you to control the top two cards of an environment deck (only one in Visionnary's deck or deck stacking with Parse though I fail to see why you would put an entry point on top instead of leaving it buried as the bottom card so even the situation you mentionned is an extreme corner case.

I agree not many cards let you manipulate the environment deck, and that it's not that likely to come up often. I also agree that it would probably make more sense for Entry Point to play first, shuffle second—that's why I was thinking whether there was any scenario it would change, and wondeirng if there were more common scenarios others could think of.  I enjoy coming up with the weird situation where these distinctions matter, having playtested other games.

 

That said, and for the sake of exercising those muscles a bit more, I wouldn't call the example an extreme corner case.  Firstly, because if there are no Rooms out, Entry Point isn't dangerous, so there's really no reason not to put it on top—if you're deciding which card goes on top and which goes on the bottom, for example, Frost's Cryo Chamber hurts you directly.  Depending on who the heroes and illains are, it's actually reasonably likely I'd put the Room on the bottom and Entry Point on top, to let it harmlessly shuffle itself back into the deck.  Also, you're assuming the Entry Point was put on top, but that's only if the deck was manipulated this round—it's easy to imagine that I put something helpful on top in a previous round, and now, one or more rounds later, the Entry Point comes up while a card I buried is still on the bottom.  For another example, maybe Ra put some helpful Rooms on the bottom on the environment deck with Excavation, and Entry Point with no Room lets them cycle back into play sooner than they might otherwise have done, so if you shuffle Entry Point ahead of playing the new card, you can potentially get to one of the cards Ra recycled right away, and/or in a different order than you arranged them.

Unusual cases, sure, but I don't think they're too convoluted, and for the purposes of discussing errata even just the fact that they exist is worth knowing.

Even without infinite loops, Entry Point is just annoying.  With four copies in the deck, you very often have to reshuffle three or four times in a row before you find a card that isn't an Entry Point.  Since it's guaranteed that if one Entry Point fails, so will any others which replace it, there's really no excuse for enabling them to chain (although if you somehow played two Environment decks at once, it'd be sort of fitting for Surprise Shopping Trip to go off a bunch of times while you're resolving this, as Fright Train is kinda doing the same thing on Entry Point that Spite is doing on SST).  I think I will end up houseruling this to be less of a nuisance.

I've pointed to the weirdness of the Wrest the Mind ruling quite often, and this is another case.  How can Entry Point even continue to resolve after it's been shuffled into the deck?  The more I think about it, the more I wonder why SOTM bothers having rules at all, if the ultimate resolution is always supposed to just come down to "do whatever you think is the most fun".
