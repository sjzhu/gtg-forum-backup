Subtle distinctions:

If there are multiple cards that happen "when [condition X]" happens, the players choose the order they happen in.

If there are any conditions that happen 'when [condition X] WOULD happen, INSTEAD [do Y]", then X doesn't ever actually happen in the first place, so nothing with a "when X happens" triggers.

If there are multiple WOULD/INSTEAD conditions based on the same X trigger, then, again, the players choose the order.

It does matter because you decide which on-destruction effect occurs first - if Sustained Influence, the card never ends up in Chokepoint's area because it goes back into play. If Chokepoint, then the card ends up in Chokepoint's area and can't be removed until the proper conditions are met (Chokepoint flips, etc).

While I acknowledged I could be wrong, I don't think I am. You have two conflicting outcomes and whenever you have two conflicting outcomes in Sentinels, players choose which outcome occurs, or specifically the order in which conflicting outcomes are applied. This can mean that an outcome is no longer possible or that a card that was causing things to happen ceases to cause things to happen. 

Take Apocalypse or End of Days - both say they destroy all cards other than those cards, character cards, or relics, but if something destroys the card before all cards are destroyed any remaining cards are not destroyed. Say you have either card take out villain and environment cards, then bee bot - bee bot destroys the card and Apocalypse or End of Days stops right there and no further cards are destroyed despite both cards saying that all cards but themselves, relics, and character cards are destroyed.

Similarly, if you have conflicting upon destruction effects - such as Chokepoint and Sustained Influence - you pick one upon destruction and the other effect becomes irrelevant. Chokepoint's card does not say that it supercedes all other upon destruction effects so there's no reason to assume that it does.
