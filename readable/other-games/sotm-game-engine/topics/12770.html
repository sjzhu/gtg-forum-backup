Redirected damage is not a new instance of damage, it just changes the target.  Thus it does not get boosted again unless the new target has a modifier that will increase or reduce it

 

The card effect in that case is fulfilled as much as possible.  So each player with equipment would destroy one, it would destroy a scion and then itself.  Unless the card has a condition on it, it does each thing as much as it can.  So in your Setback example he would remove 1 token.

I'll probably get ninjaed before I finish typing this out but here goes

For your first question, when damage gets redirected the damage is reevaluated.  I'm assuming in this case that Progeny is dealing two damages to all hero targets and that KNYFE has her shield out which reduces damage by one from the villain with the most health.   In this particular case when the damage is redirected to Scholar he would have two damage coming his way and you would need to evaluate what he has in play to see if it stays at two damage. 

For your second question that card and a scion card would be destroyed regardless of whether every hero has an equipment to destroy.  If the card read that the card and a scion card was destroyed if each hero destroyed one equipment card then it would remain in play.  Since it the text doesn't state it's dependant on the destruction of equipment from each hero it proceeds to destroy a Scion card in play and itself.  

For your next questions, in talking about the same card you would just destroy one equipment for each hero.  If your hero doesn't have an equipment card in play then you don't destroy one.  Again, Time of Tribulations is not dependent on all heroes have an equipment card to destroy for its remaining effects to happen. 

 In the Setback example you would remove the one unlucky token you have in your pool and only get the benefit of the health gained in the second line since you were not able to remove two total unlucky tokens.

You look at target 1, and access all modifiers to see what damagw would be dealt, then if it qualifies for redirection you redirect it.

When you redirect the damage you start over as if the new target was the target from the beginning.  Access all modifiers, including ones that repeat, and excluding ones that applied to the first target, but not the actual target.

So nemesis bonuses, target specific increases and decreases are only going to apply when that specific target is being damaged.

 

The reason you apply modifiers then redo it all is because you only redirect damage that "would be dealt", if no damage would be dealt then you don't redirect.  However when damage is redirected, it isn't a new target taking the other target's hp loss, they use their own defenses, and own weaknesses.

If you redirect kryptonite damage from Superman to Batman, Batman isn't taking the +20 from kryptonite that Superman would have taken.

The best way to think about it is that you should do everything on the card, literally, in the order it says.

So if it says to do one thing and then do another thing, you resolve the two things in that order. If you couldn't do the first thing, then you just go on to the second. It's almost like there were two separate cards, with two separate instructions on them.

If it says to do one thing and "if you do" to do another thing, then and only then is the second resolution conditional on the first.

Does your group play Dominion? That's a game where this sort of literal card interpretation is similarly important. There, as here, conditional actions are the exception rather than the rule, and as such they specifically say "if you do..." or something similar. But otherwise, you just do it like it says!
