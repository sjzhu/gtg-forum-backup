#60

Recharge, Upgrade, Turret, all of his modes present opportunities with cost, when you play Recharge, you don't play cards till you destroy it.  When you switch out of recharge you are deciding to stop drawing huge amounts of cards so you can play/use powers.

You may hope to draw the cards you need for the desired set-up, but if you get some options but not all you want you have to make a choice of when to stop recharging and join the fight again.

Upgrade is often a tougher choice, esp. when you have a ton of good cards in hand.  Turret mode is awesome, but it essentially locks you down as long as it is on the field, you can't draw except with Ammo Drop and you can't play cards to improve your build.

Most people play Bunker looking to achieve a certain build, and while acheiving that build might hold a lot of value to the team, often players overlook higher leverage situations in trying to accomplish that build.

"Fixing what is in front of you" simply means you do whatever you can that turn to deal with the problems of that turn, if you can take an enemy out that would otherwise live and yet wait, you allow the situation to worsen.  Often the first few rounds of the game are where the game is actually settled, esp. villains that start with cards in play.  Taking a flyer on those rounds to do better later, when others tend to also be really powerful isn't worth it.

Take the shots you get, don't dump the early game on your team and work for end game, Bunker isn't built that way.

One of the reasons I like the house rule where you can return a mode to your hand to play another at the start of your turn is that rule allows players who really want Bunker to be more of a good early, strong end game hero to do just that.
