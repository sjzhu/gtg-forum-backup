I’m a little embarrassed that I’m don’t know this one exactly, but here we go.

When a redirect changes the recipient of damage, is the recipient of the damage then the target? This would matter – significantly – for those powers that damage multiple targets. For example, if Wraith is using throwing knives against a flipped Adv. Iron Legacy, and “The first time Iron Legacy would be dealt damage each turn, redirect that damage to the hero target with the highest HP” happens, could he be hit by the second instance of damage from the power?

I’ve taken it to be the case that you can, on the same general principles as “Taiaha killing the zombie created by the cultist killed by Taiaha” and "Damage dealt equals hit points reduced". The "damage dealt" clarification doesn't directly apply, but points to thinking along the lines of "the results drive the narrative" and the redirect changes the target just as the damage resistance decreased the damage dealt. The "Taiaha targeting" clarification derives from the non-instantaneousness of the game.

From those two lines of thinking, the second knife can hit Iron Legacy, at the cost of first hitting a team mate. How far off base is the thinking?

Ah yes, I had forgotten about things where things can into play due to things, I guess I don't do it that way at all!

Another such situation is the End Of Days / Forced Deployment interaction.  If EOD first chose all its targets, then destroyed them, then at the end of it, Voss would have lots of minions in play.  In fact, what EOD does is more like

  1. Destroy an eligible card

  2. Are all eligible cards destroyed?

2a. No, return to step 1.

2b. Yes.  First sentence is therefore complete.

  1. Destroy End Of Days.

 

Similarly, anything that damages multiple targets can be "unpacked" to be a set of single-target damages, each of which gets evaluated individually.  If that damage is based on a variable (e.g. Electro-Pulse Explosive, Drawn To The Flame (against Omnitron), etc.), then the amount being dealt can change between targets as the underlying variable changes.
