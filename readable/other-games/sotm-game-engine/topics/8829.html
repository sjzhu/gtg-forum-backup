There are perfectly legitimate reasons to call those things golems outside of the fact that Unity is at least culturally Jewish.

Yes Golems are derived from Jewish folklore, but the connotations associated with words like Robot, Android, Mech, Golem, ect, are very different. Android for instance draws images of having biological and mechanical functions and closely resembling human physiology. Robots are your run of the mill A.I. controled pieces of metal. Mechs conjure images of a Robot with a pilot. Golems on the other hand are anthropomorphic beings, which has a slight distinction from Androids who are still mostly AI based. They have real human emotion and thoughts that are not coming from mechanical proccesses like electric currents and circuits, but imbued from some outside source.

Unity's bots all have distinct personalities imbued in them, at least that is the sense that I get. So Jewish or not, Golem is the most appropriate and accurate term for the things she creates.

I imagine that Unity's little friends are keyworded "golem" for several possible reasons.

1) To distinguish them from Omni-X and Omnitron, who are obviously robots (even if they are not keyworded as such) and obviously not the same kind of construct as everyone's favourite Raptor Bot. A card directing you to affect a "robot" in some way would cause endless confusion and have players trying to use it illegally on Omnitrons because they could quite rightly argue that they are robots too.

2) To reserve the keyword "robot" for future cards. I consider this unlikely, due to the Omniton issue.

3) The concept of traditional golems is thousands of years old (dates back to when mechanical objects were very simple) and relates to God creating Adam from clay (and presumably others - I don't pretend to be an expert in biblical stuff). Unity's golems are described in her bio as "mechanical golems", i.e. golems, but mechanical rather than the traditional clay type. "Mechanical golem" is a bit long for a keyword!

4) There isn't a good word other than "golem" for an autonamous being that is (magically) constructed from inanimate objects. "Robot" implies entirely mechanical, constructed in a mundane fashion rather than with magic. "Construct" is too vague.

Anyway, back to religion. While it's reasonable to assume that Unity's culturally Jewish*, we have no information as far as I know about whether she's Jewish in a religious sense.

 

* Assuming such things can sometimes lead to mistakes though - my friend Kim appears totally English in her accent, mannerisms, cultural behaviours, etc, and has an English hometown. But she's American, and has the US passport to prove it. Buyer beware.
