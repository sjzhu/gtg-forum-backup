Iron Legacy pretty much has a "soft enrage" as I like to say. In other words, you are on a timer. If you arn't kinda close to a flat out kill by the start of round 3 (if you even lived that long to begin with), you are in serious trouble. Unlike other villains which need to be attacked in a "marathon" mindset (like AkashB or Chairman), IL is a "sprint". There is little time for hero setup. You need to go for the throat immediatley or be crushed.

Yup, Iron Legacy games tend to be short and extremely damage-heavy, regardless of who wins (the fact he gets a Nemesis bonus against six whole heroes doesn't help matters much if you play using any of those). Plus once you flip him it's a case of "yay the environment can hurt him now" countered pretty heavily by "oh crap, he's got soak and is now healing a whole load of hp every turn". I believe a common tactic is to either more or less one-shot him (as in, kill him in one shot, not kill him with a card that is of the type "one shot" ;)), in which case stuff like Wrathful Retribution or the Omnicannon are quite popular choices. Alternatively, hold off on flipping him for as long as possible while you build up and then try to pummel him down with uber damage. And all the while he's beating the crap out of everyone and occasionally forcing discards and/or breaking your stuff.

Out of curiosity, how did you manage to beat him as the Scholar? Did you manage to get enough extra draw from your incapped allies that you could maintain a Flesh-to-Iron or two whilst building up your hand in order to Turn Loose? That's about the only means the Scholar has of dealing huge damage in one go (apart from a nice blast of Grace Under Fire whilst fighting somewhere with a million targets) so I was just wondering how you were keeping up with all the healing and soak that Angry Paul has.

In our one game against Iron Legacy so far, we managed to sneak out a close victory---surprisingly though, once we recovered from the initial burst, we stemmed the tide and made it more of a marathon in our favor.  I think our team was Omnitron-X, Nightmist, Wraith, and Legacy.  We were playing in Freedom Tower, which we though would give us a bit better shot, given that it was our first time against him and we figured we had a decent chance of being slaughtered regardless---overall, I'm sure it helped, but it backfired early on when something like cards 2 and 4 from the environment were both Entry Points on top of new Rooms, so the initial damage surge was just awful.

Wraith ended up being practically a non-factor, she went down so fast---and while I hated to lose her, her incapacitated powers were a pretty good fit for what we needed, I think.  The real standout of the team ended up being Omnitron-X, who was reducing quite a lot of melee damage thanks to his Ablative Coating, and Legacy took some of it for him, too, which let Omnitron keep a big set of components out much longer than I expected.  The rest of our heroes were pretty near death, too, but we managed to get Iron Legacy flipped and then Omnitron-X had a mix of Innervation Rays and his irreducible fire damage (buffed by Legacy), plus Legacy's own bit of healing and maybe some of Wraith's incap ability, to keep Iron Legacy's health low enough while we also slowly built ourselves back up.  Nightmist did as much damage as she could, including some redirected damage from Iron Legacy himself I think, and obviously she and Omnitron-X (plus Wraith) were getting rid of whatever ongoings they could.

Omnitron-X was definitely the MVP of that game---with an Ablative Coating out to keep damage low enough for his components, he brought ongoing destruction, healing, irreducible damage.  He also brought a couple decent hits, but no one on our team was really capable of a one-shot kill, so we just desperately flipped him onto his healing side and just slowly wore him down over a long slog.  It sounds from others' posts like that's not the usual way, which I hadn't realized---I would not be surprised if a lot about our game went unusually, so it will be interesting to see how differently our second game against him plays.
