I did something a while back and kept forgetting to post about it, but I've remembered so that's what I'm doing now.  I sat down to see, if I were going to design the next generation of SotM card, how would I do it.  I tried to start as fresh as possible, keeping only the essentials of what I thought the card needs to tell the player, and being flexible about everything else.  I'm fully aware that many of you think that the current cards are the pinnacle of card design and cannot be improved upon, and also that icons and keywords are like kryptonite to you, so I expect many (most) of you to hate the mockups I've made.  But I figured I'd share anyway in case there was an interesting discussion to be had.

To be clear - I have no illusions that any of these ideas will be adopted by future official card redesigns.  GtG has already made it extremely clear that they love overly wordy cards and hate icons and keywords.  These are my own musings alone and represent nothing else.

For my part, I think the changes will feel strange to anyone who's learned the game in its current form but would feel just as normal as anything else to a new player (my attack formulas are no stranger than Tactics attack grids are), and after just a game or two, these changes would feel normal to a current player as well.

kseanking:

You made a point of specifying the justification of the text boxes based on what the card does. But a lot of cards have enough text that it will just go across the whole card, which somewhat defeats the purpose of having it justified one way or the other.

Effects with lots of text will cause those text boxes to be longer vertically but the width of the text boxes is fixed, so no matter how much text is in a box, it should still be possible to justify them left or right.  The only boxes which stretch across the whole cards are the ones where I'm doing that on purpose, not because of how much text is in the box.

Having said that however, I'm not actually sure that the justification of the boxes is a keeper of an idea.  Does sliding the boxes 1/8th of an inch left or right actually give the user that much useful information?  I'm not sure.  I don't think that Recharge Mode (everything left-justified) is any less readable than Auxiliary Power Source (a mix of justifications), and it may actually be worse, so perhaps that's an optimization which doesn't really add any value.

dpt:

Is there some way to make the attack text look more like an attack, with some sort of fist or sword?

At first, I tried to make the attack formulas look very graphical, like the attack grids in Tactics, but there were a lot of problems with that (the graphics took up extra space, they didn't wrap easily line-to-line like text does, etc.)  So then I looked for ASCII characters I could insert in the text to do exactly what dpt is asking about - making the attack formula stand out more prominently and look more attack-ey.  I also experimented with colors to call them out.

In the end, I didn't find anything that helped more than it hurt.  Most of the solutions I tried (red starburst characters before each attack formula for example, or bracketing the formulas in colored { or [ or |) ended up making it look more busy.  I think you're right that there's surely a better way to represent the attacks while still keeping things simple, but I haven't found that way yet.

Creating a custom fist or sword icon as a font so that it can be inserted inline with the text is a good idea.
