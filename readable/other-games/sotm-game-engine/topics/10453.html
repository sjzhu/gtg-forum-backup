Personally, I found that playing with other people helped me a lot early on (and still does). Playing solo was fun but:

A) There's a lot of bookkeeping to do and it's nice to split it up among multiple people, especially when you're learning and prone to forgetting little situational rules and exceptions.

B) It is very easy to perceive any particular hero as a tool to do one particular thing. Seeing other people use heroes in ways that I hadn't thought of helped me to incorporate other playstyles than the ones that initially made sense to me and, consequentially, now allow me to win more often.

I do a lot of 3-hero solitaire games - generally randomly selected villain, environment, and heroes.

Things are a bit tighter with 3 heroes compared to 4 - and I did initially do solitaire with 4 heroes until I was more familiar with what the different decks did.  Slower game, more work, but also a flexible hero group and probably a better chance of getting a good mix of "attack" and "support"* heroes.  With familiarity you start seeing the secondary strategies for heroes, synergies, and also some prep with knowledge of what the villain or environment might trot out; e.g. keeping a environment/ongoing card around when playing on Insula Primalis in case the T-Rex rolls out at a bad time.

You will also discover some neat edge strategies or opportunities where you turn the environment on the villain, or even turn one of his powerful cards against him.  A good example of this is Visionary terrorizing Omnitron by using Wrest The Mind on an Electro-Pulse Explosive.

And you also also sometimes get completely trounced when playing three heroes.  La Capitan owned me for a little while before I started beating her.

* - With three heroes the difference starts to blur.  You sometimes need everyone to chip in to do damage, or to trigger a seldom used card/power to do something in order to free up another hero to do something else.

Also, all cards which trigger during the same phase will do so in the order they were played. For example, say Insula Primalis has just put out the volcano, which triggers during the Start phase of the environment turn. In the following round, Fanatic plays End of Days, an ongoing card which also triggers during the Start phase for the environment. The environment turn comes around again and because the volcano was out first, it erupts and burns everyone before End of Days goes off and destroys it (and everything else ;)).

About the only time you can mess with which phase stuff happens is with Ab'Zero's card Sub-Zero Atmosphere, which makes all villain cards which would trigger in the End phase instead trigger in the Start phase. Useful against Voss, whose Minions would normally act during the End phase (thus, any Minions who enter play during the Play phase won't be able to attack that turn because they now have to wait until the next round, by which time they'll probably have been destroyed by hero efforts). Less useful against some other villains, but very few cards/effects are always useful in every game.
