You have two choices when dealing with Dawn - try to beat her without letting her flip, or try to survive long enough for her to unflip.  If you get lucky with the citizens that turn up, you may find it easiest to just focus fire on her and try to get rid of those 80 HP, but if Truth turns up you'll be in trouble, and if both Truth and Return with the Dawn turn up, you might as well just get used to the idea that you have to weather the storm.  Damage mitigation is crucial in the latter case, whether it's something that the card plays force you into, or your strategy from the beginning.  You're going to take a lot of damage of a lot of different types, in a lot of different amounts and distributions; this makes her a lot harder to adapt to than Warlord Voss, but on the plus side she isn't invincible while her minions are out (excepting Truth, and to a limited extent Anvil and Spring), nor does she have an alternate win condition or anything equivalent to Forced Deployment.

Any random set of heroes can take on Dawn, but there are some that special utility against her:

Parse lets you control the villain deck with various ongoings, and her Buffer Overload lets you cancel an ill-timed Devastating Aurora or unwanted Citizen. Parse also has a lot of abilities to provide irreducible damage, to get around some of the nastier Citizens.

Visionary also has deck control powers and cards, and her Brain Burn card means you can afford to kill a couple of Citizens, since they'll end up at the bottom of the deck and not in the trash.

Haka can use Savage Mana to prevent Citizens from going to the trash.

Team Leader Tachyon provides everyone with a stupid amount of card draw, which can help against the Citizens that make everyone lose cards.

Wraith has limited villain deck control with Infrared Eyepiece, and Megacomputers are useful in Atlantis to minimize the threat of the kraken and gun turrets. She's also a consistent damage dealer once she gets rolling.

 

For a grudge match, I'd probably pick either Visionary or Parse or Wraith, Haka, and Team Leader Tachyon. Depending on how you want to play, you could add an additional hero or two such as Legacy (he makes everyone better) or whoever else your favorite hero is.

 

There are a couple of heroes I wouldn't play against Dawn: Expattriette is a bit iffy, and Unity will probably lose her golems if the team doesn't work together very carefully. The Sentinels might get battered down before they get a chance to be effective. Still, there's 20+ heroes you can field against her, and 5-6 that are especially effective.

Funnily enough, we played against Dawn in Atlantis last night…and we kicked her arse, it was great :D. Let's see, who did we have…it was TLT, Mr Fixer, and Tempest, in that order. Mr Fixer (played by myself) had plenty of tools and styles within a few rounds. I put out Dual Crowbars first but wasn't able to hit two things until the Kraken came out (since Truth was the only viable target for a while unless I wanted to hit myself or something - no Driving Mantis in play so no benefit to doing so ;)). Then I used Meditation to put out Grease Monkey Fist for more damage. Then I drew a Harmony (via TLT I think) a round or two later so had even more damage. We also had a Phosphorescent Chamber…well, two in fact, through the course of the game, whose damage we took advantage of as much as possible. I also got a nice set-up in which I played Bloody Knuckles for even more damage in the same round that Dawn had put out an ongoing (first it was Channel the Eclipse, then the following round it was Return with the Dawn), then Tempest played Ball Lightning on his turn to dish out some damage and nuke both Dawn's ongoing and my Bloody Knuckles (the latter at my request) so I didn't risk getting heavily pounded by anything. I think I did quite a substantial amount of the damage during the game, but not much else (though nothing else was really necessary).

Tempest got out Cleansing Downpour on his first turn and used it more often than Squall throughout the game, after the plinks we were getting from Dawn, Blood, and Winter for the first few turns. Nice when a Phosphorescent Chamber came out and we were able to benefit from an extra whole piont of healing :D. He put out his Shackles after a couple of rounds which was handy against Dawn, and he also used a bunch of one-shots to dish out some damage and finished the game with a Lightning Slash, I believe.

Dawn herself mostly played Citizens - after the first three we later saw Dare, Assault, and also Tears…she also played Channel the Eclipse and on the following round, Return with the Dawn (see above, in the paragraph on Mr Fixer, as to how we dealt with those). Return did bring back Truth but we were set up enough by then that he didn't really last long. We put a fifth citizen into the trash at one point but Dawn was defeated before she had a chance to flip, but I think we'd made sure that Assault was still out anyway, so she'd've flipped straight back had we got that far.

Had the game gone on any longer, though, things could've got worse for us - I checked her deck after we beat her and the next card was a copy of That Card (with the second maybe five or six cards below it). Phew! had she managed to play it, I was holding onto a Salvage Yard from the first turn so at least

So that was our game last night - we got lucky, I think, in that she didn't put out anything too hazardous and what ongoings she did get out we managed to deal with in the same round. Similarly, the environment didn't do too much - we had, in no particular order becuase I can't remember, a Kraken (only hit citizens and we managed to kill it), a Toxic Seaweed (TLT hit it with Blinding Speed), a couple of Hallway Collapses (the only reason I ended the game on less than 20hp), a Leaking Room (okay so TLT couldn't do much that round but me and Tempest were pretty well set up by then so weren't too bothered), and both Phosphorescent Chambers (we took advantage of the extra damage and weren't too impeded by the discards thanks to TLT).

In general for Dawn I would say that deck control is very, very useful (which basically means that our game last night was super risky as we had absolutely no way to see what was coming until she played it), so heroes like Parse and Visionary (either version) are very good. Those who can deal hefty amounts of damage (if it's irreducible, even better if Truth and/or Anvil are out) to a single target are good for taking out citizens in a single hit or whittling them down so someone else can finish them off. Haka is good if he can get Savage Mana out and start eating everyone, but that's one specific hero and one specific card (of which there are only two copies) so don't count on that tactic working if you decide to fight Dawn with Haka. Jim can be good with his bounties - No Executions probably has Citizen Truth on it for a reason, after all - he can't Return with the Dawn if he's at the bottom of the deck ;).

Environment destruction is also helpful if you're fighting in Atlantis, since it's one of the tougher environments. You're safe enough from Krakens as long as there's at least one citizen in play and all the heroes have a decent amount of hp remaining. But stuff like the Pillars of Hercules (giving Dawn an extra play at the start of every turn) can be your downfall if it lets her cycle through to a copy of That Card all the sooner. Basically, That Card is the bane of anyone fighting her, unless you either don't need any cards in play to be set up (Tachyon can probably get away with it, for example) or are so early into the game that you haven't had a chance to set up yet.

Remember also that it's a card game, it's random, and sometimes you can fight a villain with a certain group of heroes in a certain environment and get totally thrashed one time, then totally kick arse on the very next attempt. One time we had Sweat and Tears as the starting pair of citizens - we'd discarded half our starting hands before any of us even had a turn, meaning we never managed to really get set up before she killed us all. I think she may even have hammered it home even more by playing That Card just as we thought we were getting somewhere.

If you're finding her too difficult in Atlantis, maybe try her somewhere a bit less lethal…the Block isn't too bad as long as the inmates are attacking the guards or the villains, and somewhere like the Final Wasteland or Silver Gulch could be fun if you have the Naturalist in his Gazelle-Form-and-Environmental-Allies setup :D. Any environment which has the potential to let the villain play multiple cards on their turn is a bad thing with Dawn, since the more cards she plays, the more chance you have of That Card coming up.

Keep at it, though, and hopefully you'll find something that works…or just get lucky and get all the right cards while she keeps throwing "nice" citizens at you like Spring and Blood ;).

I figured that ranking Naturalist at the bottom would be controversial. I've only played a few games with him, and I might be missing a lot. But what's so amazing about him? He can achieve DR3 with some work and redirect all attacks to him (whether he wants them or not!) for a round, at the cost of minimizing his ability to do damage. Scholar can achieve DR4 and redirect all attacks to him and still be able to do damage. Legacy can make himself immune to (some) damage and selectively redirect attacks to himself, or make everyone else immune to damage, or shut down the villain deck, and buff everyone's damage or heal everyone while doing a little damage.

My feeling is that top tier heroes contribute to defeating the villain in a lot of different ways, and have the card draw/search ability to get a useful trick in hand. Bunker and the Naturalist have a lot of card draw, but they don't have as many useful tricks, so they're at the bottom tier. They're still decent heroes, and I'd expect that Expatriette, Bunker, and Naturalist can beat any difficulty 2 villain 90% of the time, and most of the difficulty 3 heroes 50+% of the time. It's not like they're bad heroes, they're just not incredible heroes like Omnitron-X or Wraith.

I would say that giving heroes a "rank" implies that some are outright better/worse than others - this isn't true. Depending on luck of the draw, a hero can have a really bad day and do nothing or they might wipe the floor with whatever's thrown at them (more/less so if the villain and/or environment has a good/bad draw themselves, of course). I think it's better to (as some people have already done in the past on this forum) try to sum up each hero with a quick sentence. Some examples...

Wraith - Good all-rounder, can dish out damage and destroy ongoing/environment cards as well as give herself a bit of healing/soak but has a bad time against equipment destruction.

Legacy - Excels at buffing other heroes' damage and can deal a bit himself but relies pretty heavily on ongoings to achieve his full potential and has no means of destroying non-target cards.

Ra - Really good at burning stuff in a variety of ways, but doesn't really do much else. Has a decent mix of ongoings and one-shots and only one equipment card.

So I didn't mention everything that each of those heroes can do (eg no mention of stuff like Trust Fund, Smoke Bombs, Danger Sense, or Excavation), but if I'm trying to briefly sum up a character for someone who's completely new to the game I don't think I'd need to go into specifics that detailed, but rather just give the briefest summary possible. And then if someone has played a couple of games and wanted a hero a bit different to the one(s) they'd just played, I could suggest someone based on what they liked about whomever they'd already used - If someone played Wraith and then decided "Hmm that was fun, but I'd prefer to do more damage via one-shots and be less reliant on equipment" I'd suggest they play Tempest, for example.

The difference between tiers in SOTM is very small compared to most games, but it definitely exists.  Wraith is an absolute top-notch hero; that fact should be obvious to everyone.  Who else can draw a card, tutor one of a half-dozen great Equipment cards straight into play, play another card, and then use one or even two powers?  Nightmist and the Scholar can become virtually invincible if they have time to set up, Ra does unparallelled amounts of damage, Unity's army of bots is unstoppable against anything other than AOE damage (or horrific luck), Tempest is an amazing beatstick with only a few weaknesses, and Legacy boosts every team's win percentages through the roof.  While other characters may not be useless, they may have trouble comparing to these high marks (Tempest, Unity and Ra are debatable as being in that top category; Wraith and Legacy are not).  And there are a few heroes - Bunker is the one most often mentioned - who just don't tend to measure up as well.  They can certainly contribute, and they have a few specialized tools which may occasionally make them the star of a match, but very seldom do they pull more than their fair share of the weight.  Both Tachyon's Lightspeed Barrage and Bunker's Omni-Cannon are good for large amounts of damage, for example, but Bunker has to burn huge numbers of cards and possibly risk losing them to equipment destruction, while Tachyon doesn't have to do anything but play cards normally.  No hero is ever completely useless, but there are definite inequalities in exactly how useful some heroes are and how often, compared to others.  I do not believe I have ever once seen a game where Legacy's presence was not a tremendous, continual benefit; I definitely cannot say the same about Mr. Fixer or Parse.
