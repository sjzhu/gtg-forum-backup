There's a lot of old discussion about damage effects and the order in which they are applied, but I still find the situation murky.  In particular, I'm not sure which of the old discussions have been overruled by later rulings.  (I always get suspicious when a thread starts talking about Imbued Fire/Flesh of the Sun God, which has since been ruled not to work.)

So here are some questions.

(1) Do damage-type-changing effects take place in the order in which cards are played?  For instance, if Ra's "Imbued Fire" is played before Visionary's "Twist the Ether", I would think the damage gets converted to fire, then to whatever Visionary chooses, while if "Twist the Ether" was played first, I would think the damage is always fire.  Is this correct?  I did find some people confused in other threads.

(2) When do damage bonuses get applied?  For instance, suppose Absolute Zero (AZ) and Ra are out. AZ has played Focused Apertures before Ra played Imbued Fire, and then AZ deals some cold damage.  Perhaps the cold damage gets the +1 from focused apertures, then is converted to fire by Imbued fire, then gets the +1 from Imbued Fire; or perhaps the damage type is computed first (and is fire), then there's only the one +1 from Imbued Fire.

(3) When AZ would deal cold damage to himself that is converted into healing by Null-Point Calibration Unit, does this count as cold damage dealt by AZ for the purposes of (eg) Thermal Shockwave?

(4) Can damage reduction get applied multiple times?  Eg, say Haka has Ta Moko out, and Legacy has Lead from the Front and Fortitude out.  Then a villain deals damage to Haka.  Can this get reduced by 1 by Ta Moko, switched to Legacy by Lead from the Front, then reduced by 1 by Fortitude?  Does it depend on the order the cards were played in?

(5) For effects like Fanatic's Undaunted, when do I check the damage amount?

(Text: "Whenever exactly 1 damage would be dealt to Fanatic, prevent that damage. Whenever Fanatic would be dealt 5 or more damage from a single source, reduce that damage by 2.")

(6) In general, I'd like to see an accounting of the precise steps involved in computing damage.  I suspect the answers to (2) and (4) are both "no, that combo doesn't work", but why not?  One algorithm I would know how to apply would be to have virtual damage floating around, modified by all cards played in the order they were played (damage increased/decreased, targets switched, type changed, converted to healing, etc.)  But I gather this is not the algorithm?

The problem with making an "algorithim" is that there are so many little interactions that give rise to so many edge cases it would be extremely hard to get a solid one down. You want a one size fits all theory of how to resolve effects, which kind of betrays the complexity of all the different interactions.

The best way to look at it is in the sense of Triggers and Effects:

For consecutive triggers and overlapping effects, the newest triggered effects take precedance over older ones. 

During simultanious triggered effects (multiple end of turn boss effects for example), you start with the oldest and move to the newest. 

That is all you need.

As for damage taken/delt, there is no distinction within the game. Delt/taken are the same for all intents and purposes, as damage delt is always the result of HP loss.

FWIW, here's my algorithm:

Given the source of an attack, the destination of an attack (target), the type  of an attack, and the amount of an attack:
 
  1. Apply all cards that affect the type of the attack in card played order. (This is only done as an separate, first step, because there are no cards which change the type of an attack based on either the destination of the attack or the amount of the attack, and there are no cards which change the source of an attack).
  2. Apply all other cards in card played order. If a card changes the destinatio n of the attack (target), reset the amount of the attack to the original amount  and return to the beginning of this step, but don't consider that card again. 
  3. If the attack is 0 or less or the target is immune, no damage is dealt.
 
Other relevant rules and effects:
  • An attack that has been reduced to zero or less can NOT be  redirected.
  • Stored increase (Haka of Battle) or reduction (Wraith's Stealth)  are applied in card played order as well.
  • There is no established order of the hero and villain character cards, so I would let that be a player decision, if it ever came up. Another thought would be to say the order is the order of turns, so villain character cards first followed by each hero character card.
 

Basically, card played order is King. Some perhaps non-intuitive results can occur. For instance, if Mr. Fixer is hitting himself due to an Infection and has a plus one from a tool and a plus one from Harmony, whether or not he can redirect due to Praying Mantis is going to depend on the order in which his cards were played. Praying Mantis before one or both of the tool and Harmony? Amount is one or two, so redirection! After both? Amount is three, so not redirected.

1:  Damage Declaration, initial Source, type, amount and target are declared.

2:  Apply changes to damage type.

3:  Apply modifiers to damage based on source and type.

4:  Source of damage steps complete, check for redirection based on source, proceed to target.

5.  If target is immune end damage calculations, no damage is dealt.

6.  Trigger all "would be dealt damage" reduction and redirection effects.

7.  Trigger all "is dealt damage" modifiers.

8,  Reduce targets HP.

Step 5 exists because of an official ruling that you can't redirect damage that the hero is immune to, since they could not be dealt that damage.  Officially inside each step you should go by card order, although on steps 6 and 7 I tend to let the heroes choose the order, because it gets annoying to keep track of which cards were played first, and arguments don't improve the game when someone misremembers.

Thinking in terms of a 1) Cards effect and 2) How that effect triggers is the best way to look at this game in general.

If there are triggered effects that happen simultaniously (like multiple end of turn triggered effects for example) then card order matters. Any other time and card order has no impact. 

Attack modifications are not simultanious, unless they are part of a trigger condition for that effect. For instance, Legacy has Surge of Strength and Inspiring Presence. Both are attack modifications of +1 damage. Now if we read the card, there is no condition that must be met to activate that +1 damage (in other words, nothing like "if X happens, do Y" where X is the trigger condition and Y is the effect). It can be understood then that the trigger for the effect is the act of playing the card. Once you play a card like Surge of Strength, the triggered effect now takes hold and all Legacy's damage is increased by 1. Since there is no explicit end point listed on the card, it is understood that the effect is consistant as long as the card remains in play. 

Back to Imbued Fire and Micro Targeting Computer/Razor Ordinance. Lets break down these 3 cards in terms of card effects and the triggers for those effects.

Imbued Fire. Increase all fire damage from heros by 1. Like SoS, there is no conditional trigger, so the effect triggers instantly upon playing, and because there is nothing limiting the duration of the effect, it is constant as long as the card remains in play. The effect has a limitation however in that the damage delt needs to be fire. The second part changes all hero damage to fire type. This is also an effect with no conditional trigger or expressed end point so it behaves the same way.

Micro Targeting Computer says to increase projectile damage delt by Wraith by 2. Like SoS there is no conditional trigger for the effect, so it happens instantly upon playing it. MTC also has a limitation similar to the first part of Imbued Fire in that that damage delt must be projectile.

Razor Ordinace has a Power. During a Heros power phase, that hero my activate one Power, acting as the trigger condition for that Power's effect. Razor Ordinace lets you deal 3 projectile damage to one target.

Let's say that all 3 of these cards are in play during a particular game. It's Wraiths turn and shes looking to do some damage. So Wraith needs to trigger an effect that deals damage. This is where the first big distinction comes in. Razor Ordinance must be triggered after the effects of IF and MTC are in play.

Lets read the Power. Wraith deals one target 3 projectile damage. If you read the power like this, you have already made a mistake. Imbued Fire is already changing all hero damage to fire. So as you are reading her powers and deciding what to use, you would not even read the word "projectile" as you are replacing it with "Fire" the instant Imbued Fire hits the field. That is the second big distinction. You can't trigger the effect of Razor Ordinance before Imbued Fire has already changed the damage type.

Now Wraith chooses to use that power, so she triggers that power's effect. Now that she is dealing fire damage, she is within the other effect of Imbued Fire and increases that fire damage by 1. 

It makes no difference whether or not MTC or IF were in play first. Why? For starters, the two effects we are specifically looking at here (the +2 projectile damage and the change all damage to fire) are not simultaniously triggered, so there is the first big tip off. The other big reason here is the fact that if a hero, at any time, fails to meet the criteria for an effect, they loose that effect. Even if you thought the +2 projectile damage is applied before Imbued Fire changes the type, as soon as Wraith is no longer dealing projectile damge, MTC stops buffing as well since you no longer meet the criteria to benefit from it (that criteria being doing projectile damage).

I think it has to do with entering play versus being played. Fowl say "When this card is played from the villan deck". Hairtigger reflexes says "Whenever a non-hero target  enters play  ". Distortions say "when this card enters play", and negative eneergy field says "Whenever a card is played".

Here's the thing though. Negative Energy Field will hit the card just played. If it can hit the card, it has hp. If it has hp, the target has entered play. This means that the timing of both effects are equal, and not that one should be first.

If they are the same, why are they worded differently. First HTR specifies a target. You could say when a target would be played, but then that would get around some situations. What if a card entered play but wasn't played, e.g. Timeshift, it suddenly gained hp (Miss Informations, etc.). With HTR wording, they would still take a hit. If it only said played, they wouldn't.  Also, why doesn't the Fowl card simply say, "when this card enters play…"? Because if it did, Darken the Skies would put the entire deck into play.

So I think the timing is the same, just that the wording fits the specific card. I also have to agree that I think the issue is with HTR stopping Matty cold, and that is why there was the ruling.
