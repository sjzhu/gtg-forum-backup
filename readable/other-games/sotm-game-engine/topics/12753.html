Notice the order of events in the End of Days loop.

End of days destroys minions.

End of days destroys forced deployment.

All minions in the trash enter play.

All minions are destroyed by end of days.

No minions are destroyed during the act of Forced Deployment putting them into play.

 

Basically when Forced Deployment is destroyed you pull every minion from the trash, then summon that stack of minions, if those minions are destroyed before forced deployment is finished they do not re-enter the que.

What I'm trying to say is that I understand why Apostate's two targets continually bring each other back.  The issue is that the loop requires Haka to deal damage to the same card multiple times.  So you're treating the relic and spirit as new targets every time they return.  Meanwhile, Forced Deployment 'remembers' each minion, and only tries to return each one once.  Finally, End of Days doesn't care about how many times a card has been targeted, and just keeps trying until every valid target is gone.  The difference between the Haka/Apostate loop and Forced Deployment & End of Days loops is that Haka is dealing with targets, while Forced Deployment and End of Days deal with cards.  So I can understand treating things as new targets, but the same card.  Which is all well and good.  But End of Days and Forced Deployment have almost identical wording ("Take all of the ___ cards from the Villain Trash and..." vs "Destroy all cards [except...] in play") and are handled in almost exactly the opposite manner.  (I mean, besides that the fact that they do exactly the opposite things).

 

I can deal with a ruling that says "End of Days works this way, and Forced Deployment works that way", but I don't like it.  The less contextual the grammar of SotM rules is, the simpler the game is.

Read forced deployment:

Take all minion cards from the villain trash and put them into play.

You take them out of the trash, then put them into play.  So once the cards are being put into play they are already out of the trash.  You don't recheck the trash, and destroyed minions would, by rule, enter the trash and not the que.

Darken the skies ruling makes it work the same as forced deployment.

The reason Apostate works is the effect occurs, then a seperate effect causes the first card to return, and when it is destroyed the first effect happens again.  It is totally different.
