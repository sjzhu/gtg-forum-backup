If you indeed mean Close Quarters Combat the answer is whichever one came into play most recently.

Static effects (Effects that do not have activation text, like being on a one shot or text starting with "whenever") have no timing, they apply to the field as soon as the card they are on hits the field.

So there is no simultaneous activation, because the effects are not activated, they come into play and sort of take over the field.

I'm not so good at explaining it, but official ruling is that static effects do not have timing, so the question isn't actually a timing question.

Order of operations comes into play here. Order of operations states that if two effects (apart from start- or end-of-turn effects) would come into play at the same time, the players get to decide which activates first. Imbued Fire and Close Quarters Combat, both being effects that change an attack's type, most definitely come into play at the same time, and thus it is the players' choice which one activates first. I'm not sure exactly what the rule on static effects was, but since OoO was brought in to simplify things, and also so there wasn't so much keeping track of which cards in different play areas came into play first, I don't see why it wouldn't apply here.

To clarify Pydro's last point (for those who don't know), Mega Computer is a text-only prototype version of the SotM app, and the team working on it have talked to the GtG team a lot to make sure the way their game engine works is consistent with the game rules. In Mega Computer, you're able to pick whether CQC or IF activate first, which adds credence to this point of view.

Yeah, the damage modifiers are added in this sequence:

First, determine damage type. Then, add and subtract to get the total damage. If the damage is redirected, recalculate to include any increased/decreased damage specific to the target, and exclude any of the same that applied only to the first target. So, if Ra were to hit Mr. Fixer (with pipe wrench and Driving Mantis) for 3, it would be reduced to 2. Then, he could redirect it to someone else (let's say Set) without Damage Reduction, bringing it back up to 3, and then increase it again because of the nemesis bonus.

  1. "Static Effects" were a forum constructed term in the pre OoO days to try and explain how interactions worked and resolved when timing clauses were absent. It's not a official term or rule and never has been. Is it in the glossary? Nope.

All of those old "static effects" are now simultaneous with eachother. They are assumed to trigger at the same time. That alone puts it under the heading of the OoO. Without distinct timing clauses, you can't rightfully argue they trigger consecutivy.

The OoO was not just about Simultaneous effects either. The heart of the ruling was to do away with "what card came out first" tracking, except for the specifically mentioned Start/End of turn tracking. And It specifically said at the end of the post that any other ambiguous situations are at the discretion of players choice. So even if "static effects" are not simultaneous, they fall under "ambiguous" due to the lack of timing clause. Either way, its OoO material.

  1. Informant actually does not break the OoO ruling because it actually doesn't fall under "simultaneous" effects at all. It is consecutive. The playing of a card happens before the text of that card activates. That was the informant ruling. So Informant plays the extra card (as it is the newest trigger), then you resolve the card the hero played.
