Welcome to the forums! I'm glad you're enjoying the game. Haka can fill different roles depending on what the team needs him to be.

Primarily, he tends to be a damage dealer - Taiaha lets him deal consistent damage round after round. If the villain or environment have lots of targets, Haka can gobble them up with Savage Mana and spit them out later for a huge amount of damage. His damage output can be augmented by using Haka of Battle to increase the next damage he deals, which is nice for bringing down the villain (particularly villains with damage reduction, where lots of small attacks aren't very effective).

Haka can also tank pretty well. He's got a ton of hit points. Ta Moko reduces incoming damage, Haka of Shielding helps to block larger hits that you know are coming, and Haka of Restoration helps to keep you healthy. Enduring Intercession and Ground Pound help to protect your teammates.

You'll notice that some of his abilities require you to discard cards in order to use them (Haka cards and Ground Pound), so you have things like Dominion, Vitality Surge, and Mere to get more cards into your hand. Playing the Haka cards helps with this too; often, it's useful to play one just for the extra card draw and then discard one card that you don't need for a small bonus.

This is a simplified view of Haka, but I hope it helps a little. There are numerous strategy guides for the heroes here that might be useful for you.

Also, for extra funsies:

#6:  Tachyon with Synaptic Interruption is attacked for 3 damage which is being affected by a Haka of Battle for +2 and a Haka of Shielding for -2.

 

This is basically the same scenario as #2 above, but it's different because in #2, removing either the Haka of Battle or the Haka of Shielding will shift the damage away from redirection (Removing the Shielding alone puts the damage at 4, above Mantis, and removing the Battle puts the damage at 0 which is no damage).  In #6, removing the Battle puts the damage at 1, which is outside the range of Synaptic Interruption, but removing the Shielding puts the damage at 5, which is still redirected by Synaptic.

 

Anyways, I see three ways of handling multiple modifiers in situations like this (all examples refer to #6):

1.  Check for changes in situation after they are all applied.  In this case, damage was 3, applying Shielding and Battle placed it back at 3, so no change occured and they aren't consumed.  I don't this method because pretty much everything else in SotM checks game state after every individual action.  It's possibly okay here because we're not necessarily making changes, just determining what the outcome would be if things were applied differently, but I personally don't like it.

2.  Apply the one-use increases/decreases one at a time, and check if they're necessary.  In this case, damage is 3 and redirected.  HoB increases damage to 5, which is still redirected so HoB isn't necessary.  Now we check HoS, which decreases damage to 1, which isn't redirected, and is different.  Therefore, HoS is necessary, the damage is now 1, and we have to check HoB again.  HoB increases the damage to 3, which brings back the redirection, which means that HoB is necessary as well.  So both HoB and HoS are necessary and will both be consumed.

3.  Remove the one-use increases/decreases one at a time, and check if they're necessary.  In this case, damage is 3 and redirected.  Removing HoB reduces damage to 1, which removes the redirection, so HoB is necessary, and we put it back.  Removing HoS increases the damage to 5, which is still redirected.  So HoS isn't necessary, and can be removed.  Now we look at HoB again, and removing it brings the damage back down to 3, which is still redirected.  Thus HoS isn't necessary, and neither HoB or HoS will be consumed.

 

So as far as I can tell, methods 2 and 3 are equally valid, but produce opposite results (this is why I switched to scenario #6 rather than #2, because they produce the same results for #2).  So now what do we do?
