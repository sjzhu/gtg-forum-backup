So, the other day, my group of friends and I were playing against Spite.  We were playing The Naturalist, KNYFE, Nightmist, Setback, and Absolute Zero.  Our Environment was The Block... this was intended to be a warmup fight.

Well, Spite ended up flipping in two turns.  Killing all his victims before we had a chance to save them during the same turn they were brought out.  On the second turn, a prison break happened, bringing out a lot of inmates, and on the same turn, Spite played Lab Raid, destroying all environment cards and replacing them with drugs.  One of those drugs reduced the first hit against him by H-2, or 3.  Meanwhile, he was doing a total of 6 damage per turn (3 hits of 2) to everyone.  Whenever someone used a power, he'd deal 3 toxic damage and make you discard the top 5 cards of your deck.  His last drug would return H-3 Ongoing or equipment cards each turn, which prevented anyone from rolling anything out.

No one really had time to get moving before everyone went down... except Nightmist.

Nightmist, whose trash was full because of that drug that made her discard cards, played Mist Fueled Recovery.  This, combined with the HP gain from Starshield Necklace and Master of Magic (which were out), immediately brought her up to full.  Of course, they returned to her hand from the villain turn.

The incapacitated effects combined with her regular turn allowed Nightmist to play 3 cards per turn.  One before her turn, one during her turn, one after her turn.

So, if she played Master of Magic first, and then made the other two artifacts whose mana values added up to 6 or more, she'd have a net gain of health per turn (Spite was dealing 6 damage to her).  She just wouldn't use powers so she wouldn't take an additional 3 damage (or 5 if she used investigate).

The environment had no surprises that could stop this.  One of Spite's damaging drugs destroyed all environment cards and did 1 damage (+1, total of 2) for each card destroyed in this way.

So, Nightmist could gain more than he could dish out on average.  She kept her health >15, Kept a mist fueled recovery just in case, and slowly whittled him down to 0 from 60 health.  It was pretty glorious.  Turns out, if the last person left alive is Nightmist, she has quite a few options.

Nightmist can get pretty tanky, yeah :D. Master of Magic is a really good card for getting the healing if you can play a bunch of spells, and of course the Starshield Necklace helps too. The Amulet of the Elder Gods can be super-useful too, especially if a lot of damage is coming your way (provided you have the cards to activate it if you're getting hit several times a round). It's a good job the other guys' Incap abilities were letting you play stuff out of turn, otherwise I suppose you wouldn't've got anywhere since as the only hero left active that means you'd've been forced to return all your stuff to your hand at the start of Spite's go (we played for a while, in the early days, misunderstanding that one and thinking it meant everyone had to return H-2 cards to hand when in fact it's a total number of cards across all heroes…so just one in our case, rather than one each). Spite can be a bit of a drag and the order in which his drugs come out (the "take damage and discard the top five of your deck" drug is probably the most annoying…the discard isn't so bad but the damage isn't much fun) can ahve a significant effect on the difficulty. But as we play with H as three, a double Stun Bolt from Wraith every round tends to keep him pretty well sorted once she's set up in order to do so :D.

We've even managed to defeat Spite a few times without him flipping - that lifetap effect he has on his front side is annoying but not insurmountable if you have enough damage to throw out…plus if you have plenty of soak (and/or redirection to someone with soak) he doesn't really get to heal very much because very little gets through.

Ahh, fun times :).

Scholar actually loses the deck burn off with Nightmist, he does nothing vs. irreducible damage and has few options to deal with big damage Nightmist has her redirection, better consistent healing with two to three cards in play and some big shot heals that Scholar can only replicate vs. certain decks.

Scholar crushes Nightmist in games with lots of small instances of non-irreducible damage (flesh to iron)  or in games with outside healing sources (since he can increase the amount)

Scholar's biggest strength in this showdown is his early setup, since he has many options if he doesn't get the right cards early, where as Nightmist with the wrong cards early is looking at some lowest HP tanking (which vs. some villains is actually more important than Highest HP tanking) or a nice juicy game of incap powers.

 

I rarely pay either of them anymore, because I dislike how Scholar turns a lot of matchups into a joke (he crushes Apostate and I've actually beat Matriarch solo with him) and Nightmist flat out rocks when she's the last hero standing and has incap powers to help her.  A lot of teams actually get better by having everyone but Nightmist die, because she scales so ridiculously with incaps boosting her.

The most ridiculous was one hero letting her play cards, one letting her use a power and Tempest making her immune to infernal.  She took down 4 members of vengeance after what was almost a 2 round wipe. (no Ermine ended up being huge)

 

They sit in the pile with Legacy and TLT (Naturalist is heading that direction too) for revenge games and special challenge games.
