This is me getting excessively theoretical for a minute.  I'm pretty sure that if a card effect says something "can't" happen, such as Projected Paralysis saying "you can't play cards", that effect is not automatically trumped the first time a card effect says you can do so.  For instance, PP has hit Expatriette, but before her turn, the Argent Adept or an Incapacitated hero grants her the ability to play a card.  The effect granting a card play is newer than the effect forbidding it, but I believe that doesn't mean it trumps the older effect.  I am however a little uncertain of exactly why.

To make it a bit more esoteric, let's say that what happens is that Omnitron Timeshifts Expatriette's deck, and puts Speed Loading into play.  Speed Loading is now definitely a newer card than Projected Paralysis, and by a strict reading of the rules, that might be taken to mean that it now trumps the older effect and allows Expat to play an Ammo card.  But even so, she would presumably still lose her normal card play for the turn.  Again, it's not so much that I believe this works, but that I would like a breakdown of exactly why it doesn't.

I don't think the issue is "can" vs. "can't" - the issue is something is telling you that you may do an action that another thing has said you can't do. Speed Loading's "may play an ammo card" is giving you the option to do so that you don't normally have, but because you can't play cards at this point that option is not valid.

I think this hasn't been asked before because the understood intention of the "can't" effects is they prevent us from being able to do the thing, no matter what other effects are in place - as Ronway stated - so it never came up as something to put in the Spiff's FAQ.

Since the play a card phase is stated "The Hero's player may choose to play one card from their hand" any time-order rule applied to a "can't play cards" effect would always result in your play phase being allowed, since the effect is there as soon as the game starts.

 

The others are right, a can't overrides a can, which is why Bunker's Ammo Drop is worded the way it is.  If a card wanted to override a can't effect, it would have to say so.
