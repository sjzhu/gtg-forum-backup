One thing I will say, when Christopher said the game had no concept of a stack, he went on to explain a queue. Then when he explained how the game actually works, he explained a stack system. Though I could easily see someone using the word ‘stack’ to assume any kind of ordered listing.
Basically, if a game is queue based, when resolving a card, nothing else will happen until the card is finished. If anything were to trigger, the reaction is placed into a queue and does not have any of its actions resolved. Once the initial card is finished, the first action put into the queue is resolved. If there are any triggers activated for that card, they are added to the end of the queue of whatever else had been added. It’s a case of whichever reaction has been waiting the longest (no matter what triggered it) is the first action to take effect. (Christopher said this game is not this).
In a stack based game, when a card is resolving, if something triggers, the current card goes to top of the stack, and the reaction to the trigger resolves. If that reaction triggers other reactions, the current reaction goes to the top of the stack at its current instruction and the new reaction interrupts. When a reaction finally finishes, the top of the stack resumes at the instruction it left off. This is basically Christopher explained the game (without using the term ‘stack’ of course).
I thought I would provide this information just in case anyone was confused between the idea of a queue based game and a stack based game.
