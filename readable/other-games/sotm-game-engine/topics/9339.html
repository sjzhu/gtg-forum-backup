It's a start, but it still doesn't really do it for me, because (1) Ambuscade's Traps aren't anything that would really be scary even if they just hit you immediately, and (2) while more HP make the game longer, with most villains (including Ambuscade) they manage to increase difficulty (marginally) without increasing the challenge.  To explain what I mean a little:

Ambuscade has no ability to destroy your cards in play, very little ability to ramp up his threat over time or make a comeback, and almost never presents a meaningful question of target priority - you'll take out the force field, then the big weapon, then the robot, then the little weapon, then the shield in that order, almost every time you have to pick.  So, when a game against him goes longer, the last point means that you don't need to keep thinking of new tactics, and the middle one means that he's not upping the stakes.  Both of these things combine to mean that a longer game with Ambuscade will ask no new questions, and thus does not deepen the puzzle (thus, the challenge). Of course, the first one makes even that irrelevant, since all your combos stay put, so you can just shut down his attacks and do massive damage effortlessly, which is why I say it doesn't actually increase the difficulty by 25% like you'd expect, either.  With 20 HP or 100, a villain with no comeback condition and no stuff destruction only gets very slightly more difficult, since almost all heroes in SotM get radically more powerful as they get their setups into place.  The only exception would be someone like Iron Legacy, who is emphatically not hard because of his HP total, but because he's designed to end the game before you ever see your setup happen (and if the heroes do get set up anyway, he crumples).  That's why most of the scariest villains are that way because of crazy amounts of comeback power, so that you're still in danger up until the very last hit: Cosmitron and the Matriarch might draw into that 15-card string of mooks, while Voss and The Chairman have rightly-feared abilities to undo most of your hard work by dropping massive setups on top of you.  All of these tricks increase the difficulty significantly, while adding some to the challenge, as well.

My favorite villain designs, which I consider to also increase the challenge notably as time goes on, are ones such as The Ennead, Citizen Dawn, and La Capitan.  All of these have some degree of stuff destruction, to keep you from resting easy, and will have multiple targets which you cannot take out in one turn out at a time.  Thus, you must both work the puzzle of target priority and that of resource management at the same time, and each turn it goes on, the puzzle has a chance to get nastier, even as you fight for a chance to have better methods to use against it.  If you gave any of them a bunch of extra HP (and, indeed, Dawn and La Capitan's flips both do simulate more health), you'd be in real trouble, since unlike Ambuscade, their games do not simply degrade in challenge every turn.

Of course, my preferred Ambuscade fix is just to set H=3, run him against 2 heroes in a resource-taxing environment, and watch the sparks fly... or to run him alongside a more credible villain with six heroes.  So, my tastes are certainly not for everyone, either!

My current Ambuscade house rules fest has been:

Play a card when you shuffle a trap into the deck.

Traps gain destruction:

The one that hits one hero destroys all their cards in play.

The one that hits all heroes destroys one card from each hero.

 

It makes the traps something you seriously fear.

 

But really, if you use play a card when you shuffle a trap and add "at the end of the villain turn play the top card of the villain deck" to both sides you get pretty dang good results.
