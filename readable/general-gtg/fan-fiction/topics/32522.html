#1

Greetings, Sentinels!

Christopher Badell and Adam Rebottaro don’t like kid sidekicks, or “families” of characters based around a central hero, so neither of those concepts are super prominent in Sentinel Comics. Well, I do, so I wrote this story.

The idea is that this story, “The Challenge of the Freedom Kids!”, is from an old comic issue originally published during the Silver Age of Comic Books in the 1960s.

I’d had the nugget of this story for a long while, and I had the option to either write out the story in full, or to basically write an overview of it, sort of like a review of the issue, or the way that C&A themselves tell stories on The Letters Page. Ultimately, I decided on the latter, because I felt like I’d rather get this story out there rather than just sit on it forever and never get it finished. “The perfect is the enemy of the done,” and all that.

I also decided that I wanted to serialise it over a few days. Comics back in the Silver Age would frequently have several chapters to them. This one has five chapters; I intend to post them over the next several days till it’s concluded.

I am, of course, not a professional comic writer. Nonetheless, any feedback, comments, questions, or any other discussion is much appreciated.

Enjoy!

Title Page

Young Pauline Parsons is the latest in the long and proud Parsons family line! But unlike all her forefathers, she is the first powered Parsons child to be… a girl! Nonetheless, she’s exhibited all the mighty powers of the Legacy tradition, though she is yet to have manifested her own, new, unique power.

As the world over knows, Pauline’s father, Legacy, is the leader of America’s finest fighting force, the Freedom Five! But Pauline herself, although possessed of many ordinary friends, has no superpowered allies of her own age to call comrades. Indeed, as far as she knows, she is the only youth in the nation to know what it’s like to wield such great powers! But all these assumptions are turned on their head when young Pauline meets four stunning new characters and must truly put her wits and powers to the test when she faces…

Chapter 1

The issue opens with a quartet of villains holding up Megalopolis National Bank: Glamour, Thermos, Dr. Rubber, and the Jellyfish. Their fantastic powers let them easily overcome the security, break into the vault, and help themselves to its riches.

Meanwhile, Pauline Parsons is walking through the city of Megalopolis when her danger sense draws her attention to the bank. She flies in and tries to apprehend the villains, but her fists bounce off Dr. Rubber’s rubbery body, Glamour uses her illusions to disorient her, the Jellyfish shocks her, and Thermos freezes her.

It looks like the villains are about to get away with their loot, when four teenage newcomers arrive on the scene and apprehend the villains. Afterwards, they introduce themselves to Pauline. They consist of…

  • Shady, an acrobatic boy in a purple and red caped costume, equipped with an arsenal of gadgets.
  • Fort, a girl in a compact tan and green armored power suit with durable plating and great strength.
  • Swifty, a fleet-footed boy in a white and red costume, who can zip about the bank in the blink of an eye.
  • And Snowgirl, a slight girl in a blue and white costume wielding a cold ray that can fire a beam of absolute zero energy.

They call themselves the Freedom Kids; Pauline thinks they’re terrific, thanks them for their help, and asks to join their team. The Freedom kids waffle; they say that in order to join, she must demonstrate that she can perform super-feats as well as they can—her defeat by the villains certainly didn’t make her look good.

But as the five youthful heroes are talking, Thermos burns himself and his compatriots out of their bindings, and the villains make to escape. Before the heroes can act, Glamour cloaks the bank in darkness, and Dr. Rubber turns them all rubber so they can bounce away.

The heroes lament the villains’ escape, but Pauline has an idea—she and they will both try to capture the four scoundrels, and in so doing she’ll prove that her powers can compare to theirs. The Freedom Kids accept, and they all set out.

And now, we have chapter 2, wherein Pauline, Shady, and Fort take after The Jellyfish and Dr. Rubber!

Chapter 2

The Jellyfish is at Megalopolis Harbor. He elongates his arms and sneakily climbs aboard a boat as it’s coming into the harbor—no one will expect a robbery before the boat’s even docked, he thinks. He uses his bizarre marine physiology to paralyse the guards watching a shipment of gold on the boat, and grabs as much of it as he can carry.

But before he gets away with it, Shady the Boy Vigilante arrives on the scene. He’d trailed the villain there without his knowing. He tries to hit the Jellyfish with a stun bolt, but the slippery villain’s altered physiology makes him immune to its shocking effects. The Jellyfish then tries to throw his elongated arms at the boy hero, but Shady acrobatically dodges out of the way and disappears into the shadows of the ship’s hull. But then the Jellyfish lights up with bioluminescence, and shocks the suddenly exposed Shady, paralysing him.

The Jellyfish grabs his loot with one arm, and stretches out the other and pulls himself to the docks. But as he does so, Pauline Parsons flies onto the scene. He tries to shock her, but her dauntless durability negates this single attack, and she grabs his extended arms, twirls him around with them, and ties him up with them.

Shady, having recovered, joins her on the docks, and congratulates her on capturing the Jellyfish. Pauline then remarks that he reminds her of the Wraith, and asks him how he came to become the boy hero he now is, which he willingly explains.

Marty Morris was a star gymnast in Rook City. His father worked as a night watchman at the Overbrook Museum of History, until one night when the villainous thief Ermine broke in. His father spotted her and chased after her, so she led him into a room, lit a fire within, and locked him inside. Although the shadowy vigilante the Wraith soon arrived and apprehended Ermine, Marty’s father perished. The Wraith, taking pity on the young boy, took him in and trained him in her ways of crime-fighting and investigation, so that he could fight back against the kind of criminals who took his father.

Dr. Rubber bounces up to the top floor of Eaken-Rubendall Laboratories. There, he finds a sample of a new miracle plastic that’s just been invented. He’s sure he can find a buyer that will pay handsomely for this cutting-edge invention.

But he accidentally sets off the lab’s alarm, which alerts the police as well as Fort, whose suit is patched into the police radio frequency. Luckily, she’s nearby, and she rocket-jumps through an open window into the lab. She tries to spray him with adhesive foam, but it doesn’t stick to his rubbery form. So then she simply tries to wallop him, but he bounces harmlessly off her mechanized fist, rebounds off a wall, and careens back into her, knocking her through a bay of scientific equipment.

Dr. Rubber then grabs the plastic sample and bounces out of the lab, only to find Pauline just outside. He tries to bounce away, but she flies after him and catches him, flies him over to a construction site, and hurls him into wet cement, trapping him there, unable to bounce away.

Fort then joins her there, and Pauline asks her how she came to have a power suit that so resembles the hero Bunker’s, which Fort gladly tells.

Jo Maxwell was a young mechanical prodigy living in Megalopolis, who’d always been able to build anything she could imagine. One day, when she and her family were riding on the city’s famous monorail, an earthquake struck, and the rail in front of their car gave way. The monorail was about to crash, when the heroic Bunker appeared and stopped it, saving Jo and her family. The young girl was inspired by his heroic deeds, so she built herself her own mechanical suit in homage to his, and used it to follow his example of heroism.

Chapter 3

Glamour, disguised by an illusion as an unassuming civilian, enters the crowded Megalopolis Museum of Art. She comes to a new exhibit: a priceless, reflective silver statuette. She creates an illusion of some hoodlums causing trouble at the other end of the hall, prompting the guards to rush over there and put a stop to it, while she discretely grabs the statuette.

Swifty, the Quickest Boy on Earth, runs onto the scene. He’d been running through the city looking for the villains, and saw Glamour disguise herself outside the museum. He dashes towards Glamour, but she throws up a burst of light and switches up her disguise to throw him off her trail. Then, Pauline flies into the museum, also after her, but before the two young heroes spot one another, Glamour casts an illusion on both of them, making each of them look like her. The two heroes spot each and, falling for the nefarious illusionist’s trick, attack one another. Pauline can easily withstand Swifty’s blows, and he can dodge most of hers, but eventually she lands one on him, knocking him out and dispelling the illusion on him.

Realising what had just happened, Pauline flies outside, looking for a fleeing Glamour. She flies by every civilian she can see, until her danger sense alerts her to one of them. She grabs Glamour, quickly finds her illusion-casting devices on her, and crushes them.

Swifty, having recovered, then joins Pauline and congratulates her on apprehending the thief. Pauline asks him how he came to have such stupendous speed just like the hero Tachyon, and he gladly explains.

Tommy Tyler was a brainy whiz kid whose scientific expertise won him a summer internship at the renowned Eaken-Rubendall Labs. But one day, an accident at the local power plant sent an electrical surge through the power grid that caused the same Particle Yield Enhancing Wavelength that had given the hero Tachyon her powers to malfunction, bathing young Tommy in tachyonic radiation. He soon discovered that against all odds he too had developed identical powers as Tachyon, and the older hero agreed to train him in the use of them.

Thermos arrives at a jewellery store. He freezes the guards on duty there and burns his way through the protective cases that contain a vast collection of valuable rubies and sapphires.

Snowgirl then arrives on the scene, and explains that she suspected he’d try to pilfer these jewels, given that it’s the store’s “Fire & Ice” collection. Snowgirl tries to use her cold ray to freeze him, but he blocks it with his fire, and then proceeds to encase her cold ray in a block of ice, rendering it inoperable.

Thermos grabs the jewels and makes a break for it, but Pauline shows up to stop him. He blasts her with fire, but her bulletproof skin leaves her unscathed. Then he freezes her in ice, but her superhuman strength allows her to burst free from his frigid restraints. Pauline then charges at him and floors Thermos with a single mighty blow.

Snowgirl then joins Pauline, who shatters the ice encasing her cold ray and asks the frosty heroine how she came to acquire the astounding device. Snowgirl gladly tells her.

June Frost was the niece of Ryan Frost, the hero known as Absolute Zero. One day, when he was giving her a tour of the Freedom Five HQ, it was attacked by the villainous duo Citizens Hammer and Anvil. The rest of the FF were out, so it was up to Absolute Zero to deal with them. Citizen Hammer hosed him with flames; the chilly hero tried to fight back with his icy blasts, but Citizen Anvil’s shield protected the two zealots. It was looking dire for Ryan Frost, so June grabbed Henry Goodman’s old cold ray that was on exhibit in the FF HQ Museum and froze the two citizens from behind, saving her uncle. Ryan was impressed with her quick-thinking bravery, so when she pleaded with him to let her keep the ray and fight crime alongside him, he agreed.

Chapter 4

With all four of the dastardly villains apprehended and turned over to the law, the five youthful heroes regroup outside the Megalopolis police station. Pauline’s shocked and confused, but before she can react, Fort charges forwards and lands a mighty wallop on her, knocking her backwards. Then Swifty runs around her, creating a vortex of air that whirls her about, dizzying and disorienting her. Finally, Snowgirl encases her in a mound of ice.

Shocked, Pauline asks them why they’d do this. Her answer comes when a figure walks into the room—it’s none other than the bane of the Legacy line, Baron Blade himself! The Baron gloatingly explains that the Freedom Kids were working for him all along! The kids corroborate this, saying that they were sick of being only the junior partners of the Freedom Five, and that Baron Blade had promised them that, if they helped him capture Pauline, he’d help them get rid of the older heroes, letting them finally step out of their shadows and become proper heroes. And Blade says that now that he has her, he’ll use her as bait for her father and set up so devious a trap that he’ll have his sweet revenge at last.

Pauline can scarcely believe this betrayal, but she says that she’ll nonetheless put a stop to this plot. She begins to crack the ice restraining her, but before she can free herself Blade whips out a vial of regression serum, and she feels herself powers being weakened, leaving her helpless!

Chapter 5

Suddenly, to the Baron’s surprise, Shady hurls a small projectile at the flask of regression serum. Blade is bewildered at this turn of events, but grins smugly as his personal force field shimmers into view and deflects the thrown object. Swifty explains that the force field is made of excited electrons, so Snowgirl freezes it, slowing them down, and then Fort punches the now-brittle barrier, shattering it. Swifty then tries to dash forward and grab the serum, but Baron Blade is ready for him, and zaps him with an electrical charge. But with the Baron distracted by that offensive, Shady is able to swing in from behind and grab the serum, then quickly mix it with a chemical that neutralizes its properties.

Pauline’s strength returns to her. She triumphantly bursts from the ice. Blade tries to zap her with electricity, but her superhuman durability negates that single attack, and she flies over, tears a sheet of metal from the wall, and wraps him up in it.

Baron Blade is confounded; he demands to know why the Freedom Kids turned on him. They explain that they never actually bought into his plan; they only agreed to work with him to lure him here where they could trap him. And Pauline adds that they let her in on the plan while they were all traveling to the clubhouse; that’s why she didn’t use her danger sense to anticipate their supposed betrayal.

Fort says that there’s just one thing she doesn’t understand—why did Baron Blade so easily believe that they resented their mentor heroes so vehemently, while still desiring to be heroes? Surely he knows that heroes aren’t that hateful. Pauline says that Blade is so consumed and blinded by his own hatred, that he doesn’t hesitate to believe that such hatred burns just as hot in the hearts of others. She then adds that she’s glad that it turned out that the Freedom Kids were on the up-and-up, so that now, together, they can be the greatest teenage heroes the world has ever seen!

Then, Pauline Parsons wakes up. She sits up in her bed. What an odd dream that just was, she thinks. It’s too bad that, in reality, there’s no such thing as the Freedom Kids. Being part of their team would have been great fun.

The End!

At any rate, as mentioned, I thought I’d write a bit of a wrap-up going through my thoughts on this story.

“The Challenge of the Freedom Kids!” Retrospective

(I swear, every time I typed that, I hade to stop myself from writing “Super Friends” instead of “Freedom Kids.”)

The general idea for this story came, of course, from my desire for Sentinel to have more legacy-type sidekicks, but the specific plot of the issue was somewhat inspired by Adventure Comics #247, the first appearance of the Legion of Super-Heroes, and the cover was specifically inspired by that issue’s cover. And the general structure of the issue, of all the heroes starting together, then splitting up and tackling different villains committing different crimes in different places, and then regrouping at the end for a finale, is very much inspired by the many Silver and Golden Age team book stories I’ve read that follow that formula.

Astute readers will notice that my portrayal of Pauline Parsons here does not quite match how she’s generally portrayed in Sentinel Comics. She never uses her atomic glare, she’s only ever referred to as Pauline Parsons, not Felicia or any form of the Legacy title (Newest Legacy, Young Legacy, etc.), and in fact it’s ambiguous from the text alone whether she’s even wearing any kind of Legacy costume for this story, or if she’s merely in civvies. The reason for all this is that I did my best to keep her as accurate to how she was in the 1960s of Sentinel Comics. Her atomic glare and middle name, and her adoption of the costume and codename, were all first introduced over the 1980s.

That being said, there are a couple of fairly big inaccuracies in this story, that I just had to introduce to make it work at all. Firstly, I don’t think her age is quite canonical. She’s born in 1955, and I believe that she’s fairly consistently portrayed as “child” rather than “teen” until the '80s. Furthermore, and more dramatically, she doesn’t actually manifest the Legacy powers until 1984! So in the canon Metaverse, it’s virtually impossible to have any Silver Age stories with a powered Pauline. I recognise that C&A have made this decision, but given that it’s a stupid decision, I’ve elected to ignore it.

I ultimately decided to have the “it was all a dream” twist ending both because it is just classic Silver Age, and, despite all the story’s differences with canon, it does sort of make it fit better into canon, as it does not actually create any new characters. And, like Rich says, if I ever want to do anything else with these guys, alternate universe.

The villains of this issue are an eclectic bunch. Besides Blade, they are essentially two Wraith foes and two Tachyon foes, though Glamour’s sorta a Freedom Five foe generally, and honestly I’ve no idea who Thermos usually fights outside of the Lightspeed story arc. Anyways, I think it’s always fun when a random group of villains team up like this, and I tried to use some of the lesser-used ones. Granted, Glamour’s a fairly big name, but Thermos, I believe, hasn’t been in anything but Lightspeed and “Off the Rails,” and I don’t think Dr. Rubber and the Jellyfish has been used in anything. The Wraith has a lot of villains, y’all. I also considered Saw-Man and the Adhesivist, but figured to not use the latter since C&A have already used him in several stories. And I had fun trying to write ways for the two goofy jobbers Dr. Rubber and the Jellyfish to actually use their powers to commit crimes and hold their own against the heroes.

Just as the Freedom Five and many other Sentinel Comics characters are, The Freedom Kids are, of course, partially homaging real world characters. Shady’s backstory involving the loss of a parent is reminiscent of Dick Grayson / Robin’s backstory, Swifty just randomly happening to get caught in a duplication of the accident that granted Tachyon her powers is basically what happened with Wally West / Kid Flash and Barry Allen / The Flash, and Snowgirl is AZ’s niece just as Wally is Barry’s nephew.

Well, there’s my attempt at writing a goofy Silver Agey yarn! I hope it was a fun read!

Keep on saving the multiverse!
