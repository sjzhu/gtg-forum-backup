"Host" is another word for a group of beings or creatures, similar to "flock" or "swarm" or "throng", but one that's specifically often used with reference to angels, eg "The heavenly host" (or, to quote a song I love, "a host of holy horrors").  However, if Apostate is listed as currently rather than formerly affiliated with "The" Host, then that raises all sorts of interesting questions....

All the references to Grannum I can find are proper names, so it might just be a reference that the designers sneaked in.  But I feel fairly confident that Nahropt is an anagram...possible translations are "Not Harp", "Hat Porn", "Port Han", "An Thorp", "North P.A.", or "Pat Horn".  Pat Horn might be another proper name, and I of course like the idea of Hat Porn, but those are probably a bit of a stretch, while "An Thorp" isn't even gramatically correct (also rejected are those that involve non-actual words, such as Pot Harn or  A Phornt).  So probably the likeliest is "Not Harp", indicating that he was once a cherub in the Celestial Choir before Falling, and being renamed in dishonor of his lost instrument.  Or perhaps it's just Nathrop, a contraction of Nathan Winthrop or Lathrop or something, and thus another reference to some friend of the >G guys.

In any event, I doubt that there's any real meaning to the use of proper names here...it's probably just them naming the characters, the way La Capitan's minions all have proper names in their flavor text (along with mechanically-meaningless type keywords).  Grannum simply is the Demonic Pugilist and Nahropt is the Imp Pilferer, or more likely an Imp Pilferer since there are bunches of them.

(One quick rules question, by the way: based on the language of the Imp Pilferers, it looks like if there are two of them out, each one will destroy two hero cards?  And three will collectively destroy a total of nine, at three specific junctures during the villain turn?  Do I have this right?)
