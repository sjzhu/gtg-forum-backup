Yep, they are awesome at problem-solving. I've seen videos of ravens who can solve chains of puzzles to get a food reward - for example, the food is in a tube and requires a long stick to retrieve. The long stick needs a different stick to retrieve from another tube, and the different stick has to be got from solving another puzzle. And they'll perform tasks for the reward of an appropriate object that looks like it's the right one to get them the food from such a puzzle.

And these are not learned behaviours, the ravens hadn't been previously exposed to these kinds of tests and figured out how to solve them with actual thinking, rather than a scattergun try-everything approach. They try out a couple of sensible things and then get it right. And that's pretty impressive.

They also show Machiavellian behaviours like re-hiding a piece of cached food if they see another raven observing their hiding place. They wait until the other raven has gone away before they do this. Doesn't sound much to humans, but that's very much higher cognition which requires understanding that other birds/people/whatever have different knowledge to you and young children actually can't do that.

And the common magpie is the only non-mammal to pass the mirror test (recognising your own reflection) so far. Again, baby humans don't pass this test.
