Jeysie:

I like five-dollar words if they’re good, but not for their own sake. To use a salient example (apart from “salient”), in Argent Adept’s deck we have cards with words like “Polyphoric” and “Sarabande”, which are excellently poetic, but we also have things like “Alacritous”, which is an unnecessarily elaborate way of simply saying “fast”, and “Cedistic”, which is just a word Christopher made up. Those cards could just as well have been given names such as “Accelerating Crescendo” or “Dissonant Refrain”. That’s just a very quick off-the-cuff example, and far from the worst example of Christopher picking the wrong hill to die on with regard to his vocabulary choices. Probably the single worst naming choice he’s ever made was “The Organization”. I cringe so hard when I see that cover from the Sentinel Tactics expansion, where they’re trying to make that incredibly bland and generic name seem intimidating.

This is one of the weaker stories they ever crafted; it’s like a skeleton without skin, it needed more meat on its bones. And I think I know what the missing flesh needs to be: this is a Cult of Oblivion. These dummies who Christopher named The Factors (bad name), who are creating the Analect (worse name), are unknowingly drawing on the same magic that Nixious later uses, first to eat the rest of his species’s CoA and then to eat all other CoA members from all other planets…one of those would have been a human if this ritual had worked. Thusly, instead of the generic “gain this power to do some terrible thing”, we get very consistent visual presentation of a style of magic never seen before, and never seen again until the appearance of Nixious.

I do think the cover is decent, and it’s neat to see Carmichael for the first time, but this episode really changed the persona that had previously been described for this character. He started out as a “magical doomsday prepper”, and instead he becomes this weird odd couple tsundere of the Adept, who’s trying to lie to Nightmist and is characterized as a borderline nihilist. I always mourn for the loss of original versions of characters, like the pre-bot Unity, and this is a case where I definitely think I liked the original better, since the later version wasn’t developed into something vastly better.
