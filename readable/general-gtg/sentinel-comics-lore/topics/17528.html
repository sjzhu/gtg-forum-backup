I have a somewhat deserved reputation for being excessively negative, but I have tried hard to combat that tendency since back in my Justifier days. But it’s been a while since I indulged much at all, so I think I have earned the right to at least say that this is one of my least favorite episodes of the early podcast (probably least up to this part apart from small interludes that don’t really count), and that I largely don’t care for the Contest itself as a story in its current form.

BUT! As I often said back when Justifying, perceiving something as “wrong” often makes me become very invested in trying to make it right. And as I listened to this episode, I found an unprecedented degree of desire to rewrite to my satisfaction. Not because I don’t like the concept, although that’s somewhat true, and not because I disagree with many of the match outcomes, although that’s definitely true. But because, in my opinion, a lot of the presented details of how the scenario is presented, both in terms of the participants and of the readers, don’t quite line up as presented. Why Jansa is doing this, why the heroes agree to fight each other with at least close to lethal force, why the villains voluntarily participate against someone definitely using lethal force, what happens as a result of the outcomes, and how the whole thing makes a comic book that would be any good… I’m trying to make all these details add up, and the answers I come up with seem interesting IMO. Interesting enough that there could be a good fanfic in it, though it would be a lot more work than any project I’ve ever come close to completing.

For now, all I’m going to say about my headcanon vision is this. This is the Sentinels OblivAeon version of a certain iconic scene in… (decides the Schmony Schmobbins bit isn’t going to cut it here) …in Retributors: Unbounded Conflict. In that scene, the Nightmist or Argent ripoff character holds up a single finger, and then shortly thereafter names the sequel film. That’s what Jansa has to be doing with this conflict. Tarogath is back from being Deadline, so Jansa again has the Pandemonium Key, and I say that every aspect of how the contest is structured, every finest detail of the rules, was decided based on seeing how every other possible outcome would result either in OblivAeon succeeding and everything being destroyed, or something even worse (eg Bugbear eats everyone up to and including OblivAeon, or Paul VIII makes some error in judgement that turns him into Sciorn Legacy and he saves America by feeding Rainek all the other countries, something outrageous like that which would mandate one of the unfair matchups or improbable upsets or the like). The Key and similar equipment has proven to her satisfaction that everything has to happen within these exactly parameters. That doesn’t mean she calculated everything, at least one of the match outcomes had to have been an upset even by her standard; the heroes and villains get credit for what they did, and the lasting repercussions (eg the end of Dark Visionary) remain. But this wasn’t just Jansa picking an Endling from Earth or anything, this was her ensuring reality would be preserved, however terrible the price.
