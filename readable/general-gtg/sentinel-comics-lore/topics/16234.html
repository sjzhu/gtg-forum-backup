OK, here it is:

Going with the "Alternate Realities" theme, my villain has the power to distort the multiverse of minute possibilities to give herself incredible luck.

  For generations, the Adhin clan has been blessed with uncanny luck. The sons and daughters always found loving husbands and wives, the family farming business always seemed to have exactly what goods were in highest demand that season, and when a deadly disease was plaguing the town, one of them just happened to notice that a stray cat which had been drinking some spilled water around the well was now dead.
    The Adhins believed the source of their good fortune was a small talisman they'd been passing down over the years as a family heirloom. It was thanks to this, they claimed, that they could always find the best uses for their talents.
    Then came Gabriel Adhin. Ever since childhood she had felt odd, indistinct sensations from time to time. Like she was moving in a hundred different directions at once, but in directions there were no words for. When she inherited the charm, suddenly it all made sense. In her mind's eye, she saw countless, branching possible realities, all different. In time, she used the charm to hone her psychic abilities, learning to bend the miasma of minute possibilities to her will. Her powers fully realized, she began wondering what else she can bend to her will...

    And that's my villain's backstory! Still haven't thought of a good villain name for her yet, but I have a good idea of how she plays:

    She has the power to distort probability. In-game, this allows her to get the more powerful cards( signified by a "lucky" keyword) in her deck out more often.

    On her starting side, she's an incredibly smug trickster who tries to confound the players by scrambling their actions and blocking or dislocating their attacks. She has her charm in play, which gives her the end-of-turn ability to, if the top card in her trash is not "lucky," reveal the next few cards in her deck, shuffle all the non-lucky ones back, then shuffle the lucky ones and put them on top.

    The "lucky" keyword has other uses, too. On her "smug trickster" side, she can sometimes prevent cards from being put into play immediately. The way it works is something like, "the first time each turn a player plays a card, reveal the top villain card. If lucky,
shuffle that villain card into the deck, and shuffle that hero card into the villain deck face up. When a hero card is on top of the villain deck, put it into play."
    Delaying the actions of hero cards like this might be too powerful, so maybe it should be limited to a certain type of hero card, or one type chosen randomly somehow. Another possibility would be to have the revealed card put in the trash, so players might try playing cards just to get more lucky cards out of her deck.

    Because she fights by manipulating luck, a lot of her attacks don't deal damage directly from her. She'll probably have attacks that redirect environment damage at heroes, and cards with things like,"All hero targets deal themselves 2 projectile damage. Any hero that dealt the villain damage last round deals him/herself 2 melee damage." She'll also have ways to increase damage dealt to heroes.
    But her favorite way to attack is to turn the attacks of heroes against themselves. For example, she could have a card that says, "All heroes must play a one-shot now that would deal one or more nonhero targets damage. Any damage that would be dealt in this way to a nonhero target is redirected to the hero who played that card."
    Other cards are designed to mess with players' plans and setups. She might put a jinx on the player with the biggest hand that forces them to, if they try to play a card, pick the card to play at random( actually, having several different kinds of jinxes she can curse players with could be a cool idea).
    Maybe she'll play a one-shot that has each player reveal the next 3 cards in their deck, and discard the ones that each player doesn't already have in hand or play. She also probably has cards that destroy all the hero cards mixed into her deck, so they won't go off at all later. She could sometimes steal peoples' equipment, and keep it under the charm card until it gets destroyed. If the charm isn't in her possession at this time, she'll just reclaim it instead.
    And here's a few miscellaneous ideas for cards or abilities she could have:
    -Something that involves making a player guess what card is comming up next
    -Something that causes heroes to attack each other
    -Tarot-themed cards! I've always thought tarot card designs were pretty cool. She could have a few of them, possibly as villain targets( since she really doesn't have many yet):
        =Ace of Wands: Could pull out jinxes or something. Maybe deals fire damage whenever a lucky card is put into play?
        =Ace of Cups: Heals her whenever a lucky card is added to the trash.
        =Ace of Swords: Probably straight melee damage, or maybe it "cuts" peoples' hand size?
        =Ace of Pentacles: Something to do with playing extra cards, maybe? Like "play and extra card the first time a lucky card is put into play each turn."

    But let's get back to the flip mechanic. It's based around her lucky talisman, an object so important it might end up being a second villain "character" card. While not necessary for her to use her luck-stealing powers, it gives them much better foresight and she relies greatly on its timeline-adjusting abilities. She may be pretty smug and playful when she has it with her, but manage to steal it and she'll become desperate, panicked. NOBODY ELSE must ever be allowed to use it, and she will now stop at nothing to get it back!
    A hero can steal it by dropping its HP to 0. It is indestructible, however. At the start of the villain turn, flip the charm, flip the villain, and place the charm in front of the last hero to damage it. Reset its HP. On its other side, the charm is now considered a hero target, and it grants a special Power to the hero it's in front of:

POWER: Reveal the top 3 cards in any one deck. Re-order them in any way you like, then put them back.

Now the heroes have a chance to turn the tables and use its power against her! Ha-HAH! But as mentioned before, she'll stop at nothing to reclaim it. On her "desperate" side, she'll cease her mind games and start attacking furiously. Possible attacks on this side include causing self-inflicted damage to the holder of the charm and playing the BOTTOM card of the villain deck at her turn's end.
    Oh, and I think revealing a face-up hero card in her deck should do something different this time. Maybe instead of playing it, she puts it on top of that hero's deck and deals a bunch of psychic damage to them.
    At the end of the villain turn, the heroes may play "keep away" with her charm, passing it to a different player to more evenly distribute the damage it attracts. If she manages to reduce it to 0HP, it flips back to being in her possession. She calms down, flipping back to the "smug trickster" side( hmmm... wonder if it would be more interesting if she didn't always flip with her charm...).

    I haven't put a whole lot of thought into her appearance so far, but my general idea right now is some sort of Carmen Sandiego-esque suit and hat. There's a picture of her attached. Heh, I was actually planning on submitting a whole mini-comic about her, where she keeps making heroes' attacks backfire. Then she tries it on Absolute Zero, and things don't go so well for her. Oh, and beforehand, she mocks him for thinking he can beat her when he's had such terrible luck his entire life to begin with. It would've been very dramatic.

    ANYWAY, that's pretty much it! Hope you enjoy reading through all these submissions, and thanks for this opportunity. As a game design student, I greatly enjoyed it!
 

     -Alex Franklin

As you can see, things changed a lot mechanics-wise from the initial concept. I was surprised they basically kept the first few paragraphs of the backstory word-for-word when they put it in the website. It's not, like, terrible, but I think everybody feels a little bit embarassed looking back at their earlier writing. It's probably fine.

Well, I'm gonna go actually listen to the episode now. When I'm done, I'd be happy to answer any questions people have for me about how she got to the final version we have now and stuff.

well, on timelines/realities. Look at it this way.

 

There was Sentinels of the -Multiverse- - The Multiverse was open, active, possible to move between timelines/realities (As per Shattered Timelines, Ivana Romanof, XTREME Prime Wardens, O-X, Be The Gate, Ect, ect... ect...). We have a central timeline/reality (that of all the heroes and villains of the card game) but there is a lot of crossing over back and forth between timelines/realities. Lots of weird things around that, so its very muddy as to what is the 'real' timeline/reality. In fact, in essence the overall story of The Multiverse is that ALL of the timelines/realities are real, all of them can be crossed over into by various abilities, powers, spells, and all of them have an impact on the cruical point (a 'Fixed Point, perhaps?) of OblivAeon assaulting.

This multiple timelines/realities P'O'd OblivAeon, who idenitified the crux of the multiverse at Earth. He starts collapsing timelines in an attempt to destroy the Multiverse.

 

After the fight, the timelines/realities are cut off from each other. We know of 2 that stabilize and remain.

 

1) - The end result is that Rook City is only damaged, and things go on (The RPG)

2) - The end result is that Rook City is destroyed, and its a bad place (Tactics)

 

There is nothing to say that either of these timelines has its origins in the exact same 'Centralized' timeline as Sentinels of the Multiverse. It is entirely possible that the Tactics timeline/reality grew from a point where Kismet did not become a villain or have powers, beyond a simple curse of Setback (or even Setback's origin is slightly different, we don't know - for instance, his good/bad luck is a natural power, not a curse) It is simply one of the stabilized timelines that is cut off from the others that we know of, and that we pick up from some time after the OblivAeon event, where Rook City has been majorly destroyed. 

 

Nothing at all states that it is the exact same up to the point of OblivAeon as the 'centralized' timeline of SotM. Only that it is one of the timelines we can follow story wise after OblivAeon. Given how muddy the timeline/reality cross over is in SotM, this is entirely possible that a side timeline, without Kismet being a villain goes along just fine until the OblivAeon event (where ALL realities and timelines cross into one fixed point) and then goes its own way afterwards. Perhaps being affected (by gaining Setback, if he didn't exist as a hero before) or perhaps just having its own reasons for things (ie, Setback got his luck powers a different way)

 

3 different timelines/realities, for instance all come together at the OblivAeon event. ALL timelines/realities come together at the OblivAeon Event. Thats kinda his point, he's collapsing them all together to destroy them.
