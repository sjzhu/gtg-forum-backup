So whenever anything bad happens, something good happens to him later, and whenever something good happens, something bad will soon follow.

We know he's worked at casinos, at least 3 of them so far. Maybe some force or power noticed him, surrounded by all these games of chance, and decided to experiment. Maybe Wager Master was in on it? or some of these cosmic forces hinted at on Deadline's information. Equity seems like he could be concerned with balancing out the good and the bad.

His normal card art and power seem to highlight his ignorance. He also seems to be kinda stupid. Kinda slow on the uptake, which is a boon for him, because it helps get him into trouble so good can happen later. But his Promo card art and power change him a lot. He seems to have come to a realization that his current bad luck = good luck later, and he can use that to mitigate the difficulties others experience, actively expending his karma, if you will.

Thoughts?

I think Setback is more over-eager than stupid.  He really wants to do what he can to help out, but doesn't always think things through before acting.  After becoming a more experienced hero, however, I think he gained a new perspective on his luck problems.  He realized his bad luck makes him a magnet for all the trouble in a fight, allowing him to protect civilians and his teammates, while said teammates take down the bad guys.  This is best seen in the flavor text for his card "Uncharmed Life":

"You know, this isn't great.  But better me than someone else!"  -Setback, Freedom Five #592

It can be seen again in his profile for the Broken City set for Tactics:

Setback has always been the heart of the team. Passionate, upbeat, and eager to help, Peter Riske's bad luck is balanced by his indomitable spirit. His favorite place to be is between his friends and danger, working to prevent any harm from befalling those he cares for. Knowing that what he does makes a difference, even given his propensity for disaster, is the best heroic motivation that he could imagine.

What you see on his Dark Watch card is Setback catching a piece of collapsing building (he knew that if he was in the building then the first part of the ceiling to fall would be the section above him instead of anywhere else) and holding out his hand for someone to take hold of.

As for how much he knows about his powers, I'm not sure he even realizes his luck is a superpower.  None of the flavor text on any of his cards has him directly reference his luck; they only show him apologizing for the bad and being suprised by the good.  In fact, from the comments on his cards from other characters, I would go so far as to say that NO ONE realizes this.  

Also, as a side note, Setbacks lists the sources of his powers as genetic and experimentation, so we know that he got his luck powers from either Baron Blades experiment, as a power he was born with (likely, since his luck was mentioned before the medical test), or a combination of the two (he was born with it but the experiment made it better/worse).  So Wager Master and the Cosmic Entity were definitely not involved.

P.S. Setback is the best hero ever.

The sources for the characters' powers that are listed on the bio pages are not based on what the character knows.  Even if Setback didn't know about his powers coming from Wager Master or the Cosmic Entity, his bio would still list "Cosmic" as one of the sources.  As an example, see Citizen Dawn's bio, which lists "Cosmic" as the source for her powers but her backstory mentions nothing about a cosmic source for her powers and gives the impression that Dawn is unaware of where her powers come from.

Nuh-uh; that is so not how probability works. There are, what, seven billion people in the world? Say we give everyone in the world a coin, and tell each one to toss it until they get a head. What is the largest number of tails that at least one person will get, probabilistically speaking?

Thirty-three. And that's with me rounding up.

Statistical flukes regress to the mean, and they do it fast enough that you cannot build a character on such flukes, especially when the things that happen to Setback are way less likely than tossing a head. His luck power could be cosmic or genetic, or the result of Baron Blade's experiments, but it is not just a quire of probability.
