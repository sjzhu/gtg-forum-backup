While I can see your point of view, I feel like the Tactics universe is starting to get a bad rap for simply following certain plot elements to a potentially logical conclusion.

For instance, Visionary.  We're told from her inception–from her earliest bio at the start of the SotM game–that she's living on borrowed time and that the burst blood vessel in her brain will kill her.  Yes, it sucks to have a character whom readers care about die, but it's not like we weren't warned about this from our first introduction to her.  

Tachyon works similarly in my eyes–we see a character who is really broken and at the edges of her power by the end of the card game, who's left an incredibly powerful foe in her wake who has reason to hunt Tachyon down and take advantage of her relative weakness.  This one was particularly sore for me, given Tachyon's status as a lead LGBTQ hero, but within the context of the story, it makes sense… (I just kind of wish that, if it had to be a Freedom 5 member, it was Wraith or Bunker, instead…)

I won't attempt to make the argument that the Tactics timeline isn't the darker of the two paths, but every story element that's there–every death, every conflict–makes sense within the context of the overall story being told.  I do wish, however, that some of those consequences echoed deeper into the rpg…but, then again, we haven't seen what plot elements the rpg holds.

I may be a bit late for Halloween, but I'm always up for creepy. 

 

Hope I doesn't come off as bragging, but it's a bit hard to creep me* out in media, at least when it comes to speculative fiction and spooky stuff. I'm a huge fan of horror, monsters and all things dark, and Sentinels plays it all in a lighthearted way (relatively speaking), so things like Biomancer's yucky antics and Gloomweavers gloomweaving end up being more fun than scary. I'll even play games in Mittermeiers Festival despite it's difficulty since I like the atmosphere so much. 

So, when something from the game creeps me out, it's serious. Let's see what counts:

 

-Spite: Like it was said before, he's the kind of monster that's close enough to reality that he feels worse than the planet-destroying juggernauts (super-drugs and resident evil transformation aside). But what makes it worse are the cards where he's preying on some defenseless person (including children), or the ones where's hes outright murdering people in front of others. Innocent bystander is the worst, showing how you could just be taking care of your business and then BAREHANDED BLOODY MURDER BY SERIAL KILLER (and you're next). 

Collateral Damage, and that bit on Letters Page where Spite smashes a hostage's head in front of Wraith are creepy with an extra feeling of helplessness on top. 

 

-Team Leader's Tachyon incapacitated art. In a super-hero story, where guys get tossed through walls getting to see a main character being defeat by something a mundane as being shot in the leg, with Tachyon even crying in pain while on the ground... That's messed up. 

 

-The victory screen against Iron Legacy on the video game, where you can see him crying while defeated. I really can't describe why, but this weirds me out so much. 

 

-About Mittermeir's Festival: Not the cards, not the music or the background screens, but that one part on the Letter's Page where they mention a guy stuck in the Festival, unable to see the passage of time or even notice something wrong while he plays the same game again and again. 

 

-Final Wasteland: Or, to be specific, the card "Unforgiving Wasteland". One of my weaknesses is... How can we call it? "Predator horror" maybe? That horrifying sense of being hunted by some bestial stalker and being defenseless against it. It's a bit hard to pull, since it needs more than a huge monster full of teeth. But some guy lost in the middle of a desert, surrounded by darkness and hungry eyes? That works for me.  

 

 

* Note that I'm a huge coward in every other situation. Last month scaled up a wall in fear of something small coming from the shadows... It was a baby opossum.
