So Absolute Zero must be kept cold to survive. Anything he touches or eats that’s above his body temperature would be like a hot poker and probably cause him massive internal damage. Anything he eats or breathes also has to be the temperature of his body. There’s no way around that.

His bio on the web site says that anything above the temperature of liquid helium feels like fire to him, and also that the suit is “capable” of maintaining absolute zero temperature. Let’s take the latter as a statement of capability and not what Abs usually runs it at. He needs <~4 K. Though he clearly can survive limited times of exposure to room temperature, given that he’s depicted on a number of cards with portions of his suit missing. I doubt he was very comfortable, but at least this is survivable.

I see no reason why he couldn’t still eat food, provided it came into contact with his body by way of cryogenic freezing and he didn’t open up the suit. Breathing is the problem. Maybe Absolute Zero can tolerate just-barely-liquid oxygen, at 55 K. Then he could be immersed in LO2. Solid O2, though, I’m not sure how he would manage. We know he can get out of the suit inside the cryo chamber, so it’s not a mechanical means.

Here’s what I think: solid oxygen is much more dense than gaseous oxygen. The density of liquid oxygen is ~1000x higher than gaseous oxygen, and a human breath only extracts a few % of oxygen from the air. So Absolute Zero, if he drinks liquid oxygen or eats solid oxygen, would need to do so about 10,000x less frequently than a normal human breathes. Let’s say I breath once per second when I exercise: that works out to eight times a day for our freezing friend. He can just crunch and inhale (they still have to go to his lungs!) some oxygen dust while he’s in the cryo-chamber, and he doesn’t have to breathe at all when in combat. His slow metabolism will be working on the solid oxygen sitting in his lungs all day long.

He doesn't breath. Or eat. His body directly converts thermal energy into bio-energy. In normal temperatures, the overload from that process causes pain. If it's cold, there's less ambient thermal energy to convert, so it's more comfortable. 

The suit facilitates the process by regulating the temperature around him and providing safe ways to vent off excess energy.  He still breathes out of habit, but he doesn't get anything from it  

Anyway, that's why fire gets all weird around him.
