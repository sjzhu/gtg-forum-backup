Hermetic.

This guy is important. And I can't figure out why. Ever since I saw his art proof, my brain has gone crazy thinking of how he fits in the greater narrative and the Scholar in general.

On the Hermetic's actual card, the flavor text mentions that the vials in his dwelling are not poisons, but antidotes. Antidotes for what exactly? What would a villain need antidotes for?

The red stone is, I assume, an attempt to recreate the Philosophers stone. 

In the Sentinels deck, Hermetic makes another apperance. The flavor text here is also very suspect. "Dont you know who I am?" That could just be hot air a bad guy says when he is caught...but flavor text is chosen more carefully than that. There is something more at play here.

The silver creature on Parses Reveal the Flaws has a clear alchemy symbol on its head. How are the Scholar/Hermetic possibly involved with it?

There is no solid connections I can make with any of this, but theres too much little bits and pieces to think its not significant.

I think that Hermetic represents a different aspect of the alchemical sciences, one that is potentially a lot nastier than the Scholar's approach.  

Here is my reasoning...

The scholar is about changing himself (through alchemical means) and influencing other people/events through advice, action, and intervention.  The changes that he enacts are based on helping people better understand themselves, so that they can continue the cycle of changing themselves (if that is their decision).  His place is to start the change and then let the other person carry it along (or not).  He spends a lot of time holding back and thinking before taking action.

I think that Hermetic represents a facet that is about changing other things/people (through alchemical means), and influencing events through them.  He enacts change, and then observes the consequences and gains understanding through the results.  It could be beneficial, it could be decidedly malevolent, depending on what he was seeking to get out of it.  I could see Hermetic providing an antidote... tailored to a poison he brewed up earlier. Or I could see him witholding assistance to the heroes because he gained nothing from helping them.   Or because he had been promised a relic/book that furthered his own ambitions.  I don't think he's a major player- i see him as a relatively nasty outsider who knows way more than everyone else and delights in hoarding such knowledge.  The antidotes could be for poisons, but they could just as easily be antidotes for social/mental traits like complacency or wanderlust or kindness (since there is no reason why they can't also be antidotes to good things!)
