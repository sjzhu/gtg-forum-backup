Bit of a hostile way to resurrect a nine-year old thread, but welcome to the forums.

To answer your question: the reason this was confusing to people was that it did not seem to match up with Haka’s power set as typically presented in the game and the surrounding lore, and indeed your interpretation wasn’t quite what the developers had in mind.

When the Haka episode of The Letters Page first released, C&A confirmed this:

When he takes the life of something, there’s a bit of void (not Void) that’s left and that he can draw in and store and eventually unleash on a foe as a debilitating attack. He typically uses this attack in the form of a shout or battle cry, but it’s not the force of the sound itself that’s doing the damage (thus not Sonic as the type), but it’s this accumulated “death energy” that he’s releasing, which is where the Toxic part comes in. He doesn’t even really do this intentionally as he’s just interpreting it as this mana concept given his cultural background.

It wasn’t that the energy he was absorbing was evil, it was just that death-energy was being treated as Toxic rather than Infernal for the purposes of the card.

Ultimately, this capability didn’t end up matching the ways in which Haka developed as a character, not the least because he doesn’t actually kill most of the foes he defeats, which (along with the awkward slightly-racist nature of the card) was why it was removed from Definitive Edition entirely.

Except, I’m not so sure it was. Rather, I think Savage Mana was actually substantially reworked into Definitive Haka’s Taonga Mana card.
