While I can understand the frustration with having something "taken away", I find accusations of "greed" to be WAY off base here.

C&A have spent the last 13 months spending anywhere from 3-6 hours per week prepping for and then recording each episode, plus another ~2 hours for each editors note.  (They've said in the past that they spend roughly the length of an episode in prep, and recording time is obviously longer than the episode length).  That means, minimum, half of an entire workday, per week, for 2 of the 4 most important people at the company, devoted to a podcast.  They then have to PAY Trevor (an outside contractor) money to edit each episode, and they then upload to a website that they have to maintain and, you guessed it, pay for.  And they haven't asked us for a dime.

Now, up until now-ish, time spent on The Letters Page has helped C&A to get their stories lined up, which has been useful both for OblivAeon and for RPG prep.  (This is, in fact, the very argument that C&A made to OblivAeon backers who complained that they were wasting time on a podcast that should have been spent getting OblivAeon finished.)  But we're moving out of that phase and reaching a point where The Letters Page will no longer be directly contributing to C&A's productivity on GTG projects.  So time spent on the podcast means time not spent developing/overseeing the development of new games.

Let's not forget that GTG is actually a game publishing company, and that publishing games is what allows them to do silly things like pay salaries to their staff and keep the lights on.  You know what doesn't pay for those things?  Spending time and money to produce a podcast for free.  If C&A were being "greedy", they'd drop Letters Page the second it's not helping them release product faster and focus on more important things.  But people seem to enjoy it, so they're willing to keep it going... but it has to at least be marginally worth it.  (If I were to look at this strictly from a revenue generated perspective, I would probably conclude that the podcast is a waste, because I'd be surprised if whatever piddly amount the Patreon brings in is truly worth the opportunity cost of C&A's time.)

So C&A took something that gets a lot less listeners, and hid it behind the lowest paywall on their patreon.  If C&A were being "greedy" they would've made Editors Notes cost more.  They could've easily made it exclusive to the $5+ groups, but instead they're only asking for a dollar.  That is less than a cup of coffee.  Before taxes.  At Tim Hortons.

Now, there probably are some listeners who truly can't come up with a dollar extra per month.  And it honestly is sad that they'll be missing out on ~1 hour of content per month that they used to get for free.  But they'll still be getting ~1.5-2 hours of free content, per week.  Commercial free (so far as we know) as opposed to many other podcasts.  That's a pretty good deal.

For everyone else, please take your moral outrage elsewhere.  C&A are asking you to pay a very small amount for something that costs them time and money to produce.  It's simple, really: decide whether or not the Editors Notes are worth $1 to you, and then either pay or don't pay that $1 accordingly.  That's how capitalism works.

(Note: If you want to whinge about capitalism, I believe the most apropriate thread would be the one for the Vengeful Five episode, in which Proletariat was discussed, and which is found here).

OK, I'm glad dprcooke made their post, because it echoes a lot of how I feel but I was terrified of everyone jumping down my throat yet again if I said any of it, so I tried to kinda sugarcoat it a little. :>

My stance on life in general is that nobody is ever obligated to do anything without compensation, and the USA being the capitalist country that it is, "compensation" in this country often involves money.

This particularly applies to companies, who exist entirely to make products in return for getting compensation of money for them.

Any time a company makes free content for you, it's them taking time away from doing stuff that makes them money. That's a very nice gesture of them, and TBH generally is often done to still compensate the company in some way. In this case it compensated C&A with getting to talk about their babies and it serves as a marketing device/secondary source for the RPG, so it made sense to do it entirely for free so long as they were talking about info directly related to the RPG... which is exactly what they did.

And yet even with that consideration, I feel they're still being super-nice having the Letters Page itself be free because many companies would charge for even the material in the main podcast itself. I have a hobby of reading RPG sourcebooks for fun, and the material in the Letters Page is material that many companies would wrap into a sourcebook and charge money for (Stuff like 10 JackPointers for Shadowrun comes to mind).

Especially since those sourcebooks wouldn't even contain Q&A sessions or anything. Having creators actually show up and regularly solicit and answer questions is incredibly uncommon. I think the last time I saw it done was Chris Avellone and the Fallout Bible, and bear in mind that Avellone did that as an entirely personal project while working at a dying company because he thought Fallout itself might die out and so wanted to do a last hurrah.

So... TBH I think C&A would have been well within their rights to make the entire podcast from now on be behind a Patreon paywall. It would certainly bum me out on a certain level, but it would be no different from the card games or Tactics or the RPG being products you have to pay for. The fact that they're making only the Editor's Notes (something that they've said download numbers show is already something only "diehards" tend to listen to) be behind the paywall is really gracious of them.

I've outlined ways I think they can get around the "paywalling questions of non-Patreon fans" problem, but people really need to accept that in order for this to work, something you find valuable is going to have to be "gated" or "taken away" because that's how you entice people to pay money. Like, I hate to repeat myself again, but the fact that people keep frequently suggesting minor perks nobody would actually care much about makes it feel to me like that fact isn't really sinking in for folks. (And PlatinumWarlock's suggestion of having all asking restricted to Patreon folks ironically seems even worse to me in terms of PR than paywalling the Editor's Notes considering how people already complain that it feels like only a tiny group of regulars get to ask questions on air.)

P.S. I do think they also should be more up front with cutoff dates for asking questions to try to avoid problems like Girl With No Name has (and which I think I've run into a few times too). There are some reasonable concerns mixed in here, but unfortunately "keep making everything for free" isn't the most realistic solution for them.
