Ascension is a fun card game - it was our favourite before I got hold of Sentinels. Basically it's a deckbuilding game where you start with a deck of ten cards, eight of which are "resource" cards (worth one Rune, the resource you use to buy new cards), while the other two are "attack" cards (worth one Power each, the resource you use to defeat monsters). You draw a hand of five cards from your own deck, while six cards are dealt out from the main deck in the middle of the board - when it's your turn you can choose to buy any cards you can afford from that central row (there are also two permenantly accessible decks off to one side, one which contains a two-Rune character and costs three Runes to buy, while the other is a two-Powr character and costs two Runes to buy).

Alternatively you can defeat any monsters if you have the Power to do so (again, there is a permenantly-accessible monster off to one side, the Cultist, who needs two Power to kill but is considered an unlimited quantity so the card is never removed when defeated). You can spend as much of your available Runes/Power from your hand as you like - you don't have to use every card in your hand and can just choose not to spend them. Any new cards you "buy" from the central row go into your discard pile.

Your turn ends when you're done with your hand (having chosen to spend or not spend all your cards). Next turn you draw another five cards and repeat. Next turn your deck will be empty so you reshuffle your trash and now start drawing cards which will contain the ones you bought previously.

Defeating monsters earns you Honour (in the form of small token thingys), as well as (usually) some other bonus, like being able to draw another card, or forcing another player to discard one/all-but-one their Constructs (cards you put into play and which stay there, granting you some kind of bonus or extra ability). When the Honour total between all players reaches a certain number (60 for two players, and I think 75 for three and 90 for four), the game ends when the last player in the turn order has had their turn (so everyone's had the same number of turns). Then everyone counts up their Honour totals, which includes the values on the cards they've bought (a number shown in a star in the bottom - every card except the ones you start with gives at least one Honour point, up to a maximum of eight, of which only one card has this value). The person with the highest total wins.

In general, the game doesn't involve directly attacking the other players or anything, but you might destroy a monster which (as mentioned previously) forces them to lose some of their Constructs currently in play. There's another monster (of whom there is only one copy in the deck) who, on death, lets the player who killed him take one card at random from the hands of every other player. You might also choose to try and buy or destroy a certain card from the central row just so someone else can't get it.

There are also some expansions to the game too (we saw them at Leisure Games on Sunday) but I have no idea whether they add in any new mechanics or just extra cards, as we currently only own the base game.

Got a few more games that we play...

Chrononauts - Card game in which you are a time traveller. There is a timeline (of actual historical events...most of them American but presumably that's where the maker of the game is from, so...) represented by cards laid out in four rows of...eight, I think, and spans various years with the earliest being something like 1829 (assassination of Abraham Lincoln, whatever year that was) to erm...1999 I think (some kind of shoot-out at a school). Events between these two years include stuff like World War II, the sinking of the Titanic, and the shootings of various people including JFK and John Lennon. As a player, you can win the game one of three ways. Firstly, by changing the timeline so that events match up to the version of the future that you came from (certain events along the timeline are called "lynchpins" - flipping these causes "ripples" which flip other cards, their events becoming paradoxes since the event that caused them suddenly never happened/turned out differently). Note that if ever there are thirteen paradoxes existing at once, the universe basically implodes and it's game over for everyone. The second way you can win is by completing your mission, gathering certain artifacts from across time and playing them (laying them out in front of you on your turn - you get to play one card per turn). The third way is to fix the timeline by playing Patch cards on paradoxes - every time you play a Patch you get to draw an extra card (ordinarily your turn consists of playing a card and drawing a card, so your hand size never changes), and if you end your turn on ten cards, you win. You can mess with other people a bit by stealing artifacts from them or forcing them to swap their ID or Mission cards for a new one (ID card is the one that tells you the year you're from and thus which years you need to change in the timeline in order to win that way).

Braggart - Another card game, fairly quick to play, Basically you are a bunch of adventurers in a tavern boasting about your various heroic deeds. You play cards representing a Scene (optional), Foe, Deed, and Result (also optional), reading them out as epically as you like whilst doing so ;). Other players may choose to play Liar or Outrageous Liar cards (if they have them) when you've boasted, letting them swap your boast cards for others from their hands - the boast cards are worth points so if someone plays, say, a Foe worth seven points and you have one in-hand that's worth just two as well as a Liar card, you could play said Liar card and swap that seven-pointer for your crappy two one. And then the person has to re-read out the newly-altered boast ;). The winner of the round is the one who played the higest-scoring boast, and gets to keep all those cards in a separate pile while everyone else only gets to keep one (of their choice) from their own boasts (if they boasted - doing so is optional). The game ends when you've gone through the entire deck, at which point everyone adds up the score from the boast cards they "won" and the person with the highest total wins.

Forbidden Island - Not a card game, though it has cards in it. You're on an island formed from lots of squares, each one representing a different location (place names include Breaker's Bridge, Cave of Embers, and Silver Gate). The aim of the game is for the group to gather (co-operatively) four treasures from across the island, which is slowly sinking into the sea. You have to get four matching cards in your hand (max hand size of four) representing one of the treasures, then make it to one of the two locations from which that particular treasure can be obtained and use an action (you get three actions per turn) to swap your four cards for that treasure. Once you've gathered all four treasures between you (it doesn't matter who gets what, or how many each person has, if any, as long as all four have been collected), you need to go to the location called Fool's Landing, at which point someone with a Helicopter Lift card (assuming someone managed to get one) plays it and you all escape and win the game. The problem is that the island is, as mentioned, sinking into the sea so locations will become flooded and possibly disappear altogether (it's possible to use an action to "shore up" a location, un-flooding it). Since each treasure can be obtained from one of two locations, it's okay if one disappears but if both go, you can't win the game (if a square is flooded you can still do stuff there, it's only if it actually disappears that you can't go there or do anything because at that point it's removed from the game entirely). You can also lose if you get cut off by missing squares, though some characters have ways around this (for example, the Diver can use an action to swim across any number of spaces in a straight line from one square to another). You can also play a Helicopter Lift card to move yourself and anyone on the same square as you to any location you like :). This game tends not to last too long.

Here's a good one: currently the only 2 games that trump Sentinels for me and my friends. the 2 games based off of the BattleCon system, War of Indines and Devastation of Indines. It's a pretty unique game, so it doesn't have a WHOLE bunch of the things you explicitly DO like, but it has absolutely NONE of the things you DON'T like.

The premise is that of a 2D fighter -- picture King of Fighters, Mortal Kombat, Street Fighter, etc. -- put in a card/board game form. you have a 7-space, 2D plane on which you move your characters back and forth, and each turn you pick 2 cards from your 8-card hand to make your attack for that turn. You'll pick 1 base and 1 style out of a total of 5 unique, character-specific styles and 7 bases (1 character-specific Unique Base and 6 Generic Bases that everyone has), meaning that each character can turn their 12 cards into one of 35 unique attacks. There are also 2 discard piles that your attacks cycle through after each turn (called "beats," since they happen simultaneously), so you can't use a given style and base until 3 turns later.

As for your preferences: It's not a CCG -- all the characters are fixed-deck, like Sentinels.

No decisions are made for you, except for the fact that each move you make has a 2-turn cooldown, forcing you to vary your decisions rather than spam a single move. That still leaves you with 15 options each turn; plenty of choices to make yourself.

No space theme; it's a fighter.

Not a "war" game, despite its name. (I assume you mean area-control, like Risk or Axis and Allies? Yeah, they aren't even in the same GENRE)

The theme is there, and very well-implemented, but the mechanics are ROCK SOLID.

The only luck involved is guessing what your opponent will do; it's entirely skill-based.

No trading, no auctions, no religion (no IRL religion at least. One character is an angel, but so is Fanatic, and you like Sentinels, right? It's kind of like that. He's like a male Fanatic.)

All movement is limited to what is written on the cards you play. If your card says "Start of Beat: Advance one space." What do you do? Wait until the Start of Beat triggers happen (immediately after determining whose attack goes first by comparing priority), and then move your character forward one space.

Traitor mechanics? PSSH! It's a FIGHTING GAME. You were never on the same side to begin with! There is a TINY bit of bluffing sometimes, when it comes to anteing tokens that grant special abilities, but it's not nearly on the same level as things like Battlestar Galactica or Shadows over Camelot.

 

What it does have:

There's plenty of screwage. Lots of the character abilities allow you to mess with the opponent in various fun and creative ways, like flipping their movement around, making them discard cards, infecting their attacks with bees, copying their attacks, canceling their effects, etc.

No Meeples, per se, but there are cardboard standups that represent your characters.

The theme is strongly implemented, well-balanced with the gameplay, and pretty immersive. The art is also fantastic.

 

Other cool stuff:

There is a LOT of gameplay in the box. Speaking specifically of Devastation here, since War is in between reprints and the 1st edition doesn't contain a lot of the extra content that Devastation has.

The cast of characters is immense and fantastic! There are 18 characters in War and 30 in Devastation (not counting promos, one of which is Legacy and the Freedom Five!), each with their own unique special abilities, cards, tokens, strategies, etc. From Cadenza, a mechanical soldier that is extremely slow but can tank every hit you throw at it and hit you back twice as hard, to Rukyuk, a sniper who uses custom ammo to enhance his attacks, leading to even MORE variety; the only problem is, he has to stop and reload every now and then, leaving himself wide open for a counterattack. Aria has mechanical drones she places that give passive negative effects to any enemies foolish enough to stand near them. Oriana has a pool of MP she can use to power her spells, but it can run out, leaving her weak if she can't hit you to get them back. Kallistar turns into a flame elemental, making herself faster and stronger, at the expense of draining her health each turn she keeps it active. King Alexian is so POWERFUL his Unique ability is actually a benefit to his opponents; whenever he hits them, he gives them tokens they can use to make themselves faster and stronger just to keep up. Gaspar makes clones of himself to execute multiple, weaker attacks per turn, which can lead to some wicked combos if planned properly.

Devastation also includes several boss characters that require 3 or more players to team up together to take down, as well as 5 co-op dungeons (think Double Dragon, Turtles in Time, or Battletoads), that you can take on with friends or even solo.

There are also multiple alternate rules and game modes, like 2- or 3- on-1, 2-on-2, tag-team matchups, and arenas that give everyone extra rules and conditions to pay attention to; they flip like Sentinels villains, leading to 2-phase fights.

Also, Tom Vassel from the Dice Tower listed it as #8 on his top 100 games of all time, and went on record saying it was one of the best games he has ever played. Ever.
