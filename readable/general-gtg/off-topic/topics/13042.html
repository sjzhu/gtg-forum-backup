You could also look at it another way - ever since WoW, the MMO market has changed and no one has come up with a good way to deal with it.  You can't make a small, simple game and then iterate because people want a game that's as fully-featured as WoW from the get-go.  That means you need more money, which means you need to try to hook paying customers people in earlier.  It also means that your game will need to deviate from the proven formula of WoW somehow in order to stand out in the crowd, so you'll need to try things which may end up not working.  On top of it all, the demographic of MMO players has grown up in the last ten years, and younger players nowadays are more "grazers" than "settlers" so they're less likely to stick with a game for as long as they used to.  It all adds up to the situation the OP described, where developers try to get people to pay early for underfeatured games which end up going free to play then dying, but it may not be so much a predatory situation as one of desperation.

Easy solution: just don't buy into an MMO until it's proven itself to be good.  Fool me once, shame on me...
