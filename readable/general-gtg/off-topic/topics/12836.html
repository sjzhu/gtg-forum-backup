I don't think you can actually convert a non-gamer into a gamer. The best you can hope for is a person willing to play with you because they know you enjoy it and they want to spend time with you doing stuff you enjoy. They might even actually like some games, but they won't become a "real" gamer, and they'd almost always prefer that you guys did something else together. (And that's perfectly fine.)

On the other hand, some people are pre-gamers. These are only distinguishable from non-gamers when you actually show then how fun games are and get an enthusiastic oh-games-where-have-you-been-all-my-life response. You usually have to find the right game to hook them, but once you do there's no stopping them - they'll ask you to play, buy games of their own, play with other people. Gamer achievement unlocked.

In my experience there are far more non-gamers than pre-gamers out there.

And for all my joking about non-gaming partners, I would feel totally uncomfortable playing with someone who wasn't really into it. Not having fun with games? Fine, let's do something else. My need to play games is not stronger than my desire for my friends/family/significant other to have a good time, but I see a lot of people on BGG who seem to think that it's vitally important to convert their loved ones to their hobby, thinking that if only they find the right gateway game they can work their way up to Twilight Imperium from there. *head shake* Having seperate hobbies as well as shared ones is perfectly fine and healthy.

So yeah. Introduce people to games if they show an interest. Stop if they aren't having a good time. Don't keep asking them to play if they aren't keen on gaming. Don't coerce them. Understand that your loved one doesn't have to be involved in everything you do - they don't have any kind of duty to enable your hobby just because you want them to like it too. Find other people who like the same games as you and share your hobby with them rather than trying to "convert" someone. If your loved one does enjoy some games, don't assume you can "progress" to other (perhaps heavier) games - play the games they like until they express an interest in trying something new, and don't be surprised if they remain forever content with their original favourites. Respect that.
