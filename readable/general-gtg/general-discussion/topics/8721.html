I was working on a system to grade the heroes, but it got really complex.  Here's a rough take on it (not personal preference, but my opinion of how overall good a hero is)

Tier One:  the Leaders

Legacy:  He can shut down the villains deck, damage, heal, boost.  He can deal really good one-shot damage when needed.

TLT (the only promo I'm including):  Tachyon is really strong, Team Leader takes 90% of her strength and adds possibly the best support power in the game, as a base power.

Omnitron-X:  what can't he do?  He is involved in so many game breaking combos it is unreal.  When set up he can draw, play cards, deal damage and heal allies and then skip his turn.

Wraith:  jack of all trades, master of. . . all?  Her only downside is she needs equipment, she counters that with the single best pulling equipment card in the game and lots of copies of her goodies.

Tier Two:  The Juggernauts

Haka:  he augments a dizzying array of damage with the Haka line of hand manipulation + a bonus cards. (seriously draw 2 discard 1 is a great card in most decks, he gets a bonus added in as well)  Savage Mana and Ground Pound are both epic additions that turn a really bruising dealer into a juggernaut of enemy destruction.

Tempest:  Massive one-shot damage, great array of powers, damage bonuses and start of turn damage, ongoing and environment destruction, he is an engine of win.  His only downside is he can be really frail sometimes.  Take away the +1 damage taken on his one card and he's tier one.

The Scholar:  Amazing healing, solid damage, somewhat hard to maintain.  DDA is one of the top cards in the game, and scholar burns his deck.  his deck mechanics are top notch, maybe only outpaced by the next entry on this list.

Chrono Ranger:  Superior deck mechanics, he can get knocked down and be back on his feet in a minute, and if you don't knock him down he will stay on the big bad guys tail gunning him down in record time.

Tier Three:  Winning.

Tachyon:  the only thing holding Tachyon back is how easily she runs out of cards when she is really rolling if she can't find trust fund.  She needs help to dominate the field, but with that help she crushes everyone.  Without it she still drastically improves your chance of winning.

Fanatic:  No one burns down a villain like the angel of vengeance.  Her damage control is amazing, but requires set up and maintenance, her damage output can b amazing, or just ordinary if things don't go your way.  Aegis is the best heal in the game, if it is up when you would die, and End of days can be game breaking in the right setup.  Like Tachyon sh'es a good fit in any situation, evil crushing demi-goddess in the right situation.

The Visionary:  that help some heroes need to make it from alright to awesome, or really good to gods of battle?  That help is visionary.  her base power can help anyone's hand, and TtE can break the limitations of a number of heroes.  She can also just shut down a villain or environment, and has a ton of shenanigans to pull with WtM.  Her fallback of Cocoon is good, but finding ways to survive without it is even better.

The Argent Adept:  Where the Visionary is about raising the floor of an ally, Adept blows off the ceiling for his team.  He can help you draw a card if he is set up right, but his real strength is doing a ton of things at once.  Early on he's good, when the team is set up he's the conductor bringing the whole team to new heights of success.

Tier Four:  The Good

Ra:  lots of damage, lots of it.  Some really cool side effects, but mostly fire and lots of it.  Need damage, Ra's your boy.

Absolute Zero:  one of those heroes you build you team around, because otherwise he might not be that much help.  Set up in the right team he's dominant, but his set up takes a bit and he may need help to shine, but dang, he's worth it.

Nightmist:  She doesn't need help like AZ, she isn't one sided like Ra, but she can backfire pretty badly.  Set up and rolling she is an unkillable destroyer, but minus a few key cards and she's just killing herself.  Depending on your experience with her I could see moving her up.

Unity:  She fills a very cool niche.  She needs way less support than people tend to think, and Hasty Augmentation is amazing.  Stealth Bot, Cryo Bot, Unity is really good.  

Tier Five:  The best for last.

Mr. Fixer:  Many teams just don't need him, but many benefit a lot from him.  know your enemy, know your allies, and you will be victorious.

Expatriette:  she ranks down here because every once in a while you get that game where your deck hates you.  Her damage is consistant and versatile.  Her ammo is really a good mechanic, and has a lot of uses.

Bunker:  maybe the most set up intensive hero.  He has many weapons, but only a few great ways to use them.  His best set ups still limit him compared to other heroes, but he can do many things, and with the right support and a little luck, he can dominate.

Real quick addition here:

I couldn't help but notice when I looked back over the list that I still managed to group them in like/dislike categories.

I do not enjoy playing tier 1 heroes, they are just too good without enough work required.

I don't really enjoy tier 2 either, although if I need a really powerful hero to play I go Tempest.

Tier 3 are my go to destroyers, when I want to be the team MVP I go for one of tier 3's heroes.

Tier 4 I don't go for either, they are good, but not my preference.

Tier 5 are my favorites, I love Fixer and Expat, they contribute so much without dominating, they are really strong at rounding out a team and filling in gaps.  Bunker is relly fun when you have some one playing a support that works well with him, or when I want a bit more adversity.

My rankings (Notes:  I am not including promos.  I have not had a chance to play the Vengeance characters.  About 2/3 of my game are with family/friends/gaming group and 1/3 solo, this is useful to take some of the descriptions below into the proper context.  Even the heroes on the bottom are still enjoyable to me, just not as much as the ones towards the top).

 

Tier 1:

Fanatic

Mister Fixer

Expatriette

These are my favorites.  I probably play Fanatic in about 1/3 of my games.  I just love that she has some of the most gamechanging cards out there in Wrathful Retribution, Divine Focus, and End of Days.  There's just something fun about finding just the right time to unleash them.  Mister Fixer is enjoyable because I never feel like he has a certain set strategy in order to play him.  When I go into a game with Mister Fixer, I don't find myself always knowing what the perfect combo is as his role varies from game to game and I like that a lot.  Expatriette is just cool.  It is fun to just unload ammo charged shots on the opposition.  Unfortunately I hardly ever get to play her except in solo games as my girlfriend and youngest cousin, two of my most common playing partners, will always take her.

Tier 2:

Argent Adept

Ra

Tempest

Being that I am not a fan of support characters, it is really a surprise that I enjoy Argent Adept this much.  I just think the different combos he can get by linking his instruments, melodies, harmonies, and rhythyms together is just really enjoyable.  I enjoy heavy offensive sluggers naturally hence my predisposition to both Ra and Tempest.  Ra when all I want to do is smash or Tempest if I feel like being more thinky.

Tier 3

Nightmist

Unity

Omnitron-X

These are my characters where some games I absolutely love with them, but others they are closer to duds.  I love the randomness with Nightmist although for some reason I just don't feel like I play here correctly.  Unity's robots are so fun, but there will be the occasional game where you just can't get anything going.  And Omnitron-X has my favorite base power in the game.  The tension right before you timeshift that card sometimes makes for a really cool experience.

Tier 4

Tachyon

Chrono Ranger

The Wraith

These are characters who I did not initially enjoy as much, but I am warming up to.  They are popular with my group so I haven't gotten a chance to perfect them yet.  I find myself eager to play them at present to see if I can develop their sweet spot.  To me, Tachyon and The Wraith strike a balance between attack and support with Chrono Ranger being a more offensive oriented hero who I am still getting used to.

Tier 5

Legacy

The Visionary

The Scholar

I consider these 3 the strongest heroes in the game.  That is a large part of the reason that I don't enjoy them as much as I have found certain situations where these 3 have almost broken the game.  Also, they are all three very much support characters and I would rather be an attack or a hybrid character.  Nonetheless still enjoyable for the occasional ride if my group picks all offensive characters.

Tier 6

Haka

Absolute Zero

Bunker

Least favorite of a great bunch.  Haka is actually moving up a bit, but the problem that I have with all three of these as they seem like you really have to play to a certain setup each game to be effective.  When you do, look out!  But I don't feel as their is quite as much choice in playing these three as the others so that's why they stay towards the bottom.

I consider Visionary, Legacy, Chrono-Ranger, Omnitronks, and Nightmist to be the "tier 1" heroes who nearly always render their teams close to unbeatable (of these, the three time-travelers are the ones I actually enjoy playing most; Nightmist is fun but a little too brain-melting and painful, and Legacy just bores me).  Scholar and Unity are close behind, but a bit too luck-dependent, as is the Argent Adept and he's more underwhelming besides.  Tempest is incredibly strong, a definite 4 on his own merits (though, depressingly, he often seems to be more valuable to the party dead than alive - his incap power has nearly won two otherwise-impossible games in two different ways).  Tachyon is strong in a simple way; I'd put her in tier 3 with Argent, and also Bunker.  Expatriette and Haka are effective but a little dull, and Ra is even more of both of those things; Wraith has a lot of options in theory, but in practice spends far too much time just shooting things.  I'd call all of these tier 2.  I am uniquely terrible with Fanatic and Zero, as they're just very good at killing themselves, and I never seem to calculate their safety margins right.  I would call them tier 1 along with Mister Fixer, who is just generally weaksauce in my opinion, largely due to poor luck.  Sure he's great if you can spend turns 1-3 setting up Jack Handle and Driving Mantis and Harmony, but I don't think I've ever once seen it happen, and Salvage Yard is good, but only incredible if you play the all-tech party.  I know it's possible for Fanatic to be effective, I've seen it happen, but I can't generally pull it off, so I call her tier 1 for me, while Fixer is tier 1 for anybody IMO, based on my experiences with him - I just don't see him being effective in most of the toughest fights, since his bailiwick is too limited, and his ability to even fulfill often goes awry.

So by my assessment, that's five tier 1 heroes, four in tier 3, and three each in the other three tiers.  But that's just based on my experiences to date, having played them all 13 or more times.

The only hero promos I've tried are Rook City Wraith, Young Legacy, Engine of War Bunker, Dark Watch Nightmist, Dark Watch Mr. Fixer, and very briefly Dark Watch Expatriette.  Of these, only Wraith and Fixer were more than one game, and Bunker was an extremely short game in which I only used the promo's power once, then changed my mind about using it (it was a forum game, so turns can be retroactively redone).

My assessments of these:

Young Legacy:  So much more fun than regular Legacy.  I've only tried one game, but this was enough to suspect that I'd probably find her to be a full tier more enjoyable overall than her original counterpart.  Using Galvanize always feels like an act of utter passivity to me, but Atomic Glare is incredible, especially with the bonus from Surge of Strength.  Legacy's deck still has a lot of cards that don't impress me (I groan every time I draw Danger Sense, and way too many duplicate Limiteds show up with no way to discard them), but that promo is definitely a massive step in the right direction.

RC Wraith:  I can't really say that she's better per se, as Stealth is probably more useful than Investigation in terms of what it contributes to a game.  (This is probably why Spiff houseruled his own version of RC Wraith; the ruling is perhaps too powerful, but without it, her utility is questionable at best).  But I really like the idea of her, just because her effectiveness varies wildly depending on the Environment you play in, and the result can really contribute to the underlying story of the game.  (EG, you're in Pike Industrial; if you Investigate, you might find Rats which could come into play with Experimental Mutagen, but then Investigating again can find more Rats that can actually destroy the Mutagen by being trashed from the deck.)  It might be better if her power worked like Dark Visionary's Turmoil, only restricted to the enviro deck; as-is, it's a Timeshift so limited, and the big problem with Timeshift has always been that it never stops the deck from playing a nasty card…if Dawn has an Aurora on top of her deck, you might Timeshift past it, but without having seen the deck, you don't know that Aurora isn't the second card therein, which you just Timeshifted into.  RC Wraith has the same problem, and so she isn't necessarily "good", but I enjoy her just because she interects directly with the Environment in a way most heroes don't.

Bunker EOW:  My experience with him was brief, but overall I'd call it a toss-up.  I almost never use Initialize, but on occasion it can be highly desireable, and you can feel pretty stupid not having it when you need it, like when you desperately need to write someone a note and you can't find a pencil to save your life.  The Ongoing destruction of EOW is a potential game-saver, but not something you'll use regularly, and you have to have one of a small subset of your deck's cards to get use out of it, so it's definitely not going to come up every turn.  I'd say overall, this one is a slight edge in favor of regular Bunker, but it definitely doesn't change his Tier…whether or not Locomotion is useful, it's certainly not especially fun.  (Drawing a card is at least always a tiny moment of discovery, IMO.)

DW Nightmist:  I only played her once, and do not feel that this was enough in her case to really get a feel for the change.  Being able to set up my spells proved somewhat handy, but remembering what cards I'd stacked last turn was taxing to my poor memory, and the inability to fill my hand with Investigation was a critical loss.  More research is definitely needed; I feel like I'd enjoy her the most with a large party, where she might hide in Mist Form and let the other heroes die, then take advantage of Incap abilities to use Attunement before playing a spell, thereby avoiding memory issues.  The altered playstyle is definitely interesting; whether it's enjoyable remains to be seen.

DW Fixer:  Overall, I'd call this a slight reduction in enjoyment compared to regular Fixer, not that he's one of my favorites in the first place.  The Bloody Knuckles combo is just cheap, the Salvage Yard synergy is mildly appreciated but would matter more if you didn't have just 2 of them, and otherwise, burning your cards in play as fast as you can lay them down is an uncomfortable prospect.  I have a strong personal dislike for taking turns in which I play a card but don't use a power (or vice versa), and so DW Fixer is specifically somewhat uncomfortable to me specifically.  Still, 3 damage as a Power is a lot, and he can stack a ton of bonuses on it…Jack Handle in particular is amazing with bitter Strike.  He needs a lot of help to really shine, even more than this is true of regular Fixer, and since I didn't really love MF in the first place, this one is kind of a step backwards to me.  But it has its charms, and I'm far from decided on the matter after only two games.

DW Expat:  I have not yet used Aim, but I have already found myself wishing I had Load instead, even though past experience has taught me that Load is often not useful.  However I have found the +1 HP to be of critical usefulness, since it puts you into a tie with Fanatic and Ra on the first turn, and thus allows Miss Flak Jacket to do a little more first-turn tanking.  Generally I would say that Load is more fun, but Aim is quite probably more useful…I've often said that every other hero wants Load more than Expatriette does, since her deck is among the most static of the bunch (I've literally seen her turn into a robot in one game, where her player had to leave after three turns, but we never missed him because she was playing herself by then).  Aim doesn't make that big of a difference, but since Load is only giving you more of what you already had (play phases), while Aim can actually help you punch through DR more effectively on certain occasions, it can definitely be useful, and you're not sacrificing too much to gain the option of it.  Thusly, I'd call it a slight advantage, but certainly not one that changes her ranking.

What I like most about the promos is that they offer a way to make up for some of the shortcomings certain heroes have, thus changing their playstyle.

I've heard some people don't like Elemental Wrath AZ, but I think that's because they expect to play him exactly like original AZ.  But, the strategy involved with EWAZ is much different; instead of a high risk/reward mechanic that has a reliable heal, EWAZ is less risky when it comes to damage but less tanky due to lower health and a base power that can't heal him.
