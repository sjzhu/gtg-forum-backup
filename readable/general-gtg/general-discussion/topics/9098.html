A lot of times I see Nightmist played where she damages herself to build up cards, constantly using her base power, then redirects the higher damages with those cards, and recovery at some point to pump her full of health again.  This works because it is so easy to buid up and discard cards and then use those discarded cards to heal.

 

The new Nightmist is going to be less hand building and discarding, and more knowing what cards you have coming.

I think Starshield will outpace Master of Magic and Amulet as the survival card, since you can put the higher value card on top and then draw->discard it.  Mostly I'd be setting up the high value cards for being revealed, more than drawing and getting played, but that all depends on your hand.  

Mistform takes on a very different role in this deck.  Typically reserved for a last act of desperation, to heal up and build a huge hand so you can solo the villain instead of losing, now that 3 card draw is something you can't replicate every round.  It stands to get used more like Recharge mode, give a round or two to get cards in hand, then pop out and keep going.

Also being able to set up for Enlighten is very nice.

I see the big turns being when you have Heedless Lash in your hand and reveal something you want to play next round, and set it up to chain with Lash.

Things like Heedless Lash playing Heedless Lash playing Oblivion won't be as much luck as a combination of luck and planning.

Health vs. cards is a huge economic balance with Nightmist, and this form really changes the nature of that balance, without a set exchange rate there will be different choices on when to redirect.

Original Nightmist with a 2[then modified] hp = 2 cards exchange each turn allowed you with Amulet to simply take 2 damage instead of the highest amount of damage you would have been dealt that round, and if no damage was higher than what your power would deal you you could redirect it and just swap cards, or bank a future damage at the current number. (investing in Obsidian Fields!) Even Mistbound with its discard had a set value of 2hp(modified of course for damage changes) a card play and power use.  {This is ignoring the competition of Starshield Necklace for your cards, just to keep it simple and on the point I'm trying to make}

Now your cards are more scarce but the amount of damage your spells deal is controllable.  The whole buy low, redirect high aspect is gone.  Now you need to conserve cards more, Like Foote said, Master of Magic will more often be preferred to Amulet, because you can't purchase that redirection with your power anymore, you have to use Amulet sparingly, or spend a lot of time mistformed and setting up for Enlighten's if you want to redirect very often, and when you can set up your spell damage, I expect more often than not your spells will outpace other damage options.  (Villains like IL and his huge damage will change that, as you could do more damage by focusing on hand size and redirecting his own damage back to him)

Your survival is a bit trickier, since the damage futures market no longer exists your power's ability to give you a high value to discard for Starshield likely becomes your best survival option, along with dropping into Mistform for a turn or three.

I'm very interested in playing the new Nightmist, and the more I think of the economic changes the more I think I might like the deck with the new promo, as the strategy is very different, and her control is more on her offense than on her defense, which I like for her build.

I had a quick moment to try DW Fixer in a quick game at my buddies place.

His playstyle become immediatly obvious, and it felt pretty wild at times. A huge departure from the original.

1) Bitter Strike, way more offten than not, will be targeting your own cards for destruction. 

2) 1st turn play a Tool or Style. Wait on power. Draw back to 4. Now, on your 2nd turn, here is where the choices start coming. Play a tool or style, whichever you didnt play first turn. Then, depending on what you have more of in your hand, destroy that card with Bitter Strike. In other words, if you have 2 tools and a style in your hand, destroy whatever tool you have. This way, your third turn will consist of replacing the tool you just destroyed. Same with Styles. Basically you start power cycling your weapons and styles in order to maximize the number of Strikes you can pull off.

This feels MUCH different than original Fixer. BMFixer will not find and keep the perfect setup of Tool/Style for a particular fight then chill with a huge hand. You will be running on a very low hand size and subject to the wims of the draw. Effects that force you to discard are particularly painful.

3) There will times you can't Strike, or dont want to. Either when you A) have no more Styles/Tools to easily cycle or B) want to move to a 3-card setup instead of the average 2 while power cycling. 

A) No Tools/Styles in hand. This is a rough spot, but there are options. You can Zen out and just draw 2, giving yourself some breathing room with hand size. You can also throw down Bloody Knuckles, as this is always a safe play. If you have a Tool/Style/Both in play, throwing down Charge and drawing without striking is a fair option in hopes of drawing something better for next turn.

B) Seting up to 3 cards is tough and will require the hand to do so. This is where you keep 2 cards in play for a few rounds (idealy) and start sacking the 3rd card. Generally this will be a Tool/Style setup where you then throw down Bloody Knuckles, Tool Box, or Harmony to be destroyed. The downside is having to not Strike for at least 2 turns.

4) Overdrive. Use this in emergencies only, or to setup one massive turn. Trying to set back up from scratch takes time, and without Driving Mantis as Fixers go-to crutch anymore, time is valuable. Since discarding effects are particularly nasty here, Overdrive makes fantastic discard fodder as well.

5) Don't be afraid of power cycling! If you find yourself in a jam and do not have the hand size or composition to keep cycling, Salvage Yard is your saving grace. And if you Bitter Strike'd any of your friends equipment cards, I'm sure they wouldn't mind having those back.

 

Anyone who really disliked the original Fixer for being too laid back and Zen will find this promo very appealing. 

As good as BMFixer is, original Fixer holds some notable advantages. The biggest one being the ability to lean on Driving Mantis as a crutch for extra damage and defense. Being able to find, and subsequently keep, a Tool/Style/Harmony combo thats perfect for the current situation is a luxury not afforded to BMFixer, and you will find yourself missing that element from time to time.

I've had several games with Dark Watch members, both as a team and individually.

Together, they synthesize very well.  DW Setback can help Nightmist with her self-harm issues.  Fixer eats unwanted things.  Expat works best as an "every other turn" character, but is quite capable if you get the exact gun you want in your opening hand.  The assault rifle and SMG become much nicer against armored targets and for crowd control.

Individually:

Expat's playstyle has always been dictated by the cards you get, and DW is no exception.  I consider the tradeoff worth it, as you can aim and hope to get a gun (should you not have one) for your next turn.  I find that she works best in smaller games (much like Bunker), where the H value tends to be less, and the games tend to be longer.  However, she absolutely shines in games with large numbers of targets, and the increased damage afforded by Aim can really shine when using the SMG (an aimed SMG and cryo rounds are quite lovely against a horde).

Mr Fixer hits like a juggernaut.  its not uncommon to see 7-8 point hits.  You can eat your own styles/tools as needed, but if you put Fixer at the front of theTurn order, he's a prime cantidate for all of the cards that would otherwise go away on their own.  There are still times that you might want a specific tool, but being able to hit things for 5 with a bloody knuckles (and no negative repercussions) seems like a very good balance.

Nightmist will take some getting used to.  My regular groups tend to go straight for thE stabulet, and base their entire playstyle around redirecting her damage and building a huge hand.  DW Nightmist is much more about control, and makes her book relic one of the ones that you shoudl put out ASAP.  always having a spell and taking the time to order the cards for best effect will really pay off.  

DW Setback looks badass. Also, I really like the way his name looks (and the tag line)
