One of the coolest things about the merger with Dice Hate Me is that we more than double our "back catalog" of games that are prepared, or nearly prepared, for publication. In fact, because of overlapping efficiencies, we are going to be able to release games much more efficiently than we would have if we had stayed two separate companies. For example, Chris Kirkman no longer has to deal with annoying logistical stuff (that's what I'm for), and can focus on developing and polishing games that he's had signed for a while now. We are nowhere near the point of being able to support the sort of release schedule maintained by big boardgame publishers like Fantasy Flight, but for the first time we are actually able to run multiple projects in the same phase simultaneously (so long as we do so very carefully).

For those of you keeping track at home, here is the status of current projects, as well as their originating "team":

1) Sentinel Tactics: Minions & Maps (GTG) - At the printer, on track for July delivery (on time from KS).

2) Compounded reprint + Geiger expansion (DHMG) - At the printer, on track for July delivery (several months late from KS).

3) Bottom of the Ninth (DHMG) - In post-production; at the printer within the month.

4) Villains of the Multiverse (GTG) - In playtesting; very roughly 50% complete.

5) Secret Wednesday KS (DHMG & GTG) - In playtesting; 90% complete. (A fun, easy trial run of a joint GTG+DHMG project!)

6) Spirit Island (GTG) - In playtesting; on track for KS in July.

7) Monster Truck Mayhem (DHMG) - In playtesting; on track for KS this summer.

8) New Bedford (DHMG) - In post-production; on track for KS re-launch this fall.

9) Sentinel Tactics: Broken City & For Profit (GTG) - In pre-production; on track for release this year.

I think the problem is the "gaming review community" wants kickstarter to be either just fun silly stuff or grow up and make really great games all the time. 

To me Kickstarter has 2 purposes outside of letting brand new entrants a place to go before they have capital to make it themselves.

1.  The "let's throw this at the wall and see if it sticks" approach, which is what we saw from story wars and cosmic conflict, a product that they liked that they didn't know if other people would like enough to return on the investment.

2.  The access/publicity approach, which is where the all Kickstarter game companies live, you pay a certain percentage to access a specific market, I think the "joke" games fit that side.  The criticism I've read mostly says that people will back things that are jokes just because, and not really evaluate the games merits, they don't want the market supporting games like this.

I can see why it bothers them, because as review blogs they exist as part of a network designed to inform people about games before they buy them so that people buy games that they really want and make the market support better games.

The "Buy this today because it looks fun and is clever" approach is flying right in the face of that, especially when it is such a short run that it starts and ends before they can talk about whether it is a good game or not.

They criticize the unmade game bundle that is out there now for mostly the same reason, you have to buy the game before anyone can really evaluate the games.

I don't mind impulse buys.  I've made some that flopped and others that were awesome.  

Two clever and fun games with titles that make them a gag game is fine.  They don't have to be great games, they aren't here to be great games, they are made to be good games that are fun and you play them because the joke is funny.

For people that take gaming and the gaming market seriously that is going to rub them the wrong way.
