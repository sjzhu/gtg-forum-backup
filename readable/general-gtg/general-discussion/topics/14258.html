Playtesting is great, and we can use your help, more and more games are coming out, and more playtesters means more chances to catch things and more brains to pick on how to make things better.

But there are some negatives:

1.  This is a big one I didn't expect:  Opening a new box will not feel the same.  After testing expansions and playing them unfinished there isn't the same surprise and joy at openning the finished box, it is nice to get the art, but you already know the cards.

2.  It isn't just playing stuff early.  There are a lot of things that just don't work when we get stuff, that's what we are here for, to fix it and make it sparkly and awesome for the rest of you.

3.  We don't see art until it is done.  Yes we get to see the art first, but only when we are done playtesting.

4.  It isn't cheap.  I use the cheapest card stock (after trying paper and never wanting to do that again), write the names on the back so I don't have to double sided print, cross out and scribble on cards when they are updated so I don't print the game 3-5 times and I still burn a cartridge every expansion.

5.  Your group might not be into it at all.  I had friends that were excited when I became a playtester, they only playtested a few times before having enough.  The games weren't always balanced or enen fun, some villains start out unbeatable, others so easy that they are boring.  Feedback like "Why would anyone ever want to play this deck" or "wow, that was boring" isn't the most useful either.

6.  When you find really cool stuff and interactions in the deck, you can only tell other playtesters.

7.  Repetition can burn you out.  I loved Sky-Scraper, put in a ton of work on her because I wanted to make sure she was a great deck, and we succeeded (IMO).  When I got the Wrath box and wanted to play the game I played her and found I was burned out on her.  I had no desire to play as Sky-Scraper, and in fact have only played her since when she comes up random.  This also happened with a few of the Villains decks that I was really working on.

Of course these are my experiences, and I get a bit obsessive about things.

 

I also must add that I find the positives outweigh the negatives:

1.  Seeing a deck go from "Meh." to amazing is a great trip.

2.  The first time your suggestion makes it into the box, and a card is played and the effect is your idea, that feels great.

3.  The community is fun.  We do, every once in a while, have a 10 page thread arguing over a promo power, where things get a bit heated, but we've only had to have it broken up once.  Most of the time we argue much more civilly.  Sometimes we even like each other's ideas and get along wonderfully.

4.  You do get to see stuff early.  Which is really cool, even if there isn't art and some of the cards don't make sense.

5.  You get your name on the rule book.  Which is fun to show people.

6.  You get to say things like, "You think that's hard, you should have seen the first draft of Progeny!"

7.  You can actually go hunt down the Chrono-Ranger Promo thread and see us argue over the most minute wordings.

 

Anyway, hope this helps anyone on the fence make up their mind.

It does vary wildly. A few of the factors, many of which vary based on who the designer is and what the game components are like:

  • How often are there updates? Obviously, more updates means you have to update your components more often.
  • Can the updates be done by hand or are they big enough that re-printing is necessary? Later updates, when you're just tweaking a couple words or numbers, tend to be easier to do by hand. Early on, especially for a new game (not SotM) where the rules aren't really ironed out yet, there tend to be more substantial updates.
  • What is your personal threshhold for having things look "nice"? Obviously, this is playtesting and we aren't using final designs. Some people are more content to use cards that have been scribbled on or just scraps of paper when it doesn't matter if one card is distinguishable from another (e.g. Tactics or SotM character cards).
  • How good is the designer about creating update-only packs? It's very helpful when you get a pdf of only the updated cards. This way, you can print 1-2 pages of only the new things rather than 7-8 pages and then have to throw away most of the things because they didn't change. You also spend less time cutting this way.

So, that probably doesn't answer your question exactly, but it's something to think about.

That's definitely a great overview of the positives and negatives of playtesting.  I work in the gaming industry which has led to many opportunities to playtest games and sit down with Beta versions of unreleased stuff.  At first, you couldn't imagine how excited I was to see some of my favorite games in their earliest stages and be able to get my hands on expansions and new material before anyone else.  But novelty wore thin pretty quickly for me and it's mostly because, as mentioned, the magic tends to disappear.

There's a big difference between playing a board/card game chock full of wonderful artwork and flavor text that crafts an immersive experience, and playing with cards printed on plain white paper, in a broken state.  And then be required to drill into the game to find the nitty gritty bugs and broken combinations that players could experience with the final product.  And in the end, you don't get that same rush when getting your brand new copy and opening it up when it's finally released.

Playtesting is for the dedicated and for those who love a game so much that they desire to be a part of the development process.  I think if you go into Playtesting thinking it's just going to be sitting around playing games all day, you'll be disappointed.  You need to be willing to put your time on the line and really dive into an incomplete experience, your satisfaction coming from the fact that you're helping the game become whole.  Playtesting is an awesome time and insanely important to the development of games; just be aware that some parts of it aren't as glamorous from the inside.
