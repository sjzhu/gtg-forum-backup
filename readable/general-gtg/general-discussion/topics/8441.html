So--a pretty simple question, and one that's probably been asked a few times when the game first came out, but not as much since Shattered Timelines has come out:

Who's your favorite character?  Who's the Hero that you find yourself picking more often than the others or that just suits your play style?

 

For me, It's Bunker.  I'm an unapologetic Iron Man fanboy, so Bunker fits that thematic niche for me.  I find that Bunker can alternate between pure DPS to tanking, all the while fueling either consistent damage (via Gatling Gun) or priming that OmniCannon for a big blast.

I'm also a huge fan of Iron/Dark Age comics, to the point where I wrote my own RPG to emulate Watchmen/The Dark Knight Returns and the like, which I Kickstarted last year.  I love the idea of the tortured hero facing down ethical challenges, which leads me straight towards Expatriette.  Plus, there's nothing more satisfying to me than pulling off a massive Unload, letting loose a whole arsenal's worth of bullets and eviscerating everything on the field.

But, when I'm in a bind and don't know who to pick?  I find myself gravitating more and more towards Fanatic.  She's just so versatile:  she can tank, she can deal damage, she can heal, she can destroy Ongoings and Environments...just an all-around great choice.

I started with Visionary and fairly quickly added Ra, to make sure I had some damage dealing I could do. First up, you've got to be really flexible.  You can't walk into a game with AA expecting "I'm going to buff damage for everyone" or "I'm going to facilitate card play/draw."  The one thing you can't control is what songs you have and, while you have a good deal of card draw, you can't guarantee having the precise one you want.  Much like Mister Fixer, you've got to go with the flow…

Second, your play order:  first up is always a song, either by itself or via Arcane Cadence.  With no songs in play, even your base power is shut down.  But, considering the number of songs in the deck and your ability to find them with Arcane Cadence, you'll likely have one very soon.  If your song is a Harmony or a Rhythm, your next best play is likely an instrument:  specifically the instrument that lets you play Perform and Accompany of whichever you've played.  That gives you two benefits to your party in one power phase.  If your song is a Melody, sling down a second song before fishing for instruments, to get maximum benefit.

Silver Shadow is almost always worth playing:  the only exception is that if you don't have a song with an Accompany text out and you don't need the additional card draw.  But, as long as you have an Accompany text to play with it, don't leave it in your hand.  

Once you have a basic "set" to play:  either Perform/Perform or Perform/Accompany, look at expansion.  The easiest way to expand, I've found is from a Harmony/Harmony or Rhythm/Rhythm into a Melody/Harmony or Melody/Rhythm.  Plus, Sarabande of Destruction is just phenomenal, so I tend to favor finding it.  That said, if you went Rhythm/Rhythm via Xu's Bell, it may benefit you more to go Rhythm/Harmony.  Don't forget, as well, that you can use the same card multiple times, once you've chained some powers together.  Double-dipping the same card can lead to some phenomenal results–and don't forget your base power!  An extra Perform text can be pretty spectacular.

Polyphoric Flare seems like a bad option sometimes, but unless you're really hurting on hp, you can easily restore those lost 2 hp and the power usage is almost always worth it.  It can be especially good early in a game, when your hp are relatively high and you've just got down your first instrument.  Again, don't be afraid to double-dip on cards.  

Now, I'm not the AA expert by any sense–one of my Friday night game group utterly adores him, and plays Adept almost exclusively (with a smattering of Chrono-Ranger, when he wants to deal damage).  But, with some additional games under my belt with AA, his complexity becomes an awesome puzzle to figure out!
