So I made it to the Greater Than Games booth at GenCon and tried a round of Sentinel Tactics. I didn't love it. It's very different from Sentinels in a lot of ways. The characters are limited, with a bunch of fixed stats and a very small deck; only a few cards can be in play at a time. I did a short writeup of the game for The Gameological Society, and took a picture of one of the temp character cards. It seemed to be a hex-based tactical warfare simulator without Sentinels' character flexibility and game balance. That said, we played a very basic 3-on-3 duel scenario, as opposed to the many scenarios the co-designer and playtest leader talked about. Those sound like potentially a lot more fun — like the ability to have multiple players take on one person playing a villain, or a co-op version with the players against an environmental hazard.

Did anyone else here get to try it at GenCon? I'm curious to hear other takes. I tested it out on Saturday when they were doing the battling-heroes scenario, but I was told that on Sunday, they were going to try out the heroes-vs.-villain scenario. I'm curious how that went. Looking forward to the Kickstarter.

While I didn't get to play, I did get to watch a demo session:   the heroes were fighting Baron Blade in some environment (I wasn't sure which).  Heroes I saw included Tachyon, Bunker, Ra, and Legacy and (I think) Absolute Zero.  The fellow running the demo was playing as Blade, moving around both the Baron himself and several minions.  The infamous TerraLunar Impulsion Beam was in play and he had a wonderful delivery as the heroes were apparently only scant rounds away from being smooshed by the moon.

I did enjoy how simple the character sheets are:  each Hero only has two "powers" out at a given time, though each turn you could swap out one of your powers with another from your hand, giving each Hero a full suite of abilities.  Every attack is a contested series of d6s, a la Risk, though each attack has certain numbers that act as automatic misses.  Generally speaking, you want to roll high, but avoid the "miss" numbers:  i.e. if your miss number is a 4, you're better off rolling a 3, because that's still a potential hit.  

I'm interested to see how the theme of SotM comes to the minis arena--the interview Christopher gave on The Spiel made it sound like both companies are really on the same page in trying to bring the comic book feel to the game, so that's a big plus for me.  I do wonder exactly "how far" they're going to go in terms of expansions--as in, are we going to see Fixer/Expatriette, Nightmist/Argent Adept, and Chrono-Ranger/Omnitron X?  Or, are we going to be sticking with the "core" Heroes?

Ooh, speaking of?!  Are we going to see a massive CosmiTron drop-ship?!  That'd be pretty awesome on its own!

I've been lucky enough to play it a couple times, now. My thoughts:

  • I think it's more thematic than the card game, as the heroes get to pick from everything - what powers/abilities you have isn't defined by a random card draw, but by what you choose. You get to pick from all your cards.
  • Some heroes work better against villains than against heroes. AZ, for example (who I've played in most of my games) does really poorly against heroes, but put him up against villains and he can really shine.
  • Each hero has a specific role, but is very versatile in how that role can be filled. They're doing a good job of fleshing that out.
  • The scenarios are interesting and entertaining, to me.
  • I think they've really captured the personalities of the heroes and villains.
  • Personally, I prefer all coop games, so having someone play the villain is actually a (very small) strike against it for me, but I still enjoy the game a great deal.
  • They're still balancing the game - that's why it's in playtesting status. :wink:

1v1 it is Omnitron-X and Legacy stomp RA, Ra stomps everyone else.  There are a few decent matchups, but the real fun is in 2v2 or 1v1v1, or in the F4 vs. Wardens fight, that was fun but long.

 

In multi hero games Argent dies fast, because you want him dead before he gets going.  Wraith and Smoke Bombs really helps him, unity and AZ stay alive.

There are so far two baic rule differences which change the game significantly:

If you use an Environment deck (I prefer this way) then any card that destroys ongoing also destroys equipment.  Play that the choice of what gets destroyed is the hero who played the destruction's choice.

If you don't use an environment deck then change environment to be Equipment.

I put my rules and a bit of what I've found out here: https://greaterthangames.com/forum/topic/pvp-training-room-fun-3859

Expatriette is a bit of a different story, I think.  Obviously, her schtick is ranged attacking, but diversity in the card game comes through her weapons.  Perhaps something like this:

  • Pride and Prejudice:  Moderate Range, target two separate foes with a bonus die for accuracy (since they are her signature weapons)
  • RPG Launcher:  Long distance, cannot be used on foes within X hexes.  Choose 1 hex: attacks all foes in or adjacent to that hex.
  • Shotgun: Very short range (maybe only adjacent); Heavy damage and pushes foe 1 hex away.
  • Hairtrigger Reflexes: any foe that enters Expatriette's LoS, she gets a free attack against. (This would make her into a stealthy sort of character, who ducks behind terrain only to blow you away each time you try to follow)
  • Napalm Launcher:  Leaves behind a trail of fire in X hexes for one turn, damaging any who enter it. (Good for battlefield control)
  • Taser:  Moderate distance with low damage.  If used to target a minion-type creature, that creature cannot act next round.
 

Just a few ideas off the top of my head.
