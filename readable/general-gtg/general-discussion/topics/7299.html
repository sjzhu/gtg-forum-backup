I have played precisely one game with the new Shattered Timeline cards: The Scholar, Omnitron-X, Unity, and Chrono Ranger vs. La Capitan in the Time Cataclysm.

The thinking was that a nice, easy difficulty-2 villian would be a good way to sample the new content. It was a good game, but it was brutal. At the end of the villian’s first turn there were 7 crew out. The Scholar saved everyone’s bacon time and again (1 or 2 iron skins + Alchemical Redirection FTW) and even came back from the dead. Specifically, the environment card which makes everything else indestructible came out while Alcehmical Redirection was up. This was handy because it meant all the card destruction La Capitan’s forces were doing was pointless, and it meant all the self-destructing hero cards (The Scholar’s elemental cards, Omnitron’s Time Leap, Unity destroying equipment to bring out bots) could be kept around. The Scholar got knocked down to -5 health and was only “not dead” because of that environment card. Omnitron-X used it’s ability to play a card off the top of The Scholar’s deck that healed him back up to 2hp (a really lucky break) before the environment card self-destructed, taking several no-hp crew with it.

The “Unexpected Shopping Spree” anomaly was a real boon. La Capitan had just flipped with 7 cards underneath her, healing her and her 6 remaining crew back up to full. However, the constant rain of… luggage?.. whittled away her buffer of stolen cards as well as the HP of her crew, allowing the heroes to claw their way out of the hole they were in. Alcehmical Redirection + Iron Skin soaked all the damage dealt to the heroes, making the luggage hurt only the villians.

Anyway, Unity never really got off the ground and wound up incapacitated (that samurai and that viking make for nasty hero-killers), though her incapacited abilities were a real boon. Omnitron-X was able to hide behind The Scholar long enough to get built up and do a ton each turn. Eventually Chrono Ranger was able to use his “Bring 'em in Alive” bounty to put that darn pirate ship on the bottom of the villian deck, and then the team was able to slowly beat back the crew, then corner La Capitan herself. In the end, La Capitan had 3 bounties on her, so Chrono Ranger was doing 15+ damage a turn. She was finished off by The Scholar dropping all his elemental forms and using his “discard hand, blow stuff up” card to knock her down to 0 hp precisely.

It was a really good game and we won despite one incapacitation and two heroes getting down to 1hp (or less, in the case of The Scholar) at least twice. La Capitan’s very strong opening followed by a fair amount of sub-optimal play (we were still figuring out the new heroes) made it surprisingly tense. Good times. And I quite like all the new heroes, though Unity (who I realize is not new to Shattered Timelines, though she is new to my gaming group) will take some figuring out… time to read Flamethrower49’s guide to her again. And The Scholar was easily the table favorite and team savior, though both Omnitron-X and Chono Ranger had their break-out moments.

By the way, I’ve recognized most of the events being referenced in the Time Cataclysm deck (both from other cards as well as the comic) but I don’t get where the Shopping Spree card is coming from. Anyone care to enlighten me on that?

Nemesis damane only applies via the actual villain, so when the Dreamer flips and starts doing damage at the end of her turn, then she'd deal Nemesis damage to the Visionary. The projections deal damage themselves - it doesn't say that the Dreamer deals the damage, so I figure no Nemesis bonus applies.

We've played quite a few games now, and have fought every new villain and played in every new environment. We've also used every new hero multiple times. So…let me see, what can I say about them…

Chrono Ranger - Probably my favourite of the new heroes. He's so cool! His bounties are a really interesting mechanic and you can do so much stuff with them - I made great use of the "The Whole Gang" one to take out the Dreamer's projections when we fought her, for example. Him with damage bonuses (from Legacy, for example) make for some pretty savage damage when using the Compound Bow versus a target who's got both the "Ultimate Target" and erm…the other bounty where they take +1 damage from everything. In fact, even without damage buffs that bow and those two bounties make for nice hits :).

Omnitron-X - Good fun. Not so much if you can't get built up (eg if someone keeps breaking your stuff) but very cool if you can't draw/play cards since he gets to do so much in his Start phase once you've got a few Components out. But yeah, quite good 'cause of all the stuff you can do. The fact that Components can stack (ie they're not Limited) is pretty epic as well :D.

Scholar - Again, very cool. The whole "mass healing" thing is groovy so he stays alive for ages. Cards like Grace Under Fire and other mass-hitty stuff is cool as well. He's pretty good 'casue there's loads of stuff you can do, and loads of methods for doing it.

Dreamer - Interesting mechanic. We've only fought her once so far but it was quite good fun. As mentioned under Chrono Ranger, using the "Whole Gang" bounty really helped to demolish the projections two at a time :). We fought her in the Final Wasteland and the few times she was a target we just redirected the damage to someone (fortunately it was only ever one or two points and didn't happen very often). She didn't get hit once the entire fight and we didn't have major problems, really.

La Capitan - Stacked a couple of bounties on her (yes, I was Chrono Ranger again). The crew got wiped out pretty quickly after coming out. Let's see... The Dreamer must not be damaged, Visionary has cards that protect her from damage... Dreamer redirects environment damage, Visionary reduces environment damage. Dreamer plays with her trashed cards, Visionary plays with decks a lot.

No , really, the "rule" that nemesises have similar decks is a figment of imagination. Blade and Legacy are not very similar in play style. "Dawn has lot of minions, Expat lot of guns" is more a joke than anything else. "One is a woman with an army, the other is a One Woman Army" feels better :) . Argent Adept and Akash'bhuta have nothing in common.

Some pairing are really close - Like Omnitron and Omnitron-X, they even share the "component" keyword, for sometime strange effects (like Cosmic Omnitron flipping). Or Chrono/Rat, who are in the habit of putting cards of their deck on opponent targets.

Some pairing have "special" cards that feel like the center of their "good at fighting each other" combo, like Tachyon playing her sonic assault card against the Matriarch. Or Ra's immunity to fire versus the many fire cards in the Ennead's deck. Or Tempest "damage everything" vs Voss's "have many minions".

Some are mostly thematic, like AA/A'B, or the Dreamer/Visionary.  Or Haka/Ambuscade.

Or take Nighmist/Gloomweaver : one is "about" damage redirection, the other about making zombies and cursing people... They both use relics. The "central mechanism" of the magic value of cards is not even alluded to by Gloomy.

The "decks look alike" theory doesn't hold, in my opinion. It is, I think, more about having decks that can "match" on a tactical level, and give an advantage in some circumstances - like Tachyon playing "You can't deal damage and all your birds are dead !" and "No dear, because my cohorts reduce your damage to nothing!". Or Wraith controling Spite's Deck for rapid drug discovery or victim protection, among other little thing these two can do to each other. But it is, always, about theme and background more than mechanisms.

For Visionary, playing her Cocoon and trying to tank most damage seems the best thing to do, with controlling the decks for faster/easier projection cycling. Or taking advantage of her environment damage reduction power to help prtect the child. Or redirect Env/Villain damage for faster resolution.

I think it is how the Nemesis relationship informs the deck construction : the theme is first, then you build decks not to be similar, but in a way that give each opponent both weakness and tactical opportunities when fighting each other. Like Haka savage manaing the cloaking device, while sonic mines prevent a rampage. Or Legacy going all fisticuffs against Blade because this nemesis bonus is handy against damage reduction - and Blade doing the same. Or Tempest putting back spaceships in the deck while hit by "lowest HP" damage.

More a "this combo will work very well for these two" than "their decks have to reflect each other".
