Wendell:

(And arenson9, btw, how would you rephrase 'online spanish store' to make it sound more natural for a native English speaker? … ) 

I think this is less of an issue about native vs. non-native speakers and more of an issue of ambiguity of meaning.

Spanish can refer either to the Spanish language or to the country of Spain. So what does it mean for a store to be Spanish? Are we saying that the store is in Spain? That it serves a Spanish market? That it sells things that are Spanish? That it uses the Spanish language?

Since the phrase 'online Spanish store' does not have a comma between the two adjectives, it puts more emphasis on the 'Spanish store' than on the 'online store', connotating that the 'Spanish store' is more inseperable than the online store. This serves to emphasize the ambiguity as to what is meant by a 'Spanish store'.

Using a comma (online, Spanish store) or reversing the order (Spanish online store) would help deemphasize the ambiguity of what a 'Spanish store' is, but the ambiguity won't go away unless more words are used to specify what is meant.
