This is another good point; just because you don't personally care for that character/theme doesn't mean your friends will agree.  In fact, that could be the difference between getting someone into the game or not; being able to offer them something that suits their interests.

For instance, I have a friend who plays a lot of Arkham Horror, so for his second game I told him to take Nightmist (no way I was letting him play her on his first game) and we did a match against Gloomweaver in the Realm of Discord.  I explained that the hero and villain were both designed by Richard Launius who was the designer of Arkham Horror and he was enthralled.  He had a blast finding all the references hidden within the cards and flavor text and I like to think because Sentinels gave him the freedom to choose that experience, he became as big of a fan as he is.

So I guess, good arguments overall:

  • You spend less money on shipping while getting more value if you get the full sets vs picking and choosing

  • Don't knock it til you try it; you don't know how interested you are in a hero/villain/environment until you actually play it

  • What you don't like, your friends might like; giving them more options helps get more people into the game

His namesake, Geoff Voss, is also a really nice guy.  I had the pleasure of playing a few games with him over at KantCon last year.

Sadly I cannot recommend The Chairman or Miss Information for a starting game.  The Chairman is one of the most difficult villains in the game and is very swingy depending upon which Underbosses he pulls in what order, and your work can be completely undermined by a Prison Break.  Miss Information requires an incredible amount of patience to even deal damage to her in a three person game, let alone a five person game.  Had you gotten Infernal Relics I would be recommending Akash'Bhuta, Apostate, Ennead, or even Gloomweaver.  So, I guess I'm saying that you should totally buy IR/RC when you get the chance!

Of what you have, Voss is a great choice.  Citizen Dawn also can be great in a five player game.  Be prepared for a hard battle, however.  She has a board wipe that hurts something fierce.

Final Wasteland and Silver Gulch are fine locals to fight in.  Insula Primalis is Dawn's hometurf, so that might be fun if you want to be thematic, and Voss is quite an intense fight in The Wagner Mars Base if you want to keep the "Space" feel.  Final Wasteland does some fun stuff with minions, so that might be a treat if you want some shenanigans to be had with the villain.  Ruins of Atlantis are very aggressive, so be prepared for a harsh world if you fight there.  I use to recommend Megalopolis all the time, but it has an immense amount of denial cards, so I'd actually avoid that for a first game with your friends.

If you are looking for a fun, minion-centric time, I'd defintely go with Voss.  He's  a perennial favorite among my friends.  One of my favorite things about him is that he doesn't tend to destroy your stuff (he has one ship that can destroy stuff, but thats it), so its very rewarding for setup intensive characters.  Its also rewarding for controllers, tanks, and damage dealers!

A few things to note when fighting him, for maximum enjoyment:

1. Don't pick Atlantis as an environment when fighting him for the first time.  Its a GREAT environment...but it has a lot of potential to give him cards out of turn, and that can complicate things quite easily.  Insula Primalis heightens the tension with damage boosting potential, and is a great way to ratchet up the tension (if thats what you are looking for).  If you want Atlantis, be really careful about anticipating Krakens and be on your toes for the Pillar of Hercules at the WRONG time.

2. There are likely to be some painful turns when all seems lost!  He's got some nasty tricks with his ships and forced deployment, and once he gets a swarm it can look pretty bleak.  For me, this is where team synergy and cleverness really started to shine, and you got the most out of control characters, damage reducers, massive AoE, sacrifice plays, and clever ways to tank.  You can absolutely pull a victory off, even when he's got an army!

The variant I've tried is a more substantial modification (on par w/ Mad Bomber Blade):

The basic idea is that Ambuscade, as a hunter, prepares before he engages and then focuses on one target. The rest of it is just to distract the people he is not hunting. 

Setup: Find H-1 traps and insert them face up.

Front Side: Every time a hero plays a card reveal the top card of the villain deck. If it is a trap or a device, play it. Other wise shuffle it into the deck. At the end of the villain turn, if there are H devices in play, flip Ambuscade.

(Advanced: At the end of the villain turn, reveal the top card of the villain deck. If it is a trap or a device, play it.)

Back Side: Ambuscade is immune to damage from every hero except for the hero with the highest hit points at the start of the villain turn. Redirect all damage dealt by Ambuscade to the hero with the highest hit points at the start of the villain turn. When a villian card would be played Ambuscade regains H hit points and deals the hero with the highest hit points H projectile damage. And the end of the villain turn deal the hero with the highest hit points 5-X energy damage, where X is the number of devices in play. At the end of the villain turn if the personal cloaking device is not in play and there are fewer than H devices in play, flip Ambuscade. 

(Advanced: At the start of the villain turn Ambuscade deals the Hero target with the highest hit points H-2 fire damage).
