You can certainly try to pick a different way to set up the game--if you typically choose characters, go random.  If you typically pick randomly, instead try to select thematic set-ups.

 

You can also try the campaign variants that people have created (boardgamegeek.com has some in their forums) where you play a linked series of games and success or failure affect future games.  There are also variant heroes and villains to try (with some variation in quality).

 

Of course, you may have already done these things, in which case try another game for a while.  Legendary is probably the best super-hero themed game (other than SOTM of course) right now.  I recently picked up the Mage Knight board game and it's spectacular, but be ready for long games.  Or check out some of the "modern classics" like Carcasonne, Settlers of Catan, Puerto Rico, or Agricola.

I've almost certainly clocked more than 300 games in the 13 months I've had Sentinels (playing online means I get in more games than the "average" player), and like Ronway I'm nowhere near burnout. There has to be literally billions of different hero/villain/environment combinations, and even with the exact same setup you'll see different cards and a different game. Not to mention promos…I believe randomising everything helps a lot. If you always play Haka or prefer to take Tempest every time you face Voss, you're more likely to get bored. 

I've played almost 100 games in 5-6 months.  And I play different heroes nearly every time (for the Nemesis beatdown I played 8 games using the same heroes for 3 and then 5 of them, but that was an exception to my usual rule).  Like I said, I'm not burned out yet, but I suspect I will be eventually, if only during the long cycles between expansions coming out (Vengeance is arriving before I'm entirely tired of everything before it, and those five new heroes will last me a good long while, but the next expansion is probably going to add only two new heroes, and so I'll likely be starting to lose interest by the time its successor is visible on the horizon).  And the billions of combinations are only a technicality; realistically, the difference between Haka/Wraith/Tempest vs. Voss and Haka/Wraith/Tempest vs. Rat is not that huge, so in more meaningful terms (IMO), the number of possible games is more like the 200+ villain/environment combinations, times about 10 to 20 for the various ever-shifting roster of hero teams.  It's likely that playing a lot of solitaire contributed to the sense of burnout, which is why I've cut way back on it…if I'm only playing one hero instead of an entire team, it helps provide more variety.  But I'm still beginning to feel as though most of the villains are a solved puzzle.  If you're against Apostate, kill his Relics.  If you're against Iron Legacy, bring Ongoing destruction.  If you're against Plague Rat, prevent damage.  I have to work a bit to stave off the first stages of boredom, and that process will gradually increase…I'll need to come up with more elaborate scenario rules and so forth, in order to keep my interest up.

I've been able to teach new people the game over the last month while still getting solo (and some forum play) in.  So I get a mix of "newbie games" in while also still getting used to the heroes and such from the RC/IR expansions.  Some interesting highlights in the past few days that show that the game can always be interesting in new ways:

  1. Group with 3-4 games under their belt and having just trashed Akash'Bhuta got tossed The Dreamer as a curveball.  Went down heavy in the 2nd half of the game as a swarm of projections proved too much along with the Dreamer herself inflicting damage.

  2. A 3-hero solo game I thought would be a boring kill (Nightmist, Young Legacy, AZ vs Baron Blade @ Insula Primalis) was anything but.  Ended up with Nightmist in Mist Form fighting a flipped Blade and a swarm of dinosaurs.  Game ended with a Volcanic Eruption finishing off Blade literally micro-seconds before it got Nightmist.  (In other words, they both went down, but by rule I was able to make Baron Blade be snuffed first for the win.)

[Plus the realization that having an incap AZ giving Nightmist a +2 damage bonus to her next damage and having it accumulate for five turns is not necessarily a good thing for Nightmist when she has no chance to get a certain piece of equipment out first.]
