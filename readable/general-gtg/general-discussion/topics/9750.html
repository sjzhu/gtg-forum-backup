Reckless:

Is there another medium that you find has a better track record?

It might be an apples and oranges comparison, but in general the small subset of movies based on comic books (as opposed to all of Hollywood) has something close to a 50/50 excellence/crap ratio, instead of the 10/90 that comics as a whole probably boils down to.  The Raimi Spider-Mans were fairly terrible, as were the third and fourth Batman movies, Green Lantern's not great, Daredevil was slightly tepid and Elektra was just ridiculous, the old Superman tetralogy (and its unofficial fifth entry, Superman Returns) vary from decent to awful, Sly Stallone's Judge Dredd was fun IMO but definitely not exactly good, and of course there was that 1994 The Punisher which was just a Rambo clone that claimed to be comics-based.  But conversely, you have Iron Man, Thor, both Hulk movies (the Ang Lee one was weird as heck but I considered it still reasonably good), Batman Begins and to a lesser Extent The Dark Knight (really, only Joker was much good in that one, but he counts for a lot), and for a very tenuous qualifier, The Incredibles.  I haven't seen Iron Man 2 and 3, The Avengers, the new Punisher or Dredd movies, Amazing Spider-Man or Man of Steel, but I've heard that most of these were well-reviewed.   So overall, if we grouped these movies into "great", "okay", and "bad", the piles would be roughly equal in size.  Whereas doing the same to comics would result in about a bookshelf or two worth of "great", a few truckloads of "okay", and a very literal mountain of absolute dreck (most of the absolute dreck titles you've probably never even heard of; I've found samplings of quite a few in the bargain bin of various comic stores, and wanted my dime back).

It's kind of an unfair comparison since the movies are generally based on the handpicked best (or at least okayest) of the comics.  But it works; Marvel Movies is batting about 900 from what I hear (I can personally confirm about half of it; the only one of the post-IM movies which I found slightly less than good was Incredible Hulk, and even that was pretty decent), and pretty much anything written by David S. Goyer is awesome.  Now if only someone would handpick the best of Hollywood's movies and turn them into usually-excellent comics…

People are assuming that I'm saying things which I'm absolutely not saying.

I didn't say that comics aren't literature. I said that comics aren't books, twice, but novels would certainly have been a better word. Comics aren't novels.

I didn't say that books can't have pictures. I said that the information in books (should have said novels) is primarily conveyed through the words. If you read the book out loud to someone they understand the majority or all of it without actually seeing the pages. Many children's books with pictures fall into this category too - the pictures are nice, and add to the enjoyment and may provide some extra info, but aren't strictly necessary for delivering the story. The Very Hungry Caterpillar still works if you can't see it, even though it's less fun that way.

With comics, the pictures are vital. And that's the distinction I'm making.

I was reading the kind of children's books that have only occasional pictures and have chapters and such with only minimal "I've never heard this word before because I'm five and I don't know what it means" assistance at five too. My parents used to read to me and taught me my letters and how they made up words before I went to "proper" school and so I learned to read earlier than most of my classmates. My school used those reading scheme type books mentioned, the first of which I remember very clearly had pictures of various acts in a circus, with the word "Look" on each page and nothing else. I thought that was completely pointless as a learn-to-read exercise for me, and said as much (my teachers loved my intelligence, but weren't fond of my outspokenness). That book? It was a comic, essentially, and not a book.

Maybe my dislike for the comic form stems from being encouraged to read books with fewer pictures and more words as a child - at seven I was reading about paleontology and natural history, spent all my pocket money and birthday money on books and shunned anything that was "for babies" and had more pictures than words, because the enjoyment for me was increased if I had to work to get it. Now I know that much of the comic form is aimed at adults, but I can't help letting my childhood prejudice colour my judgement. That child would say that comics are too easy and therefore less fun. The adult me is hopefully a bit more subtle than that, but the feeling is essentially the same. 

I'm very glad your daughter likes reading so much, and that you're doing so much to encourage her. That's wonderful. :)
