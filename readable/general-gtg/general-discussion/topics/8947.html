Try playing against her with a hero or two who can stall her deck.

She can be pretty brutal. You'll want at least 1 hard-hitting damage dealer, like Ra or -- even better -- Chrono Trigger. (Whole Gang is great for crew, the hat can play multiple cards, there's reliable AoE, and No Executions is great for the boat.) Some sort of damage mitigation and/or redirection is great, since those crew members are TOUGH. Battle Forged and Final Breath will WRECK whoever has the lowest HP, and if L'Episte shows up mid-game, he can hit for double-digit damage. The Scholar is good for this, and Smoke Bombs go a LONG way too.

At first glance, Unity really struggles, but you can bait them into destroying Bee Bots to destroy her particularly obnoxious ongoings easily, and with smoke bombs you can protect the golems as well, to help her build up.

Wraith, of course, has stun bolts, infrared eyepiece, and decent multi-target damage, plus combat stance, which is great against her multitude of violent cohorts.

you may also want a healer or support character as well, to recover from the frequent board wipes and damage. Argent Adept is a no-brainer (but really, when ISN'T he?).

 

 

So maybe try a game with Chrono, Argent, Wraith, and Unity, or maybe switch Chrono or Argent out for the Scholar.

She's a tough villain, but there are a few ways to make teh fight an easier one.  

I do subscribe to the idea that any villain can be beaten with any combination of heroes, though some definitely make it harder than others.  that being said, here are some of the general tips, strategies, and thought processes that I use when fighting her.

 

Prioritize Her Support Structure

This goes beyond the sip, in some ways, though getting rid of it will certainly make life easier.  By herself, she's not that bad.  She has a single nasty hit (on her starting side), but her strength lies in her crew.  

The main ones to get rid of ASAP are Mable and the Battle-forged.  Both enable her to get extra card plays, which will make your life hell.  the others will hurt you, but they will strip you of resources, go after the weakest, and enable La Capitan to grow stronger.

The ship is a good thing to get rid of, but if don't prioritize it over everything else.  The reason is because La Capitan has a card that can get the ship back from the trash- so if you pour everything you've got into getting rid of it (potentially ignoring crew, ongoings, or setup), theres a chance that her next card will bring it right back at full health.  Look for ways to accomplish multiple objectives or ensure some breathing room- like scouting her next few cards to see if its safe to get rid of the ship, or locking her deck, or getting the ship out of the trash. In the long term, if you can get rid of it ASAP, go for it- but be aware that unless you know otherwise it will be back, and at the worst moment.

 

Its Dangerous to Go Alone (Take This)

Team synergy and tactics are an absolute necessity when fighting her.  Always be on the lookout for ways to help other heroes.  You're going to be losing ongoings/equipment, so make sure that you have some sacrificial stuff ready (or at least be aware that you may lose what you have).  

Any card/power that gives you some breathing room is essential.  If you've got a specialist (like Ra or Chrono for damage or the AA for support), it helps to do everything you can to make sure that they can do their thing before getting your own cards down.  This is because generalists can adapt ot changing situations (usually) better than the more specialized heroes, but the specialists can usually crank out wondrous things once they are set up. Spend a few turns protecting them, helping them do stuff, or sacrificing your ongoings/equips for them.  They'll pay it back in spades once they start setting the world on fire, blasting crew members full of holes, or (in the case of the Argent Adept) letting everyone else go crazy.

If you've got a team of generalists, stay flexible.  Try not to obsess over the "perfect build" (something I am still learning how to do), and make the most out of the options you have.  There will be frustrating moments...but you also may find new combinations and tactics that you never considered before.  An example I learned recently (for Unity) involve using her "Scrap metal" card to take a low-hp bot back into my hand AFTER it does damage. It keeps them from being munched by the Battle-Forged.

On the topic of Hero Order, it definitely matters.  Heroes that support others through an external focus (by increasing their damage, letting them draw cards, play cards, etc) tend to do better at the start of the turn order.  Heroes that deal high damage tend to do best at the end, where they can perform clean-up duties.  In the example of fighting La Capitan, it helps to have the heavy hitters go later in the order because it gives the other team members time to scout out her deck (to see if any dead ships or crew will pop right back out), get rid of her ongoings (since she has one that makes it painful to take out her people), or generally weaken the minions and bring them into killl range.

I'v been playing a Kismet exclusivly on advanced for a while now. There are a few things about her that really make her quite swingy.

1) The biggest thing to know is that if she is going to hit you and hit you hard, 9/10 times it will be with psychic damage. Legacy single handedly defangs Kismet with Next Evolution and renders her totally useless, advanced or otherwise. 

2) Her front side is downright horrifying by the time 1-2 jinx cards hit the table. With the Charm on her side, her SoT AoE gets brutal and the advanced text dealing another base 2 to anyone jinxed is not to be messed with. Ample amounts of ongoing destruction obviously is the key to keeping her damage output low while she has the charm. This leads us to

3) Lady Luck. Depending on when/if you see this hit the table, you could be in trouble as it makes managing her front side very difficult and very painful since ongoing destruction turn very unfavorable in the risk/reward department

4) Any combination of Lady Luck and more than 1 Jinx in play will ultimatly force you into playing "keepaway" with her Charm to avoid her racking up +dmg modifiers. Her flip side has an innate +1 on advanced, that combined with her trading a SoT AoE for an EoT single target zap make that the much safer side to operate on. Damage to the charm needs to be tightly monitored, that last thing you want is for the environment to knock the Charm back to Kismet sending her back to her front side with a Jinxes on the field. 

5) The final component to her swingy-ness is Inconcievable Obstruction (man I hope I remembered that name correctly). If Lady Luck wasn't bad enough, this card will be directing a good bit of your damage away from Kismet to the enviro. Obviously this is troubling, as the deeper into the round count you end up going, the more time she has to rack up the jinx count.

 

2 & 5 are where the bulk of the randomness occurs. If Kismet finds neither of those, playing keepaway long enough to drum up some ongoing destruction should not be a problem and Kismet should go down with few problems.
