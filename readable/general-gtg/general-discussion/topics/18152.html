From what I learned from e-mailing with Jodie and calling FedEx here in Germany, the following caused the customs issue:

 

FedEx made errors when processing the packages for international shipment and attached a wrong (or even made-up) commercial invoice over $19. When the package arrived in Germany, it was applied for local customs by FedEx.

German customs needs to know the cost of goods to calculate the customs amount. Of course, they doubted the value of $19 as per invoice for such a big package. Also, as far as I know, €22 is the amount of customs exemption, which makes it even more dubious. Jodie sent me correct invoice, after I explained her my issue.

I sent the correct invoice to FedEx. But FedEx now has the procedure of asking for a proof of payment, as the invoice is in doubt. The responsible clearance broker was very stubborn. He did not understand that there was no proof of payment but kept asking me for one, since it seems to be FedEx standard procedure.

The solution was to call FedEx and specifically asking for re-applying the package for customs with the correct commercial invoice I've sent them earlier per e-mail. This worked out for me at least and today I received the package.

 

Additional information: FedEx told me that they contact the sender of an international shipment first, before they return a package. So I guess, there isn't so much stress behind this "five days or else" rule.

 

I still feel bad, since I already received all the contents and have a strong feeling that I pledged way less on kickstarter, than what I have received or will be receiving from you. Not to mention all the time and work you put into this. I will contact one of you today or tomorrow, because I want to somehow correct this.

I think I might be the person you're talking about. You know, since you quoted me and all

First of all, let me be clear that I doubt that anyone will pay the ridiculous amount Fedex quoted me. That said, I am now somewhat sure that this courier service in particular is staffed by slightly moistened socks.

Let's get to the numbers first: Fedex told me that customs estimated the goods to be worth 350€. This was despite me sending them the invoice - first the wrong one (GTG accidently sent me the wrong one and I did not bother to double check - that's on me), then the right one. I never asked them to estimate the value. It would later turn out that nobody at Fedex has even read my emails ( I was told that on the phone by a custome service agent who also insisted that any contact should be mail only), so them estimating the value kinda made sense. This would have put me at a customs bill  of ~96€ (RobK is right in that there is 19% tax, but in additional there is a 7% customs charge to be paid). But, and that's a really cute one, customs charges have to be calculated including shipping - which, for reasons I can not fathom, Fedex told me would be roughly 250€. My potential bill is now suddenly ~165€. If you're feeling cheeky, consider adding the random processing and warehouse fee that Fedex (unlawfully, apparently) demands and you might understand why I was (and, to be honest, still kind of am) freaked out about this. Yes, 165€ would be a nice price for the game, but I already have all of it. It would be wasted money.

 

Now why did I even have it processed? Surely I could have just denied accepting the parcel?

It's a combination of lack of information and, bluntly put, lies. First of all, well, I ordered something via kickstarter from GTG, GTG sends me a parcel, checks out. Why would I deny that? I only learned later that this was an error on GTGs part. When Fedex calls and tells you they have a parcel for you that's stuck at customs, it's from a company you ordered something from and you need to allow them to process you parcel and send them an invoice - and if you don't, it will be send back at your expense - of course you'd have it processed. If you wouldn't, well, clearly I'm not quite as clever as you are.

Of course now, after properly checking all the facts, I know I should have just denied accepting the parcel. I know I would not have had to pay the cost of having it returned. I know that I have a right, by law, to deal with customs myself. I know that the people I have talked to were either ignorant of how importing with their service works - or maybe they lied to me on purpose.

 

However, in the end, I agree that all seems to be well for me. I have received the parcel and I learned a lot about how dealing with customs using a third party works (it's "Oh god, just do it yourself"). I also learned to stay the hell away from Fedex. I have learned how I should react when something like this happens and I have learned a little something about actual customs law in Germany (did you know we're not allowed to import bats with nails in them?). And last but not least, I got a mildly interesting story out of it that will doubtlessly regale my friends for years to come. I've got to admit, I'm still a little anxious about Schrödinger's customs bill, but you know what? If I ever get one, I am now armed with dangerous half knowledge and prepared to deal with it when it happens.

 

As for the duplicate games… most of it will go to some friends to complete their collection. I suppose the rest will go up on boardgamegeek in the math trade. Dunno, really, for now it's just a box taking up space in my basement (that I should really clean out, dammit, everything's in the way).
