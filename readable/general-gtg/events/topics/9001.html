Well, discussing mechanics is probably good practice for imaginary designers, so let's look at it this way.

 

In cases where you're being dealt H-based damage, Cohesive Unit will block one of that damage. Some times, yes, that'll be going from 1 to 0, but that's not exactly unheard of. Cohesive Unit can totally block the Matriach's H-2 retaliation damage when H=3, but so can Twist the Ether, or that one vat in Pike Industrial Complex, or any of the cards like Fortitude when the hero in question is the one with the highest HP. I mean, Legacy alone, whose deck it would be going in, has at least 3 ways he could block the Matriach's retaliation damage at H=3 (Fortidue while being the highest, heroic intervention while not being the highest, or lead from the front and next evolution at any time, which would also work at any H too). So, blocking one damage: not exactly game breaking, right?

 

Other times, Cohesive Unit might save a person from taking damage (if an effect hits H-1 or H-2 targets), or stop a card from being played (if an effect looks for H cards of a specific type), or possibly being played (if an effect looks at the top H cards of the villain deck and plays all of a specific type), or stop an ongoing or equipment from being destroyed (yada yada). These are all handy! But there's just as many times when it won't do anything at all: there's plenty of bad guy plays that don't involve H at all, and you might be sitting there wishing you had spent your turn playing a Heroic Intervention or Take Down instead, even if those just last for a single round.

 

Which, leading things back to more general hypothetical-designer talk if I may, is a good thing. It's important (at least in my humble opinion) for new cards to create new and interesting choices: something that's always better than an existing option in every situation  or good enough in general that it's an obvious must-play the second you draw it, removes choices, instead.
