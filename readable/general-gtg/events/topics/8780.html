Here's what I've learned about providing a live stream:

 

Option 1: Google+ Hangout

 

  • Created by going to G+ and clicking on 'Start a video party' in the lower right.
  • Can invite the public -- make it so that anyone with a G+ account can join the G+ Hangout.
  • The URL for the Hangout can be found and shared (via forums, Twitter, Facebook, G+, email, etc.)
  • Only ten people, however, can be in the Hangout at any one time.
  • People in the Hangout add their own audio/video to the Hangout, and can use a group chat interface inside the Hangout.
  • People outside the Hangout can not see or hear anything, as far as I can tell
  • One or more G+ posts or YouTube videos can be linked to the Hangout so that people outside the Hangout can comment and those comments would be seen by the creator of the Hangout. Comments could then be selectively displayed to the Hangout.

 

Option 2: YouTube Live Event, via Google+ Hangout on Air

  • If you have been blessed as worthy, there will be an option in YouTube's Video Manager to create a Live Event
  • Creating the Live Event results in both a YouTube video and a Google+ Hangout.
  • The G+ Hangout is like the above in option 1 except that you can not invite large groups, much less the public, to it.
  • The YouTube video shows the G+ Hangout with a several minute delay.

 

Option 3: YouTube Live Event, via custom encoder

  • Haven't explored this much. Apparently you can download free software to encode and broadcast your video stream without going through Google+ Hangout on Air, but I don't see how doing so is going to lead to any more interactivity between viewers of the stream and people delivering the stream.

 

Having thought about this a bit, I see the following potential goals:

  1. Allow remote people to see the fun that's happening
  2. Allow remote people to post messages that will be seen by other remote people and by the Vengeance party
  3. Allow remote people to videoconference (share their own audio/video) with the Vengenace party
  4. Allow remote people to play games with partygoers

 

If we just want to do #1 (see the party) option #2 (YouTube Live Event) works. I don't see a way to do #2 (share messages) with either option above. We can do #3 (videoconference) for up to nine people with either option. For #4, either option would be fine.

 

My current thought is to split the ideas of people playing the game from people watching/interacting with the party. We already know how to have games online. That's basically option #1. We could do that with a dedicated table and computer for a given game. For everything else, perhaps we could set up a station with a camera with a wide view of the party, or even someone with a tablet walking around.

 

My questions for people who will be remote:

  1. Do you want to be able to play games w/ people at the party?
  2. What else might you want -- see the party, post messages, videoconference in, something else?
  3. Anybody have good ideas about how to make this happen?
