So, there was totally a tournament today. I see no posts about it, so, uh, maybe y'all want to hear about it?

TLDR: It was super fun! No, really. I'm not just saying that. I was amazed out how polished and professional everything was, and I had a lot of fun playing.

 

I wasn't volunteering for this event, just attending as an entrant. We arrived a little after doors opened at 8am. Greater Than Games continues to expand, having taken over the adjoining space to their HQ/warehouse. That was only a week ago, so renovations are still to come, but in the meantime, they'd set up lots of beautiful banners as decorations.

After picking up badges in the entry area, we entered into a mid-sized room playing triple duty: The main area had three tables for playing Tactics. In one corner was a production control center, and on another wall was a screen showing the livestream. Around the corner was a small studio space from which the commentators broadcast. In the next room was the main stage, if you will, a table set up with a video rig to show top down shots of the board. During play on the main stage there was not only a judge, but two people manning video cameras to get shots of various angles of the players and board. This allowed the livestream to see the cards players were considering playing, as well as see the actions being taken. The production control center was taking in the three video streams from the main stage, the audio from the main stage, and the video and audio from the commentators, plus adding in some really great game graphics that showed the current status and which actions were being taken. They could pop up visuals of player mats and cards. It was just really, really excellent.

Seven teams had preregistered and three other people showed up to form an eight team. The tournament was organized into a double-elimination bracket. Losing teams went down to a secondary bracket. For the semi-finals, two teams from the top bracket played against the remaining two teams from the bottom bracket. The teams from the bottom bracket would need to win twice to advance to the finals. Then, in the finals, games would be played until one of the remaining teams had lost twice. For each match there would be a coin flip. The winner of the coin flip could choose to either have the first move or have the first pick of characters.

We were randomly assigned for the first round, with one of the matches randomly being put on to the main stage. Our team was put on one of the other tables. My teammates and I had little illusions that we would do wll. We were excited to play, but didn't have much experiene and no practice time together. We'd gotten together to discuss some strategies around which characters we would choose, but had mostly focused on picking those characters that were more straightforward, since we didn't know them very well.

Our opponents won the first toss and drafted first. Ultimately they ended up with Baron Blade, Citizen Dawn, and Legacy. We had Bunker, Beacon, and Wraith.

We got _DESTROYED_. The games are played to three incapacitations, with a 50 minute time limit. We lost 3-0 in 30 minutes. Our team name was 'Happy to be Here', and like I said we had little illusion that we'd do well, but that was ... harsh.

BUT, we learned a lot. The main tactics our opponents, The Gatecrashers, had defeated us with were: Use Citizen Truth to take lots of attacks from the main characters, use Legacy to Buff Baron Blade, and then use Baron Blade to do lots of big attacks.

For the second round we were in the bottom bracket and playing a team that had also lost in the first round, still in the larger room rather than the main stage. We were quite subdued, but at least armed with some new ideas. Our second opponent wasn't much, if any, more experienced then us and we were able to draft the exact team that had beat us in the first round (Blade, Dawn, and Legacy). We won that round 3-0! That was great, but realistically, I still wasn't expecting too much. After all, we didn't have lots of experience, just a couple ideas we'd picked up, and the team we beat wasn't experienced. I started thinking about how many teams _WERE_ experienced, though, and realized that it might not be too many.

Two teams were eliminated after those first two rounds. The teams that won both the first and second round got to take a rest while the remaining four teams with one loss and one win played to see who would make it to the semis. For the third round we were again not on the main stage. We tried to follow the tactics that had won in the second round, but the other team took Dawn. We ended up with Blade and Legacy, and for our third took Bunker. So there we were, facing the puzzle of how to get past Citizen Truth to actually damage the other team. Along with Dawn, the other team has Ambuscade and Absolute Zero. Dawn put up her Citizen Truth defense and the other two covered our starting area with traps and glacial structure hazards. 

Our answer came in the form of Legacy buffing Blade and then Blade unleashing massive attacks against Truth. I was playing Blade and one-shotted Citizen Truth twice. With that defense out of the picture Legacy, Bunker, and Blade piled on a ton of damage. In the end time was called before we anyone got to three capacitations, but we were ahead 2-1 and doing well.

We were in to the semis! To my surprise, we were the little team that could. We had somehow scraped through -- mostly on borrowing tactics from our first round blowout. And then I saw who our semifinal opponent would be -- the same team that had beat us in the first round. As it turns out, both of the teams that emerged from the bottom bracket were the two that The Gatecrashers had beat in the first two rounds, so they had to play one or the other of us. 

All games for the semis and finals were on the main stage, so we finally got to see what it was like there. On the other tables, it wasn't much different than being in the booth at a convention -- lots of background noise from other tables, well run, but not particularly formal. The main stage was a whole 'nother deal. We had precise instructions on where to roll dice and where to put power cards in play. It was a complex orchestration of production that we were mostly free from worrying about, but for which we had to be careful not to cause unnecessary disruptions or difficulty.

We won the draft and took Citizen Dawn. Now we'd get a chance to see what Gate Crashers could do about it. They ended up with Blade, Legacy, and Omnitron. We took Unity and the Wraith. The first round went pretty well for us. Unity sent Swift Bot right up to Legacy and Blade and with some reconfiguration/volatile parts shenanigans, bloodied both of them, with two hits each. I had Wraith use her reach to do three damage to Omnitron. We'd hit all three of them, and left an annoying turret bot creating hazard spaces near them. 

In turn, though, we got to see how the Gate Crashers dealt with the Dawn/Citizen Truth problem: Interpolation Beam. Omnitron simply moved up and then used Interpolation Beam to separate Citizen Truth from the rest of us. Then they put the beat down on Dawn, hitting her for four of her five points. Next it was Dawn's turn and, sadly, we made the decision that probably cost us the match. Dawn used Devastating Aurora to knock out Legacy and Omnitron, trying to buy time to heal. The effect, though, was to let the other team get five HP back while still having Blade knock her out on the next round. We were forever beyond from that point. When the game was called for time, we were down 2-0. It was far closer than the first beat down, but still not particularly close.

The other semi-final match was also won by the team from the top bracket, the Mighty Morphin Chronorangers, so the final would be a best of three series of matches. 

In the first game the Might Morphin Chronorangers took Dawn, but the Gatecrashers got Blade and Legacy. The Gatecrashers also took Omnitron -- their answer to Dawn's protection from Citizen Truth. The Chronorangers also took the Wraith and Ambuscade. The Gatecrashers did the same thing to the Chronorangers that they did to us -- push Citizen Truth out of the way and then just pound away. With Wraith slipping into shadows and Ambuscade using the cloaking device, all three incaps were done against Citizen Dawn. The final score was 3-0.

That was a massive defeat. The previously undefeated Chronorangers were left sitting dejectedly at the table. They hadn't lost the tournament, but they'd been blown out so badly, how would they come back and win not one, but two matches?

The next match, however, had something we had not seen before -- two teams who both knew how to stop Citizen Truth's protection of Dawn. The drafting went: Chronorangers: Blade. Gatecrashers: Dawn. Chronorangers: Omnitron. Gatecrashers: Legacy. Chronorangers: Ambuscade. Gatecrashers: Beacon. The Gatecrashers had Dawn and Legacy and had chose Beacon as their damage dealer, since Blade wasn't available. The Chronorangers had Blade, Omnitron as the answer to Citizen Truth, and Ambuscade. I wasn't quite sure why they kept taking Ambuscade. Sure, he's hard to hit, but how much damage was he doing?

The match started out as most of the previous ones had, with the Gatecrashers setting up Truth to be their protection. As expected, the Chronorangers brought up Omnitron and first separated, then knocked out Truth. Then the game started to get interesting. The Gatecrashers abandoned the use of Citizen Truth and went on the offensive. The board was filled with bruisers and a lot of mobility. The attacks were flying quickly. Along the way Chronorangers unleashed a suprise move from Ambuscade. Instead of filling up a bunch of spaces with traps, they put ALL of the traps and decoys on the same space, next to an enemy, and then moved a _different_ enemy on to the stack, causing all four explosions to hit both of them. It wasn't always super effective, but it sometimes was. This game also saw Blade get pushed into the middle of the volcano. It came down to the wire. The Chronorangers pulled out a win, 3-2, but would probably have lost on the next turn if they didn't. The Chronorangers had now dealt Gatecrashers their first lost, just one game after having their own, first, stunning, demoralizing loss.

Final game. With both teams having seen the Citizen Truth gambit be defeated, how would they react? Chronorangers took Blade. Gatecrashers took Legacy. Chronorangers took Omnitron. Gatecrashers took Beacon. Chronorangers took Ambuscade. Gatecrashers took ... The Wraith?! I did not understand that choice. Why not Bunker and get some great firepower, or even Dawn, who can be deadly on the attack.

As they took their first few turns, it seemed that the strategy was to use Legacy to build up buffs on Beacon and Wraith and then unleash both of them to make big attacks. Unfortunately, Baron Blade got close enough to them in the first round to punish their build up with attack+1 tokens with his oneshot that deals damage to an area using as many dice as tokens. The results were devasting -- two characters down to one or two HP each. The Gatecrashers put up a fight, but never seriously contended from that point on.

Chronorangers had done it -- coming from a loss behind to win two consecutive games and claim the victory. 

The last match finished around 5ish, I think.For reaching the semis we received a small cash prize: $75 for the team. Finalists got $150 to split. Champions got $600!! to split. The top two teams earned the right to compete in the nationals at GenCon next year. There are other qualifying tournaments planned , but I'm not sure if I'm allowed to say anything about that, and I don't know definitive information anyway.

It was a fantastic day. So happy to have played, done better than expected, and seen some really exciting matches. There were eight teams and I counted about 16 staff and volunteers -- about forty people in total. Well done, Greater Than Games.

I would love to hear what  people who watched  games via Twitch thought of what they saw.

Matchstickman:

I failed to mention above that along with the judge and two camera people at the main stage, we also had Jodie with a tablet entering data to control what was displayed in the graphics overlays. The app on her tablet shared data with a server, from which data were drawn to be displayed as part of the stream.

As I understand it, the four person production team consisted of a producer, a person doing sound, a person handling the twitch stream, and a runner to go handle issues that might arise at the main stage or broadcast booth. Everyone at the control center, broadcast booth, and main stage were communicating via mics and headphones. Christopher had an override switch he could use to provide rules info quickly. It was really quite elaborate and impressive.

Matchstickman:

The only disappointing thing about the matches was the lack of variety in the characters chosen, a lot of the same faces played in most matches, but that could be because there aren't a huge number of characters to chose from right now, expansions might rectify that 'problem', get right on that >G!

I'm sure a lot of it was also a relatively small pool of teams that had actively prepared for the tournament. Certainly the two finalists had, but I'm not sure many of the others had prepared overly much. There was a lot of people picking up on tactics from earlier games and using them in later games.

There were fifteen matches played, with eight of those being broadcast on the main stage, so if you saw the whole feed, you saw half the matches. There wasn't a whole buch of difference out on the other tables, though. 

I look forward to hopefully seeing the statistics from the tournament. GtG kept track of every character chosen as well as which ones got incapacitated. Thinking back through the seven games that I saw, Baron Blade was on the winning team for every single one of them.

Now that the videos are up, I suppose it's time to actually write about the event, even though I'm going to post before I watch the vids. So, I'm Pat of the Gatecrashers, the 'leader' in that I found info on the event first, and was serious about getting teammates who were willing and able to practice most days in the lead up to the event. Barry and Colin were champs at getting time to play and talk strategy in the two weeks we had to practice.

 

With the scenario being set at 50 minutes, first to 3 incaps, we quickly eliminated any character that was vulnerable to a lucky shot or just a couple of small hits. Honestly, my first questions I sent through our communications guy (Barry) were 'Are villains available?' followed promptly by 'Citizen Dawn can actually take a Citizen card into a scramble?' After we played a few games our 'perfect team' became apparent; Citizen Dawn for a solid hitter with a good selection of effects (movement, healing, Truth/other citizen), Baron Blade for superior hitting power & maneuverability, and a third to problem solve. Legacy with his buffs was a primary go-to, but Wraith and Ambuscade were each serious secondaries if the other team had a good target for them.

 

Once we had our 'perfect team' we spent the rest of the time we had playing against it. Solving for 'the Truth problem' quickly became a thing with us. Given the time constraint, we never really thought of Absolute Zero as a serious choice; without mobility, he just doesn't participate enough at all on tiles that have lots of elevation changes. The Operative was considered as well, but we found she really only shines against 1 opponent at a time, and that costs her an active power slot. We felt she had too many restrictions to work well. Omnitron is the easiest answer for Truth with a no-roll auto push.

 

That covers most of our prep. As for event day, it was really a good time. As Andy already gave a great overview, I'll just say that I was surprised at the lengths GtG went for this event. The cash prizes were more than tempting, but at the same time I don't think we had a bad play experience over the entire day with any player or staff. Before I watch the videos, I'm prone to saying I'm probably the one who got the most testy about our last game. Because of how the tourney worked, the Gatecrashers only played 3 teams, despite our playing 6 games. That was a little unfortunate, but at the same time, meeting Andy's 'Happy To Be Here' team twice was a pleasure.

 

So, the only game that I have anything to say on is our last. We were obviously aware of Blade and had a strategy to deal with it... but things did not go well. I take nothing away from the Chronorangers at all – they played very well and their Ambuscade combo was powerful. From memory though, our offensive dice went ice cold very quickly that game. The biggest hit I remember our team getting was 3 damage on Ambuscade (who promptly used his camo-suit to get off the board for a turn, appear in a safe area, and use his Heal 3 one-shot to clear the damage).

 

Again, Greater Than Games put in a lot of effort and money to promote this organized play and it really shows. They seemed excited about how the finals went and have plans to continue this style of event through 2015's convention season at the PAX events filtering to an invitational at GenCon. I do have some thoughts on the event though; while we practiced on both city and jungle maps at home, it would have been nice to have maps change up, even if just for the semi-finals/finals. Secondly, the scenario being set as it is …. is rough. Sentinels: Tactics is an amazing scenario game. The heroes play like you expect them to from SotM, as do most of the villains; however, with a 'first to 3 incap' scenario, the villains have a seem to strong lead on power. I like the concept of a single-character ban, but I could also see something like 'one villain per side' or marking certain characters as especially powerful and limit those to one per side. A more sweeping idea like 'you cannot choose any character played previously in the same event until you have played every available character' or for less restriction, 'you can't play any character your team played in the last round you played in' would really force some varied play. Those types of restrictions could also reset on Semi-finals and/or Finals games to allow for a little more choice at the top tables... or could not, forcing a choice on playing your best team early or hoping you'll get to a later round to use them.

 

Well, there's my wall of rambling text. I'll watch some videos now and see if I remember anything correctly about the games I played in, as well as get a chance to watch all the video of games I didn't get a chance to see.

1. I think having the game end at 3 incaps is a huge limiting factor on the characters and teams that can be selected. Wow.  I'm glad my txtwall generated character discussion... that I'm really wanting to have.

 

First off, looking over the cards it's quickly apparent that the base character card is pretty close to standardized; mostly characters have 5 hit points, 3 actions, 3 defense, and 2(up) movement.  Innate powers are almost always a basic attack that isn't super bad or super good, and mobility, if the character flies or something.  Stats over or under the norm are very noticible; anyone with 2 actions often makes up for that with powerful active power cards - hazards or team buffs seem to be a common thing.  Low hit points often point to powers that effect enemies w/out the character being actually involved (Unity) or far enough away to stay safe (Tachyon / Visionary).

 

Blade is possibly the most powerful singular released character in S:T at the moment; he has nearly ultimate battlefield mobility with his t-port card, the ability to gain +1 tokens on his own, and some of the biggest guns in the game.  There's just not a downside to him.

Dawn is a very solid character at her base - mobility, 3 actions, good attacks, and gets +1 tokens for seeing allies fall.  The fact that for the price of 1 of her active powers she can take an entirely seperate Citizen(s) ally card that activates independantly blows my mind.  Truth is awesome for absorbing at least 1 big attack.  That alone makes him worth taking, but if the opponents are hoping for the death of a thousand cuts - getting 1 or 2 pts of damage in... Truth just kicks that strategy in the teeth.  On the other hand, look through those Citizens; the combat team of Assault & Battery is brutal: 8 wounds and big attacks, Citizen Spring for healing (Dawn w/Spring and Legacy w/motivational charge heal all teammates they can see for 3 each round), Blood Sweat & Tears for being a pain in the ass.  The scramble scenario is what makes Truth a huge problem; Dawn bringing a second character into the game is the real imbalance.  Look at any 'good guy' active power and show me one that's close to bringing those Citizen cards to the game, I dare you.  I'll even argue the Citizen add on is better than Tachyon's extra turn card, in that for Tachyon to use it, she gives up 1/4 of her hits, vs Dawn giving up nothing at all except the active power slot.

Ambuscade... is nasty alone, but stupid on a team.  The all the bombs at once trick is cute, but getting to take your playing piece off the board and return it anywhere you like every other activation is almost better than the Baron's t-port card.  Gaining +1 Attack tokens on his own is sweet too.

Proletariate might be the villain that breaks the mold for me.  He's got nice powers, even cute ones, and if the opponent lets himself get massed on by Proletariates, it IS a problem.  The thing is, Proletariate is just too vunerable - he has no defense powers, just a power that lets him take less damage by loosing a body, but that doesn't help much against a serious area attack that can hit 3 or 4 of him at once.

Whoops; one hero to be mentioned is Wraith - her card 'Slip into Shadow,' played well, makes her nearly immune to attack when playing in the cityscape, and pretty close if cover is nearby on the jungle.  It really is almost not worth attacking her if the opponent knows how to position her.

Allright.  Babble done on a handful of cards.  Basically, there was a reason the top 2 teams at this event ran with Blade, Dawn, & someone.  Without some amount of forced change I don't see how the first pick in each list isn't one of those characters until we see a new release.

He has 2 of the best defensive powers in the game that totally reduce hits over 2 damage down to 1 or 0 AND he can heal himself for two by sacking a body. You can overwhelm a dodge action to get as many dodge tokens as bodies. Unless the player makes the huge mistake of clumping them up in a small area, he is rediculously hard to incap and requires a lot of action econemy from the other team to try and do so. Pro should be played spread out over the map, maybe only swarming with 2 bodies to get the extra attack die on Peoples Hammer. So I dissagree with your assesment on that front.

The issue for Pro is that he can't get much damage done at all without leaving himself wide open to be one-shot (or at least in a position where he has to spend a round just healing/duplicating instead of attacking and positioning). He is very squishy because of his multiple bodies. If he keeps one of the two main defense cards in play, he can't have both overwhelm and Tac flanking. If he makes use of Tac Flanking, he sets himself up for AoE. If he Overwhelms Peoples Hammer for burst and Tac Flanking, he cant Overwhelm Dodge. If you try and Peoples Hammer without Tac Flanking, it is really lackluster. Then take into account its all melee attacks and 1 action always has to go to movement to get in range, so you are forced to decide betwen 2 hammer attacks or 1 aimed hammer.

Pro is really good at exerting board pressure and presence, being able to be in many places at once no matter where the opponent is. But he doesn't serve as a major threat to score incaps on his own unless he sheds any and all defensive mechanisms. This leaves him best served as a kinda "cleanup crew", fighting for those last 1-2 HP points to get an incap since he is hard to run from. Opposing teams will be wise to ignore Pro for the most part (due to the reasorce econemy it takes to incap him), and focus fire your other teamates. Bunker and Omnitron make great teamates because they are hard to focus fire effeciently and bring a lot of damage and utility from range (plus they are great picks because Pro really struggles vs both). Beacon or Ambuscade can make a well rounded 3rd to give high damage threats and utility. 

Pro has some bad down sides, and if you want to use him effectivly you have to build your team around his strengths. But he is really well served for a defensive/control type of gameplan.
