I was playing a game with Parse and Nightmist, and it's raised some questions about exactly how flexible Syntacic Analysis is.  The card text:

"Select a player.  That player reveals the top 2 cards of their deck.  They may play, draw, or discard each of those cards in any order."

Now, I've always played this card with the assumption that the word "each" means the selected player can choose a different option for each card.  But, now it occurs to me that the word "may" implies that the player is not required to choose any of those 3 options, in which case the card would return to the top of the deck, since it has only been revealed.  This is particularly relevant to Nightmist because of cards like Enlightenment which check the spell icon value of the top card of her deck.

So, for example, let's say Nightmist drew an Enlightenment (1) and a Tome of Elder Magic (4).  I think she chould choose to process the Tome first, pass on all 3 options to return it to the top of the deck, then play Enlightenment to give someone a guaranteed 4 card draw; or play the Enlightenment first to gamble on the draw (since the Tome is still being revealed, it's not technically on top of the deck), then return the Tome to the top of the deck to power one the spells in her hand on her next turn; or choose to pass on both cards to guarantee an optimized Oblivion on her next turn.

But, I feel like I'm making some assumptions with this interpretation, so does anyone see a problem with it?  Thanks.

May is not there for "you may choose one of these, or not", it is there for "you may chose a different option for each card".

Primary rule of Sentinels is Read the cards literally, there is no option to put it back on the deck, so you can't do that with this card. The options that you "may" select from are draw, discard or play, for each card separately. If you do not choose from one of those selected options then the game has stalled as Syntactic Analysis is still waiting for the 2 revealed cards to be played, drawn or discarded.

I thought for a while that I had a counterargument:  Tachyon's base power (Rapid Recon).  If read the way Matchstickman suggests, Tachyon would *always* have to discard the top card, and that doesn't seem right.  But her power doesn't actually say "reveal", it says "look at".  So the card is still technically on top of the deck (even if the player actually picked it up to look at), and doing a no-op doesn't leave any cards in an untenable state.

This Reveal/Look At distinction can also be significant when fighting Ambuscade.  If I understand correctly, "reveal" actions cause traps underneath the revealed cards to go off, whereas "look at" actions do not.
