It's hard to be sure, but it's likely that Ennead members hitting each other extra-hard was an oversight; the point was almost certainly that Ra, the Ennead members, and Anubis should all be good against each other.  House-ruling that nemesis doesn't apply within a single team is not unreasonable, though I for one think it makes the Sentinels a little more interesting to picture them constantly bickering like the Fantastic Four.  It's just unfortunate that some nemesis bonuses are more one-sided than others - Chrono-Ranger gets tremendous mileage out of his bonus against Plague Rat because of how often he shoots, while Visionary gets absolutely no benefit against the Dreamer, but on the contrary can get a Game Over just by playing two Psychic Maelstroms because Nemesis acts as a strict disadvantage in her case.

JimB:

More importantly to me, is that the actual intent of the nemesis icon, or was it meant to provide shorthand to indicate which villain is most dangerous to which hero(es)?

The ruling in my FAQ is exactly what was stated by Christopher on the forums - for simplicity's sake, ANY character with a particular nemesis icon will deal/receive extra damage to ANY OTHER character with that particular nemesis icon, except when damaging itself.  He explicitly said that members of the Ennead and Anubis would deal extra damage to each other if they were somehow made to hit each other.

So, it's not a misinterpretation of the designer's intent for the Sentinels members to have to deal extra damage to each other if they were ever to hit each other.  Again, the rule was done this way for simplicity, but of course you're free to houserule it away if you want.  We tend to play that the nemesis rule is only effective against cards from decks other than the attacker's to get around just the problem you're expressing.
