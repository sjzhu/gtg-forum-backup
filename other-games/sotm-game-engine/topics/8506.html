#1

Suppose that Haka has been incapacitated, uses his incapacitated ability to 'Select a hero. Decrease the next damage dealt to that hero by 2', and selects Mr. Fixer.

 

Suppose, then, that Mr. Fixer has Driving Mantis in play, which allows him to redirect damage that would be dealt to him if it is 2 or less, and then Mr. Fixer is attacked for 3.

 

My guess is that the way this would be resolved is that the attack would be reduced to 1, Mr. Fixer may redirect the attack, the amount of the attack remains reduced from 3 to 1, despite the fact that Mr. Fixer is no longer the target, and the next attack against Mr. Fixer would not be reduced.

 

I think another reasonable interpretation is that all of the above is correct except that the attack is reset to 3 for whatever the next target is. Another possibility, which I don't like as much, is that after the redirection, Mr. Fixer actually maintains the reduction of 2 for his next attack because he wasn't actually hit.

 

By definition, Haka's character card would have been in play before Driving Mantis, but what if this reduction had come from another card that came into play _AFTER_ Driving Mantis?

 

The only cards I can think of that redirect an attack based on the amount of the attack are Driving Mantis and Synaptic Interruption, and I can't think of any cards other than character cards that can reduce the next attack on Mr. Fixer or Tachyon. Haka has Haka of Shielding, but it only works on him.

phantaskippy:

I'd like to make the distinction between would be dealt and is dealt , but that doesn't seem to be about timing as much as grammar, I could be wrong there, at which point anything worded is dealt would not be allowed before redirection trigger timing occurs.

I've just completed a survey of all hero cards looking for redirect effects and found 10: Alchemical Redirection,  Amulet of the Elder Gods, Divine Sacrifice, Driving Mantis,  Enduring Intercession,  Lead from the Front,  Smoke Bombs,  Stealth Bot,  Synaptic Interruption, and Wrest the Mind.  8 of those use the phrase "would be", and those 7 also describe the targets the damage gets redirected from in some way.  The 2 cards that do not use the phrase "would be" are Divine Sacrifice and Wrest the Mind, which do not describe the original target of the damage to be redirected.   Given the clear consistency of this pattern, I think it's safe to say that the choice of wording is deliberate.

So, here's how I think the order should be:

  1. Damage is declared, which constitutes determining the original amount, type, source, and target of the damage.

  2. Damage increases/reductions that affect damage dealt by a source are applied.

  3. Damage "is dealt" by the source , at which point one or more of the 2 redirect effects might modify the target.

  4. Damage "would be dealt" to the target, at which point one or more of the 8 redirect effects might modify the target.

  5. Immunity may cancel the damage entirely, ending the sequence immediately.

  6. Damage increases/reductions that affect damage dealt to a target are applied.

  7. The target's hp is reduced.

Edit: Upon further consideration, I have no opinion on whether 3 and 4 should be 2 different steps or on what their order should be.  But, I think it's clear that damage is not dealt to a target that it's redirected from, so damage modifers that depend on a target being dealt damage shouldn't in any way apply before damage is redirected.

I think the order is:

 

1) Damage is declared, which constitutes determining the original amount, type, source, and target of the damage.

3) Damage "is dealt" by the source, at which point one or more of the 2 redirect effects might modify the target.

-- Steps 1 and 3 are the same thing
 

2) Damage increases/reductions that affect damage dealt by a source are applied.

6) Damage increases/reductions that affect damage dealt to a target are applied.

-- Steps 2 and 6 are the same thing. Also in this section is anything that changes the type of the damage. All of the increases, decreases, and type changes are considered in card-played order, but because the amount can't affect the type, it's easier to do the type first so as not to have to go back and redo the amounts.
 

4) Damage "would be dealt" to the target, at which point one or more of the 8 redirect effects might modify the target.

-- And if redirection occurs, one needs to return to the previous step of considering increases, reductions, and types, except that type is never based on target (to date), so that can effectively be skipped.

5) Immunity may cancel the damage entirely, ending the sequence immediately.

7) The target's hp is reduced.

 

I'm sorry I don't have references for them, but my memory is that the designers have clearly stated that immunity happens very last, after reductions, and that after a redirection, all increases and decreases are recalculated based on the new target.

7.  Trigger all "is dealt damage" modifiers.
