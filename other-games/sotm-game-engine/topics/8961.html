  1. A property of the damage dealer,
  2. a property of the damage receiver, or
  3. two properties, one belonging to each?

An example:

Wraith, while Galvanized, uses Throwing Knives to damage a villain who has By Any Means played next to them. Under normal circumstances, with no other relevant effects in play, Wraith would deal 3 damage. If Wraith is Isolated, here is how I would interpret the situation for each of the three answers to the question above.

  1. The damage being dealt is Wraith's damage. Since Galvanize and By Any Means directly affect the damage and that damage belongs to Wraith, they cannot be used. The villain takes 1 damage.
  2. The damage being dealt is the villain's damage. Since Galvanize and By Any Means affect the damage and that damage belongs to the villain, they can be used. The villain takes 3 damage. I imagine that most people will agree that this interpretation is not correct.
  3. The damage occurs in two phases. The first phase is Wraith dealing damage. Galvanize affects the damage that Wraith is dealing, so it cannot be used. The Wraith deals one damage in the first phase. The second phase is the villain taking damage. By Any Means affects the damage that the villain is taking, so it can be applied. The total amount of damage dealt to the villain is 2.

I think that 1 and 3 are equally valid interpretations - neither of them is obviously correct, or this discussion would not be happening.

Imbued Fire would never effect a hero through Isolated Hero, because that is a hero card directly affecting another hero.  The affect of the card is to increase fire damage by 1, if the damage being increased is the effect of a hero card, then the interaction cannot occur.  If the damage being increased is the effect of a non-hero card than it can be increased.

Imbued Fire's boost to fire damage would however make an environment or villain card that deals fire damage deal +1 damage.  Whether that villain deals that damage to a hero or not would not matter, it gets the bonus.  If a hero is the target of that damage the hero is in no way causing or being affected by the affect of imbued fire, since they are not dealing damage their damage can't be boosted.  The villain card effect is being affected.

 

 

By any means would not work because while By any Means is affecting the villain, the effect does affect a hero card effect.  The hero deals damage, the damage is a Hero Card Effect, it cannot be increased by a hero card effect through Isolated hero.

Twist the Ether on a villain would work because damage dealt by a villain is a villain card effect, and Twist the Ether can affect a villain card effect through Isolated Hero.

 

Summary:

1.  Imbued Fire's increase of fire damage could not affect damage caused by a hero card effect or dealt by a hero, but could increase damage dealt to a hero by a non-hero card effect.

2.  By any Means is a Hero card, and cannot affect the effect of a hero card through Isolated Hero, therefore it cannot increase damage that is dealt by a hero or hero card effect.

3.  Twist the Ether could affect damage dealt by a non-hero target by a non-hero card effect.

 

Rational:

1.  Damage is the effect of the both the dealer of the damage, and the card that causes it to be dealt.

2.  Taking damage is not an effect, but the result of an effect of the card and dealer of the damage.

3.  The wording of Isolated Hero stops interaction between Heroes, their cards and card effects.

Isolated Hero is meant to stop effects that specifically target other heroes and their cards. HP regain, damage increases (Increase damage dealt BY HEROES), destroy their ongoing cards, etc. and vice-versa. It is not intended -- nor can it logically be interpreted -- to affect any INDIRECT interactions, like increasing damage dealt TO villain targets (because it targets the villains, not the heroes), destroying villain cards that affect hero cards, destroying environment cards, dealing damage to any non-hero cards, etc. Because of the way By Any Means is written, it doesn't affect the hero at all, and it doesn't boost the hero's damage; rather, it just says "oh, this villain is taking damage? increase it!"
