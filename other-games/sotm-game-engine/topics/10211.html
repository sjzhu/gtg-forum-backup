You probably already know about how Sentinels of the Multiverse was panned by "Shut Up & Sit Down". It's easy to respond to criticism of games you love by shifting into "apologist" mode, defending the experience, the art, the intention, whatever.

But how can we approach criticsm with a clear head?

>G hasn't made any direct statements in defense of SotM (as far as I know) and, more importantly, no tabletop game is perfect. The SU&SD lads raise two major criticisms of the game, as I understand it:

  1. The lack of a simple, central, game mechanic
  2. The lack of synergy between powers, characters, and ultimately the players.

 

Perhaps there is some validity to these objections.

  • Do you experience these problems in your game?
  • If so, how do you address them? 
  • Does SotM have a simple, central mechanic that SU&SD failed to identify?
  • Do you have experiences of fantastic teamwork between players?
  • Do you use any house rules to address these or other concerns?

I'm really interested to know! I hope this generates some discussion.

In all honesty I'm impressed that you managed to pull those criticisms out of the video.  I personally thought they didn't address the aspects of the game that a lot of people actually criticize (swingy games dependant on character/villain/environment selection, some characters being "weaker" than others, certain villains consistently being difficult to the point of being unenjoyable or easy to the point of tedium) and a lot of their criticisms seemed to be:

"Sentinels is a terrible game!  There isn't even a board to play on or dice to roll!  They don't even have little character markers!  How can you possibly have a game that doesn't have character markers!?  Or a board with dice!?  There's no central game mechanic, like defeating the villain by reducing its HP to zero or play/power/draw for the players!  What madness!  I know!  Let's play dress-up and laugh about how silly the characters are!  That will be the most accurate, legitimate, and insightful review ever!"

There are valid objections to Sentinels.  They just didn't really address many that I have seen others voice or that I've experienced firsthand.

To answer your questions, though.
 

  • Not personally, no
  • I would address their concerns with further questioning.  What is their defintion of a "simple, central, game mechanic"?  In what ways do they feel the characters' interactions with one another are lacking?  I need more information to address their concerns, which I find somewhat disappointing that their comments were poorly conveyed and primarily composed of ad hominem arguments ("There isn't really a game here." What does that mean?  "Ra just throws Fire damage?  Fire?  What's up with that?" I don't know...what does fire damage do in this game? "Lady-arangs." Ah.  That was a joke, I guess. "You have to read all of the cards!  It's so difficult!" What is the difficulty?  Why is this a problem to your gaming aesthetic and what would you prefer be done?)
  • Cooperate with your teammates through playing cards and using powers to defeat the villain by reducing its HP to zero or otherwise defeating it.
  • In my experience?  Yes.  However, it's quite easy for players to not communicate, or not consult other players about their battle plan, hide information from the team for a bit of flair or an odd flare of competitiveness in a cooperative game.  This game isn't objectively the best game ever in mechanics or theme (though it is my favorite), but to say it lacks teamwork without giving me any examples or demonstrations for how it breaks down?  That isn't a review.  That's a few guys playing dress up and making fun of something.  Which is fine.  Just don't call it a review and don't pass yourself off as a show worth Penny Arcade's time.
  • My group actually attempts to play the game as intended, so we often explore the rules forum to make sure we're doing just that.  There have been some instances of rules clarifications that have bummed us out a bit (damage being required to keep certain cards in play such as Solar Flare, Pushing the Limits, Chastice, etc.) but as soon as we understand we have not been playing a rule as intended we make sure we play it as the creators intended.

 

Forgive me if I seem heated.  I have a literature degree and really get frustrated when I see bad criticism.  I'm not saying their criticism of the game was bad because I love the game and they're stupid.  I'm saying that their review was full of empty jokes and dress up, poor conveyance of what their criticisms were, and a lack of exploration of the game's mechanics/theme/story.  I've seen these guys do better work.  I know they have it in them.  This review was laughable.

I have friends that say similar things to what the review said.

The real problems they have with the game is they don't ever get to screw anyone over, or claim dominance of the game.

Because of that they never pay enough attention to really get the game, understand the nuances, and always try to give "objective criticism" of the game that ends up sounding dumb.

SU&SD do great with competetive games, I haven't seen them do any other co-op only games so I wonder if that was how they started off wrong.

Also they didn't have the best synergy in their team.  Ra, Redeemer Fanatic, Expat and Nightmist.

 

They do make one very good point, and that is the point of playing Sentinels.

What do you get if you win?  What do you get if you lose?  Really the result of the game isn't a big deal.

The point of the game however, is not that there are tons of heroes, the point is the theme.  You don't play Sentinels to beat a villain, you play it because being a hero and doing cool stuff with your friends is fun.  The point of the game is just having a damn good time being a hero on a team.  The beauty of Sentinels is there is nothing in the game that ruins that feeling.  Yes the rules are on the cards, but they are your cards, your hero's cards.  Every hero has their own rules, and those rules often let you have some great "But wait, I have this card that let's me ____" moments.

When I demo'd the game at Pax people really got into the setting, fighting to get to Baron Blade's volcanic island base, giving little care to our own survival as we desperately tried to save the Earth.

Saving the Earth was awesome, but the flip, the second half almost didn't matter.  You can die on Baron's flip side and the story still rocks.  15 cards in the trash is the worst loss in the game, because it is the worst ending to any of the stories in Sentinels.  In fact I think the group that had the best time was the one that had 12 cards in the trash and ended up with 3 incaps when they pulled it out.  They really loved the game, largely because the story was awesome, Baron felt ridiculously powerful, and we barely pulled it off.  Those fights are the most memorable, because the loss becomes more real, you start to feel the cost of failure.  Those are the moments when you fall in love with Sentinels.  That is why you play the game.

At least that is my experience.
