Greywind:

So, because they're 3 males and 1 female, they're an homage? The FF reflect the four elements.

They are a group of four individuals who are essentially a family, with one member who's a bit more of an outsider and one who's somewhat more juvenile and dependant than the remaining couple (Thing, Torch, and spouses Reed & Sue; Writhe, Idealist, and roommate HLPs Medico and Mainstay).  The sentinels's nemesis is La Capitan, who is an admittedly not super-obvious homage to Kang the Conqueror, one of the more persistent foes of the FF (Doc Doom is more squarely their nemesis, but was already co-opted as one of the inspirations for Baron Blade).  And as for elements, Medico is an energy being like Johnny, Mainstay is a "brick" who wields a metal chain, Idealist is a psychic who conjurers images out of thin air, and Writhe has fluidic shadow-stuff wrapped around him.  They can thus be identified with Energy, Solid, Vapor and Liquid, which is the more science-fictioney* translation of Fire, Earth, Air and Water.

  • As seen in Piers Anthony's Cluster series, and probably elsewhere.  Sentinels in general is a little more "sciency" than most comic universes; even Nightmist has a Lovecraftian bent which suggests that her magic is more of a psychic super-science that man does not and perhaps cannot fully understand, but which obeys certain rules that are internally consistent in their non-Euclidean way.  Gloomweaver is an extradimensional being from a universe with different physical laws that he can bring with him to empower his cultists.  Apostate claims to be a demon, but it remains unclear exactly where him and his servitor-creatures come from; fan theory suggests that the relics powering him and Fanatic might be products of ancient Atlantean super-science, which is also what Ra and the Ennead are empowered by (though it's implied that Anubis may be a real god after all).  This leaves only Argent, Akash'bhuta, the Naturalist, and possibly Kismet and Setback (and perhaps Anubis) as outright mystic forces which can't easily be equated to some form of superhumanly advanced technology.  On the scale of SF "hardness", I would say Sentinels is second only to Alan Moore's "science hero" universes published under the America's Best Comics imprint, where absolutely everything is put in Doc Savagey terms and "magic" is essentially nonexistent, with the exception of Promethea (who details it in such achingly precise terms, mostly derived from the Qaballah, that it might as well be just another branch of science, rooted in the powers of the mind and thus akin to psionics, which claims to be more science than magic, although it is admittedly arguable there).  The Big Two universes are much "squishier" about these things, often making little if any effort to justify why things work as they do, even when they are supposedly scientific (Green Lantern, I'm looking at you).
