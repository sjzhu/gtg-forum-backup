An incapacitated hero doesn't play a card or use a power, so they are intentionally given extremely strong incap abilities, because that's all they're good for.  It's true that some of them are VERY potent, but this just acts as a balancing factor in the game.  If you haven't lost yet, just give it time.  Even the most potent incap abilities are only sometimes the correct solution for the problem at hand.

If you think the villains are too easy, try fighting the Chairman (arguably even harder than Iron Legacy), the Advanced Ennead (about triple as hard as the non-Advanced version, and a heck of a lot of fun), The Dreamer with five heroes, or Advanced Citizen Dawn with three.  Make sure that you're selecting heroes at least semi-randomly, rather than picking ones you know will be strong in the match (whether alive or dead).  Among the less-strong heroes are Expatriette, Bunker, Haka and situationally Unity or Absolute Zero (those last two are "break the game in half" heroes if played to greatest effect, but can be a bit fragile; the others, except perhaps Haka, are simply a bit on the weak side); if too many of your games feature Legacy, Wraith, Nightmist, Scholar, or Omnitron-X (let alone several of these), who are the likeliest candidates for "tier 1" heroes, then you're probably performing a bit above average in general, though any hero is particularly strong if played against a villain whose weaknesses match what they can do.  (Dead Tempest, for instance, is extremely strong against Iron Legacy in particular, since almost all of Tempest's damage is the same type...against Miss Information or Grand Warlord Voss, both of whom have two damage types on their own card and more on their minions, he's much less efficient.)

With experience, you'll see that the balance point is pretty close to where it ought to be.  Sometimes you get utterly destroyed by a seemingly weak villain, sometimes you faceroll the tough guys.  I doubt very much you'll stay undefeated for more than a dozen or so games.
