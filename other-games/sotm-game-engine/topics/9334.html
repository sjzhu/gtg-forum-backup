I agree with Silverleaf.  If this were a movie, yeah...foreknowledge would spoil a lot of the drama inherent to one team member learning that "Oh, no!  My allies are about to walk into a trap!"  But, this isn't a movie and narrative tropes don't really apply here.

In the context of the game, the heroes are supposed to use every advantage at their disposal to defeat the villain.  The biggest piece of that arsenal is the use of human intelligence, as opposed to the Villain and Environment decks, which run pretty much automatically.  Sharing information falls into the purview of human intelligence.

Beyond that, this is a game that has a finite "win" and "loss" condition.  Either you defeat the villain or you don't.  The goal isn't to "play the game in an engaging manner" but rather to "help the team defeat the villain".  Where an RPG can still be interesting or even fun when the heroes lose, as a loss provides interesting plot points to hang new stories on, there's nothing beyond the single game of Sentinels (barring continual campaign-based SotM games, which are certainly not the norm).  In seeking a more desirable "win" over the "loss", sharing information is always a better idea than not.

For what it's worth, my group does use "closed" hands most times (though we do have players that play with "open" hands--it's personal preference, really).  However, we often ask or say things like "Is someone able to take care of that Ongoing this turn?" or "Hey, focus your damage on the big bad--I've got the minions this round."  And, anytime we reveal a villain or environment card, those get shared with the group.
