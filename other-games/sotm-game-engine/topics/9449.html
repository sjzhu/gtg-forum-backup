We're arguing about how to resolve the timing of the steps on the card.  In some cases, when an effect happens is absolutely critical.

Hypersonic assault (for instance) caused a debate, because there are targets that deal damage in retaliation, or in response to cards being destroyed (such as the chairman dealing damage when being hit, or the Matriarch hitting when birds are destroyed).

The debate centered around WHEN the secondary effect (targets can't deal damage) happened- did it happen as soon as a target was dealt damage?  did it happen after ALL targets were dealt damage?

If you resolved the entire card one target at a time (which was the eventual ruling), then you could pull off a lot of fun tricks like hitting the Matriarch first (so that she couldn't deal damage), and then taking out her entire army of birds with the rest of Hypersonic Assault.

 

In response to your question:  

you can apply the additional damage to multiple targets.  You do this one target at a time- deal a target 1 energy, then if it took damage, you decide if you are going to deal it 1 more energy damage.  Once this is accomplished for one target, move on to the next target.

A second instance of damage is a seperate effect.

It makes a much better comparison to Pride and Prejudice than Hypersonic assault.

Just Like Shock Rounds is not a modification of the damage dealt by the gun it is loaded in.  It is a seperate effect, triggered by the first effect.

 

Another comparison I would make is Imbued Fire vs. Twist the Ether.

The cause is damage being dealt by a qualifying dealer.

The effect of Imbued Fire is a modification of that damage.

The effect of Twist the Ether is a choice of effects.

 

There is not a perfect comparison for this game, but there are far better comparisons than Hypersonic Assault, and certainly they are not similar enough to use the same ruling.

I have no disagreement with how the card officially resolves.  It makes sense.

But it does not fall under a ruling from a card that different than it mechanically.

Remember. SotM is a co-operative card game.
