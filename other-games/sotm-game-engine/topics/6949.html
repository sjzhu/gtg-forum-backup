You played it right. The time when that doesn't work is when the Environment or Villain plays a card that prevents you from dealing damage. If Legacy is last and uses Galvanize and the environment flips a card that says "Heroes cannot use powers," then you kinda just wasted that Galvanize. The buff is still in effect, but you're not in a position to utilize it.

For this reason, I find that heroes that boost other heroes like to go first. That way you have the most information about the effects of your boost before you activate it and there's no craziness that can interfere with it this round. Also if you have draws it's nice to have the cards in hand to use this round instead of having to wait through an extra villain phase. I also find that heroes who debuff the villain like to go last becuase there's less computation and coordination involved in seeing what effect your actions will have.

Usually I'll be first or last so that I can manage the villain and environment turns in a row with mine. I generally play fairly efficiently and have the most experience with the game amongst my friends so the duty typically falls to me. This also means that we don't really try that hard to put all the heroes in their optimal spots because I like playing Ra as much as the next guy. I mean, if we were playing on advanced with a bunch of experienced players and Legacy really wants to get the damage boost out so that we can wipe the villain's setup minions on turn 1 instead of turn 2 then it absolutely does make a difference. But I often play with some inexperienced players and we rarely care to be that hardcore about it. Minmaxing can be fun from time to time, it just doesn't happen to be how I usually interact with SotM.

Oh the villains are most definitely meant to represent a range of difficulties. It seems that they fold in some consideration of complexity, but difficulty is a major factor.

If you're finding the game easy try skipping straight to one of the harder villains and see what happens. I actually like to introduce the game to new players by playing once against Blade, Ambuscade or Omnitron depending on what they're interested in, and then for the next game toss them at somebody really brutal like The Organization, The Martiarch or Advanced Ennead. The idea is to set us up for a loss or narrow victory. Whether we win or lose that second game, for my friends at least it tends to energize them about the game rather than demoralize them, especially if we just came off a really easy win.

If you only have the base set, maybe try Dawn? I find her more fun than Voss, personally. The times I've lost against Voss have usually been when he pulled every minion in his deck with Forced Deployment and we just couldn't burn through them in time. Which can feel kinda cheap and I probably wouldn't want that to happen in somebody's second game. When I've lost against Dawn, it's generally been a feeling of getting slowly overwhelmed by her Citizens, which actually feels really cool and fair and winnable. She's easily my favorite villain in the base set because her mechanics are really varied but still thematic, and tactical play pays off big.
