In the next edition of your card game, can you guys print a 3QR code, (sometimes called a 3D bar code) on every card?

This would be really slick, because with a small amount of space on the card’s face, you could facilitate a smart-phone style app that could be used to keep track of everything happening in the game so things don’t fall through the cracks.

Recently I picked up the Vengeance expansion and go together with my regular people to enjoy it, and although we were excited about the heroes, no one wanted to take on the villain encounter because the general consensus was: more cards in the game, more stuff gets forgotten (or even if not, the game is just elongated.) No one was thrilled to add four more villains, their cards, and the process of going through their turns.

The game is a lot of fun though.

Since we’re all software developers and designers, we had a quick conversation about how one could overcome such a challenge, and a QR code on each card would be it.

With QR codes, we could setup something like:

 

Specify hero players and turn order

Specify villain

Specify environment.

 

For each turn (villain/all heroes/environment)

Start phase:

                Show all of the math that has happened: dmg out, buffs, etc

                Have players resolve decision points (ties for most HP, selecting an ongoing to destroy, drawing a card, etc)

Main phase:

                Villian/Environment: draw and resolve a card

                Heroes: progress through the turn (draw/play/power or draw/draw)

End Phase:

               Basically a repeat of start phase, but for end-phase effects

 

Anyway, it wouldn’t take much to write a simple app to do this, and the QR codes would allow the smart-device to quickly know what is going on.  Obviously the concern is “wouldn’t it be just as easy to make an all-digital Sentinels of the Multiverse?”, but with this system, you still get the ambiance of playing a card game with friends,  and making sure everything happens true-to-form with the rules and nothing is missed (also, it wouldn’t take two hours to finish a game)
