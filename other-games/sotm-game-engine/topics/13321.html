[quote=“Fireside Chats”]

When a card enters play, the following order of things occurs.

  1. Ongoing effects on the card become applicable.
  2. Cards that react to other cards entering play now act.
  3. One-time effects on the card happen.

A well-known example of this is Informant. Suppose Informant and Combat Stance are in play and Legacy plays Inspiring Presence.

  1. Damage dealt by hero targets is now increased by 1.
  2. Informant causes The Chairman to play a card. Suppose it’s Undivided Attention, damaging The Wraith. Combat Stance reacts and its damage is increased by 1 due to Inspiring Presence.
  3. All hero targets regain 1 HP.

Another example: Supercooled Trisolvent Vat and Synaptic Interruption. Suppose damage dealt to Tachyon is increased by 2. When Tachyon plays Synaptic Interruption, the Vat does 3 cold damage to Tachyon. Tachyon may redirect the damage and destroy Synaptic Interruption.

[/quote]

Maybe it does have a stack, but not the same way MtG does.  In Magic, the stack is discrete on the ability level (entire abilities resolve before the game state reacts to the changes) and there is time to respond between the triggering of abilties and their resolution.  In SotM, the 'stack' is discrete on an action level, and abilties resolve as soon as they are triggered.  The second one matters less (since SotM doesn't have 'instant speed' actions (yet)), but these differences make the SotM system sufficiently distinct from Magic's stack that trying to understand SotM timing using Magic's stack will frequently provide the wrong answer, which is why we try to discourage people from saying that SotM has a stack.  Maybe it does, but the comparison is rarely helpful.

 

In this particular instance, the point I was trying to make was that spells in Magic need to distinct terms for 'playing' and 'entering play' because they use the stack to enter a limbo of 'will happen, but first giving you a chance to respond' that SotM doesn't have.  In SotM, the moment that you play a card it starts happening.

Text that is a static effect, like Heroic Interception's immunity or Inspiring Pesence don't activate or de-activate.

If the card is in play the effect is active.  If the card is not in play the effect is not active.

For eexample if The Informant is in play and Legacy plays Takedown no card will be played by the Informant's effect, because "Villain cards cannot be played" is a static effect, and the card is in play, so it is in effect.

Mistbound cannot stop the card play because the response happens before the text on Mistbound resolves.  Text on one-shots is resolved after the card enters play, by rule, and since the card play prevention is not a static effect it is not active when Informant responds to Mistbound entering play.
