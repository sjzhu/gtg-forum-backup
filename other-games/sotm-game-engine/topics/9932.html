This part of the topic will probably be split, but I feel it's important to highlight how frequently a person or group can play games of Sentinels and the desire to "rank" heroes.

I personally think a lot of the ranking systems here are not universally applicable.  A lot of it usually stems from the rankings' creator's personal preferences.  It's not that their insight isn't useful, but it's not useful for players with different playstyles.

That being said I do understand the desire to figure out which heroes are going to be the most useful in any given situation based on a limited timeframe to play games.  8.8 games a week?  That is awesome.  I wish I had played 8.8 games in the past two months.  I've been busy.  My group has been busy.  We've been exploring other games and playtesting a few new ones (while still finding Sentinels to be one of our absolute favorites, of course) in our limited amount of free time.

Being horrifically crushed by the villain the one time in a month that we can gather together the team and fight evil is an incredible way to bring the party down and feel really, really lame.

So yes.  We do occasionally try to figure out the "best equipped" or "most efficient for the given situation" heroes in harder games sometimes because this may be the only game we're going to get in for a while.  We can't just say "Oh well, we started the game two hours ago and started losing fifteen minutes in.  We can just start another!"

This isn't an argument for a particular playstyle, a criticism of other players ability to play more games (which, again, I'm wicked jealous that you can get so many games in and I'd love to play that many myself), or a diatribe that ranking systems are totally boss and anyone who disagrees is a big, dumb, jerkface with smelly cards.  It's my perspective on what the most noble intent of ranking characters and their abilities are.

I've yet to find a ranking system that I've found to be accurate, fair, or informative enough for my use and I don't really have any desire to create one.  But I can see why people want to try to convey this information.  Sometimes you just want to get your homework done, clock out at work, and team up with your buddies to put the smackdown on La Capitan without getting smeared across her Timeship for a couple of hours.  When we have free time we try to kick it up a notch, but when we've finally had the planets align long enough to be heroes for an afternoon we just want to try to make the game a little less swingy.

Apart from one day a month when I play with three or four other people (two of whom are PWatson and Matchstickman), generally it's just me and my partner playing, over the weekend (well, more like Sunday-Monday these days rather than Saturday-Sunday), in which we usually play anywhere between three and twelve (sometimes a little more - last weekend we managed to play in every environment and then had two more games after that, for a nice big sixteen) games. He plays two heroes, I play one - I prefer to just concentrate on a single deck. Plus I run the villain and environment decks as well. I don't play solo, though I do recall attempting a couple of solo games not long after I got Sentinels.

Another thing to take into consideration when setting up a game isn't just which heroes you're taking versus which villain (and in which environment), but how many heroes - it's generally accepted that the Dreamer and Miss Information are much easier when H=3, whereas someone like La Capitan is less of a hassle if H=5 because you have more people to dish out the damage and give up their stuff. Since I play most often with only three heroes, obviously I'm going to think of certain villains as less of a struggle than others.

There are so many factors to take into consideration when determining whether a game is easy or not - the number of heroes, the heroes themselves, their turn order, how well each player knows the hero(es) they're playing, the villain, the environment, and the order in which every deck plays its cards. As such I wouldn't ever try to rank heroes in any way because there's just too much stuff to take into consideration. Plus you could play the exact same setup (this villain in this environment, with these 3/4/5 heroes going in this order) several times in a row and I'm pretty sure that not only would no two games would last the same amount of time, but some would be wins while others were losses.

About the nearest I'd get to "ranking" heroes would be to say "he/she is good against this villain because…" or "this hero is pretty easy/difficult to get the hang of because…", but that's about it.
