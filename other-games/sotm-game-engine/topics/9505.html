#3

You only apply modifiers to the final target of damage, regardless of how much redirection occurs.

Example one:  Ra plays Inferno to fry all of the Ennead members.  There are only five in play, so Ra directs the sixth one-damage hit of Inferno to Mr. Fixer, who redirects it with Driving Mantis to hit one of the Enneads.  That Ennead member takes 2 damage, not 1, from the redirected Inferno hit, due to the nemesis rule.  If Fixer had simply taken the damage rather than using Mantis, he would just lose 1 HP (and call Ra a jerk).

Example two:  Nightmist has the Amulet of the Elder Gods in play.  When she redirects the villain's own damage back at himself, it is not increased by the Amulet; only damage she actually deals goes up.

Example three:  Argent Adept performs Syncopated Onslaught and Counterpoint Bulwark, both on Tachyon.  Tachyon plays a Sonic Vortex, dealing 3 + 1 damage to various targets. She selects herself as one of the targets, so the damage is reduced by 1 due to Bulwark.  That still leaves 3, so she can Synaptic Interruption that damage against some other target...and now that it's not hitting Tachyon anymore, Counterpoint no longer reduces it, so that target takes 4 damage from the redirected hit.

#5

Redirection is not new damage, it is just finding a different target, so any modifiers that would apply only do so once.

Let's say Adept puts a -1 damage taken on Nightmist.  Mightmist would take 5 damage, reduced to 4 (thatnks Adept!) she decided to redirect it.

That -1 only applies to damage dealt to Nightmist, so once she redirects it it is 5 damage again.

Same With Tachyon and Shielding Winds, say Tachyon has synaptic interruption in play and Tempest has Shielding Winds, Tachyon is going to be dealt 5 damage, and shielding winds would reduce it to 3.  Tachyon decides to use Synaptic to redirectthe damage back to the villain, the villain would take 5 damage, because shielding winds only applies to damage dealt to heroes.

 

Response damage, Wraith's Combat Stance, Ra's Flame Barrier, Omnitron-X's reactive and AZ's Isothermic are seperate instances of damage.

First they take damage, then they deal a new instance of damage.  Those new instances get all the applicable modifications.
