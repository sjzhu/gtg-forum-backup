When damage is reduced to 0 there is not damage dealt. Whipacorn's secondary effect only triggers when damage is dealt, so the hero will still be able deal damage.

Now something like the Wraith's Stun Bolt, it isn't reliant on damage being dealt, so it will still activate even if damage was not dealt.

Just for the sake of clarity, the game isn't balanced for 2 heroes - it's meant to be played 3-5 heroes. (2 players can play 2 heroes each for a 4 hero game.) Thus H (the number of heroes) will always be between 3 and 5. (Otherwise cards like the Whipacorn are just silly and don't do anything.)

If you're home-ruling a 2-hero game, that's not an issue - there are a lot of folks on teh forums who do that and there have been conversatiosn on it in the past. You just need to understand that it breaks the game and find ways to account for it (such as always use H=3).

Foote:

You see no sense in a very viable two player alternative? You may be convinced that your way is superior, but that does not demote every other idea automatically to nonsensical ramblings.

The incap idea has two immense problems.  It's like playing with three players but one of them is instantly killed, which means you're handicapped; 3-player games are tough in the first place, without a third living hero you're in even worse shape.  And conversely, access to incap abilities on the first turn is not something that normally happens, and may cause the game to go pear-shaped because the game doesn't assume it's possible.  For instance the incap ability (found on Tempest and I think Bunker) "take a card from your trash into your hand" is extremely strong, and being able to get that so early in the game may well result in Ra playing Flame Blast every turn and the villain dying far sooner than they should.

The game is designed to use three (living) heroes.  I figure it's better to trust that it's been rigorously playtested that way, and not to break the mold.
