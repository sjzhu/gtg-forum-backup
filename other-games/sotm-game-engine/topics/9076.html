So, there's a friend who comes to our game group who I've shown SOTM to once; for her first game, we gave her Haka to play, and we happened to be fighting Iron Legacy for the first time (not really the ideal entry point, in retrospect).  When pitching the game to her, I admitted that if SOTM has a fault, it is that sometimes the game "kind of plays itself", and after IL finished pounding us all into jelly, she agreed that this had been an accurate characterization.  Today I spoke to her again, asking whether she'd try SOTM another time, and she agreed that one game wasn't enough to really judge by.  She said it "wasn't the best superhero game", (which, since she was probably comparing it to the DC Heroes Deckbuilding Game, is obviously ridiculous), but I did admit that starting with Haka would tend to contribute to the impression of simplicity, and she indicated a willingness to try another time, to see if the game would appeal more to her when it wasn't such a quick and straightforward slugfest.

So, now I want to set up a game for her next time, recommending a Hero she can play which will offer her a lot of choices (without being too overcomplected - I'm thinking the Argent Adept is not an ideal choice, for instance), and pick a Villain and Environment which will likewise help to create a favorable impression.  So I'm collecting suggestions on how to construct a game which will be fair, offer her a good sense of autonomy, and not be too complicated.  Off the top of my head, my best guess would be something like Baron Blade in the Tomb of Anubis with her as The Visionary...can anyone come up with a better suggestion?  Blade is not incredibly difficult, but he has some depth and variety with his various Devices and such, and if we play on Advanced, he'll have a good fast clock going to make sure the game offers a credible threat.  ToA is one of the hardest Environments, and in particular gives players several choices (mostly on the Trial cards), so I'm hoping that will reduce the sense of automation that I alluded to.  And while Visionary is probably better played by a slightly more accustomed player, she does at least give the player a LOT of choices (far more than she can routinely use), which is what I was going for here.  The biggest argument I can see against using her is the head-scratching scenario of having Brain Burn in your opening hand and going "why would I ever want to do this", but of course Brain Burn is literally designed to combat Baron Blade, so that's one more reason I think this might be a good match.  And ToA has plenty of dangerous cards to make Mass Levitation a non-dead card, plus the Baron has minions to Wrest and Ongoings to Divergence...I think it should be a fairly solid and well-rounded battle, but I'm certainly listening if someone has a better idea.

Yeah, I burned a few of my friends by giving them Visionary and Legacy as their first hereos, when we fought Akash-buta for the first time. Don't get me wrong, I like this guy, but that was low. Especially since his blog is rather popular in Poland).

 

First of, heroes. A short list of heroes you should NEVER give to new players

-Absolute Zero: I love him and he can be used properly, but giving someone whose main power is "hurt yourself" is a bad idea, even if you explain it.

-Fanatic: A lot of cards require some forward thinking and preparation, which an new player won't have. Most of the time they'll waste their cards or do nothing, having no fun at all.

-Visionary: Same as Fanatic. We all know how Wrest the Mind and Twist the Ether can dominate the game, but don't expect a newbie to catch that on their first try. My guess is, they'll spend their turns using Mind Spikes and maybe controlling other decks. Not fun.

-Argent: No.

-Nightmist: "Oh cool, I can use spells and stuff! Lets see, this one hurts me... okay, the next one... hurts me even more, the next one doesn't do damage... this one makes me throw away cards and to get them I need to hurt myself more... this one lets me get more spells, which will hurt me... why is she your favourite again?"

-Omni-X: "Oh, cool, I have all those weapons and... wait, they're gone? But he only hit me once, what do you mean...?"

 

And my list of BEST for new players:

-Bunker: Sure he takes time to set-up, but see their grins when you destroy a few Voss minions while Ammo Drop is in play. And when they fire their full powered Omni-cannon at Voss's face.

-Tachyon: "She sucks, there's no damage, I just use those cards and not much happens, when am I going to do something usefu-... wait, Lightspeed Barrage? What's that? Oh. Wow. WOW!"

-Wraith: Let them know to tell you when they draw Improntu Invention. When they do, tell them "congratz, you won". Because they will.

-Haka: Smash. That is all.

-Ra: Burn. That is all.

-Tempest: This guy does EVERYTHING and hardly ever gets a bad card in play.

-Chrono Ranger: Time traveling cowboy with bunch of futuristic weaponry? Awesome. And he's cool to play to.

No words on Expat or Fixer, but I'll say 'NO' to them.

 

Villains, this list is short because anyone from expansions should be left for later. That said:

-Baron Blade: Has everything you need to know about villains, plus he is rather fast to play with.

-Voss: Despite rating 3, he's pretty easy and simple to understand. Especially reccomended when she uses Bunker with Ammo drop.

 

Enviroment: Megalopolis. Just Megalopolis. It's one of the fairest Enviroments, has a bit of everything and thematically, its a giant city, which is easier to imagine then island full of Dinos or 'Realm of Whattheheckisthis'. And it's much more forgiving then Rook City.

Different situations are different and different people like different things.

 

While I'm happy to make a general suggestion (Baron Blade; Insula Primalis; Tempest, Ra, Chrono-Ranger, Haka, Fanatic, Tachyon, or Expatriette while someone else plays Legacy), if you know something about the person you're teaching, you can take that into consideration.

 

For instance, if they will be bored with too easy of a game, maybe stack Blade's deck or use Voss instead. If their attention needs to be grabbed right away, stack Insula Primalis so the most exciting dinosaurs are on top. If they will be bored if they don't have a lot to think about in regards their hero, consider the more complicated heroes but with some introduction. I've seen both Absolute Zero and NightMist be grokked immediately by new players. If the new player might be overwhelmed by the analysis of Absolute Zero, by the complexity of Argent Adept, by the obtuseness of The Scholar, and by the self-mutilation of NightMist, they still might enjoy someone like Tachyon if they are primed up front that they have two cards that take advantage of the number of Burst cards in their trash or Bunker if they get an intro to his modes and the advice to make the best of what you've got rather than try for the ultimate combination.

 

Finally, let's talk about The Wraith. She's been called overpowered, she doesn't get called very complicated, yet she rarely comes up in discussions of heroes to give to new players. She has a reasonable amount of direct attacks, so I think even new players that might not appreciate her other capabilities as much will have something to do. For someone who might get bored with a more straightforward damage dealer, I think The Wraith would be an excellent choice. Plus, female, and I think it reasonably likely to appeal to a female gamer to get to play a non-exploitative female hero.

Heroes: I usually have new players choose between Wraith, Tempest, Ra, or Haka. The first two give them a lot of choices but don't require a ton of planning; the last two let them do a whole mess of damage, which new players often enjoy.

Environment: Never Megalopolis. It's relatively simple, but there's just way too much of: "Wait, what can't I do this turn?". I want new players to feel energized and inspired, not shut down. I usually play in The Block or Silver Gulch. They are easy to explain, relatively simple in terms of mechanics, and they give the players more things to do rather than taking them away.

Villains: I always have them play regular Baron Blade first. His mechanics are fairly straightforward. He's got a compelling storyline that you can relate in about a minute. And the reveal when he flips is great. I don't tell them about the front/back side mechanic beforehand, so they don't know that he'll pull himself out of the wreckage and start chasing after the heroes until it happens.

My aim is usually for the first game to be quick - to get comfortable with the mechanics. I let them make all the mistakes they want while facing Blade. After we steamroll him anyway, the next question is: "Ok. Now do you want to try a more challenging battle?" The answer is nearly always an enthusiastic yes.

Hey Envisioner,

 

I've had the most success giving newer players Tempest, Tachyon, and Visionary (depending on their personalities/preferences).  Tempest always feels like he's being relevant, with several powers and ongoings that hit several enemies.  Have someone on your team play Legacy and give him a damage buff and your friend is sure to enjoy swatting down minions like flies.  Tachyon is in a similar vain; she always feels like she's doing something.  She can typically play a lot of cards in a turn which makes your turns feel more fun, and once you get ahold of her "count your burst cards" attacks, it can be a blast racking up a big hit.

 

Visionary is a little different; I'd let her use her if she wants something more complicated than Tempest/Tachyon, yet is okay having control rather than dealing damage (most of my friends hate Visionary because they don't find deck manipulation fun; they'd rather be bursting down minions with Ra).  But on the rare instance you get someone on your team who likes that sort of gameplay, Visionary is perfect for them.

 

When choosing an environment, I say Silver Gulch.  It's thematic and a little cheeky, yet is pretty balanced in terms of aiding/hurting the players.

 

For bosses, I'd say someone who is easier, yet still provides some fun twists.  Voss and his Forced Deployment can cause a complete "Oh ****!" moment for everyone, creating a fun yet tense moment as you all pool your heads together to figure out how to deal with the impending army.  Baron Blade has a little bit of everything; he creates a dramatic early game with his moon beam (and if your friend plays Visionary, she'll have fun using her deck manipulation to put the Villain trash on the bottom of the deck) and has enough threatening cards to create a good, medium difficulty.

 

Others that can be fun are Gloomweaver (easy, but thematically awesome with his zombies and cultists and relics) and Apostate, though don't let her use Tempest against him since he has a few ways to get -1 damage, which can make it boring.
