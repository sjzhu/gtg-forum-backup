This came up last night; it was more or less academic when it finally came up so I went with the nicer answer, but I wanted to see if there's any official wisdom on this point that I've missed.

The question: If Wraith has Smoke Bombs out and multiple hero targets are tied for lowest HP, can all of them be treated as "the target with the lowest HP"?

In this case, Wraith and Unity were in the same game, and Unity had out two Raptor Bots, both at their full complement of 2 HP. An environment card dealt 2 damage to all hero targets. We knew everything but the Raptor Bots was taking 2 damage (modified appropriately by whatever they had in play), but we weren't 100% sure which of these two scenarios played out with the Raptor Bots:

1 - We designate one Raptor Bot as the lowest HP hero target; its 2 damage is redirected to the hero target with the highest HP and reduced by 1. The other Raptor Bot takes 2 damage and is destroyed.

2 - We declare the first Raptor Bot is the lowest HP hero target and the damage is redirected as above; then declare the second Raptor Bot as the lowest HP hero target and redirect the damage as above.

Both have things about them that are counterintuitive; on the one hand, Scenario 1 makes the most sense in that only one target is ever the one with the lowest or highest; on the other hand, when there is a tie you get to pick who is highest or lowest for purposes of that effect, and you determine low and high HP for every iteration of damage.

Again, this did not change the situation in the game significantly - Unity was rolling so many bots at that point that the loss of one Raptor would not prevent her from KOing the villain during her end of turn phase - but I realized I wasn't sure what was correct.

2 is correct. You evaluate each hit individually, so if the card has the lowest hp, it will redirect regardless of who else does. If you have ties for highest HP you cn also choose which of those to redirect it to, but you work this out each time you apply the damage.

I am still, slowly working on refining my 'master plan' for how to resolve an attack.

The latest:

Given the source of an attack, the destination of an attack (target), the type of an attack, and the amount of an attack:

1) Apply all cards that affect the type of the attack in card played order.

2) Apply all other cards in card played order. If a card changes the destination of the attack (target), reset the amount of the attack to the original amount and return to the beginning of this step, but don't consider that card again.

 

I rather like the above. I'm toying with extending it to include a couple of other things -- when and how the highest and lowest targets are determined, the order of attacks if multiple attacks are done, and dealing with card played order for multiple cards in play at the start of the game. After that, I'm considering broadening it to be about generally resolving actions, instead of just resolving attacks, dealing with such things as effects that occur when a card is played or when a power is used and dealing with such things as the ambiguity of dealing damage -- initiating an attack versus causing HP to be lost.
