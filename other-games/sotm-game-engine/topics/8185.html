Been playing Sentinels with some friends for about 8 months off-and-on when we can.  Recently I got all the expansions and we've been playing a lot more.  However, our win ratio is terrible.  We can barely hold a 25% win ratio (my best guess).  We all really like this game for numerous reasons, but a really big factor is that we can play heroes we like.  At least that's what we used to be able to do.  Nowadays it seems that if we don't metagame and find heroes that should do well against a boss, we just get stomped.  We all have favorite heroes that we just enjoy playing more than others, but I feel like we can no longer play those characters when we want to if we want to win.  It's not nearly as much fun when you lose all the time.  :(

We've noticed recently that it seems most of the villains chain terribly.  We'll be going along just fine, taking out minions, trading damage with the boss, chipping away at him...then he chains and we're all dead.  Plague Rat, Citizen Dawn, Iron Legacy, The Ennead, Akash'Buta...we've had games with all of them where we hit a point in the game where they chain and just wipe the floor with us.

If I had to place blame, I'd probably put 60/40 between the villains/environments.  Environments are definitely out to get the heroes, and it shows.  Either the environment triggers something for the villain, or vice versa, and sometimes both.

One of the most severe examples was a game we had recently against Omnitron.  I forget which environment it was, but we had to play the top card from the villain and environment decks in order to get rid of an environment card.  We did that on the environment's turn, which pulled out Omnitron's 15hp device that does damage at the start of his turn based on it's hp.  That hit everyone for 15, then his play phase pulled out his card that does damage based on Equipment you have in play.  Between the end of environment and start of villain turns, Wraith went from 26hp to dead without any way we could have anticipated or mitigated that.

TL:DR

My questions:  Do you guys find that you need to metagame to win?  Do you see the villains/environments chain frequently?

It's gotten to the point now where I'm now building a "cheat sheet" of short snippets about each villain/environment so that we know not to use certain combos together and/or what to expect.  IMO, that takes some of the fun out of playing but I feel that we either have to do that, or we lose.  It could just be that we've had a bad luck streak...but it just doesn't feel that way.  Any input is appreciated.

Thanks  :)

I think it may be helpfull to adress specific aspects of metagaming seperately. I would like to focus on deck familiarity, deck efficacy and player efficacy. Other posts have advised becoming more familiar with the hero, enviroment and villain decks, and doing so will greatly reduce the need to metagame, but it does take time. For now, you and your group may benefit from acceleration and reveal effects. Wraith’s Infrared Eyepeice, Visionary’s Precognition and Nightmist’s Astral Premonition all let you reveal cards from the villain deck and choose which card will be played next. If you are unfamiliar with a deck or haven’t played it in some time than this gives you a refresher without stopping the game. If those characters are early in the rotation then later characters can react accordingly.
Certain decks are more effective against certain enviroments and villains, and deck familiarity will help identify that. In your example Visionary is very effective against Omnitron, and Wrest the Mind can shut down and Electro-Pulse Explosive. Did Wraith have an Infrared Eyepeice in hand or play? A well timed Inventory Barrage can also save Wraith from Technological Singularity. Equiptment heavy heroes in general will have a harder time with Omnitron.
I have been playing since before Rook City, and I generally only pick a character specifically for a villain when playing with new players or when someone else is playing a character for the first time. Legacy, Team Leader Tachyon, Argent Adept and Scholar are all good choices for protecting or assisting a specific player or character. I hope at least one of these thoughts is helpfull.

As Mr Fixer once told Unity (in the "A day at the workshop" one-shot special collector):

" Superpowers don't make you a hero. What makes you a hero is victory on evil.

You can't teach victory. But you can teach, and learn, how to use whatever you have at hand in such a way that you, or your team mates, or someone else, will ruin the bad guy's plan. It's hard work. Days and days and days of training. Lots of defeats, too. And lots of hurt pride. 

You don't save the world by shaking your hand and yelling "I'm OverPawred !". You save it by going over your defeats, tending your wounds and going back to fight again. Even if you have no chance to win. Even if no one will know. Because, if a hero falls to a villain in the forest, it doesn't matter if there's someone to hear it : innocents will suffer in the end.

So stop sulking and rebuild this Baron Blade Bot, we need to go back to training."

 

The villains chain often, and can hurt a lot, but they are not the only one to do it : the heroes, too, can chain, either independently or as a team - or sometimes as a counter to the villains chains.

Howhever, they don't chain "automatically", like the villain : learning how to make "damage control" on the villain, or how to use a specific combinations of cards or heroes takes time. As does learning to "feel" the rythm of the game.

I play a lot, but still learn a new thing every game. The way I play Absolute Zero or Mr Fixer today is completely different from the way I played them a few months ago. It's also true of all the others - I don't play Legacy at all like I did in the beginning...

There is a lot to learn with each hero deck. You can know the cards by heart, it doesn't help : you have to learn how and when to use - or not use - the cards. Each hero works differently - each new hero is whole new skill to work. As is each villain, even each environment. Taming a Kraken is no small task.

I remember very well how Baron Blade was tough, at first. He seemed unbeatable. I fought against him many, many times, until I "suddenly" opened my eyes and found how to handle him, how to use my heroes against him. I still fear the Chairman, but it's because I never "trained" against him.

I would suggest, next time you lose, to do the same fight again, witht the same decks. Just shuffle, and back to work. And again. and again. Or concentrate on a given set of heroes, and have them face different villains. The more you play a given hero or villain, the more you can "innovate" and use old cards for new effects, new combinations that suddenly become so "obvious" that you wonder why you never thought using them before, and so on.

The point is : there is a lot of subtle tactics and strategies in SotM, things that are not obvious at all. Some can be explained (Flamethrower's guides are wonderful), some can't.The more you play, the more you learn - and the game "changes".

As for "metagaming" and chosing the heroes before hand, I couldn't say. The only time I didn't randomize my games was when I decided to fight all villains in sequence with the Freedom Five -and even then their order was random. They were defeated by Omnitron and Iron Legacy.

 Sometimes we randomize, sometimes we just pick whoever we feel like.   It's extremely rare that we pick a hero specifically to be good against a villain.   We play advanced mode,  I'd say we win on advanced maybe 60-70% of the time.

 

 

A lot of it honestly is knowing the cards.   If you're playing Omnitron in Rook City,  you need to be aware of the fact that playing a villian card on the environment turn could bring out Electropulse explosive.   If you're caught off gaurd by that, you pretty much lose.  However, now that you know that can happen, you might not be so quick to voluntarily play villain cards on the environment turn vs Omnitron next time around. 

 

  General Advice:

 

To stop the chain effect, or at least help against it,  playing a "Control" type hero can help a lot.   Visionary is a good choice for avoiding the worst stuff in the villain deck.  Tempest's Into the Stratosphere card can give you more time by putting cards back on top of the deck, thereby slowing down the villians plays.   Legacy's Takedown, or Nightmist's Mistbound can stop the villian from playing cards entirely.   Using these effects can go a LONG way towards victory.   If you can reuse them (Reclaim from the Deep,  Vernal Sonota)  then so much the better!

 

Don't undersestimate cards that can nullify damage for a turn,  these are some of the most powerful cards out there.   Ground Pound,  Hypersonic Assault,  Grease Gun, Heroic Interception.   Reuse these as well, if possible (Reclaim from the Deep, Vernal Sonota).

 

Take down priority targets,  ignore ones that don't do much damage (so long as they dont have an annoying accompanying effect, such as Gene-Bound Gaurd).  It's much better to take down a Frost Hound (2 damage to all heroes)  instead of a banshee (3 damage to 1 hero). 

My wife suggests:   If you're still losing; pick your team well.   Not necessarily against a villian in specificly,  but pick for synergies between heroes.   It's a team game,  so make sure your team is strong.    1 damage dealer is a good start,  1 team support character is good too.   If you're going against a villian with Ongoings,  it's nice to have someone who can deal with that.   

 

She says maybe pick Legacy and Ra as 2 of your heroes, and build the rest of the team however you like   (Tachyon would be a supreme 3rd choice to fill in the role of ongoing/environment control,   Wraith would also be good here).  

If you still have problems,  come to GenCon,  and we'll personally help you out =p.

In short, no, metagaming is not required. It was stated long ago that any set of heroes could defeat any villain in any environment, it would just take a bit more ingenuity or luck in some cases. After a few expansions, I still think that to be the case.

I like to play random games, so the roll of the die chooses our party composition and environment, but we sometimes choose which villain we want to face. After randomizing the heroes, we choose the play order and assign accordingly. This is the important step, since heroes have certain synergies that help out the team in certain seats. Visionary is a great candidate for going first, setting up the heroes and/or villains for the rest of the phase. Legacy, too, is a first-turner, mostly to help me remember Galvanize, but oi.

Of course, I've played a ton of this game, and I know the general feel of most of the decks, what to expect, what to look out for, and that helps tremendously in judging how hard a time a team might have in the upcoming fight. But it's that feeling of overcoming the severe obstacles that make the game such a joy. I used to think Matriarch was the most difficult villain of the bunch, but with certain heroes' abilities, she crumbles to the floor and cries soon after her arrival. There's a level of understanding that comes with the game as you play more and more.

And, yeah. Winning less than a quarter of the games seems extremely low. We win far more often than we lose. Unless we play Iron Legacy, in which case, he wins a bit more than we do.
