There have been some arguments about what happens when a character has 2 effects which redirect the "first damage" they take in a turn.  The two positions are:

A) First is first, period.  There is only ever one "first", and so only one of the effects is actually used.

B) By a long-standing ruling, "damage fealt = HP were reduced".  If the damage was redirected, HP were not reduced, so the damage was not dealt, so this was clearly not the "first" damage, as it was not damage at all.  The second effect remains available to use.

Possible refinements to A / counterarguments against B:

A1) When a card says "First damage dealt to <foo>", it really means "First time <foo> is assigned as a target of damage."

A2) Both redirect effects actually *trigger* on the first damage, even though only one is able to have an effect.  The other one is expended uselessly.

Thoughts?

It's worth noting the difference between damage reduction/increasing effects and damage prevention/redirection effects.  Damage reduction affects damage being dealt, while prevention/redirection triggers when damage would be dealt.

 

Reduction effects have been covered with Word of God - if the damage is redirected or prevented, the reduction is not used up, unless the reduction was necessary to trigger the redirection or prevention.

 

Prevention/redirection has been covered in previous threads (which were linked to in the thread you linked to) - all redirection/prevention effects will be used up the first time that damage would be dealt.  (I do not think they trigger if reduction has already reduced the amount of damage to 0, though).

 

I'm pretty sure that covers all scenarios.

My thoughts:

When you activate a "Next damage" effect, it is the same as saying "the first time you would be dealt damage after activating this effect. . ."

The First time each turn effect is the same as "At the start of your turn, the next time you would be dealt damage this turn. . ."

Beyond that my argument is:

1.  Redirection cancels all the effects of the initial attack, changing it to a new target.  Twist the Ether shows us that, the damage alterations are canceled, and the attack starts over with a new target.

2.  Because of this, once Soldier's attack on Mr. Fixer is redirected it is treated as not happening.  Mr. Fixer wouldn't take damage, that attack never happened.  This is supported by the idea that "Next time" effects are counted as not applying to redirected damage.  If the attack counts as clearing the "Mr. Fixer would take damage" hurdle, then it was the next time Mr. Fixer would take damage and those effects would be gone.

3.  The ruling on Mantis vs. Next damage effects show us what effects are still treated as having happened, and that is the ones that are critical to the redirection or prevention.  Therefore if a "Next Damage" reduction is needed for Mantis to redirect the damage, then after the attack is restarted Mantis and the "Next Damage" are consumed because they were both required for the redirection to happen.

Side Note:
