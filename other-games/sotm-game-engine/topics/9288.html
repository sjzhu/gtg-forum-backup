Skipping your turn does not give you an extra card draw. Skipping your turn is not a option the game gives to you.

If you do not play a card in your play phase or use a power in your power phase, you may draw 2 cards during the draw phase.

As Foote pointed out, in order to draw that extra card in your Draw phase, what you actually did was you opted not to play a card during your Play phase and then opted not to use a power during your Power phase. This would not have any impact on any Start of Turn or End of Turn actions/events. 
 
Playing a card during your Play phase, using a power during your Power phase, and drawing a card during Draw phase are all optional activities so you may opt not to do them at will... however, going through each phase of your turn is not optional so you cannot just choose to skip any portion of it without explicitly being given the option to do so by another card.

Also, cards that act during the same phase of a turn do so in the order they came out. So if there was a Meteor Shower out and you're playing the Scholar and play an elemental form card, then next round you would make the check for the Meteor Shower first - if you choose to skip your turn (to get rid of the Meteor Shower), then your turn ends right there without getting onto making the check to discard something to keep the elemental card in play (so you would just keep it in play without needing to discard). I'm pretty sure that's right, anyway :).

If a card tells you "Do X, then immediately end your turn" (eg the Scholar's "Know When to Hold Fast" or Nightmist's "Mist-Fuelled Recovery"), you still get your end phase (if you have anything which would trigger at that point).

Also, something which lets you draw an additional card during your draw phase (eg Nightmist's Mistform or Visionary's TK Cocoon) lets you draw three cards on every turn after the one in which you play the card, since you're neither playing a card nor using a power (therefore would normally draw two cards).

Playing cards, using powers, and drawing cards is always optional unless a card text states otherwise - if it says "You may draw a card", you don't have to. If it's like TLT's base power, "Each player draws a card", there is no "may", so you have to do it.

It also depends on WHEN the trigger takes place. Usually such effects say "At the start of a hero's turn, that player may skip their turn to do X." So you follow the order-of-play rule, as previously mentioned.

 

Also, "Immediately end your turn" is different from "skip (the rest of) your turn." Ending your turn does not allow you to skip end-of-turn effects, as it skips your play, power, and draw phases and moves directly to the end of turn phase. Skipping your turn skips the end of turn phase as well.

If the Scholar has played an elemental card and then, say, a Meteor Shower comes out, when it next comes to the Scholar's turn he looks at what things trigger during his Start phase. The elemental form card was out first, so he checks that and decides whether or not to discard to keep it in play. Then he looks at the Meteor Shower and opts to skip his go - pass straight to the next player, the Scholar gets no further phases.

If Omni-10 was playing, and the Meteor Shower came out after he'd put out all his components, all of said components would get to activate and then he would make the choice as to whether or not to skip his go and get rid of the Meteor Shower.

Compare to a Nightmist example. She has a pin stuck in her by Gloomweaver which will cause her to take damage at the end of her turn, but the round before that she had played her Starshield Necklace. On her Play phase, she plays Mist-Fuelled recovery, shuffling a trash into its deck, restoring some hitpoints, and ending her turn. That is, she moves straight to her End phase (thus is unable to use a power or draw any cards) and may choose to discard something to heal from her Necklace (since that was in play first). After choosing to do so (or not), she then takes damage via the pin (unless she has the Amulet out and chooses to discard two to redirect said damage).
