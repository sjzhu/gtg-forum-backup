“”It’s thematic” should never be a discussion point when talking about balance. That’s the sort of seat-of-the-pants game design that leads to terrible games. Repeating that “it’s thematic” doesn’t make the argument any stronger.

#30
Powerhound_2000:

Just because you’re not a fan of the mechanic it does doesn’t make being thematic bad game design. 

This is a spurious argument. That theme has zero to do with balance has nothing to do with my personal tastes. It’s something that’s objectively true. By trying to make it about personal taste you’re attempting to avoid discussing the issue based on its merits.

Theme isn’t intrinsically balanced or unbalanced. Theme can enhance a player’s enjoyment of a game, but being thematic doesn’t fix balance. It’s like arguing that a bridge with engineering flaws can bear more weight because it’s a nice shade of blue. If someone points out it might collapse, “you just don’t like blue” is a meaningless counterargument.

Ignoring game balance is bad game design. I can’t count the number of designers I’ve worked with who have done exactly that, made up mechanics based on “feel” with no consideration for balance at all. Seat of the pants design can make decent games by accident, but usually it doesn’t.

I mentioned that there are lots of nasty cards in villain decks, but Fickle Fans is different because it penalizes effective play. Usually it’s the opposite - it’s much easier to deal with an EPE, for example, if you’ve cleared the board of all other threats.

Jail Break is a stronger analogy, though not exact. Jail Break, Get Back In There, and Forced Deployment undo the elimination of threats, and one way to play around them is to wound targets rather than eliminate them, because you know a “bring ‘em all back” card is coming. Those cards penalize you for eliminating rather than damaging targets, which is usually good play.

In the case of Forced Deployment, you have advance warning, unlike the other two. You can play cards to defend, like Grease Gun or Heroic Interception. If you eliminate Forced Deployment yourself, instead of waiting for the villain turn, you have an opportunity to eliminate minions before they can act.

Still, Fickle Fans is different, because bringing back minions isn’t an immediate loss, and there are things the players can do about it. That’s not true of Kaarga’s scoreboard, there are ongoing cards which reduce damage to heroes, but no ongoing cards which reduce abrupt gains in crowd favor.

I think mechanically Kaargra is one of the most interesting fights as there are lots of elements to keep track of. It’s easy to lose in my opinion because Kaargra’s win condition is essentially a clock - if she gets to 20 she wins or if she defeats the heroes she wins.

The heroes have a slightly harder job - they must get to 20 and make sure Kaargra is off the board. This makes the game interesting as you can’t just damage race her and win - you need to control your favour so that you’re getting to 20 as you take her off the board - too many actions spent killing her before you get there are wasted (as Get back in there can come out and ruin your day) and too many actions that score points before you defeat Kaagra are also risky. 

Fickle Fans helps to keep the fight balanced in my view - it acts as a limiter on how overwhelming the heroes can be and also can act as a catch up if you’re falling behind. I’ve definitely lost to it, but it’s much easier to play around than some of the other super cards villains have - some of those might not instantly win the game, but some can easily spell the start of a slow enevitable defeat easily enough. Knowing Fickle Fans exists makes it much easier to play around though and I’d argue that as long as you know it’s a possibility you can limit its impact on your game so that it isn’t hurting you (and can even help you).

I’m not saying Fickle Fans is some amazing card; I do see the argument that the existence of a solitary card that’s so game wrecking that you have to work out a way to negate it is in fact bad design. It’s one of the things I don’t love about Sentinels, and other people here have pointed out that Kaargra’s deck is hardly unique in that respect. Omnitron and Dawn among others can punish you for doing too well too. And a draw of Fickle Fans literally handing the game the win might feel outrageous, but there are other situations where that can de facto happen with other villain decks even if it takes more than a turn to play out. I’ve had setups with Citizen Dawn where my heroes never had a turn with cards in hand and the game was essentially unwinnable after the initial villain turn. That didn’t feel fun either.

I don’t like getting my board wrecked either, but in my specific circumstances that possibility spiced up my end game. I really like Parse too, but when I feel like victory is assured but I still have a bunch of game to play out, I’m not really exulting in the engine I’ve built up as much as I’m just going through the motions to see the win screen. It’s not literally cheating, but I do feel like I rob myself a bit when I’m playing with the perfect setup to counter a villain. Bringing deck manipulation to Citizen Dawn so I never have to see Devastating Aurora feels like I’m bypassing the challenge her deck is designed to present. I prefer to avoid bringing GI Bunker to fight Apostate or Mr. Fixer to fight Plague Rat too. The game being in contention right up to the last round is what made it fun.
