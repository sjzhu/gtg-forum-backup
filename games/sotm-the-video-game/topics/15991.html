I've been playing lots of games lately with the new Season Pass 2 heroes (Parse, Naturalist, Sentinels, KNYFE), and while I've had great success with the first three, I've found K.N.Y.F.E. a little hard to use effectively.  

 

Any K.N.Y.F.E. loyalists out there -- can you tell me why you'd pick her as a damage dealer over Ra, Haka, or Chrono Ranger?

--Ra has better one-shots (Fire Blast, Inferno, Flame Spike), a great passive damage card in Flame Barrier, better AOE (Scorched Earth, Inferno), an awesome buff card (Imbued Fire) better card draw (Excavation, Summon Staff), limited self-healing (keep destroying the Staff of Ra and replaying it for 3 HP), and some situational cards like Scorched Earth and Flesh of the Sun God that can be devastating at the right time.  

 

--Haka doesn't have quite as much consistent damage, but his excellent card draw (18 of his cards give him extra cards in some way/shape/form) pretty much ensures that you'll get the Taiaha out and start doing 3-6 damage every round.  He doesn't have Ra's self healing, but Ta Moko will keep him in a fight longer than KNYFE; his "damage everybody" AOE card (Rampage) trumps KNYFE's, Ground Pound is a game-saver; Savage Mana can completely neuter villains and environments that count on playing targets and getting them back out of the trash, as well as provide a devastating 20-30 HP finishing blow.  

 

--Chrono-Ranger is, of course, the damage master, because he can do so much out-of-turn damage, and Hunter and Hunted is a big risk/reward card.   And again, not only does he have great card draw to get his party pieces out, all of his card-draw cards also do damage: Get a bounty or two in play, then play (ex:) Displaced Armory -- boom!  do damage and pull a new weapon.  Or play Sudden Contract -- boom! do damage and pull a new bounty.  Or play Eye on the Prize -- boom!  do damage and draw/play.  Or play any of the get-bounties-out-of-the-trash cards -- boom!  do damage and put a bounty back into play.   His dart-thrower makes him a capable damage-reducer, and he even has a little environment/ongoing control with Temporal Grenade.  His only real damage limit is AOE

 

KNYFE has poor card draw, no environment ongoing control, can't buff other heroes, can't prevent damage to herself or other heroes, can't heal, can't do villain deck-control...in short, it seems that she does no more damage than Ra/Haka/CR, but without any of their extras.  

 

The biggest problem for me is that I can't seem to find a KNYFE "master strategy."  With Ra, I generally try to get the Staff and Blazing Tornado in hand--which, given Summon Staff and Excavation, is not very hard.  By then, I usually have a bunch of cards in hand, and can pick and choose from the various one-shots as necessary.  

 

With Haka, it's all about Savage Mana.  Get the Taiaha in hand, get buffs either from Haka of Battle or other heroes, and begin storing up cards/preventing the villain from getting cards back, readying for a huge 20-30HP blast.  In between, play Ground Pounds when necessary to save your fellow heroes, and an occasional Rampage to clear the board.

 

For CR, it's working towards Hunter and Hunted.  Use his great card draw and Jim's Hat to get as many useful bounties out there as possible, then play Hunter and Hunted and do massive out-of-turn damage.  Yes, it gets him smacked, but with 4-5 bounties and Danny Boy or Masada in hand, there are very few things he can't kill before they kill him.  Or, keep smacking the villain with the dart-thrower and reduce the villain's damage.

 

With KNYFE, there doesn't seem to be anything to work "towards."  Get her knife and gauntlet in hand, and then just play one-shots until she runs out of cards...which is usually soon.  There have been many turns in which I've skipped playing a card because none of them seemed all that useful...or they depended on KNYFE doing herself damage, and she was too low on HP...or they needed a buff/equipment that she didn't have in hand...or they needed more cards for her to play/draw, which she didn't have.

 

Any thoughts?  How do you make KNYFE effective?  Right now, she is sitting far at the bottom of my hero-drawer, like Expatriette and Bunker.

For a long time I was like you and didn't much like KNYFE, favoring her slightly over Ra because of while she also lacks nuance she is slightly more complex in the combos she can set up. I think it's important to note that she is the only complexity one hero released outside the enhanced edition so she isn't necessarily made to open up new possibilities everytime she plays. Still, I think she has more to offer than you (or I, at first,) saw:

While she is great at focusing or going Mano a Mano against the main villain (Hello, Progeny,) being able to consistently put out tremondous damage as Powerhound pointed out, with Flawless Execution she is also able to handily take care of low health minons when needed (and has lots of multi-target damage cards to spread that destruction around.) 

While Rampage might have better raw damage output, Incidental Contact is more focused and can allow you to selectively damage non-hero targets less if you so desire (if say an environment card is going to attack the lowest target and you want to make sure that's not you,) plus if your teammates can redirect damage you can get double the benefit from Contact. If you team her up with Visionary and Twist the Ether that card becomes a monster attack. 

Of the four heroes you compare her to she has arguably the best ongoing destruction. Ra and Haka have none at all, and while Chrono can search for his it destroys itself, while KNYFE's sticks around (albeit it must be used indirectly.) 

Her lack of card draw doesn't bother me much, all heroes have weaknesses, but Null Shield is there if you need a quick boost. 

With the possible exception of Chrono, KNYFE also has much greater potential for monster turns than the other heroes: if she has the health she can pull off several power uses in a turn, each time she uses Battle Field Experience she can follow it up with a damage dealing one-shot, and she can begin her turn with a Primed Punch if needed.

What she lacks in Ra's damage consistency she makes up for in combos, what she lacks in Haka's draw potential she makes up for in focused and carved attacks, and what she lacks in Chrono's top damage potential she makes up for in less risk and less time setting up. At least for me. 

Plus with Rogue Agent you can help your teammates out while still setting yourself up, that's certainly not something the other three can do. 

I don't think you're ever going to love KNYFE, especially if you're looking for something to build towards (since she has explosive and destructive turns,) but she can do a few things no other hero can (Servo Gauntlet's ongoing destruction, Flawless Execution,) and might grow on you after a fashion.

KNYFE is a great 3 player team member, at least her original card is.  She is not reliant on burst damage, she has a solid base damage that increases with almost all of the cards she gets.  She has a lot of good cards, and very few big turn cards, so she doesn't really need that card draw to get her good cards, most of them are good.

She self buffs, self defends, has an ongoing destruction option, and can run some bursty turns when called for, but still be reliable the next turn as well.

 

Chrono is a fantastic damage dealer in a larger team, but his low early damage means you need help to get through the initial villain setup, while his indredible ability to scale up in damage makes the end game Chrono time.

 

Haka is a really strong and versatile damage dealer, with multiple big turn scenarios and Savage Mana.

 

Ra cannot match the other's single turn outputs, but makes up for it with Flame Barrier and really solid damage each turn.  He doesn't have big single turn cards, his best is setting up a big turn with Solar Flare, which requires a setup round and self damage.

 

Of the four of them KNYFE is my favorite for a 3-hero party, Chrono is amazing in a 5 hero party, Haka is good on any team and Ra I like best with 4 players.

Not that I limit their use to those team sizes, but that's where I think their style does the best.
