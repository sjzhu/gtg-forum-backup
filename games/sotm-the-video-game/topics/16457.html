Well, a simplified example would be if there was exactly one environment card you wanted to avoid.  In a normal game, after 15 rounds there is a 100% chance that you will have encountered that card (barring any other deck discarding effects).  If you used RC Wraith's power every single turn, after 7 rounds there's approx 50% that you dodged that card.  After the reshuffle and another 8 rounds there is another approx 50% you dodged that card again.  So in this case there's a 25% chance you got to avoid the card after 15 rounds as opposed to the base case of 0%.  So it's a relatively minor improvement and unless it's 3 player or Akash, most games don't last 15 rounds and it costs Wraith 15 power uses.  Also consider after 7 rounds, it's 50% chance you dodged the card, but in the base case, it's also 50% chance that that card hasn't shown up yet.  So for shorter games there's basically no improvement.

But consider looking for exactly one good card.  RC Wraith essentially goes through the deck at double speed, meaning you should be able to get your card 1-7 rounds sooner.  For short games that may only last 5-7 rounds that's a great boon.

Regarding RC Wraith, it is perhaps useful to realize that because the environment deck is entirely randomized and the card that is looked at is removed from the deck, a power which looked at the bottom card of the deck rather than the top card would have a stastically identical effect.  So you can see that (at least until around the 7th round when the power use affects whether or not you reshuffle) Wraith's power doesn't really effect the cards that you get.  What it does do is a) gives you a pool of cards that you can put into play if you wish, and b) give you information about what is left in the deck.  

 

So you can't use Wrath's sleuthing to avoid bad cards, but you can use it to get good cards, and to know how likely it is that certain cards will appear, making it easier to plan around them.
