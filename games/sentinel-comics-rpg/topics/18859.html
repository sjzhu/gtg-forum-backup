Played issues 1 and 2 of the starter set today. Had a lot of troubles with it, hoping anyone could provide some advice.

1) Powers/Qualities - sometimes it seems very hard to match up qualities/powers with defined abilities. For example, Legacy's Galvanize just doesn't seem to readily match up with any of his powers.

2) Twists - really not sure what principle based twists are supposed to look like in play. Should they have immediate definable effects or be mostly flavor? The sample generic twists are all fairly specific and mechanical, but many of the sample environment twists are just sort of "flavor". Like what would "Which whippersnapper just showed you up" actually DO?

3) How are you handling deciding whether a hero is at close or long range and how many foes they can hit with a "burst" effect (Like Legacy's Flying Smash or Bunker's External Combustion)?

4) Boosts/Hindrances - how specific/generic should these be? It is pretty easy to think of boosts that could apply to many situations, and also easy to think of very specific ones. Like, can Bunker just upgrade to get "targetting enhancement", which would help with pretty much any attack? Or would you do something narrower in field, that would only help with specific attack actions?

5) Collections - each hero starts with a collection. Is the intent that you only refer to things that have happened in the episodes so far to use it, or can you just make up stuff from imaginary prior episodes?

6) Along the 'make stuff up' lines, how about Bunker's Principle of the Tactician? Are you supposed to actually prepare before hand to use this, or is the intent that you can invent how you prepared on the spot?

7) Overcome - seems like some of the scenes have involved overcomes that were kind of hard for the PCs to figure out. How much detail do you give about these being available?

Guess that's a start...this was all pretty awkward over all. Seemed odd to be so strongly mechanical, but then so up-in-the air about how to apply things. And seemed like the heroes could only succeed because the GM is specifically sandbagging the enemies and initiative order.

#5

1. Ask the player what they’re doing to galvanise the team, are they keeping an eye out and shouting warnings about upcoming threats, then awareness, making a display of strength, then strength, flying around and attempting to look inspiring, then flight, making a speech after taking a massive blow from the enemy, then vitality.  If your players can justify why they want to use that power then let them use what they like.

2. Principle based twists should have an effect on play, for most you can get away with using one of the other dice in their pool as a hinder or as damage (the whippersnapper showing up Legacy caused his concentration to falter, AZs suit overloaded and hurt him etc, etc.), for others you might even justify adding additonal minions to the scene (Tachyon didn’t notice additional robots arriving as she was too focused on her destination).  The principle based twists are there as suggestions if you can’t think of a relevant twist off the top of your head so using them to justify hinders, damage and minions is perfectly fine so long as it makes sense.

3. Fudge it, but keep it sensible, if three robots are attacking bunker then he can definitely hit all of them, but if he’s in the park near the mothership, he can’t hit the snipers on the rooftops as well.

4. As specific or generic as you want them to be, in general I’d say permenant, non-exclusive buffs should be more specific but it’s up to you and your players how specific you make them.

5. Imaginary stuff is fine especially for the starter collections, so long as it’s something that could happen in that collection.  It’s worth noting that the starting collection is the characters origin issue as well.

6. Absolutely make stuff up on the spot, how many comics are there where the hero sets everything up beforehand but keeps it from the reader until the plan goes off.

7. I generally give the first steps for known objectives, for example during the first issue I made sure to have locate argent adept as an objective on a card in front of the players along with the enemy lists so they’d remember why they were there in the first place.  Remember that the players are on a semi-strict time limit so making sure they remember what their goals are is a good thing.

That being said they didn’t know that the argent adept needed medical help until they found him, so feel free to keep back information they haven’t uncovered yet.

i hope this was helpful to you.

Yeah, the "there aren't rules for this" is touted as a feature, not a bug.

Twists don't necessarily contain mechanical effects, no.  If the outcome is flatly undesirable, like a bystander being injured, that can be the whole thing.  It can even set up an additional challenge that has no mechanical consequences for failure (the bystander slipped and is about to fall, you can save them with an Overcome).  The heroes are meant to be heroic, and them failing at things they value is punishment enough.

Boosts/Hindrances - how specific/generic should these be? 

I think there have been people dealing with this issue for years in the comic book industry. I think I first heard the issue encapsulated by this phrase.
Q: "How strong is spider-man?"
A: "As strong as the writer wants him to be."
When writers take over a character they are not given a set of stats about them. I would assume that their best reference is the previous stories. Read enough stories of Spider-Man and you will know how to handle most anything he will encounter in your story.

The problem is that not all of us know what these characters can do, Just how big is Bunker's External Combustion? I have no idea, could be 10 feet radius could be 100, could be several city blocks. Maybe people who have been religiously reading up on all these characters might know, but I don't.

In the case of this RPG, you the player are the writer of  your character. You decide how large External Combustion is. Of course your editor (GM) might have some feedback for you. Some people might see this as great freedom, and I agree, but every bit of freedom you have, adds a small burden onto the players and GM to adjudicate the effect while in play. You comfort with this will vary.

However, if you do think the burden is too much, I think that this issue will be circumvented with the new character creation rules. Now you will be able to imagine how your character works as part of your character creation, and since it comes from you and your imagination it should feel like less of a burden to you (though the GM will still have some say in all of this.

Each principle should have a leading question for minor and major twists
So if you have Bunker and the Principle of Order you have these questions
Minor Twist
What element of disorder causes your plan to fall apart?
So if you used this to let’s say fortify a building that is about to fall over. I can think of things like this.
You forgot that the water pipes in the building have already been stressed and are leaking, causing your supports to slip. You have to constantly keep adjusting your solutions into the next turn giving you a Max+Min hinderence on the next round.
The air conditioning units on the roof have slipped and fell to the streets causing chaos (causing the scene tracker to increment by one).
Major Twist
The reinforcements where almost in place when you found that the building had slipped too far for you to put them in place, you use all of you strength to push the building back into place causing damage to your suit taking Max+Mid damage.
Your physics application ran out of storage space, in your haste to free up memory, you deleted your rare bootleg copy of your favorite band in concert. I don’t think we have any abilities in this game that are “activate to lift two extra tons”. Rockstar has “The Principle of Strength” which does two things. 1) It says that you do not need to roll for mundane acts of great strength. Throwing a car? Not mundane. Throwing a dude or breaking down a wooden door? No roll. She just succeeds, because that is “mundane”. Will that definition change per table. Likely. The second thing it does is overcome a problem with brute force. Does that mean she can throw a Mobile Defense Platform? I don’t think she could do it with just the strength of her arms, but if that player decided to reinforce their body with rocks and it was a small thing instead of a mobile city? It might work.

 

 

The bigger question though seems to come in with the bolded part. Why do we have ranged combat and close combat instead of just combat? The same reason we have “Electricity” and “Infernal” even though mechanically they fill the same slot. Because the story is the important part of the design. The action you describe would be close combat to me, because the more important action is the slam and flying through the air.

 

Difference between Strength and Close Combat? I’d allow someone to throw a chunk of masonry with strength to hit a flying enemy, but I wouldn’t allow a close combat person to do that. But, if the person wanted to run up some rubble and tackle that flier out of the air, I’d let them use Close Combat. Because that makes sense as something they should be able to do.

 

Can a person run up to a villain and punch them, if that villain shot from the hallway of a convention center. Maybe, I’d say likely. I could see a super hero get a panel of run and then a panel of punch bad guy.

 

It might be hard for certain groups and certain playstyles, but this system was consistently designed for being kind of vague with distance and the like so that the action of the scene wasn’t stopped by people being a single 5 ft square to far back to do the awesome thing.
