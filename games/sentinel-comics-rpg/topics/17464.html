Sounds like you've mostly got it. (We're also looking at ways to clarify this in the core book.)

When an environment acts, all environment threats also act. The environment itself can either introduce a new threat or activate one of its twists on its turn as well. The same is true when someone chooses the environment to take one of their twists. The environment has access to Green, Yellow, or Red of twists/threats depending on what status the scene tracker is in.

It is generally rare that there will be more than one threat for the environment, but like you say, there are exceptions, and they'll all act when the environment does.

Hope that clears it up.

Alright. I'll go through all of your questions to the best of my ability.

All minions of the same type get their own turn in the turn order, so if a military base environment created 3 soldier minions, those soldiers don't go on the environment turn, but they do all go on the same turn. (This means if there are 20 of a type of minion and it becomes their turn, the heroes have to deal with 20 actions, so it's probably a good idea for players to keep the herd thin.) On the other hand, if you have a soldier minion and a sniper minion, those are two different types of minions, so they have two different turns.

On the environment's turn, you can do one of two things: activate a twist or introduce a threat. If you activate a twist, you take an environment twist from the appropriate GYRO section and activate it (or you make up your own cool twist. Being creative is part of the fun. And remember, some twists can actually help the heroes out.) It can be minor or major, but you're right. Specific major twists can only happen once. If you introduce a threat, you take a threat from the threat section and put it into the scene. Any minions (and I assume lieutenants) you put into play because of a threat or twist don't act until the next round.

Insula Primalis gives you a threat section that tells you to have every one of its threats act on its turn instead of taking their own. I assume it does this because it introduces a ton of unique dinosaurs and having them all take their own turn would make the round super long. In any case, as it's different from what's in the Gameplay Guide, I believe this is a special exception and not the rule.

On Insula Primalis's turn, you would first move the scene tracker, and then introduce a threat if there is no threat. If there is a threat, all of the threats act on Insula Primalis's turn. I have no idea if you still get to activate a twist or introduce another threat. Sorry.

A basic environment turn would go something like this.

Advance the scene tracker ---> Introduce a threat or activate a twist ---> Pass the turn order to the next person

I hope that helps! If I got anything wrong, please feel free to correct me.

The spider groups all have their own turn and aren't connected to the scene tracker. Since there's no environment, the scene tracker gets its own slot. (The only thing that can go along with the scene as written is Argent Adept if he's been saved.)

2) I had the impression that the environment specificly acted "between rounds?" Example: Bunker acts last and passes turn to Baron Blade to start the next round. Since that's a new round, should the envrionment do it's thing before Blade goes?Or, should it be that, assuming the envrionment has yet to act, Bunker must pass to the envrionment, which then passes to Blade to start a new round? And wouldn't this mean Blade could then pass right back to the environment for its turn in the round? Yikes.     

The second one is correct - the environment/scene is a "character" that has its own turn slot. It has to be passed to at least once each turn. However, as a GM, keep in mind that most environments aren't explicitly working with the villain, and the decision of who to pass to isn't necessarily an "in character" decision. Unless you really want to be an aggressive GM, the environment should pass to whichever character makes the most interesting story.

First, important to note here that a Megalopolis Environment (capital E-environment as far as rules goes and not just the setting for the scene) it would have a writeup that includes other possible twists for the Environment to do on its turn - like Insula Primalis and Tomb of Anubis have but not anything in issue 1.

  1. Robot Spiders take turns as minions, followed by Megalopolis Environment taking a turn and activating police2) Robot Spiders take turns as minions, Police take turns as Minions, then Megalopois Environment takes a turn and does something else entirely 3) Something else entirely like the Police getting activated twice or something.

Robot Spiders would have their own slot in the Action Order. They're villains/bad guys/etc. 

The Environment also has a slot in the Action Order, under which the following things would happen (GM can choose which order): 1. Advance Scene Tracker, 2. have all Environment-created minions act, and 3. introduce new environment threat (if it doesn't have one already) OR activate one of its twists based on the status of the scene. 

Hope that clears it up.
