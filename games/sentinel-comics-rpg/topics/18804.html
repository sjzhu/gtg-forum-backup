In our group, there's often a resistance to setting a game in a preestablished fictional setting (like Marvel, Dresden Files, Star Trek, etc) because depending on the setting, there will be people who know a lot of the lore vs those who don't. And while it hasn't seemed to come up when we play in published RPG settings like Golarian or Torg, that "I don't get that reference" thing seems to be a bigger deal. 

That said, when I run one-shots, it's always with canon characters and folks have a good time. 

I honestly don't know how folks will feel about playing in the Sentinel Comics setting vs an original super-setting.

Yeah, when I run games in established settings, I always make sure to avoid using established characters (even if the players don't think I'm playing them "wrong", I always feel like I'm doing the authors injustice), and to write the story in such a way that knowing the setting's history isn't helpful at all.  Even then it can be tricky once you reach high-level threats--if something is going to destroy all Faerun, why aren't the epic-level heroes like Elminster getting involved?  I vastly prefer to design my own settings using concepts and models from existing ones, unless the game's default setting exists only in the game and doesn't have any lore any of us could be familiar with.

If I were to run a Sentinels game, it would be either a Disparation universe, or cover obscure events in the past that could reasonably be unknown to SC fans.
