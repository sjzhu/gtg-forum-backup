#12

The five basic action types are actually effects, not really actions.  Bunker can absolutely bring down the ceiling no matter what mode he's in, but what he can do with it is restricted.

  • Attack: Crush and damage the enemies.
  • Hinder: Trap or otherwise impede the enemies.
  • Boost: Clear a path to make an action easier for an ally.
  • Defend: Create a barrier in between enemies and allies.
  • Overcome: Hard to create a blanket case, because Overcomes are usually done to beat a specific challenge or remove a (named) mod.  But clearly there are situations where it could work.  But an Overcome isn't going to deal damage or create a mod.

FastasaTrain:

Technically, to remove Boost mods (in this case, the sniper bots’ cover), shouldn’t a Hinder action be taken instead?

I would agree, except the “shouldn’t” is “couldn’t”. There are multiple ways to do things – it depends on what someone wants to do! You could take an Overcome to directly counter the specified bonus (the mod provided by the Boost action), but you’d have to descriptively word it in a way to do so. For example, if someone has a bonus because they got a shiny new knife, you can take an Overcome to disarm them and break the knife. You could also attach a device to them that causes them to lose control of their muscles, giving them a penalty through a Hinder action that could negate the bonus.

FastasaTrain:

Regardless, if we were to go back to my initial example, could Utility-mode Bunker have used the overcome action to stop the spider bots in their tracks (but not damaging them) by having a heap of rubble fall on them?

Again, how are they descriptively doing that? They can’t shoot the wall – weapons aren’t functional in Utility mode. But can they pull it down? Probably not without pulling it down on themselves, but that would depend on the situation. Maybe they find an I-beam they can use to leverage the wall away from the building, crashing it down on the bots. And if they don’t roll well enough, maybe the twists involve people hiding inside the building; some of the wall DOES hit Bunker, damaging the suit; or some part of the wall slams into the suit and punctures it, acting as a Hinder.

Focus on how they’re doing it more than what they’re trying to accomplish, and it might be easier to see possibilities. Get into that description and get the players to do so, also, and it’ll help. One of the things we talk about in demos is describing what the panel looks like in the comic showing this situation, which helps get folks into the right mindset.

NOTE: Different GMs are going to have different approaches. We’ll see if the final rules clarify all these questions when they’re available…
