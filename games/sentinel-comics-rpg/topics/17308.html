So yeah, I'm bad at Thread titles, but also I want to make sure that people who are going to be playing the RPG with me (which I think so far only PlatinumWarlock lives close enough that might be an issue) don't get a potential spoiler.

 

So, platinium, don't read ahead please, just in case.

 

 

So, part of my idea for where I want to take the Sentinels RPG is this idea of a brand-new city built as a "turning point" of the world in the wake of Oblivaeon. However, I realized that building a city in a year is absolutely ridiculous. So, I asked myself, how could they acconmplish this.

 

I came up with a potential answer, but I'd like to run it by Sentinel Lore people to get their take on how possible this could be.

 

They rebuilt a version of Omnitron. Sort of.

 

Omnitron was a learning robotics factory geared towards warfare. Now, there are two major stupid moments here, 1) learning and 2) warfare. Combining them is even more monumentally insane. However, looking at Omnitron 4 and Omnitron 1, we see something potentially interesting. A near endless supply of robots, building things.

 

The timeline would seem to make the original Omnitron event as somewhere near the start of the Freedom Five, comic time is wierd, but that had ot have been almost a decade ago easily, so what if someone went back and looked at what went wrong, and decided to do it better.

 

Use the Omnitron Tech, without the Omnitron Programming. Potentially possible, Omnitron pieces were under study in multiple labs before Oblivaeon made Cosmic Omnitron and the old lab which built Omnitron probably had some paper files which were retreivable.

 

So, you rebuild the original "omnitron", and change two things. First, you do not program for war, you program for engineering and architecture. That slashes the danger immediately, because there is no reason to program anything involving actually weapons systems. Then, you highly limit or eliminate entirely the capacity for learning. You don't really need a learning robot, you want a mobile factory which can build a city by following the plans you give it. I can't imagine any advantage to learning software at that point, though predictive software and software to go back and correct problems like fixing the angles of a building that was drawn improperly could be useful.

 

I'm planning on calling her Brighid after the Celtic Goddess of Smithing, and the company which tested the technology by building this city with it is defintely keeping things on the down low, they spent years invested in being careful with this and making sure it wouldn't become another rampaging monstrosity, they don't want people avoiding it out of fear if they learned or suspected the origin. Which, I'm happy for complications to arrive later, but I'm not looking for a rebirth of Omnitron in my city, I'm just looking to use existing technologies, because if you stop and think about it, this sort of system could revolutionize construction in a lot of very interesting ways, and is a far better use of the Omnitron system than trying to solve War.

 

Thoughts or anything obvious I missed?

I like the idea from a overall technical standpoint, but there pretty much has to be complications built into it or else it ends up being just a superfluous detail. (i.e. it's nice for verisimilitude but doesn't have any real impact on anything) Stuff like giving it back the learning capacity and having it constantly rebuilding things in ways that make sense to an AI crunching a vast amount of data but is bewildering to its non-AI inhabitatants with lesser brainpower (not necessarily lethal ways, but in ways that do stuff like cause amusing new oddball groupings of people or give villains things they can take advantage of to cause trouble), or having it be hackable by both villains and heroes alike.

Since you could go with more mundane alternatives; like a small rural city that takes advantage of its security through obscurity protecting it to try and build itself up into a bigger city to attract survivors from other cities to build an economy, only to find that also attracts trouble they weren't prepared for (particularly if the city already has a small cadre of lower-level heroes used to dealing with similarly low-level threats). I feel like if there's going to be something more out there going on, it should be something that has a likewise more out there influence on the storyline.

I would say mainly: don't overthink it.

First, it's your RPG game.  Do what makes sense to you and facilitates the story you want to tell.  If someone (including you) thinks of something that could go wrong, use it if you like it, don't if you don't.

Second, this is based on a comic book.  Comic book writers are full of bad ideas.  And by "bad ideas" I mean all sorts: things that just wouldn't work, things that would go catastrophically wrong in the real world, things that aren't socially/politically/economically feasible, etc.  It doesn't necessarily make the stories worse.  Sometimes they get retconned (which is fine), sometimes they just gloss over them, sometimes they go through convoluted methods to explain them (which themselves may or may not make sense).  It's all good.

Is Birghid reasonable or practical in any kind of "logical" sense?  Not even remotely.  It would be astronomically expensive, require permits that don't even exist, and would be a matter of worldwide debate and protests.  The programming is also a non-trivial issue--bear in mind that self-driving vehicles do use machine learning techniques, and Birghid includes that and much more complicated tasks besides.  But does she make for a good story?  There's clearly a lot of potential there; it's up to you how you want to use it.  If I were a player in a campaign set there, I'd have no complaints.
