I got to try out a game of this at Gencon.  I am pretty sure we were using the starter pack.  We played as the freedom 5 plus Unity in a plot involving some robots showing up.  Overall I did not enjoy the game, and I think at least part of this was due to a GM not being fully prepared.  And I don't hold this against them.  Gencon is hectic and exhausting, and this is just as a participant.  I can only imagine how difficult it is for the people actually there to work.  And I know this person wanted to do there best but for whatever reason could not.  So there is that.

I would say apart from that, my impressions of the system were not great.  Part of this can be written off as a bad one shot con game.  And one game is a very tough spot to judge an RPG from.  But several features indicate that this is not my kind of RPG.  The big, glowing in neon lights, stand out problem that I have with it is the metaplot.  It became very very obvious that the game was designed almost entirely to drive players through a pre established metaplot, which will be supplemented at regular intervals with new published material, and which already has several "issues" that players are expected to go through.  I am a huge fan of Sentinels, and I love the fictional universe Christopher and Adam have created.  But I do not play RPGs as a kind of cheap amusement park ride to see all the stuff someone else has already made.  This is a personal preference, and I think that if you are drawn towards the RPG as a way of seeing first hand the kind of stories they want to tell about the sentinels universe, you will likely have a much warmer impression of the game.

The mechanics are mostly fine.  They exist, and are there.  They mostly won't get in the way of anything, but on the flip side don't do anything interesting on there own.  I suspect they were kept fairly generic and bland so that a GM would not have to wrangle with the players as much while trying to force the metaplot down a certain direction.  Cam Banks (designer) has compared it to FATE and Apocalypse World.  I am fairly familiar with both, and I would say that it is reminiscent of FATE in a lot of ways, so if that is your jam, it might be worth looking at.  But it is absolutely not a *World style game, and does not have the features that make such games compelling.  Do not get this system if you are looking for fiction first super hero action.  Its very rules light and cinematic, but fiction will just as likely follow the mechanics than the other way around.  

I got a chance to look through the how to play booklet, and it did not impress me.  If the GM was struggling because they lacked experience, then this would not be of much help at all.  The GM section was generic in the extreme, and will not give solid advice to GMs who are looking to improve but are lacking an understanding of what will make them successful vs not.  For this reason alone, I would not recommend buying the starter kit unless A.) you want the adventures that are packaged with it, and B.) you have someone who is experienced with GMing traditional RPGs who is willing to run the game, that way he can use whichever style works for him rather than being completely in the cold in having to learn it without effective tools.

I realize this is a fairly negative post.  I am not trying to be down on the fun that people will undoubtedly have with the product.  This is more a case of "If you are like me and are looking for x, y, and z, this isn't it".  And for everyone else, I really do hope you have a blast playing this game.  If my complaints aren't things you'd worry about, it should hopefully be really fun.

@liarliar  I would say that Sentinels does not use that principle.  As an example, when Absolute Zero attacks a minion, he gathers his cold die, his ranged attack die, and his health die, and rolls those.  You can encourage him to describe what he is doing, but "I am doing a ranged cold attack" generally handles it.  Nothing breaks if you ignore the fiction.  Whereas if you ignore that principle in AW stuff just doesn't work and you end up with nonsense scenarios that don't make sense until you go back to fiction first.  

And yeah, published adventures are typically railroady, not denying that.  But my impression from the starter kit is that this is pretty much the meat of what they give you.  The starter kit doesn't give you enough to go off and make your own scenarios with your own characters.  You'd have to do a lot of hacking to get those essential pieces.  And even then a lot of stuff seems to be missing.  When the full rules get released, a lot of these concerns may go away, but in that case you still wouldn't really want the starter set unless you want the metaplot.

There is something I think is really cool, which is major and minor twists.  These are nifty ideas that can lead to fun gameplay, even though they are bolted onto a system I don't like.

There will definitely be rules for creating your own adventures.  The purpose of the Starter Kit was to provide potential players and GM's a taste of how SC:RPG plays, not provide a core rulebook from which to build characters and adventures.

I'm not sure if you've listened to The Letters Page podcast, but Christopher and Adam spend a lot of time on there saying "this is the story of [X] in the RPG timeline, but do whatever you want if that doesn't work for you".  So yes, the core set is going to contain a lot of metaplot adventure, but it's also going to contain the building blocks necessary to allow players/GM's to do whatever they want.  C&A have mentioned on numerous occasions that being able to do whatever you want with the RPG is very important to them.

As for fiction-vs-mechanics, that's basically been entirely left up to the players.  If the player wants to themelessly describe that "AZ makes a ranged cold attack at the spider bot near Unity", they can, but the door is also open to say "AZ forms an ice javelin, leaps over the nearest spider bot and while in mid-air launches the ice javelin at the bot attacking Unity, landing beside her on a small ice platform created before landing".  Players who are naturally more creative and/or embrace the comic-book-ridiculousness will go for the latter approach, but there's nothing stopping players from going with the former.
