You don't need to be online at the same time in a forum-based game at all. People can take it in turns to post in order of their turn, or everyone can just post what they want to do an the GM sorts it out. Combat can be tricky to deal with, yes, and it will take a while. I've tried to come up with a way to get around some of the issues by the method of switching to PMs once everyone (well, I only have two players) has rolled Initiative, then the players can give me their planned actions there and I'll write one post for that whole round describing what happens (playing the monsters and rolling for the players using whatever powers/skills they told me they wanted to use). Since I use MapTools for the map, I update that as I write the post, moving the combatants around and updating their hp and status effect icons and so on, and then when that post is done I screenshot the map (after hiding anything the players wouldn't be able to see - MapTool lets you do that for stuff like PoV) and upload it to the "Map" section in the game in RPOL so the players can see how things now look.

This way each round is only a single post in the main thread and it doesn't become full of player queries about what they can do and dice rolls all over the place and stuff. It does mean that the players don't get to describe their own actions or make their own rolls since I'm doing it all, but I get them to give me as much dialogue and narrative description as they want and then work that into the post. So their content is there, it's just written out by me. It's obviously not perfect and it's a system I'm tweaking a bit here and there as we go but we very rarely have combat (at least, so far) so it doesn't come up very often ;).

Yeah, I'll wait to see what people want to do if stuff changes during the round. For example, if one player is last in the Initiative order, chances are that whatever they wanted to attack at the start of the round may not be there at the end. But I can tell them "By the time it's your go, this guy will have moved up, these guys will have attacked you, and <fellow PC> will have tried to nuke that pair of bad guys over there", because even though I won't have actually done any rolling or writing at that point, I know what the enemies will have tried to do and can also say what the other player has told me of their plans. I stick to Initiative order so if the players happen to be adjacent in that order it makes things easier because I can just make my one-round post go from the end of their previous turns to the start of whichever of them is going first, rather than posting the start-to-finish of each round. Since I only have two players, this has a reasonable chance of happening ;). But we've had about two or three combats in the two or three years I've been running this on RPOL so I don't need to worry about it that often ;).
