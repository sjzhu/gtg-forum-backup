Hi Sentinel Comics RPG Peeps: Before I start I wanted to give you a rough idea of my backround so that it will frame the questions and comments.  I have a TON of experience running superhero RPG campaigns.  Most of my experience is with Champions but I have a fair of experience with various Powered By The Apoc games as well.  I have purchased and read the starter kit.  Ok...now my comments and questions...  ;)  (Sorry my books are at home.)

1. Crazy First Adventure

My first reaction after reading the six scenarios is that they feel like they are in the middle of big campaign and I have no clue what most of it means.  I guess with a starter kit I would have hoped for something that felt a bit more grounded.  It will probably play ok with the right crowd but it jumps into the savage lands, dimension hopping, etc right out of the gate.  I guess one of those in the mix might have felt cleaner to me.  Also I have zero experience with the Sentinels Universe except for this product...and it kinda feels like the product didn't help me.  I realize a full setting book is likely but in a starter I guess I wanted a cleaner start.

2. What About For Teens?

I run games for an adult crew and a teen crew.  I would love to use this for my teens but I may need to scale it back on the setting.  Also not sure how they will take to the system.  Their experience right now is with Star War WEG and Savage Worlds.  I think I could explain it to them but it might take some time...

3. PBtA Questions

There are some serious changes in this skinning.  Some looks SUPER cool.  The ratcheting green, yellow, red is just brilliant and feels like it will really capture that feeling of when the heroes are down and fight back!  Love that bit.  Also love the lack of detail on powers and what you can do with them.  Yes I love that.  Again...I can to the detail but I want that extracted.

So first hardcore question.

3.1. In this version of PBtA attack and defend just automatically succeed.  The roll just gives the effect.  Interesting.  Does this cause issues?

3.2. ADORE the initiative where the PC drive when the bad guys go.  Fun.  Interesting that the GM rolls dice for bad guys.  Gotcha.  Does the initiative flow work well?  I love the way it feels like a movie in other PBtA games.

3.3. Is there anything I need to watch out for in this version of PBtA?

 

Can't wait to give it a go.  :)

Each adventure focuses on just one of those weird locations, so I don't think it's too much to swallow.  Each one is pretty short but should give enough time for each to grasp where they are and what they're doing there.  Some of them do introduce a fair number of characters quickly, but fortunately you don't have to go to far in depth to any of them.

I've run a couple issues so far with two players, aged 6 and 10, and it's gone great so far.

 

They've both had some exposure to the setting. Both have played the Sentinels of the Multiverse card game, and the 10-yr-old is catching up on issues of the podcast (and the 6-yr-old asks a lot of "Who's that, what are they doing, how did that happen?" during the card game, which I'm increasingly able to explain as the podcast continues). They don't always know exactly what something looks like or behaves on the first description, but I haven't had a problem filling them in on what their character would know about something. 

 

As for mechanics, they caught on fast. I've tried a little bit of some other RP systems with them, and usually run into "too many rules to navigate" issues. Here, with the action being a lot more goals-driven, they either do something they've done before (I shoot more ice blasts!), come up with their own idea (I, um... make a bot with flailing hammers to attack!) or we do some collaborative brainstorming (6: I want to move a lot of rubble. 10: What about making an ice ramp? 6: Yeah, can I do that? Me: Go for it!). It does take a while for them to start to get the hang of a character, but it's a fun process.

Phantom5613:

3.2 In every table-top system I've played in my years of gaming, I don't think the GM has never not rolled dice for enemies. Do you mean the villains being REPRESENTED by dice or something else?

One or two D&D editions had official variants wherein the players rolled most of the dice.  When enemies attack, the players roll saving throws and "defense" rolls (which work exactly the same way: d20 + AC modifiers vs. 10 + enemy's attack modifiers).  When PC's attack, they make attack rolls.  The DM still makes damage rolls for the villains, but it's easy to see how the DM could be relieved of all dice.  I like that variant, personally, as it increases player involvement and feeling of control.
