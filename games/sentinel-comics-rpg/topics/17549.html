Arcanist Lupus:

the benefits to having the heroes go before the villain are less then the downsides that come with letting the villains take two turns in a row.

This.

In some of the scenes, there could be quite a lot of villain minions, lieutenants, etc. The heroes all going first before the villains act won’t put a huge dent in the villains, but the villains going twice in a row will put a dent in the heroes.

Besides, you’re creating a story together, and all the heroes going, then all the villians, doesn’t make for a very exciting fight scene/story, does it? (And if that’s not enough to convince your players, just kindly remind them that a villain double-turn will hurt, and that you won’t hesitate to make it hurt a LOT of that’s the kind of game they want to play.)

It's sort of like this:

Start of round

Heroes all take their turns, passing to villains

Villains take their turns, passing to Environment

Environment take's its turn

End of round/Start of round

Environment passes to Villains, since Villains haven't gone this round

Villains take their turns, passing to the Environment

Environment take's its turn, passing(FINALLY) to the Heroes

If a DM feels like being a jerk, they can lump up all the opposing turns and really whail on the PCs/Heroes. That's why the book suggests to mix up the turns, trying to create a more interesting story than the standard 'everyone punches eachother' that most RPGs boil down to.

I have done that in round 5 to let the villains steal a thing and leave the scene. Left enough underling to slow down the heroes so the villains and get away with the plans for Project SteelChase. A few days later they had to find the hideout and fight the robots made form the stolen plans. It was a difficult battle, but they manage to recover from twice. (They are yet to find out that the villain copied the plans and sold it, a plot brewing in the distance).

Once the let the environment go last near the end. I rolled a positive thing for the environment, the villain benefited from it and let the environment go next, changing it to something that benefited the villain. Then I let the Heroes go.

I would do it all the time but a well-timed double villain turn can make things interesting. Christopher said that he had a group that prefered going hero-environment-villain-environment-hero-hero repeat. not only do the villain go twice so do the heroes.
