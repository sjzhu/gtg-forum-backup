From what I can tell, the 9 chambers aren't right next to each other. The heroes are working their way through a difficult to navigate tomb with labrynthine passageways and deadly traps blocking their progress. Succeeding the overcome means the hero has successfully found their way from one chamber to the next, but succeeding with a twist means they get caught up in some environmental trap on the way there. The exits described in each of the milestone chambers's description leads into the next dangerous part of the tomb.

The milestone chambers themselves are devoid of threats, but the spaces in between them aren't. And environment threats that aren't defeated are presumably chasing the heroes, so "devoid of threats" probably isn't quite true. I don't believe the Secret Passage is a way to skip rooms. It's probably just one of the nine milestone rooms. Presumably, the GM has to describe how the overcoming hero found it.

The section of the booklet explaining the challenge mentions that it assumes that the heroes are all moving together, so yup! The heroes are moving together! If Legacy is punching skeletons on his way from the Antechamber to the Burial Chamber and Tachyon succeeds the overcome, the heroes find their way into the Antechamber and the skeletons chase them inside.

I think the main thing that's confusing you is that these rooms aren't directly connected to each other. There's a large amount of space seperating them, and that space is pretty dang dangerous.The heroes are actively on the move as they are fighting the environment threats, dodging traps and other such obstacles that seperate them from their next milestone room.

I hope that answers at least some of your confusion.

Think of it more like you're mapping it but the map isn't defined in advance. (You can even draw maps as you go if it would help.)

It's one big action scene, with the scene tracker running the whole time. Twists that come up - as well as the environment's turn- are the traps, bad guys, and other turns that take while exploring the tomb. A hero might spend their turn making an Overcome to explore (and encourage them to be creative with the way they explore, not just generic "spot checks") or they might have to deal with a horde of mummies following behind them. In play, heroes won't just explore from room to room to room and ignore everything along the way.

Also, consider it less like the halls being the challenge, but the twists from exploring being the challenge, and whatever the environment introduces.

Chaosmancer:

I think this scenario could have been far more interesting if the halls weren’t the challenge, but each room presented a challenge for the players to overcome, then at least I could have something to tell them other than “find the next chamber”.

As GM, there’s nothing stopping you from creating/making up a challenge for each of the the rooms.

Perhaps the planets in the astronomy chamber aren’t properly aligned (the Egyptians wouldn’t have known that the Sun is at the center of the solar system) or there are too many planets and the excess one needs to be removed? Perhaps upon entering the larder, the door closes behind the heroes and one of the vats begins emitting noxious fumes, requiring the heroes to open the correct vat and find a chemical to counteract the fume-emitting one? Maybe the long featureless hall continues endlessly in both directions no matter how far the heroes walk or run?

The only problem you’d need to avoid/balance against is doubling up on the number of overcomes the heroes need to pass in order to advance, since there is a timer on this… It looks like it should take 8 sucessful ‘advance to next room’ overcomes to get to the Hall of Mysteries, and if you make your heroes overcome to find the solution to your challenge and then again to implement it, you’ll have doubled that number to 16. But you could solve this by having the heroes use their ‘advance to next room’ overcome to find the solution to the puzzle, and let them perform the solution as a free action. Or better yet, have all the heroes roll a free awareness overcome and give the player who rolled highest the hints they need to figure out what the problem is. This would give the heroes a chance to discuss together and figure out the solution, using their overcome action to implement the solution…

This still seems like the number of players will dramatically change how the session plays out.  With six heroes, if they don't roll any twists, they can indeed pass through six chambers before the environment gets a chance to slow them down.  With three heroes, they'll get half as far and then have to deal with a d8 lieutenant or about 4 "Mummies!" (or 1-2 Skeletrons, the easiest threat--and when it says "1 for every 2 heroes" do we round up or down?).  3 heroes probably won't deal with either threat in one round, which means an additional twist, which will only make that threat harder.  Then they'll probably only get through another one or two rooms at most before this all happens again.  Action conservation will be extremely critical, which means no examining any setpieces (like the orrery or the robots) that don't directly lead to advancement.
