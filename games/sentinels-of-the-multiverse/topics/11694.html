Most games, unless they have an online requirement, can be played offline. In fact, if you don't have a connection, you can log in in offline mode, and access any games you have already installed that don't require you to be online and play them

A Steam account can be logged into by one machine at a time, though it is easy enough to log into an account on one computer, start a game, log out of Steam and log in on another computer.

If you opt into the beta, you can do things like Family Share and streaming, but it's not necessary (and your Steam client updates pretty much every day with more bug fixes.).

 

If the game has a purely offline mode (like it is now), there would be no requirement for you to be online, except for the original installation.

As much as I like the suggestion there are a few issues with this idea.

First is the fact that they have to design another 50+ soundtracks to do this and make each distinctly unique for each character and environment, then you have copyright and trademark issues to take into account, from your post alone any superman-esque theme runs the risk of sounding close enough to any one of the hundreds of Superman themes to count as an infringement.

Second is the question of where to play these tracks, during each players turn?  What about poor Legacy, in comparision to Tachyon most of Legacy's turns are over too quickly for the theme to be heard.  And when we get online multiplayer, what do we do about someone who purposely draws out their turns because they like the theme.  And picking a random one for the entire match can result in people dropping out because they dislike the theme chosen.

Right now with the theme being chosen by the Environment we're in a sweet spot of design and game cohesion.  For the design perspective it means that they only need to make 2 themes per expansion, for the game cohesion it means that you know what the music will be going into the match, and it helps the environment (especially ones like The Block, Silver Gulch and MDP where they spend most of the game either fighting each other or doing nothing) have a greater impact on the game.

Also, I like to stack multiple copies of the same card on top of each other in order to save space (so in this game I had three copies of Stun Bolt in-hand taking up three cards' worth of space when they could easily just be in a pile with the top of the lower cards sticking out so you can see how many you have). It makes it feel more restricted and less like a proper "virtual tabletop".

~ It would be nice if you could make the play areas resizeable or something, to save having to scroll back and forth along your hand when deciding what to play. I can see that being a pain if you're playing a hero who ends up with a big hand or a lot of stuff in play (someone like the Adept or Omni-10, even if many of the latter's cards go off automatically).

I forgot to check whether you can look at other heroes' hands/play areas when trying to decide what to play on your current hero. I'll try and remember to do that next time ;). But all in all, it wasn't bad and I was able to mostly follow what was going on (though I failed to spot whether Stealth was actually soaking damage since I didn't notice it in the damage report message...maybe I didn't read it before it disappeared though). Also, these are just the things that stood out to me the most after one short game, so don't think that I only have grumpy, moany things to say ;).
