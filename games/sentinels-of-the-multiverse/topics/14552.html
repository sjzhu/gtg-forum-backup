Jim is among my favourite heroes :D. He's really good at focusing down single targets, and somewhat less good at most other things. I suppose it is kind of repetitive but I've not got bored with him yet...though I don't think I've ever played one hero twice in a row except for rare occasions when all the heroes get wiped within the first couple of rounds meaning I've been taken out before I had a chance to really do anything. It's good that Jim has means of dealing damage even if the bad guys have soak - it just adds an extra layer of challenge, really. Bad guy got soak? Get out the Masadah and any Bounty (the more the better, obviously) and you can at least hurt it with your Power phase (plus the bonus Power use if Ultimate Target is out and that target hits something) :).

With Omni-10, the fact he does most of his stuff at the start of his turn is kind of his thing, really. I like the fun of getting all eight components out, then having them all go off and then going "...and now it's my Play phase" and doing even more stuff :D. On more than one occasion I have used Singularity as a finishing move by blowing up everyone's equipment and dealing 20+ damage (assuming the other heroes have a few equipment cards each) :D. I think with Omni-10 once I'm set up I kind of see the Start phase as the main bit of his turn and the rest as a bunch of bonus actions that I happen to be able to do (even though with every other hero the only time they tend to get to do anything in their Start phases is when one of their ongoigns runs out or they skip their turn to take out an environment card or something). He has a bunch of nice cards - Disruptive Flechettes is nice in a high-target environment, for example, regardless of whether or not there are any hostile ongoings you want to deal with. Defensive Blast can get ridiculous if you can get a few damage buffs. Ablative Coating and an RPS against Plague Rat is fun, too - Plague Rat hits you for irreducible toxic damage, you hit him back. You get Infected and hit yourself for irreducible toxic damage, you then hit Plague Rat and heal yourself off your Innervation Rays :D. Omni-10 is also amazing at getting around any "You cannot do X" things, such as half the cards in Megalopolis, as nothing ever stops you doing stuff in your Start phase...and evne if you use said phase to skip it to get rid of an environment card that requires that you do so, you still don't get to the "skip your turn" part until you've used any components that were in play before the thing that requires you to skip your turn. So you still might be able to do a bunch of stuff if you had all of them out and then the Volcanic Eruption showed up ;).

But all that aside, what you find enjoyable is going to differ from what at least a few other people find enjoyable - it may be that neither of these heroes are the ones for you and that you will just always have more fun with certain other ones. I enjoy playing both of the Shattered Timelines heroes - as mentioned at the start of this post, Jim is one of my favourites because it is fun to just pound away at something with self-buffed damage and wreck it :D. But my other top wo heroes are the Adept and probably the Naturalist, both of whom are pretty versatile and the former is my top-top favourite just because I can do sooo muuuuch thiiiinnnngs :D. And I hardly ever deal damage with him, so it's not like I only play heroes to set up and nuke stuff (or take it out a chunk at a time, as Jim does). I think I just like versatility, to be able to still do something even when some other action is blocked.

Chrono Ranger, for me, his big blow-out turns are about Hunter and Hunted once you've got several Bounties out -- combine it with The Ultimate Target against someone like Akash'Bhuta, The Matriarch or The Operative who's going to do a lot of out-of-turn damage. I've had CR knock dozens of HP off targets in a single turn, wipe out minions with Danny Boy or just keep the villain's damage so low it's inconsequential.

You haven't seen what he can do until you've popped off Hunter and Hunted, followed by a Terrible Tech Strike and the Compounded Bow all in one round -- it's just plain devastating.

He's also, as far as I know, the only hero who can search his deck for Environment/Ongoing destruction.

Part of the issue, I think, is they're both, in a way, utility based. A lot of CR's bounties are great, but situational, while OX does a lot of plinking with no inherent way to boost his damage. He's very much a finesse character.

OX is one of my favorites to play around with because he can do everything. While it's true that you'll end up with rounds where you don't play much (I almost never use Self Sabotage or Singularity, and always seem to end up a couple copies of each in hand), his power is always useful (any set-up heavy hero loves it, Unity especially), and he can singlehandedly keep the Environment in check and smack the villain around at the same time. He's also very reliable when it comes to irreducible damage -- between the Focused Plasma Blaster and his plating cards, he can practically take out Voss single-handedly, and he's The Organization's worse nightmare, especially in a 3-hero game.

OX is really a team-player, nearly as much as AA. If you find yourself with little to do, look to Timeshift your pals, especially if you have someone who can let you peek at or stack the decks.

One fun team I had started with Scholar and then OX. Scholar soakes up all the damage, then OX heals him twice every round so he can blast stuff.

Don't underestimate Chrono's other weapons, and don't overvalue the hat either.  If you're fighting an enemy with damage reduction, such as Apostate, The Masadah can do a lot for you.  Neuro-Toxin Dart Thrower is good for enemies where you get a lot of milage from reducting their damage output, such as Iron Legacy or Spite.  If you don't have a lot of spare cards then the hat just isn't essential, and I never use Displaced Amory to get it.

OX can be played not only as a character that builds up, but also as support.  If you're fighting an enemy that is likely to blow up his gear, you probably want to play him more in the support style.  Some heroes also benefit a lot from his support style.  His inherent power lets you play cards, or throw away cards, from any deck.  You can have Tempest put a card in play back on the opponent's deck, then have OX discard it, for example.  Unity benefits a lot from the extra plays since you don't have to lose cards to get the golems in play.  You can make other heroes that require setup get a jump-start the first few turns.  It is a very versatile power.

My favorite combo will always be the zero-turn win in Lorwyn/Time Spiral standard.  Yes, there was a zero turn win made using only recent (well, recent at the time) cards.  Of course, it required that pretty much your entire starting hand be exactly what you need, so the probability of pulling it off was something around 0.0000002%, and the deck that would be needed would be pretty terrible otherwise (which is why it never saw play), but it was possible!

 

(For MtG players who want to know how the combo worked:  remove 4 Simian Spirit Guides from you hand for 4 mana.  Manamorphose two of that mana to blue, then play a [http://gatherer.wizards.com/Pages/Card/Details.aspx?multiverseid=130638]Pact[/url], but counter that Pact with Spellshift.  Spellshift reveals cards from the top of your library.  The next instant or sorcery is Dragonstorm, which then allows you to search for four Bogardian Hellkites, which enter play and deal 5 damage each to your opponent.  Boom.)
