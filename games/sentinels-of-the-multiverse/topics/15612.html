This may seem like a silly question.  Particularly since this is my first time posting a thread to this site, and my first time even logging back on since the ARG back at the beginning of the year (Ah, good times~).

 

But alas, I do have a question about the new Completionist Guise power:  Is there an official ruling on the difference, or lack thereof, between a variant and a base hero card?

 

My understanding of it is that a variant is an alternate version of the base hero character card.  Therefore if Completionist Guise were to use his power on, say, Dark Watch Mr. Fixer (who only has two characters cards, one the base hero and the other the variant currently in play), the power's effect wouldn't trigger because Guise can't swap the variant character card for something other than a variant card (and since the base character card isn't a variant, he has no other variants to replace it with).

 

I may be totally off-base here.  It may very well be that, for purposes of Completionist Guise's power, all hero character cards are considered variants.  But given that we don't have anything but speculation to go on (as far as I'm aware of, at any rate), I feel my interpretation is the correct one.  As such, until such a time as an official ruling on what the definition of a "variant" is in game terms, the power seems to be more than a little confusing.

Wraith would be a variant of Rook City Wraith.

Look no farther than the definition of Variant:  A different or distinct form or version of something.

The original character card of a deck is a different form of the other character cards for that deck.  Yet they are both the character card of the same deck, so they are the same something.

In the above example, because your current version of the deck is using the Rook City Wraith character card, any Wraith Character Card other than Rook City Wraith would be a Variant of your current character card.
