My issue with the costumes is mostly with Wraith and the thigh cut-outs (and, to a lesser extent, her clevage).

To preface this: I believe a woman should be free to wear what she wants and her choice of clothes should be first, and foremost, about what she enjoys, her tastes, and what she feels empowers her.

BUT...

Superhero comics have a long, LONG history of showing female flesh through revealing costumes as a way to excite and titalate the male audience. Such displays have almost always been about appealing to horny guys and the female gaze and not about sensible crime fighting or tactical choices.

So, to be blunt, I'm disapointed in Adam and Christopher over this design. Sentinels of the Multiverse has always been a forward thinking and inclusive game. LGBTQ folk, people of color, those on the autism spectrum, and women have all been well represented in the various expansions. In Marvel and DC, women rarely get to be the smartest person in the room and, when they are, they are almost always the bad guy. In Sentinels, the world's smartest person is both a woman and a lesbian. The world's greatest detective (and an engineering genius in her own right) is a woman. 

Sure, Wraith wants to look good. That's awesome. She, like every individual, has a right to enjoy how they look and control how others perceive them. But why would an engineering genius with no powers go with LESS clothing instead of more? Why isn't she wearing some really awesome but light-weight battle armor? Why would she leave her thighs, and thus her femoral arteries, open to attack? I imagine she knows exactly how quickly she can bleed out if she takes a nick across the big target those arteries represent.

I don't know. I don't begrudge Adam And Christopher's right to make their characters look however they like. But I'd love to be able to show this card to my daughter and say, "Isn't what she's wearing awesome? She's wearing it not because it looks sexy and shows off her thighs, but because it looks good AND protects her in combat."

I think Wraith can be gorgeous and smart. This costume just says gorgeous to me.

As to criticising art, no artist should be immune to being called out if they create something problematic. Being able to draw shouldn't make you magically absolved of all responsibility.

I'm all for freedom of creativity, but I'm also for freedom of criticism.

And let's be blunt here: Adam is making a product. The intent is to sell this product to customers. We, as his intended customers, are perfectly entitled to express our opinions of his product whether they are positive or negative. Feedback is valuable enough to companies that they pay money to focus groups to find out if they need to make changes - by criticising, we're actually giving something to the company.

Imagine you bought a beautiful wedding cake from a local baker, and when you cut into it you realised that they'd used mud instead of frosting. Would you be angry and complain that your cake was inedible, or would you say, "Oh well, the baker obviously likes it like this, let's just eat the cake and convince ourselves that mud isn't so bad because none of us can bake wedding cakes."

They are the villain that teach you the strategies you will use later in more complex fights, like the value of sacrifice - or the fact that a good set-up is not necessary to do something.

Blade with his countdown is a good example, I think : to beat him, you have to learn to hit hard and fast, even if it means not losing time trying to prevent the damage he does. Even if it means having AZ use all is one-shots without taking the time to put a module in play. It's one of the reasons I rarely use him as the first Villain to teach new players, by the way. Another being that nearly all the cards in his deck are frighteningly powerful - his only weakness being that he rarely does anything more than playing one of them and politely ending his turn…

The fact that they have devastating cards  is what makes them still interesting to play for an experienced player, in my opinion. Yes, they are a tutorial - but not a tutorial on how to play the game, like in a video game. They are a tutorial about how to think when playing SotM.

The higher the villain difficulty level, the harder it is to juggle all the seemingly less devastating effects they have.
