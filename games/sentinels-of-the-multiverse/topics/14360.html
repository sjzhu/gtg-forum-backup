Alright. So, I've started trying to do some work on my own on trying to put together the Multiverse story. Trying to beat the potential book telling the real story from >G that's been speculated about. It seems like there are a couple of things that have, to an extent, frustrated this process in the past. What I'd like to do is to come up with some rules that are, generally speaking, true, to help tie down some of the content that we have more securely to storyline. Here are what I see as things that have made the process difficult.

1. Absolutely anything could be an alternate timeline/vision of Visionary/alternate reality created by Nightmist.

2. The art on the card does not necessarily tie out to the flavor text and comic issue citation.

Going through the forums for information, I've seen either/both of these derail pretty good detective and assembly work. After all, how are you going to argue with "you could just be completely wrong"? With that in mind, I'm going to make two assumptions:

1. Aside from Shattered Timelines cards/cards that explicitly show Visionary/Nightmist and imply visions/monkeying with things (e.g. Prophetic Vision), things shown on the cards are in the main timeline unless that is demonstrably untrue (e.g. creation of Iron Legacy).

2. Because the art loses lots of value unless it can be temporally placed (e.g. it could be ANY fight with Voss!), I'm going to assume that unless there is a large disjunction between quote and artwork, the art is from the same issue. In some cases, this isn't super relevant, because the art, while good, doesn't show much story-wise. But when there is real story content, I'm going to assume it's from that book, again, unless this is demonstrably untrue.

It would sure be nice to know from Christopher or Adam if these are reasonably accurate (true more often than not), but that might be too much to hope for. ;)

The other real questions that I have are related to real-life expansion timing and storyline timing. Has anyone ever asked whether, generally speaking, the events depicted in the base set happen, then Rook City, then Infernal Relics, then Shattered, etc, with an occasional outlier card that shows something previous or later happening? Or is it truly any card could happen at any time. And, just for reference, when I talk about something happening "before" or "after", I mean with respect to when it is experienced in the main storyline. 

On the forum scouring front, here's another little tidbit:

Adam (http://greaterthangames.com/forum/topic/what%C3%A2%E2%82%AC%E2%84%A2s-vial-doom) – The matter in the “vial of doom” on the Baron Blade card “Hasten Doom” is “actually created by Baron Blade, it's a radioactive substance designed to interact with the Parsons' specific genetic code and drain them of their strength. He's clever like that.”

Thoughts?

1. You can't take anything from expansion-timing at face value to inform storyline-timing. Captain Cosmic was released in game as the last of the Prime Wardens team and a few exansions after Infernal Relics. Storyline-wise however, Captain Cosmic was the 2nd member of the Prime Wardens and assisted Argent Adept in his first fight against Akash. Some things might fall in line this way, like the Moonfall arc(Baron Blade's teralunar beam gambit) going right into the Sunrise arc (with meeting Citizen Dawn for the first time), but that is not always gonna be the case. The storylines, like in real comics, will be much more intertwined and fluid where expansions are packaged together by theme.

 

2. Some things are just gonna be flat out impossible to place in the story timeline. Take War of Heliopolis for example. There are no clues whatsoever as to when this takes place other than "after Bearded Ra and Enneads first appearance but before OblivAeon". Super helpful. If you used some context clues and some educated guessing, you might be able to place the short series Baptism By Fire (Fanatic's rescue of Ra from Set) as happening right Heliopolis, but when does that take place? The answer is "after Fanatics and Beard Ra's first apperance but before OblivAeon". That didn't get us very far did it? Can we use more clues to find out when Baptism By Fire took place? I'm not gonna say there arn't any, but I have yet to find one. This whole example applies to a bunch of other stories as well. This one just illustrates my point well.

 

3. If you were going to tackle this endevor, the best place to start would be the Freedom Five Anuals. Those are the yearly issues of major hero teamups to combat major villian storylines. If this were a physical puzzle, the Anuals would be all of the edge pieces you put together first before digging into the middle pieces.

 

4. Flavor Text vs Art. As a rule of thumb, if the flavor text doesn't directly reference what is happening in the art you cannot make any assumption about them being connected or not. And honestly even then this method has broken down in the past. The most notable example of this is Unity's Platform Bot. The flavor text references the bot and it's cuteness, but it has been confirmed that the art does not match the flavor text (take note that the text is from a Vengance issue, but Baron in the art is post-scar-healing which places the art fairly far past Vengance.

 

I don't wanna be a debbie downer here. But if this was easy, we would have done this a long time ago. We are all happy to help you though. Let us know what you find.

This issue here is that you are going to start basing an argument around an assumption that just doesn't hold true in a lot of cases. It's fine to make assumptions and have theories and all of that. Hell I do it all the time. But it's hard to make a serious argument when the very foundation of it has serious and confirmed flaws.

If you are going to start at the basis of just assuming the flavor matches the art, at some point you are going to need some kind of additional context clues or reasons to prop up that assumption. It just can't be taken at face value. I think doing so will veer you in the wrong direction more times than it will help you. Especialy if the goal is to create a single story timeline, you will find yourself trying to fit square pegs into round holes making all the assumptions you make fit together, and the worst of it is that you will know in the back of your mind that a handful of your puzzle pieces will be wrong since those assumptions can't all be correct.

I guess maybe a couple hypothetical examples would be worthwhile here. If four cards quote Freedom Five #'s between 130 and 133, and the art on all of those cards shows Voss in some way, I think it's probably reasonable to conclude that there may be a story arc in issues 130 to 133 that involves Voss. It's pretty unlikely to be pure statistical randomness. I think, in the end, I'm agreeing with you - one piece of art with one quote doesn't represent a conclusion.
