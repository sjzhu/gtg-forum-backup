I've found that Scholar of the Infinite's base power isn't that good in the early game, a bit like Dark Watch Mister Fixer. It is too easy for your health to be critically low without your base healing. Yes, base Scholar can deal more damage, but Infinite can be a smoother ride as you don't have to worry about taking damage to dish it out.

Playing The Scholar it is all about getting as much card draw as possible and then playing as many Form cards as you can. But rather than Solid to Liquid with Mortal Form to Energy as a combo, I start with Flesh to Iron. Have one Flesh to Iron, your self damage bounces and you hit for two. A second, you now hit for three and you now have a minus four to damage dealt. Unless it is irreducable, you are now in the tank zone. If you can get a third Form out there I then go with a Mortal Form to Energy so that when I play my healing cards I'm doing some more damage.

It's the way I try and play Scholar of the Infinite, if it is of any use. This week's one-shot, as a pure and simple damage race isn't the best game for him, but in longer games he can be pretty effective. It is a good thing with the variant heroes, you do have to play them slightly differently.

I've found that the main way Scholar of the Infinite affects the big combos is, because I play Flesh to Iron right away, he takes less or no damage, and therefore can't do much with Get Out Of The Way anyway. I end up using him more for support in that case -- using Proverbs and Axioms and Don't Dismiss Anything to help others get cards, heal up, etc.

In that context, his base power becomes a pretty solid 2 HP attack until he gets Truth Seeker out. If I do healing-based attacks with him, it's usually in a short burst (i.e., I've got a couple Keep Movings in hand and a Get Out Of The Way), not as a regularly sustainable thing.

I hand-picked a team of Argent Adept, Grandpa Legacy, Team Leader Tachyon, Ra & Scholar of the Infinite to see how much damage the latter could do: 100 points or so in the 5th round (actually Spite had been knocked around a bit beforehand, so I'm not completely sure).

I built up his hand size to over 20, via multiple uses of Tachyon's power (with the assistance of AA & Legacy), and drawing cards like (Bolster Allies, Fleet of Foot, Know When to Hold Fast, Transmutative Recovery). On the killer turn those allies allowed extra uses of his base power after he played Know When to Turn Loose. The Imbued Fire/Flesh of the Sun God combo prevented the collateral damage; you might be able to substitute Omnitron-X for Ra if you know when Expect the Worst is atop your deck, but unless you also have one in your hard you won't be able to use the base power on his own turn without taking damage.

Inventive Preparation is also useful to the variant Scholar because it discards directly from his deck, boosting his base power. That also allowed Tachyon to fill her discard pile with Burst cards, in anticipation of what's usually more than one Lightspeed Barrage. The scary thing is she also had several Hypersonic Assaults, so his hand could have been even larger....
