We ran an experiment for this, three heroes, treat (H) as 4, but for the fourth turn we would divide up the play/power/draw so each hero got to do one.  Did nothing to offset lower initial health or smaller total cards in hand.

We dominated.  Largely because the accelerated card draw and play let heroes build up faster, and sending power uses to heroes that are set up to use them, or even to the hero best suited for the situation, is much more valuable than hoping hero 4 can be that hero.

Essentially, being able to play one of 12 cards will almost always be better than playing one of 4, and selecting a power from the whole table is significantly more powerful than selecting one from your play area, and sending card draw to the hand that needs it most is better than just always drawing yourself.

Cards that net (H) actions or benefits (team leader, DDA, Galvanize) will be even stronger, but even just turning your power phase into "One hero may use a power now" is incredibly strong, because the team gains access to (H)-1 more play areas to find the best power for the current board state.

In oue experience, the place where new heroes are less valuable than repeating the other hero actions is roughly 50%.  If over 50% are support you are diminishing value because the loss of play areas to find solutions starts to offset the ability to double down on the good stuff.

There are some limits to how useful "action amplification", as the OP puts it.

  • Extra Card plays are limited in their effectivness by handsize, handsize growth potential, and to a lesser extent, the situational usefullness of the card play
  • Extra Power uses are hard limited to the number of powers you actually have in play to use per turn. Obviously extra power uses outside of their natural turn are more potent, but still limited to a turn-by-turn basis

The only action that is always usefull is Card Draw (lets throw out situational bias here because I know someone will be all "But Omnitron can damage you for card draw"). Fact is that Card Draw is natually one of the most powerful effects cards can have given the nature of deck based games. Card Draw enables the usefullness of the other two actions as well, having more cards to play and having extra cards which grant power uses sooner.

 

While all these can be powerful effects in their own right, I think it's important to not overlook just how situation and board state oriented games of SotM can be. Not having an immediate or appropriate answer to threats on the board can, and often do, lead to losses. For instance, It's all well and good to have a ton of card plays in a single turn, but if none of them can deal with the 15 AoE damage Omnitron is about to deal you, then you'd have to ask yourself how valuable were those plays. "Action Diversity" is very valuable in SotM, as it gives you a greater chance to be able to answer whatever the game throws at you in a favorable way. It should be noted that Card Draw is consistently and exponentially usefull in creation "action diversity" as you are digging through all the answers your deck could offer in a much shorter timeframe, allowing you to have the answer you need when you need it more consistently than the other 2 actions.

In the end, I'm not sure exactly how you would quantify the amplification vs diversity debate. In my opinion, I think they are all equally as useful and both are tools to help you win games. Both are subject to wildly shifting practical values in how useful they are given a current situation and board state. The only real true outlier is Card Draw which is always giving you its maximum value regardless of board state

The difference with Magic is stuff like how you can play as many cards in a turn as you want so long as you can pay for it, how usually you only get to use a card once, and how you usually only get to go through your deck once. It's a lot easier to trade cards 1-1 in terms of removal and counters. Your opponent is often building up to their deck's payoff turn by turn. So card draw is more important because you really need to make sure to have the right card at precisely the right time. Also you can only have seven cards before needing to discard.

Sentinels has a lot of stuff that changes that dynamic significantly. You typically only get to play one card per turn. You can cycle through your deck as many times as you need to. Even before that pulling stuff back out of your trash is often much easier and less costly. The number of cards that can trade 1-1 for a threat are more limited in number. The villain board state tends to vary more from turn to turn rather than slowly building up to a finale you're racing against.

So I feel it's a lot easier to end up with a clogged hand in Sentinels than it is with Magic. Sure, it can be useful being in situations where you can basically play any card in your deck any time you need it, but a lot of the time cards in Sentinels are way better when they're actually on the board than when they're in potentia.

I missed a bunch of discussion.

  Here's my thoughts.

Disclaimer:  of course exceptions don't just exist, but abound in sentinels, we all know them, so i'm not covering them.

1.  Card Play:  card play is strongest when your play area is empty, and for most heroes, diminishes as you get more cards in play.  Extra card play early is incredibly valuable, letting you set up faster, get in front of limited destruction, and generally get past the initial set up of most villains faster and in better shape.  In my experience, the better you handle the initial villain set up and the faster you recover from destruction/reach  the more likely you are to win, that is why in my value system, card play is the most powerful of the 3 actions.

2.  Powers:  powers always have value, because heroes have innate powers, but they tend to increase in value as heroes get more cards in play.  They tend to be less valuable early, because innate powers tend to be less powerful than one shots, and rarely accelerate setup.  Powers are stronger the more setup heroes have, and also when card play options diminish due to hand size.

3.  Card Draw:  card draw is most valuable when your hand is empty, and loses value as you get better cards in your hand.  Card draw increases your options, but once you have the best option in hand adding more cards will not help your next card play, but only increase probability that you'll have the best card later.  Card draw value reaches 0 once you have access to all your options.

Card draw is also more complex, because it does nothing immediately, it has probability of improving later actions, but no direct impact.  Team Leader is the best example of this.  The card draw is fantastic, it makes the team almost completely immune to hand denial, and gives everyone more options constantly.  Once you have 10 or more cards in hand though, it isn't likely to improve your next card play so much that you would take more cards over using a power or playing a card.  At that point (H) card draws aren't even worth a power use, let alone a card play.  That's one reason Team Leader gets replaced by Research Grant eventually, because that discard becomes more valuable than every other hero getting a card (unless they can discard for damage on their own).

The value for each is dynamic, if your area is empty card play is best, if your hand is empty, card draw, if you are setup powers.

My work on valuing the health impact of each (from right after Shattered Timelines) ended with card play at roughly 3 points, powers at roughly 2 points, and card draw at roughly 1 point.

That means Team Leader is valued at (H) points, making it one of the stronger powers in the game.  (Of course it isn't perfect, card draw is actually stronger in low (H) games, because increasing options for hero play from 12 to 13 is more valuable than increasing from 20 to 21, or to put it another way, the more hands and card plays your team has, the more likely one of you has a card to deal with the current situation, so digging for the right card drops in value)

But again, that valuation is from my own testing, which means it is biased by the data coming solely from games and experiments I was running.  Im my experience early game strength and recovery from destruction are the most important factors in a win or loss, averaged across all villains.

Also, note that while I finished the process and came upbwith the 3-2-1 point valuation, the real answer is "it depends", because each situation in Sentinels is so unique.  The chief value of the process has been the ability to look at innate powers in playtesting with a rough guide on how good they are.

Drawing cards instead of an action that progresses the game toward a victorious ending lengthens the game.  Which has negative value that needs to be factored in.

I understand the desire to see your hero get to their most powerful version, but stretching the game to reach that point almost always increases your chance of losing.  There are places for card draw to take precedent, but they are more rare than common.

I know my main post was long, but a lot of the points you are making are in it.  It is an average, it isn't universal, because situationality is a key factor in SotM, and a lot of the pro points were investigated.

One problem with the "every card is moved up 1" is that those cards aren't entering play, just your hand.  And the value added by each card getting moved up one is offset largely by whether those cards are better than options you already have.

That's why the idea that card draw stacking is really powerful is largely false, because you almost always reduce the value of future draws once your hand gets big, unless you can internally discard for value (which is value added to discards by other cards, not value inherent to card draw).

Getting a hand full of great cards is awesome, but in the time you spend playing those cards the value of every card you continue to draw has been reduced, because you have reduced the odds that that card will be better than what is in your hand.

Card draw stacking does add value to the team, but the more you do it, the less value each individual card draw has.
