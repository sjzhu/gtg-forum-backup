I'm sure this has appeared somewhere before, but I can't find it anywhere. I'm going to be playing SotM this weekend with 5 buddies. Now we could just have two 3-player games, but I was wondering if anyone had any homebrew rules for having the Villain be hero controlled as well. I have an idea of what may work, but if someone has an idea that is tried and true, I'd prefer that. 

My thoughts:

  • Villain starts with H-1 cards in hand
  • Whenever the Villain would play the top card of the Villain deck, they instead play a card from their hand, then draw a card once that card is resolved
  • If the played card asks that the Villain play the top card of the Villain deck as a resolution effect (i.e. One-shots/When this card enters play), then play the top card of the Villain deck rather than a card from your hand (To both avoid fearsome pre-planned combos and ensure that the hand doesn't empty)
  • The Villain decides ties when dealing damage to the Heroes (Heroes still determine environment damage)
  • Damage from Environment cards is decided by the Heroes in the case of a tie between Hero and Villain, but the Villain may choose which of his cards are injured in the case of a tie. (i.e. Scholar is at 21, and GloomWeaver has 2 relics at 21. Damage is dealt to the lowest health target by the Environment. The Heroes decide the damage is dealt to the Villains relic, but the Villain chooses which relic is the target)
  • Hero cards that manipulate the top of the Villain deck still affect the Villain deck itself, not the Villains hand

I anticipate this will certainly provide more of a challenge to the heroes too, since the villain is essentially picking from the top 2-4 cards of his deck

Does anyone have a better system than this? Mine is still untested as of right now, but if I get the chance to try it out I will be happy to post the results here.

I’ve played 6 player, and we have done it in the simplest way possible. The sixth person plays the villain and environment decks. They’re not on the villain team, and no rules are changed. They are just the single person responsible for flipping those cards, adjusting hp totals, making decisions for the targeting of those deck’s cards, etc.

Admittedly it isn’t the same experience, but they get at least as much play time and interaction as every other player. And there’s always been more than one volunteer to do it.
