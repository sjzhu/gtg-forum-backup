[color=white]The Celestrial Trubunal brings back the keyword Trial, the cards that have it are placed next to a target. Then you have the Tribunal AI, one type deals damage to Targets next to Trial cards and if damage was not dealt it will instead play a card. The other reduces damage dealt to Environment targets and will also reveal cards from the Environment deck to get a Trial into play. Not only does the Represenative of Earth bring forth a new Hero, but it is also a Trial next to said Hero. That Hero also only has a max of 10 HP. Another thing, due to it being a Hero target, it will be targeted by the Villain as well. So this Environment can be very deadly when the Villain hits the everyone or even just the lowest. However! The same card that allows Heroes to use the power of the Represenative will also allow every target next to a Trial to heal (H) HP if each player discards a card. [/Color]

My thoughts on user-friendliness, most to least friendly to newer folks:

1.  Court of Blood:  Not really strong but can build up, the Mob is fun, and the Halls are scary.

2.  Magmaria:  Con be helpful, but too often newer players might not see their impending doom before it is too late.

Impressions:

5-player matchup:  Biomancer -> PW Argent Adept -> The Operative -> PW Tempest -> Greazer -> Best of Times Chrono-Ranger -> Sergeant Steel -> Adamant Sentinels -> Bugbear -> Desperate Naturalist in Madame Mittermeier's Fantastical Festival of Conundrums and Curiosities.

3 hour game.

This was the first time we'd seen the villains & environment, the heroes didn't have good damage-dealing capabilities for the first couple rounds, and Maze of Mirrors on the first environment turn made it harder for the heroes to deal any damage to villains at all (but kept the heroes fairly safe from most of the villains, too) so there were certainly good reasons for it to drag out.  I expect to be able to try out the other five villain decks in one of our next game nights.  I don't expect to play against these villains very often, because a single game taking 3 hours is less fun for our group than getting in 3-5 games in that same timeframe.

I did quite enjoy what we saw of the villain decks; they all have very strong themes and we all liked the mechanics and flavor of each of them.  The game format just isn't going to be anywhere near as popular as the base style for my particular group.  We've only played against the Vengeance crew a couple times as well, for the same reason, so this wasn't really a surprise to me.  It's also entirely possible that a 3-player game with us knowing the villain & environment cards better would fit into the comfortable 40-60 minute play time so that's a possibility for the future.

 

Last impression - although I started off slowly as the Desperate Naturalist, by the last turn I took I was able to draw some comments of "Oh, wow!" which was fun.  Croc form card plus Rhino aspect from the previous turn, Feral Fury to deal two instances of 3 damage, Desperate Prey to gain Gazelle aspect and play Primal Charge to draw 4 cards and deal 3 damage and deal 4 damage, Natural-born Vigor allowing Natural Form's Power to make someone draw 2 cards and me gain 2 life and deal 2 damage to 2 targets.  That kind of turn was exactly what I'd hoped to be able to do with that promo hero, which made me very happy.

I've fought everyone at least a couple times now, so I'll give my impressions.

Ambuscade - Relatively straightforward boss who wouldn't have been out of place in Vengeance, followed by the craziest incap ability of any villain. Dealing with a mini-nemesis boss rush can hurt, and God help you if you let Ambuscade live long enough to reshuffle his deck.

Biomancer - Hate this guy. His damage reduction and healing mean that he's hard to kill even with his low max HP, his homunculi have damage reduction making them a pain to devote resources to, and so they'll be hitting everyone pretty consistently. Then you have his hero clones, which are almost always must-kill targets, and then a card to bring them all back from his trash pile. That one's more fair than most, since you can destroy it, but you might not have much time depending on where he falls in the turn order.

Bugbear - Mostly a beefy damage dealer, not unlike Fright Train. He does a bit less damage than Fright Train in general, at least until the body count starts racking up, but is much sturdier thanks to his lifedrain. Makes for an interesting decision; do you wait before taking him out so that you don't have to deal with his healing until later, or do you target him early so that you can avoid his insane damage in the lategame?

Citizens Hammer and Anvil - Between these two and Anvil's starting equipment (which is almost always a priority target), you have something like 45 health to punch through, and it has to be in a certain order. That's not taking into account the chance that Anvil pulls one or two Bastions to make things more of a pain. Their damage isn't too high, but Bastion means that you'll often have to focus on Anvil before anything else. Basically, they're the party tank.

Greazer - Would probably be one of the most dangerous villains of the bunch if he wasn't so single-minded. He leaves once he's incapacitated his target, and even though his incap ability is a strong one, it's sometimes the right move to let him get his target just to get rid of him. Also, he has one of the funniest interactions in the game: if you can use an ability to make his hair do damage to itself, Greazer will respond first by counterattacking his hair, and then counterattacking himself for damaging his own hair.

La Capitan - A support character for the villains, her deck's not very threatening but she has a few discard effects (and she becomes more dangerous paired with someone like Ermine for even more discard), and some weirdness like switching a hero's ongoing for another random one, taking extra turns, or letting the environment take extra turns. Also, took me some time to figure out who the woman on the back of her character card was.

Miss Information - Also a support character, she has some dangerous ongoings, and she can make the earlygame surprisingly difficult considering that she's supposed to be a difficulty 1 villain. She's probably harder than La Capitan, honestly, and will probably be one of the first villains targeted in a match. It helps that her incapacitiated side is beneficial for the heroes, at least as long is she isn't on La Capitan's team.

The Operative - She does damage, and has a couple random debilitating effects. That's about it, really, aside from the rare game where Iaido Practitioner actually does something. I was pretty disappointed with her, to be honest, considering that she's half of the villain generally seen as the most nightmarishly difficult in the game.

Plague Rat - Kind of the opposite of the Operative for me, I'm not a fan of his normal fight, but as a member of a villain team, he's a blast to face off with. Most of this owes to the Ghostbusters, who provide an interesting dynamic for the fight. They're pretty dangerous, but they're also the only things keeping Plague Rat in check, as without them, he can do as much as 7 damage to a character with a one-shot, plus 3 to everyone at the end of the turn. 10 damage to a character is a lot for a Vengeance villain. But on the other hand, without the busters around, Plague Rat can also help you take down the other villains, not to mention himself. Very interesting decision-making here. His incapacitated side can also be a life-saver, destroying pesky environment targets.

Sergeant Steel - A nice way to tone down the expected tedium of upkeep for a mook-maker villain in the Vengeance format, it's nice that only one of his minions will act at the end of his turns. On the other hand, they're still very dangerous, and beating them will feel fruitless as it just means that a different one will act at the end of the turn instead. He also has low health, so, you know: kill him fast.

While I'm posting this wall of text, might as well talk environments, too.

Magmaria - A lot more than the "lava burns everyone all the time" place than I thought it would be, it has something of a currency system where you can destroy some environment cards, keep them, and trade them for bonuses later. A system that I'm sure would be really cool if I ever drew those payoff cards before the end of the game.

Court of Blood - Vampires beat people up. It's a very straightforward environment; but it feels quite fair. I like it as far as gameplay goes, even if the accents in the flavor text make me cringe internally.

Zhu Long's Temple - Probably benefits the heroes even more than Freedom Tower does, and wouldn't surprise me if it ended up being considered the easiest environment in the game after all was said and done. There's only one card that's uniequivically more help to the villains, and any environment destruction can just be saved for that one card. It's a fun environment, though, and I recently noticed that the True Form isn't just the Fixer's nemesis - he also counts as the Operative's nemesis, which allowed him to hit her for 12 damage in a single turn.

Fantastical Festival - A little too much going on for my tastes. Can get very out of hand, very fast, with no inherent ways of getting rid of many of its cards.

Celestial Tribunal - Not technically part of the expansion, I know, but oh my God this place is so cool! Personally, I like to let the randomizer choose which character to take out of the box as the Representative of Earth, to avoid just picking the obviously powerful ones like Legacy, Team Leader Tachyon, or Captain Cosmic every time. Though sometimes this means you'll get a useless one like the Naturalist, Absolute Zero, or Prime Warden Cosmic.

Overall, Villains of the Multiverse might be one of my favorite expansions. I wasn't actually that excited for it for a while, since it had no new heroes and I rarely played the Vengeance format, but what I found was this: The Vengeful Five, on their own, were basically a single villain with a few extra bells and whistles. But adding the ten new mini-villains really makes the Vengeance format feel like a brand new game.

I had not seen this mentioned yet but Chrono Ranger's promo changed from the initial reveal during the pre-order seen here http://sotm.wikidot.com/heroes:chrono-ranger. It is now:

Power: Select a non-hero target. Until after the next time you use a power, all bounty cards also affect that target and are not destroyed when that target leaves play.

The original reveal was "until start of your next turn" and "are not detroyed when a target leaves play"

Thoughts: Lasting until after his next power gives Chrono Ranger a chance to attack the target he put all the bounties on with is normal play and power. It also means that extra power given by other heroes will not let him stack all the bounties on multiple targets, which could result in some craziness. (though Guise can get the bounties on a third target but he's Guise).

Second, "that" target instead of "a" target means bounties won't get stuck in limbo as they would otherwise if the target it was attached to originally is destroyed. It would have been easier to charge up the Masada and Danny Boy if bounties could get stuck and make the Hat's ability to destroy bounties more useful.

Overall I think this version is better than the original reveal, so kudos to the playtesters if they were involved in the change

Played a game on Monday and finally have some time to do a write up!  Here are my first impressions.

Heroes: We played with all the new promos, so Naturalist, Scholar, Chrono Ranger, and The Sentinels

Villains: Decided to play with characters we weren't familiar with, namely Biomancer, Bugbear, Sergeant Steele, and Greazer

Environment: The Carnival (MMFFCC)

Turn Order: Biomancer, Scholar, Sergeant Steele, Chrono Ranger, Bugbear, Naturalist, Greazer, Sentinels, MMFFCC

 

Hero Impressions: Overall, I like the new promos a lot; they are powerful in their own way and I think The Naturalist might have the best power for some nasty chain combos with activating animal abilities.  The Scholar also seemed incredibly powerful as he gets a power that allows him to consistently deal himself damage and deal an enemy damage.  This makes Flesh To Iron an even better card than it already is.  The Sentinels new suite of powers work nicely enough and offer a good variant.  I got Sentinel Tactics out on turn one, which helped combo a lot of their powers (Mainstay + Idealist really works well for neutering a villain).  Writhe's new ability didn't get any use, but I can see how it would be useful in situations where villains continuously resurrect stuff or against Dawn/Voss.  The new ability that was used the least was Chrono Ranger.  I can see it's applications (and I'm sure there are a ton of combos that I'm missing), but the player controlling him just never got enough Bounties on the board to make good use of it.

 

Villain Impressions:  They didn't give us a tough time luckily, and that was mostly due to the Scholar using his nuke cards to destroy Biomancer extremely quickly.  We also used Neuro-toxin darts and Idealist to neuter Bugbear's damage completely, meaning he wasn't healing.  It also helped that his Bloodscent and Greazer's Nemesis card stayed on Scholar the whole time, who was rocking -4 damage with his two Flesh to Iron cards.  Sergeant Steele's agents didn't get too crazy either as we were able to keep them out of play easy enough.  Greazer was probably my favorite of the bunch; I can see how he would spend most of the game going after a single target and not doing much else.  Unfortunately for him, Scholar took a whopping 0 damage from him the whole game, so he was largely useless.  In fact, we went out of our way to make sure he killed himself because of his hair getting messed up, which was fun.  Overall, I think given a different suite of heroes (aka, NOT scholar), this would have been a much more difficult fight.

 

Environment Impresssions:  The only thing that nearly screwed us over was MMFFCC; due to some terribly timed Hall of Mirrors coming into play, and Biomancer's flip side being active (Heroes deal +1 damage to themselves and other heroes), we had a moment of panic.  But, we were able to control it easy enough.  We got two of the runaway ferris wheels out though, which made the environment quite sucky to deal with, though nothing else that came out screwed us over too badly.

 

Vengeance Format Impressions:  I can see my group and I playing Vengeance style much more now.  The problem with the Vengeful Five was that the book keeping simply got out of hand as there were several instances of "Play the top card of every Villain deck" comboing nearly endlessly, as well as a lot of criss-cross reactions that made staying on track more difficult than it needed to be.  For VotM, the decks we played seemed more focused on their own abilities and cards rather than giving each other plays and abilities.  Less thematic?  Probably.  Easier to track?  Yes!  A thousand times!  I liked how almost all the villain decks had cards that they placed in the hero's play areas.  It made their damage modifiers much easier to keep track of as you get a physical reminder right in your play area.

 

Overall Impressions:  I cannot wait to try the other villains.  The first game was pretty light on difficulty, mostly thanks to the scholar, but I can see how some Villain team ups would be quite sadistic.  I'm excited for the community to figure out which combination of villains is the most difficult and give it a shot.
