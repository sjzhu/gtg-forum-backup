Riffle shuffling is good for stuff like playing cards you can easily replace when they inevitably get bent/curved (as happened to many a deck of cards mom and I have owned over the years), but the "mash shuffle" is generally the one favored in Magic and similar games where you want to keep your cards in reasonable shape. And it's much faster and easier to do that with sleeved cards as I found out the hard way in my first few games.

Also once you get the hang of it, mash shuffling is really fast, which is handy in any game where you have to shuffle frequently... as I also found out in my first few Magic games. XP

I do not use sleeves in this game. (I do use them for any CCG/LCG I play, but they are usually required for tournaments, and I want to a) break the sleeves in so they stay stacked - ie, rough them up through play as many varieties are very slippery when first put together and b) get used to how the cards feel sleeved while playing)

 

For recomendations, (in order to stay mostly on topic) I am a huge fan of Dragon Shields and KMC Hyper Matte. However, both of those are rather expensive and not worthwhile in the pursuit of several thousand cards worth of sleeves required for Sentinels. Penny seems best. (Dragon are usually 10-15 per 100, and KMC are usually 10-15 per 80)

 

My philosophy on board game sleeving is two fold.

 

a) If I play it enough that I might consider sleeves a worthwhile investment (as with Sentinels) then I am not against just buying another copy if the game becomes so destroyed it needs it

b) If I don't play it enough, then I'm not going to spend money on it.

 

This philosophy may change when my 10 month old daughter is old enough to start playing games with me.  Even then...

 

 

As for shuffling... I do a side riffle with sleeves (because otherwise you'll end up getting some cards caught in the open end) - a technique I picked up in the original L5R CCG days. (So instead of the two halfs of the decks against each other ends (short sides together), they are side by side along the long side of the card. )I can even bridge this way. It does take a bit more force, but doing a proper Riffle+Bridge shuffle, while it does start to break down the cardboard over hundreds of shuffles, does not leave a bow. Plus, as stated above, this helps rough up those glossy dragon shields (and even the Hyper Matte's are slippery at first) so they stay stacked in a deck.
