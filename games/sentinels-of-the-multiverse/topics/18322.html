Yup. Edit that.

Oblivaeon is designed to ruin your day. Any card of his that doesn't, generally would have if you were playing a different team, or were in a different situation. 

I've played twice so far. We technically lost the first time, since about 2/3 of the way through we realized we were doing several rules wrong, and should have had an even more difficult time.

The second time we BARELY won. As difficult as Oblivaeon is, it's significantly harder if "The 90 HP" Scion comes into play, late game. Without giving much away, if he flips, your team is in BIG trouble.

Standout performers in Oblivaeon so far: 

The usual- if they were top tier characters before, they're still great now.

Setback (basic)- being able to get a new hero after he's incapacitated means you can afford to take more risks, and can actually pull off some impressive moves, as long as you fill up his unlucky pool as full as possible.

Idealist (Super Saiyan)- I underestimated her at first, but once she gets her cards out there (and hopefully the mission rewards designed for her), she can really carry your team.

Mainstay- WOW. Potentially top tier, in any Sentinels game.

Nightmist/Benchmark/other tanks and healers- healing on a large scale is a great thing. We wouldn't have won the second game (Voss flipped) if Nightmist, Benchmark, and Tempest weren't so good at midigating/healing damage. And MVP was definitely Nightmist.

Equipment/Ongoing based characters- there actually isn't much in Oblivaeon games that target ongoing cards (aside from one Scion), and there's virtually nothing targeting equipment. It gives the characters that need more 'setup' some breathing room, and time to shine.

 

However, one character that loses big is Captain Cosmic, besides his Requittal version, which is still good. There's no other way to keep his constructs alive. It's also a good idea to have him be 1st player, when playing him, just to make sure the other players get some use out of the constructs, before they blow up.
