Hello!

I don't know exactly where to put this- but it was a good thought and I really wanted to put it out here. 

I know Christopher stays away from custom content as not to be influenced by it in his own card creation- but The Cauldron is absolutely amazing.  I've been playing it for years now and as new stuff keeps coming out it continues to grow even more incredible not only mechanically- but artistically as well. 

If Christopher can't look at it- can Adam?  It's such a super cool thing that GTG has allowed with this amazing fan expansion- so I guess the point of this post boils down to this:

I think GTG could benefit by working with Matthew Bishop to continue on in the world of Sentinels in card format. (or another world)  Not only does this relieve Adam on the art side of the workload- but once Christopher is done working on the Earth Prime line of products he could have someone else passionate enough to continue the project on and work with to continue to make magic happen.

Not something I would expect to see soon- but who knows for the future.  Afterall- Matthew has done such an amazing thing (and continues to do so for the love of it)-  imagine what he could do if it was official for GTG.

Just a thought...

Well, with Handelabra I know they're very definitely considering trying to implement Steam Workshop support whenever the time is right to divert resources to that sort of thing, so then not just Cauldron but people could make whatever fan stuff they wanted for digital Sentinels in general.

Honestly I think the setup with Cauldron is already really good. Being allowed by a creator to manufacture and sell your fan idea based off an existing IP, even if it's not for profit, is a gigantic concession already. It might not actually be that great a deal for the guy as a result.

I would assume no one working for Greater than Games can look at the Cauldron stuff.   The problem is if someone makes fan content and then <G makes a deck that is similiar,  if the fan were not cool he/she could legally try to claim their idea was stolen.   So every game company I can think of has a very strict policy of not looking at any fan projects or excepting any fan submissions.  

After Oblivaeon,  once they are done with the game,  they could potential decide to put a policy and process in place to review fan submissions for official release but that won't happen until <G is done creating cards themselves.  

The fact that they worked with the Cauldron guys and setup a licensing deal so that you can get cards through DTC is really awesome though.  It shows that the guys making SOTM appreciate the fans and are not paranoid about "their game being stolen" by others.    Its an attitude that sadly not every game company in the industry shares, I can think of a couple who would have slapped the Cauldron guys with a C&D order the moment they put up their first custom card.
