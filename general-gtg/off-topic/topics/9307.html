I would contend that the Seven and the Nine were distinct in their powers and effects, each granting power according to the nature of the races for whom they were forged. It seems clear, for example, that the Nine granted invisibility, while the Seven did not.

It is my belief that these were the original powers of the Rings, as imagined by Celembrimbor. From a mythological standpoint, 1, 3, 7, 9 are all powerful symbols and numbers in european mythology with a LONG history in stories, religion, and mysticism. 

1- anything singular (as in utterly unique and unable to replicate) equates to power. Works for gods, items of power, monsters, heroes (last member of their tribe, race, ninja clan, etc).  I don't think theres any other number/concept which is used as heavily. It reverberates throughout storytelling with such elements as the lone wanderer, the King, The Emperor,  the sacrifice (a single person giving themselves for the good of all), the first (insert thing here) being the most powerful, or the last (insert thing here) being the most powerful.   

3- the number three is similarly heavily used, be it in mythology, religion, and storytelling.  3 gods (often three aspects of a singular god, to get both the goodness of three and one!), such as the Christian trinity, the 3 fates, and the three aspects of the Morrigan.  This is echoed in mystical elements from classical literature, such as the three witches (which again tends to echo the Maiden, Mother and Crone- its turtles all the way down here...).

It can also be seen in a lot of fairy tales- 3 brothers set out, only 1 makes it to the end (or learns anything).

7- This is one of the best known power numbers, with deep roots in numerology and multiple cultural underpinnings.  It can be seen in eastern and middle eastern mythology  (7 gods, 7 roads, 7 brothers, the number 7 meaning togetherness, etc).  In european mythology, you see things like the 7th son of a 7th son being special and able to see through falsehoods and magic.  there are 7 days in a week (with one of them being special to a tripartite, singular god!)  Its also a number significant to alchemists and numerologists, being all about study and wisdom.  

9- at a most basic level, this is 3 times 3 (and thus powerful, but lesser).  It has roots in indian mythology, but is largely special because of it equates to three sets of three.  

 

 

Bonus- the number 4!

4 is also important, but always as part of a set.  things like the 4 elements, or the 4 treasures of ireland.  Its all about things coming together to be more than the sum of their parts.  you can see why that wouldn't be all that useful for rings (except for summoning captain planet, but that takes Heart- which equates to the 5th element, that being spirit) Basically, 4 and 5 tend to be intertwined, and are often about 4 things uniting into 1 stronger one.  Which isn't really what the rings were about.  they were more about being subservient to/ruled by the singular thing, rather than coming together to MAKE a single thing.
