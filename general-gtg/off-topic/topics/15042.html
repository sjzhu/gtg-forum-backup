I'm not a big food guy (also not big on pasta or pizza), but here are a few suggestions. They're mostly on the north side; accessibility may vary depending on your method of transportation and willingness to walk, but I'll try to pick things near the train:

  • Sun Wah BBQ (5039 N Broadway) - good Chinese food; known for their crispy bbq duck, but the menu is huge; can be difficult to get a table in the evening because they get very busy; there's plenty of good Chinese food in Chinatown if you're down that way
  • Tank Noodle (4953 N Broadway) - I'm not that into Vietnamese food, but everyone who is seems to love this place; several other Vietnamese places nearby, but this is the one that I hear about the most
  • Thai Pastry (4925 N Broadway) - good Thai food (in case you couldn't guess from the name)
  • Big Jones (5347 N Clark) - southern cuisine with good cocktails
  • Hema's Kitchen (2411 N Clark) - most of the good Indian food is west on Devon Ave, which is probably not too convenient for you; this place is more centrally located and tasty; just west of Lincoln Park which is a good spot for an after-dinner stroll
  • Cho Sun Ok (4200 N Lincoln) - supposed to be amazing Korean food, but I haven't managed to eat there yet; there tends to be a very long wait - lunchtime might be better than dinnertime
  • Laschet's Inn (2119 W Irving Park) - solid German-American restaurant/bar
  • Kuma's Corner / Kuma's Too (2900 W Belmont / 666 W Diversey) - heavy-metal themed joint with excellent burgers; often has a long wait
  • Pierogi Heaven (169 N Wells or 329 S Franklin) - hole-in-the-wall pierogi restaurant; my go-to when I'm in the loop
  • Rickshaw Republic (2312 N Lincoln) - Indonesian street food; I've only been there once, but it was pretty tasty

All of the above tend to be small, casual restaurants. There are lots of new American gastro-pub type places, but I don't know enough to make many recommendations. The Wicker Park, Logan Square, and West Loop neighborhoods are likely to have a lot of options. A few that I have enjoyed:

  • The Gage (24 S Michigan)
  • Uncommon Ground (3800 N Clark or 1401 W Devon)
  • The Promontory (5311 S Lake Park)

If you're looking for beer, I'm sure there are tons of places, but two that I know about are:

  • The Hopleaf (5148 N Clark)
  • Map Room (1949 N Hoyne)

 

Chicago has parks all over the place. Here are a few of the bigger ones:

  • The lakefront is off-limits for development, so most of it is open park/beach area with bike/running paths for most of its length. If you are up as far as Montrose, there's a cool bird sanctuary / local planting area northeast of the harbor.
  • Millennium Park / Grant Park downtown is pretty big and has all sorts of things going on: public sculpture, art museum, playgrounds, music venues (this is happening on Monday) and more.
  • Lincoln Park is a pretty long north-south park by the lakefront. It makes for good wandering and includes a free zoo.

 

I can probably come up with more, but I'm going to hit post before a browser mishap eats the whole thing. If you have questions, feel free to ask.
