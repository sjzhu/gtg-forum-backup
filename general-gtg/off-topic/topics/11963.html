My grandfather's grandfather was apparently a well known Jewish leader of some type, at least in his region of the Pale of Settlement, earning the family name Kadushin, yiddish for Holy One.

When we were in Israel in 1982, we went to a Kibbutz where that same grandfather's cousin lived. My sister and I got to meet our 3rd cousins, though we couldn't talk to them much, since we only spoke English and they mostly only spoke Hebrew.

My grandmother, in her 80s, revealed in an interview that we have on tape, that she was 'a love child'. Her birth broke up two families. She had a lot of older half-siblings, many of whom resented her and treated her poorly. Her husband, my grandfather, grew up in an orphanage. He had many siblings in the orphanage, but said they were kinda just like everyone else, rather than feeling close. Due to both of them missing out on strong family ties growing up, they were both very focused on family and it has led to fairly strong bonds in their children, grandchildren, and greatgrandchildren.

My wife likes to tell the story of her family tumbleweed. When she was researching her genealogy, she started coming across a bunch of familiar names and realized that not too many generations back a couple of close cousins had married. In fact, she also tells the story of, I think her grandmother talking to her mother, saying something along the lines of, 'I wonder what life would be like if we'd stayed on the farm and you'd married your cousins, like everyone else.' That's rural eastern Kentucky for you.
