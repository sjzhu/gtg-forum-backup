It managed to develop the characters reasons for fighting each other effectively and gave the principal villain a real reason for wanting them to, two things that I thought BvS completely dropped the ball on.

I loved that the villains end point plan wasn't some "destroy the world" super plan using the soviet resources, but was a very human scale and understandable endgame. I think they developed him well and there really is the question at the end about whether or not he won.

Antman in the airport fight, I'm very glad they did that and that it wasn't in every trailer, made for a nice surprise.

On the airport fight, a minor quibble, Vision disappears for 90% of that fight, Scarlet Witch isn't in it very much either given her power level, kind of wonder if they're supposed to be squaring off and that's why both of them are MIA.

On Vision, I really enjoyed them showing off his intangibility, he didn't do much (anything?) with it in Avengers 2 and the "fight" between him and Hawkeye showed it to great effect.  

Loved pretty much every character, they do a good job with ensemble work, everyone got a scene where they were more than just background, the exceptions were probably Hawkeye and Antman, but both of them come in late and have ha d alot of development recently (Avengers 2 and Antman respectively) so I can forgive that.

I hate it when they kill named characters (Crossbones in this) comic book continuity thrives on the bad guys coming back time and again. minor quibble.

The Eulogy was a great geek-out moment, seriously was hoping that speech would be in there and was not disappointed.

Basically, the whole movie was amazing. Quibbles aside, everything worked.

I enjoyed the movie quite a lot, though not as much as Guardians of the Galaxy (surprised that one hadn’t come up in the Marvel movie comparisons!).

I have to wonder - what’s with the Marvel villains and their circuitous plans that rely on the heroes doing specific things and also random events transpiring in certain ways? As an example, Zemo plans to have the Avengers capture the Winter Soldier so he can read the trigger words (man, was “Manchurian Candidate” a perfect dig) in the guise of the psych evaluator. But Zemo didn’t know about Black Panther - what if he had succeeded in killing the Winter Soldier in the tunnel car chase? So much for the villainous plot!

I also have to say that the disagreement between Iron Man and Captain America definitely could have resolved with a little more discussion. You don’t get so angry that you come to blows and then have a reasonable exchange of views and leave the other guy your cell phone at the end. It escalated to violence rather quickly. I mean, you need to move the plot along, but they were too close to be really fighting. Great example: Hawkeye asking Black Widow if they’re still friends. If the answer is anything but “no,” why are they fighting instead of trying to de-escalate the fight?

In terms of sides, I have to say I’m kind of with Stark. He was a jerk about it, but look at current events and all the instances of organizations running away with abuses of power. US intelligence agencies are spying on their own people, the police has problems with race relations, and the military is happily developing weapons that get other nations declaring they would respond with nukes. There have to be checks and balances on these things to keep one guy at the top from deciding to build a doomsday device. The heroes’ argument got me thinking in terms of the American founders: Cap is a Federalist (wants power centralized in a strong federal government), while Iron Man is a Republican (in the old sense! he wants power distributed among many smaller factions that can balance each other). Cap’s idea works well if good people have the power (himself), but it falls down completely if the people at the top are Hydra, or simply make a mistake. I thought it was a superb twist of irony that the best argument for Iron Man’s position came when Stark found out about his parents and flew into a rage. Stark needed a check against abusing his own power, which was exactly what he believed in!

I totally agree that Spider-Man stole the show. I loved that our introduction to him wasn’t in the big fight, but came in a quiet, awkward scene at his home.

As I’m not familiar with the comic background, I’m curious about some of that. What’s the eulogy speech?

Also…who else is excited for the upcoming Argent Adept movie???
