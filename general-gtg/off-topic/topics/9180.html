io9 had a lovely article about how each Doctor's final episode kind of sums up their entire character.  For me, It felt like an appropriate sendoff for Matt Smith, in that it was hectic and glossed over some of the important bits (wait, he has a new best friend/lover that he's known forever that we're only now meeting?  AGAIN?)...but thats been a key component of his character since Amy Pond's departure. Its one of the reasons that it felt so right that she made a cameo at the end to take him away.

For me, it felt like the doctor died when Amy couldn't be saved.  they were tied together, and without her he drifted.  There were adventures, but many of them happened without us (the audience).  He was starting to pull himself back together with Clara, but it felt like the 11th doctor had lost his steady anchor.  Clara is a good companion, but he held her at a distance- dropping her off between adventures, "saving" her by sending her off, and keeping her at arm's length.  

I can't deny that the show itself had pacing problems- but I didn't mind the narration, because to me that's what the Doctor's life WAS without Pond. He'd gotten more withdrawn and fatalistic, and the only way anyone could know him anymore was from the outside- even those that he flirted with or helped.

Amy Pond, and his adventures and interactions with her was his entire life.  And when he saw her again, he could let go and be someone else- hopefully one who can let people in again.

That is a pretty big what.  It is the biggest single flaw with the show, the fact that it makes only a tenuous effort to make sense, and constantly undermines its own integrity as a result of the ever-changing creative team.  Imagine what a mess SOTM would be if new people took it over starting in Infernal Relics and rewrote all the continuity, so that now Tachyon was actually a space alien from the planet Speedulon while Tempest was really Legacy's undersea clone-brother with a lightning-variant AZ suit implanted under his skin which turned him green.  SOTM retains its quality largely as a result of being kept under the control of its original designers.  If the people who keep taking over Dr. Who and reinventing it would instead have created original shows, similar to Dr. Who but with their own continuities that don't constantly reinvent the past of a profitable franchise, the result would likely be substantially better.
