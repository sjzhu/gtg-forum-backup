I think a lot of the problem is still socialization, and the classifications we use in gender and sexuality are often doing harm as people who don't fit the "normal" categories try to fit into "alternate" categories that they don't fit into either.

Part of the prolbem is a universal one, language cannot accurately portray truth, it just gives us points along a spectrum that are acceptably inaccurate.

The problem with socialization even now is that since we are language dependant we ascribe to categories, people are Gay or Straight or Bi, Male Female or A-Sexual.  There are even more than that, but they will always fail to perfectly describe who someone is.

I was frequently mistaken (often accused) as gay, and I always was confused as to how to tell people I was straight.  They assumed that I was gay because I grew up with women and no men, and so always had more female friends than male friends, as well as being awkward around guys.  (Why is being awkward assumed gay?  Maybe because we make it awkward to be gay?  I don't know.)

Anyway My response when people accused or just tried to insult me was to just say "what's wrong with being gay?" Which in my small christian school got a lot of fun answers.

Where was this going?  Oh yeah, it is really hard to be comfortable in your own skin, even more so when you don't have words to describe what you are.  A lot of humans do themselves harm by trying to fit into categories that don't adequately define them, and a lot of portrayals of sexuality go wrong because characters need to fit into a category in order to convey what they are to other people.

I too was dissappointed by how they handled Willow's change, although I'm sure some people do go through changes like that, I know a guy who went from straight to bi to gay over time, it was a lot of internal mess he had to sort through.

I don't know if that mess was unavoidable, or the creation of society, or how much or either it was made up of.

I've also been (unwittingly and to my shame) a mojor source of anxiety for a friend coming out.  To the point that they were pretending to be straight until we moved away, and then thought we'd react badly to the news.  It was eye-openning, and really humbling for me.
