While that does sound like an incredibly fun (though very intimidating) concept, Christopher said (and I am paraphrasing here) that his SotM story had an end.  And with the story's end there is an end to the product as well.  I don't know if that means they'll not do any promo cards in the future or a mini-expansion every five or six years, but he seemed pretty firm that SotM wasn't going to be a neverending game.

The Sentinels Multiverse, however?  He said nothing about leaving that alone after SotM and Tactics.  I look forward to what we get to experience with this setting and these characters in the future outside of the established games they've already been in.

Hi All,

This is my 1st post! I've been lurking for just under a week, having jumped into SotM just this montha fter playing at a friend's house. 

Anyway, I think another important thing to remember, from a game designer's standpoint, is that simply sticking with 1 game (no matter how awesome that 1 game is), can get...boring? Boring may not be the right word, but limiting, maybe? Like a writer who pens a great novel, or a director who comes out with a brilliant movie, they don't JUST want to keep hammering out sequels because everyone loved their initial releases, right? They want to try something new, and not risk jumping the shark (ignore Guise here) on their initial hit. 

So maybe >G wants to try and make a new hit game. Let their creativity expand, and hopefully come up with something else excellent. So GSF isn't as loved as SotM. Tactics is a hit with some. Sure, it's in the same universe...uh...multiverse as SotM, but it is a different game. And you can tell that >G really wants to promote it and see it gain traction. There are already 2 new expansions up for pre-order, and comments that I've read online here from Paul I think where he said SotM is "near" its end, but Tactics is not. Then you have Spirit Island coming up, and that's a completely different game not in the Multiverse (most likely), right? I'm sure they hope it finds its own place in a good number of homes and can be called a successful release.

And really, once SotM is truly complete, and done, will you be unhappy? You'll have an awesome game with tons of replayability, a completed cast and storyline, and something you'll be proud to play with your friends and even introduce new people to. Or would you rather it expand and get, dare I say it, stale, with too many new mechanics and uninspired characters and such? Where you'll still purchase new sets so that you can have the "complete" game, but will at the same time be lamenting the past when every release was awesome. If the creators have a fleshed out and complete story in mind, let them complete it! Then they aren't forced to, well...force out something new just because.
