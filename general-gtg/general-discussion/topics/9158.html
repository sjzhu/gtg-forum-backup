AoG Spite:  I'd call him a 3, and general more difficult than his base version.  The primary reason is that he does more damage, with a secondary one being that his drugs are on a timer rather than randomly coming out.  He does much less healing, but compensates by increased aggression.  Flipping him can be a huge risk, since he's likely to get a monster damage turn before you start pulling hostages out of the safehouse to decrease the damage and get extra plays.  Because of the damage increase, getting rid of collateral damage is absolutely essential, as he can chain-kill all victims with a single hit (initial huit kills a victim, causing him to do damage, which kills another victim, etc, etc, etc)  

Controlling the drugs is difficult, but not impossible.  You need to be either be aggressive and take a lot of risks in playing his own cards (in an effort to get enough victims out), or stack the deck so that you know whats coming.  the first round is likely to set the tone of the game, as an initial play of a lab raid, forced entry or collateral damage (which destroy victim he starts with) can detrail any sort of strategy.

He's slightly easier with 3 heroes, in that theres a better chance you'll be able to flip him before he has all his drugs out.  5 player games may never reach his flip side.  Good samaritans are a HUGE risk, but the only real way to get out ahead of the drugs.

 

Skinwalker: I'd call him a high 3, possibly a low 4.  A lot depends on the heroes and environments, as giving him lots of targets is an easy way to get far more cultists/pins/relics/etc than can be contained.  He's suprisingly resilient, despite the 50 HP, and a few bad plays can really make life miserable.  AoE damage is very, very, very useful.  Damage control is very useful, especially as H goes up.  

I've found that the initial side tends to be the most dangerous, as its easier for things to spiral out of control.  If he flips with a large army, its going to be a bad scene, but if he is on his own when he flips you can leave him to chew himself up.  Selective immunity/damage control is very, very awesome, as is redirection.    In general, he wins or loses the game on his initial side.  If he can get you low enough, strip away enough cards, or amass an army before flipping, he's in good shape.  If you can control the flip, you're in a better position.  As always, Vast Followings can completely screw up plans and pull things back into play that took several turns to bring down (I'm looking at you, Pouch of Bones...).

Consider 3 heroes with 25 hp each, and an attack of H-2. Let's see how many instances of damage are needed to wipe out the team:

3p game - No DR: 75 / 1 = 75 instances

3p game - each with DR 1: Nullified

4p game - No DR: 100 / 2 = 50

4p game - each with DR 1: 100 / 1 = 100 Instances

5p game - No DR: 125 / 3 = 42 instances

5p game - each with DR 1: 125 / 2 = 53 instances

In each case, DR does help. Now, its true a DR of 1 can nulify the damage in a 3p game, but look how many times the heroes need to be hit before they are killed. Even in a 5p game with no DR, it would still take 42 times of being hit before being incapped. That's huge. You can often kill the villain before then, so it doesn't really matter, and villains don't play mroe cards baed on the number of people.
