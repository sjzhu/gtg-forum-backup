King of Tokyo is always a good pick for newcomers.  Just make sure you're playing with other people who make it fun.  Last time I tried to get a non-gamer into games via King of Tokyo, she thought it was incredibly boring because the other players were incredibly serious about it and took like 30 seconds in between each roll to figure out their most optimum strategy.  It's like "Dude...just roll the damn dice.  It's supposed to be a light, fast paced game."  There's a time for being competitive; playing King of Tokyo with a few first-timers who are trying to get into tabletop gaming is not one of those times.

Dominion is very good for introducing card playing mechanics to newcomers.  The theme and elements are light enough for anyone to get into and it also features phases so they can get familiar with the concept of order of play.  Just be careful: they might get so addicted to Dominion that they'll never want to move on to Sentinels :)

Red Dragon Inn, as you mentioned, is also a great beginner game.  And like King of Tokyo, it really shines when people aren't taking it too seriously and can just have fun.  Most newcomers to board games don't want to be competitive; that's one of the biggest barriers to getting into tabletop.

Smash Up! is also quite perfect.  It's incredibly light on mechanics (play a minion, play an action, draw 2), but has a lot of room for the play to begin to see "connections" and "combos" on their own without anyone holding their hand.  After only playing a few rounds, they'll begin to see moves they can make happen. "Oh, I see, I can play this zombie from my trash at the beginning of my turn for free, then use this minion who let's me play an extra action, then use these two actions to..."  That really creates a sense of having fun; being able to figure out how to play optimally on your own, and Smash Up! let's players do that with ease.  Plus who can argue to playing a game where Dinosaur Wizards fight Ninja Zombies?  No one, that's who.

Now I'm going to throw out two games that a lot of hardcore tabletop gamers tend to absolute hate with a fiery passion.  But hear me out.  Those games are Munchkin and Fluxx (or Star Fluxx/other Fluxx theme).  Now, I understand why gamers loathe these games, but putting that pure hatred aside, I think they can serve as great introductory games for the card-playing genre.  It's, again, the simple "Play a card, do what it says" element that these games do well (or in the case of Fluxx, completely uses it as a crutch to do whatever it wants).  Plus, they are light and silly and can definitely serve as party games.  I don't think they should always hit the table; by all means Smash Up! does most of what these two do mechanically and is a much better game.  But, if you're playing with a player who really doesn't take kindly to "rules" and the whole card-playing thing, these might be the good, fast paced drinking games that can serve as the gateway they need.

I actually consider this a "gateway game"…if you like playing it the first few times, move on to the Lord of the Rings deckbuilding games, which are much better-designed implementations of the same basic mechanic.  In DCH, Enemy cards are just like normal cards that you buy the same as anything else, and that feels utterly wrong for a superhero game; the Ambushes in LOTR are at least a nod to the idea that the game is supposed to be fighting back.  Plus the Supervillains in DCH are in a random order except for Ra's al-Ghul, and that means that you can get stuck in the middle of the game with an almost-invincible villain like Parallax or the Anti-Monitor, then have the game end with a whimper versus Black Manta or Captain Cold.  The Archenemy Level mechanic in LOTR fixes this nicely, ensuring the game always has a fairly smooth progression from easier to harder AEs.  And LOTR has a strong political element which, as long as you have at least three players, makes it a far more interactive game that's not just a race to the most VP.  The only thing about LOTR which I consider a step backwards is the absence of special powers on the character cards…technically, the oversized character cards don't actually do anything, since the characters differ only in which special card they have in their starter deck.  This was obviously done so Frodo could have the One Ring, but the Two Towers game changed that up in a very effective way, and now FOTR looks kinda pathetic by comparison.

TLDR - DCH is a good game to play a few times, but I don't advise buying it, unless you're a regular "games ambassador" (or a really diehard DC fan).  It's essentially a trainer for the LOTR FOTR DBG, which in turn is a trainer for LOTR T2T; play DCH with a friend's copy a few times, then buy those two instead, that's my advice.

I would really NOT recommend Pandemic, simply because there are 43 different ways to lose and they can crop up very fast.  You can do everything perfectly right with the dream team of characters... and go from completely in control of the sitution to a game over in 2 turns.  You can also get into a situation where you have everything you need to win but there's not enough time to accomplish it, or a situation where you know you can't possibly win but have to wait a bunch of turns to actually lose.  For people with no tabletop gaming experience, that can be super-frustrating and off-putting.  There's just so much of the game that's up to chance rather than skill.  A friend of mine has played 10+ games and has never actually won.

Forbidden Island/Desert, on the other hand, while mechanically similar, are a lot more forgiving and tend to be better for newbies.  They can still go downhill in a hurry, but there's not so many lose conditions and they tend to be more fun even if you're losing.  Carcassonne is an amazingly fun game and super-easy to learn, but I'm not sure it's a good intro game for Sentinels.  It's just too different to be used for that purpose.  Carcassonne is probably my second favorite boardgame after Sentinels, though.  I'd still recommend it if they refuse to believe good adult boardgames even exist, however.
