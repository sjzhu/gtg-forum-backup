I found that since I'd gotten the decks as collections it was easier (like McBehr seems to have done) to keep them arranged as collections. I was already used to having the first ten heroes together, so when I got two more (Rook City), it was easier for me to put them after the first ten then to alphabetize them.

 

Also, with the first ten, I found that sorting through all ten alphabetically was ... unfun? ... not sure the best adjective to use there. What I did instead (again, as McBehr seems to) was to put the freedom five in front and the other five in the back. I then didn't bother to sort each collection. When I want a hero, I go to the section where those heroes are and then I have at most five heroes through which to search, and I don't have to stop to think about alphabetical order. This also has the benefit of not having to worry about how to alphabetize The Chairman or La Capitan, etc.

 

My decks are sleeved and in a briefcase with four columns. The first column has two collections -- the freedom five and the other five from the core game. The second column has all of the other heroes in three collections -- Rook City, Infernal Relics plus Unity, and Shattered Timelines plus Scholar. There is also a fourth collection there -- the four villains from the core game. The third column has all of the other villains in three collections -- Rook City, Infernal Relics plus Ambuscade, and Shattered Timelines plus Miss Information. The four column has four collections for environments -- Core, Rook City, Infernal Relics, Shattered Timelines plus the Block and Silver Gulsh, and then has space for all of the playtesting decks from Vengeance.

 

This briefcase, however, will probably not hold the official, sleeved decks for Vengeance, much less then fifth and sixth expansions, so I'm not sure what I'm going to do in the future.
