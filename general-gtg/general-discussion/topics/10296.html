Agreed.

Drink lots of water (preferably warm, even though it's disgusting), breathe in steam as often as you can (the shower works well for this), don't shout, and don't whisper. And rest your voice as much as you can. Avoid the "numbing" throat sweets like the plague, since you'll do far more damage if you don't realise how much your voice hurts, like injecting anaesthetic into a broken ankle and then walking on it. Alcohol, spicy foods, and (for some people) dairy products are also probably best avoided if you have a sore throat, but that's probably asking too much at a convention, right?

As a singer I'm mad paranoid about losing my voice or doing permanent damage to it - my choir's been raising money for a charity for vocal injuries for the last two years so we've learned a lot about vocal health. Risking losing your voice long-term just isn't worth it.

arenson9:
Silverleaf wrote:
 don't shout
 Not shouting, unfortunately, is nearly impossible when demoing in a large hall 

Then learn to project, and engage muscles in the back, neck and head to support the sound and make it louder without the vocal fold-damaging turbulence caused by shouting. When you shout you usually make yourself more indistinct anyway. 

My favourite trick is to imagine I'm creating a heavy bag of groceries in each hand, using the appropriate back and shoulder muscles (you can also try putting your hands in a prayer position and pushing them together to "feel" your back muscles working). It's amazing what difference things like that make to your volume - it's how opera singers can project over a full orchestra and/or choir without a microphone. 

Another thing to consider is that if it's loud enough that you have to shout, you're also damaging your hearing at the same time if you're there for any length of time.

The volunteer dinner was pretty low key, though well attended and lots of fun. We were at the Rock Bottom Brewery and there must have been about 30 people there. I didn't even manage to say hello to everyone. Paul handed out exhibitor badges. Craig came around to each of the tables to check in with the volunteers and make sure everyone knew what was going on. There is a meetup planned tomorrow morning before the show opens where volunteers will get their t-shirts and Kevin and Luther will be showing people how to play/demo Sentinel Tactics.

 

I'm not working at the booth during the con itself, but helped a bit with setup on Tuesday. The booth is 20 feet by 40 feet. A storage room has been formed on one end using shelves. Black fabric is draped around the outside of the shelves and then banners are draped over the fabric announcing Sentinels, GSF, Tactics, Sidekick, and the coming digital version of Sentinels. There are also various price lists, including a few games that GtGs sells on behalf of other publishers. In front of one side of the storage room two long, tall trunks form an ordering counter that holds two cash registers. The storage room is absolutely packed with boxes, about eight feet high. On Tuesday my contribution to setup was helping Christopher hang the fabric and the banners around the shelves to form the storage room. While Christopher perched on top of the shelves, I brought him the fabric and banners. He then used a staple gun to put everything in place. During that time Paul, Adam, and Matt were unloading boxes and organizing them on the shelves. It took a few hours to do all that. They had mostly completed putting together the shelves before I arrived.

 

The other 2/3rds or so of the booth has ten tables crammed on it. That part of the booth is along an aisle on the long side and then the end abuts the back aisle of the exhibit center, so there is lots of room for people to line up and/or watch over the games in progress, as need be. 

 

I've managed to get three games of Tactics in the last two days. The convention hasn't even officially started and I'm already going brain dead from the overload of things to do and people to see.

Whew. GenCon is finally done and I have survived, mostly.

My attention can only be split so many ways and GenCon keeps coming up with new ways to split it. Before Greater Than Games existed I was already trying to do way too much all at once and now I want to go by that booth all the time. Oh, and GenCon's ramping up their music track, so I went to three awesome concerts this year. And my good friend had never been to GenCon, so I wanted to go and do all the cool things that he'd never had a chance to do before. And I have a big group of friends that have decided in just the last couple years that GenCon is the place they want to meet up for a convention. We took over the hallway in the basement of one of the hotels until the wee hours on Sat.

Early in the week I was still reasonably cogent and able to make conversation. By the end of the week, I'm afraid I was pretty much just grunting while whirling maniacally from event to event. Pydro, next time let's plan in advance and carve out a time away from the exhibit hall to talk for at least five minutes.

I didn't work in the booth at all during the show itself, but did lend a hand a bit during setup and teardown. It was a really surreal experience, in fact, to sit down a couple of times as just a player in the GtG booth rather than being the person running the game. My thanks to Nick and Tara for an excellent demo of GSF (as well as my thanks to Kyle for creating that demo). My thanks, of course, to Christopher for the demo of Spirit Island.

GenCon is billed as the best four days in gaming, but my GenCon started three days early, meeting up with people who came in Monday in preparation for two days of booth building. By Wednesday I'd already essentially been at the con for three days, talked to a ton of people, and gotten in three games of Sentinel Tactics. With booth teardown, hanging out with friends Sunday night, and a close friend staying with us for GenCon who flew out this afternoon, it only really feels like GenCon ended tonight -- a full week after it started for me.

I saw SO MANY PEOPLE -- forumgoers and people I'd met at other conventions. We had Germans! That was very special for me, seeing Daniel and Antje who'd worked in the booth at Essen.

For those who haven't already seen it, the charity auction garnered more than $1100.  Christopher auctioned off fifteen promos: Each of the Freedom 6, Eternal Haka, Dark Visionary, Mad Bomber Blade, Cosmic Omnitron, Horus of two Horizons, Reedemer Fanatic, Rook City Wraith, and _TWO_ copies of Young Legacy. 

My thanks to all of the people who helped me have such a wonderful time. I'm not going to name names, so as not to risk missing someone, but if we even said hello at some point in passing, I'm appreciative.
