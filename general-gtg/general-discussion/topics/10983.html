If you watch the videos on the kickstarte they are some pretty sturdy material. if the pegs are too big a little work with an exacto knife will do the trick. A tiny drop of modeling glue (i like to use the ones with the long wire thin applicator for this kind of work) will seal it up fine. If you do manage to mess up a foot, a tiny bit of flock (fake grass) or some miniature rocks will do a nice trick around it to keep the majority of the base clear for play purposes, and yet still look great

I'm using a mix of Reaper, Testor, and Privateer Press paints on the washed and scrubbed but otherwise unprimed miniatures.

The figures are a little hydrophobic, so water based paints will sometimes recede from where you put the paint down. A little persistence will pay off and the paint will hold, though. It does make it hard to do some stains, so if you're heavily into washes and stains, you'll probably need to prime the figures first.

Also, some of the figures lack detail in key areas, especially the faces. I'm sure really skilled people can make them good, but they're tricky and you'll really need good lighting to make out the details.

All that said, I can't see why Citadel paints wouldn't work, and I'd rather work with a paint set that I knew (in terms of colors, thickness, etc) then get a bunch of new paints that I couldn't trust.
