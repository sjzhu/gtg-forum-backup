I'm waiting for the promo pack personally; I'll play the promos in solo games if I'm at my computer at home, but haven't played enough games that I feel I need them in real games (apart from the three Dark Watch heroes I have - no Setback yet).  I'd rather buy them from >G directly and not from the Printer's Studio, even though I think it's really cool that >G allows folks to do otherwise.

On stories - they definitely should have endings, but they need not have a "The End"...what's best is to do arcs.  For instance, I'm currently reading Fables, and there are at least three definite arcs to that - The Empire, Mr. Dark, and the current story which focuses on a prophecy regarding Snow's children.  If you just read up to the point where the Emperor is defeated, that's essentially one complete story (albeit with a few loose threads still hanging), but then there's another story there for you to move on to if you wish.  It's a good model, and story-wise it's what >G is doing with Multiverse and then Tactics, but in gameplay terms it does still mean SOTM is going to be "complete" at a certain point and not expand any further; there are good and bad aspects to that, as it means the replayability factor eventually stops rising (not that it isn't already tremendous) and you stop having "new shiny" to get hooked on, but at the same time you also can eventually rest on your laurels and say "I'm done" and take satisfaction in the fact that you've got the whole game.

For a great example, look at the Arkham Horror game series.  One base game, then a long string of expansions, and then finally they're done - but then they move along and publish similar-but-different games like Eldritch Horror and Elder Sign.  That's not to say I wouldn't be excited if more Arkham Horror came out, but there's some satisfaction to knowing that there's an endpoint, that your collection is actually capable of reaching completion.

On stories - they definitely should have endings, but they need not have a "The End"…what's best is to do arcs.

Sometimes an End is correct though, it depends on the need of the story. Whilst the Terminator films have each had an arc (even if Salvation felt like a different film crowbarred in to the Terminator franchise), at some point, it would be good to reach the end of Skynet. I tend to treat the Matrix as ending at the end of the first film (though I'll never get the time back spent watching the sequels…)

In the case of Sentinels, having an arc for each gameline works, you need that possibility of continuation for the game. It's why I feel Lord of the Rings games tend to be unsatisfying - you can't simply recreate the events of the book, but nor are the events that follow it that interesting (not enough threat). There might be some scope for games dealing with the Witch-King of Angmar (as was) but even then, you start to get away from the book. I guess also it's why I'm more interested in Star-Trek games than I am Star Wars, even though I much prefer the latter (also the simple issue of having many more factions in Star Trek making a richer gaming environment)
