Has 'Sentinels' taken over your everyday vocabulary ?

I used say 'flip' when I meant 'becoming really angry', as in "Good thing he left, one more minute and I would have flipped."

A while ago, I realised  that I was starting to use it more in a sense that Villain Cards flip, which in most cases means that they become really unpredictable/crazy/hard to deal with/harder to get rid of. Which, I guess, sounds exactly the same as the other 'flip' but , hey,  I know that there is a difference.

(I have had other games which found their way in my everyday vocabulary, for example, I still call all negative effects 'Bad Stuff' - a relic from when I still played a lot of Munchkin.)

So, have you ever encountered your boss on Advance mode? 'End of Day'ed an entire project? Referred to someone who was off sick as 'Incapped'?

And more importantly, can you think of any more ways in which Sentinels can 'improve' the English language (or at least make it a damn sight more colourful)?

There's a term our group uses a lot in SOTM and in other games.

It's based of a card in the Wraith deck called "Impromptu Invention" featuring the Wraith holding what looks like a hair dryer rigged as a weapon. Every player in our group dubbed it as being one of the most powerful cards in the game since it allows her to set up her other equipments very quickly. Arguably it is her best card and the first card mentioned to anyone new to playing the Wraith.

So anytime there's an ability or mechanic that is considered the best for a certain character, we call it their "hair dryer"

 

E.X. Playing D&D with same friends as a conjurer. I keep using teleport, a 5th level spell regarded as my character's "hair dryer", since everyone relies on me to transport them places and I'm the only one in the party capable of teleportation.
