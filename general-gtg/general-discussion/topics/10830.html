Spirit Island has at least three elements of considerable complexity -- 1) There are a wide number of possible actions each player can take, 2) The interleaving of slow and fast actions with the colonists' actions leads to a challenge in holding in mind what will happen when, and 3) The precession of which types of lands will be affected in different ways by the colonists each turn leads to a further challenge in holding in mind what will happen when.

 

The result, for me, is a tremendous about of brainspace required to do what I'd normally like to do, think through all the possibilities in order to choose the one I think best.

 

To enjoy Spirit Island, then, I had to let go of my perfectionism and allow myself to be happy with dedicating what brainspace I could. This was a lesson I learned the hard way on Galactic Strike Force, which took me quite a few plays to really enjoy very much, though I eventually did. The end result for Spirit Island was that it was super fun for me by the second game. I'm really looking forward to more.

 

My favorite parts of playing so far have been: 1) Feeling as if I am this unique spirit who is collaborating with other spirits to work to protect something I love, 2) The unfolding of the various random elements -- new powers becoming available, colonists and natives doing various things.
