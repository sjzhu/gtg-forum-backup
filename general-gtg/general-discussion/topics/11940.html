It's much more difficult to balance, you can't flex your foot in the same way to push off from the ground, your calf muscles can't move as freely and are therefore less efficient, your ankle muscles are constantly readjusting to keep you upright.

Most of all though it's painful. Much more of your body weight is on the balls of your feet than usual, rather than being evenly distributed over the heel, ball and outer arch. As far as the balls of your feet are concerned, you're putting twice as much pressure on them as you do naturally. This is why women who wear heels when they go out often buy those gel pads for their shoes and still end up taking the shoes off before the end of the night and swapping to a pair of soft flats they keep in their bag, or even going barefoot.

There are treatments you can get nowadays where fillers are injected into the balls of your feet every few months to cushion them, like semi-permanent gel pads. No one would do that if heels didn't hurt.

It really depends on your heels and of course, training. Dancing or running in block heels, for instance, is no biggie, provided they are closed and not a silly sling/open toed deal. While running, you lean forward anyway, so the block actually helps you push off with the ball of your foot. As for dancing, I can't really tell you why, but heels change the way you balance your body, and ballroom, tap etc, actually becomes easier in heels than in flats (Tap dancing in flats is pretty much impossible, actually).

Pointy heels are a different matter, but it is entirely possible to run with them, and run fast at that. Where I live, there is a race at the Pride festival each year, where the contestants run in spikey heels (most of them are dudes), and I have never actually seen someone fall. The actual heel doesn't have contact to the ground when they run anyway, so with a pair of shoes you are comfortable in and some practice, you can run as fast in heels as in sneakers.

So yeah, if you want impossible, try running in flip-flops. Now that is a nightmare!
