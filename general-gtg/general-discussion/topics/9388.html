Okay, well with my finances where they are, I'm not certain whether I'll be able to buy Vengeance when it hits the shelves, so I gave in and read this spoiler.  So I don't know what the cards do and I haven't seen any of the other art, but I've got enough here to form opinions with.  Here are some thoughts I've begun to form.

  • Man-Grove is the most amazing name ever, closely followed by the Radio-Activist.

  • Choke looks really cool, from the little bit of her I can see in that rather small picture.

Equity is also a really cool concept, particularly going by his name - presumably he's a lot like Two-Face, only with a corporate background rather than a legal one.

  • Highbrow is a very obvious Leader homage, but that's a great name for one.

  • Citizen Slash may be "No Executions", and the Crackjaw Crew is almost certainly "The Whole Gang", plus Doc Tusser is obviously "Dead or Alive" as well as Terrible Tech-Strike and Eye on the Prize (although it seems likely my theory of him being an immortal alien is shot; more likely he mutated himself with drugs, perhaps acquired through a predecessor of Pike Industries).  This means we know who everyone in CR's deck is!  (Actually by now we probably know everyone in just about all of the pre-Vengeance decks.)

But with those quick comments aside, there now comes a rant.  Now, admittedly, I'm not seeing most of the picture here, so I may be way off base.  But it seems to me as though a lot of these nemesis-assignments are rather forced, having been done to force these characters into a "one nemesis per hero (including three yet-to-be-revealed heroes)" pattern, just for the sake of doing so.  I'm not going to worry about the things that we obviously have no way to guess at, like why Tantrum is Sky-Scraper's nemesis when we have so little information about either of them.  But these questions seem glaringly obvious to me:

  • Why is The Seer Fanatic's nemesis when we've only seen him fighting Nightmist?

  • Ruin makes some sense as Akash'Bhuta's nemesis, but very little as far as I can see for having a grudge against AA or vice versa, any more than he would against anyone else.  Why not pair him with Natch?

  • Equity is almost unquestionably one of the four figures who are seen on Rook City Wraith's incap side.  Two of those individuals are Wraith's nemeses, and the third remains unknown.  Why exactly is Equity not a Wraith nem?

  • Vyktor definitely should be Tempest's nem, but shouldn't he maybe also be Sky-Scraper's?

  • Is Calypso Ra's nemesis for any reason other than a lame "water beats fire" shtick?

  • Highbrow is a very obvious Leader homage.  Since the Leader is Hulk's nemesis, and Haka is the closest SOTM analogue to Hulk, shouldn't Highbrow maybe be another Haka nem?  (This one is not terribly convincing even to me; after all, Hulk's mini-nem is Hippo, who's obviously equatable to Rhino, who's opposed to Spider-Man…not sure if we really have a Spidey analogue in SOTM, probably Setback is closest…but if Hippo was Setback's nem then I'd be asking why he wasn't Haka's, for the same reason as my first point above.)

  • And to really get nitpicky about it, on one level I like that the Sentinels got a four-member team as their mini-nem, but at the same time, I'm looking at the Vengeance Five, the Ennead, and minion-based villain decks like La Capitan and Citizen Dawn, and going "why the blue blazes would four individuals be one target card?  Are they physically attached to each other?  If not, why are they not distinct individuals?"  I realize there's probably a good answer, and there doesn't even really need to be one; again, I'm semi-satisfied with the way this comes out, but I still think that it begs the question.

If there is information on Vengeance cards which helps to clear any of this puzzlement up, please feel free to share.

Take a hero like Spiderman, and look at who his is Nemesis. The Green Goblin? Doc Octopus ? Both are valid candidates - and neither is especially a "spidermanly" villain if you consider their powers or abilities. The Goblin may even be seen as ridiculous. But they are among the greatest and/or more frequent ennemies of Spidey. There are also others - like the Vulture, the Sandman - who are frequent Spidey ennemies, even if they are not as "glamorous". They would fit the mini-nemesis idea. And neither has powers and/or abilities especially "anti-spidey". At least at first sight.

Nemesises are not "deduced" from the heroes abilities - sometimes even they have no relation at all to his background. They " happen". They become the hero's worst ennemy because they make a great story, or because they come back again and again, or because the readers (or writers) are inspired by them. It's not a question of power level - the hero most frequent opponent may not be the most powerful. It's a Nemesis, though : a recurring villain that you immediately associate with the hero. Not because the marketing department constructed them to be a "nemesis", but because the publication's history made it so.

A Nemesis can be a frequent adversary - even a low power, or ridiculous one. Or it can be a memorable villain who ever appeared once, but was a big challenge, or a memorable story. Or it can be a very powerful ennemy. Anything that, for a reason or another, gives a front seat to the character in the hero's stories.

SotM follows the comics' tropes. There are big guns - the villains with a deck. And there are lower level villains, who team up against team of heroes. And there are the "small" villains, the one who are just powerful enough to be a challenge for a lone hero, not for a team. The mini-nemesises who would appear a lot in the comic books issues of the related hero, but are not powerful enough to fight alone a whole team.*

The reason we may find the pairing strange, or some characters not interesting, is that we didn't read the comics. Alas.

 

* untill >G publishes a new line of expansions/games where one lone hero fights one lone villain - SotM:Duels ;)
