This was inspired by some discussion in the Quiz topic. Could any of the other playtesters help talk about how currently-published heroes, villains, etc. varied from their current state during playtesting? I thought it'd be interesting to talk about. My particular input was how generally BAD the Scholar was in his first few incarnations. copy-pasta'd from the other thread:

Ok, so, at first, his elemental cards were all limited, and not NEARLY as utilitarian as they are now. Mortal Form was "whenever the scholar would regain HP, he deals that much damage instead. Power: destroy this card" Solid to liquid was "double all heroes' HP regeneration. you cannot deal damage. Power: destroy this card." Flesh to Iron was pretty much the same as it is now, but with a 2 card cost per turn.

 

He did have SOME things he could do. Expect the worst caused him to turn all damage he would take into HP, and would only go away once he actually regained HP with it, so having it with Mortal Form would turn him into an infinite redirecting machine. But that's pretty much it, and as any game developer will tell you, when a game with a cast of characters like this develops a single, dominant strategy, that's pretty bad.

 

If you have any playtesting stories or questions for us regarding game elements (that are already out; please don't tempt me with spoiler questions) as they were in playtesting, here's the place for it, I guess.

Good ol' playtesting, been a playtester since Rook City. Back then, I would of willingly admitted that Mr. Fixer was a weaker hero, because the first build we had recieved wasn't really the best.

  • The Meditation we know today used to retrieve styles from the trash or it could retrieve a Overdrive from the trash aswell. While another card allowed searched his deck for a style.
  • Riveting Crane increased damage dealt to the targets hit by Fixer. Which practically made it the go to Style, probably the reason for the change.
  • Harmony and Charge were not a thing
  • There was a card that allowed Fixer to draw a card at the start of his turn if there were no environment cards in play.
  • Salvage Yard only retireved equipment cards from the trash equal to the amount of cards in the environment trash. Lacked the put Overdrive into play. (As you probably guessed after Meditation and the other were joined is when Salvage Yard got that)
  • Dual Crowbars allowed for hitting a third target. For a bit it lost the +1, which made it a worse Jack Handle, so instead it lost a target and regained the +1.
  • Harmony (went by Synergy) almost increased Fixer's damage for the turn when he played a Style and/or a Tool. Which would be able to give him a +2 for the round if he could play 2 cards.

 

I tried a lot to try and get Bloody Knuckles to remain in play, but sadly Christopher would never budge.

Expat didn't change much, only Speed Loading (at the start of her turn, it allowed her to search the deck or trash for an ammo card to put into hand. It lacked the "put an ammo into play" part) and Shock Rounds (was a whenever the gun is used to deal damage, it increases damage dealt to that target by 1).

Just for anyone who may not know how exactly playtesting for >G works. Heck, I am not even 100% sure what happens before the first batch are posted for us to use. However, from what I have gathered, the >G crew actually playtest quite a bit before it arrives for us. So most mechanics and even cards a pretty set in stone by the time they arrive to the Forum Playtesters. Currently the only deck that has gone through a complete redesign is Setback (sorry, I hope this isn't rubbing it in anyone's face, I try my best not to come off that way), most other decks only have some cards altered (such as increasing/decreasing damage/health), some sub text are moved from more powerful cards to the weaker ones, and the occasional card replacement. Don't mistake this as me saying that Playtesters have no say, a lot of the changes that a made are direct request of playtesters.

Argent Adept was really insane when he was first introduced, Syncopated Onslaught and Counterpoint Bulwark worked on all hero targets rather than just 2. Plus the Accompany on Alacritous Subdominant didn't destroy itself. Early on Rythyms were only Performs while Melodies had both Perform and Accompany. He also had a target in his deck, that had HP equal to the Instruments plus 2 or 3. What that target did was allow Argent to activate a accompany immediately after that accompany was activated. He pratically made all the heroes invincible, while making them a wrecking crew.

NightMist really didn't change much, most of her changes were card name changes. The biggest change I can think of is the Amulet however (so fortunate for that one) which would let her draw a card at the start of her turn, but at the end of her turn she would have to discard one. Really not that great, especially compared to the current. Also I Heedless Lash (at the time called Energy Blast) discarded the revealed card instead of putting it into play or hand.

Unity's biggest change was making it easier to put bots into play. Before she would have to destroy 2 equipments for her base power, discard 2 for Modular Workbench, and Construction Pylon only put 1 into play. I also don't think she had a way to heal bots in the beginning. Most other changes were HP totals of the bots.

Akash'Bhuta and Ennead I believe went mostly unchanged. At least I can't think of anything...

GloomWeaver was severely weakened. However I think there has been a thread about this at one point.

Apostate's changes consisted mostly of HP totals. However Corrupted Effegy had another name, and worked like Undaunted. Runes of Malediction only gave a DR1, but also gave it to Demons. While the Orb only redirect damage from Apostate.

I can't recall any drastic changes from the environments however.

 

Edit: I shall toss this in, I was a bad playtester for Shattered Timelines. So I can't say anything I remember from those.
