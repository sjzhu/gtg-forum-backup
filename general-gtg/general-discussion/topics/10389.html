Frankly, it's impossible to show everything that you can see on the table in a game of Sentinels on a tablet screen. On a 50" 4K TV, perhaps. We need to work within the capabilities we have. That means showing the most important information at a given time, while making it easy to see everything else you may need with as few interactions as possible.

On the screen posted here, you can scroll through the hero & environment cards horizontally, and/or bring up the "thought bubble" view as seen in the previous post. And just as before you can bring up the full text of any card quickly.

However, as mentioned in the past, we are planning to include another screen (the "overview") that attempts to show as much information as possible. The overview can be activated at any time, and would show at least the title & HP of all cards in play. Now you can imagine what can happen in a game of Sentinels and think about whether that screen could include artwork & text.. the other day we played Matriarch and she had every single fowl in play.

I wonder if it would be helpful to have hero, villain, and environment views that only showed those play areas, and then an overview view that showed HP totals and number of cards in play across all the play areas. Maybe the titles of the cards could be shown, too. For each individual play area, maybe there'd be enough room to show the text of at least some of the cards? Not sure about that.

I'm envisioning that on my turn if I need to see other play areas, I would switch to those views as needed and then back to my play area in order to play, use power, draw, discard, etc.

 

With these individual views, I can envision that if one is playing alone, one would see the buttons/functionality for taking actions for each play area, but if one is playing with other people, then one might still be able to see their play areas, but wouldn't be able to take actions in them.

 

I'm envisioning a startup screen, like Artemis has, where one chooses which ship one is on and then chooses a set of stations to be available when playing. Some of the stations can only be chosen by one person (like a hero's play area), but some could be controlled by multiple people (like the villain or environment play areas).

 

Artemis achieves this in part by having one of the connected computers be the place where games are started. I imagine you wouldn't want to have to have a dedicated central computer for this, though.
