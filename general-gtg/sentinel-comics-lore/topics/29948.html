I’m not the kind of fan who likes their fiction super meta, so I’m not fond of the implication that Grimm exists in the story the reader is reading because it’s a story and someone’s reading it … that’s not my jam. However! I’m not complaining about this episode, because! (just imagine exactly the inflection Christopher uses when he says that), I think that based on the way they told the story here, Grimm isn’t some meta being that exists in a metafictive narrative space outside the Sentinel Comics universe, I think he is actually an entity within the setting who has specific, defined (if loosely) magical and multiversal powers. Similar to OblivAeon, he does a thing within the universe that affects things outside that universe, yet he is playing by a set of rules and can be bested. That’s my interpretation even if it contradicts the canon, and this is a rare case where I feel able to give C&A more credit than they perhaps deserve, which is nice given how often I do the opposite.
