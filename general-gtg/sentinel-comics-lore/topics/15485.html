I think that the homages are what make the story possible at all.  Realistically speaking, a card game does not have enough space to tell a story as rich and complex as the one Christopher and Adam are telling.  (If you like math:  according to what I could find online, the average comic book page has ~150 words and ~5 panels, and the average comic book has 24 pages.  That's 3600 words and 120 "pictures" per issue.  SotM is trying to tell its story using around 60,000 words, and 1,500 pictures.  That's about 15 comic book issues of material, to tell a story that would span hundreds of comic books if it actually existed.)

The only reason that this is at all possible is because they are using a vast ocean of preexisting material as the glue that holds their story together.  They don't need to spend time familiarizing players with Maia Montgomery's personality - all they need is an opening hand of Utility Belt, Razor Ordanance, and Trust Fund and any player will know that the Wraith is going to be brooding and reclusive.  Sure she's not exactly Batman, but now Christopher and Adam can use their remaining space on filling in the blanks and refining her character.  The players get to know who she is much better than would be possible if they had had to start from ground zero.

I believe Homage is truly the key word here. Each SotM character is an amalgamation of traits borrowed from / inspired by multiple pre-existing comic characters. Hence one can never draw a direct comparison between a SotM character and any single pre-existing comic characters. It only makes sense when multiple pre-existing characters are used to describe (or analogise) a single SotM character.

(Exceptions may exist but I can’t pinpoint any off the top of my head)

Having said that, with the sheer volume of pre-existing characters out there, and the looooong history of comics, is it even possible to create a truly original character? I suspect no matter how original a creation is, you will always be able to say “ah but this character is just a combination of [this character] and [that character]”.

To then answer the question that is the title of this thread:

Within such constraints of pop culture, I think the SotM characters should be entitled to the label of ‘original’, to the degree that anything can be original these days. And by the same verse, the characters are also ‘copies of other characters’ to the extent that all pop culture things are most likely copies of other pop culture things.

Mr Rambles out.

I've been on a big superhero literature kick recently. Three of the best stories I've read were the "Wearing the Cape" Series, "Grrl Power" Webcomic and Worm: The webserial (you can PM me for links if interested). Going to try very hard not to spoil anything.

Flight, Super Strength, Enhanced Durability, no laser eyes though. However, Astra, Maxima, and Alexandria are all completely different characters, and they are also completely different from the source material.

So even when we say Legacy is like a mix between Superman and Captain America... we're only talking in the broadest strokes. Legacy isn't an outsider as much as those two are, he isn't an alien or awoke 50 years later after being frozen. He's a family man, vastly different from those other two who only had flings or steady girlfriends, Legacy has a wife and daughter, possibly Aunts and Uncles, Nieces and Nephews. He doesn't keep these lives secret, he invites his companions to his place for a barbacue. And all of this information is in the cards or the rulebook background. This isn't something that takes a Sentinels expert to uncover, it just takes reading flavor text.
