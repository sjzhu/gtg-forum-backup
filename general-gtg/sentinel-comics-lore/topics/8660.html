When I unboxed my order of Shattered Timelines and all the Mini-Expansions, I was frustrated to note that, having bought Unity and Ambuscade in part because there were dividers for them in the box, I now had more dividers for them.  I'm not really complaining about that, it makes perfect sense to sell them with a divider, and it makes a little sense to have had the dividers in the EE box as a teaser, encouraging you to buy more product (hey, it worked on me).

But there was also another seemingly useless...but rather cool...piece of cardboard in each of the two packages: a little summary card which describes the character's backstory, lists their complexity number, and possibly gives a little other information, I forget exactly.  Essentially, a trading card.  My Scholar and Miss Information did not come with such cards, and as far as I can tell, they exist for no-one other than those first two promo heroes (which is a little sad, as it means there's no short-form backstory for the second pair of promos, as there is in the rulebook for all normal heroes).  On some level, even though cards like these serve no purpose whatsoever, I still kind of wish ALL the decks came with one (this is the kind of wish that, upon receiving a lifetime supply free pizza, wishes it could have included a little packet of dipping sauce; I'm not actually complaining or demanding anything here, just making small talk).

Any suggestions as to what I should actually do with these things?  Or the extra dividers (I have 4 in total, since I was ordering duplicates of U and A for a friend)?  Or, for that matter, the empty Shattered Timelines box now that I have everything packed in with the EE?

The reason EE came with dividers, originally the Mini-Expansion didn't, plus Ambuscade and Unity was in the same print run as EE, so why not toss them in there to make things easier, probably cheaper as well. After the next batch of Mini-Expansions were released, they decided having them come with dividers would be easier, as to not make anyone that gets Scholar/Miss Information/Final Wasteland/Silver Gulch have to buy Shattered Timelines to gain dividers for them. The little card that is also no included was probably an idea they had when they needed to reprint the first 2 mini-expansions.

Probably the next printing of EE will only carry EE material dividers. Or they will continue to carry them for awhile longer, just incase anyone let to the band wagon of upgrading decides to buy it years from now.

Actually, I found a potential use for the box.  Usually if I want to go someplace and play, I'll want to carry choices of villain and environment, so the potential new player can get a sense of the variety.  But if you're going to a regular meetup and you know what heroes all five players will want (or which five heroes would be best for three players to choose from), and what single villain to fight in what environment...as it happens, you can fit all the decks for a single five-player game in the three spaces of the box's plastic cradle.  (Two hero decks on each side, the fifth hero and the villain and environment in the deeper center space, and for a little more room and comfort, you can put the hero character cards in the middle chamber and then distribute them before or after passing out the decks they go with.)  Thusly, you have a smaller box to carry.  (You might be able to fit at least some of the tokens under the cradle, or just keep them out in ziplocs; the result would still be less of a space hog than the full box.)

Now, if the boxes were a centimeter or two bigger in at least one dimension, it would be possible to fit eight or nine two-deck stacks into the box without the cradle, though they'd probably get mixed up in shuffling unless you constructed your own cardboard dividers to tape into the sides of the box and create compartments.  As it is, you can still do this, but you'll probably want styrafoam rather than cardboard, and it would take some doing.  You could get I think six stacks, which would amount to perhaps eight heroes, two villains (four games if you play on both regular and advanced), and four environments...plenty enough to make a decent demo version of the game.  (With this, you'd definitely have to carry the tokens outside.)
