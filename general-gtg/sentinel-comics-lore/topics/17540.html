I too made most of my decisions based on what would make for a cool story rather than who would beat whom in a fair fight.  That was mostly due to my misconception that this would be a magazine, with 2-3 battles per issue, with lots of time for talking.  I didn't think it could be a single book since it would have to be hundreds of pages to do them any kind of justice.  Once I realized that, most of what I would have found surprising (not having Ra vs. Fanatic, Setback vs. Expatriette) made much more sense.

I figured The Operative would beat Fright Train.  His armor isn't one solid piece, it has gaps, and she'd find them.  I did not expect Plague Rat's infection to have an impact on that fight, since Revocorp cured Plague Rat of his infectiousness, and it only came back in the Miststorm timeline.

Like so many others, I thought Bugbear would feed off the Proletariats and grow stronger.  Seems he was the only one to not get at least some sort of explanation and incentive to fight before being tossed in.

Biomancer vs. Isis was a big one for me.  I had no idea he could transmute himself.  I figured that, like the Chairman, he'd bow out as quickly as he could manage.

I know lots of folks expected Citizen Dawn to beat Progeny, but I wasn't one of them.  In Progeny's original form, the only way to beat him (in the comics) is to exploit the weaknesses he creates in himself when he adapts to attack forms.  Dawn only has one attack form.  The fact that he was both in a weakened state and effectively solar-powered changed this completely.

And, of course, Benchmark in general.  The fact that the writers of Cosmic Contest were at odds with the writers of Benchmark's other appearances threw me off completely.  I expected him to do far better.  Writhe, too; he's supposed to rely heavily on inventions, but didn't use them at all, so I didn't expect him to be a one-trick pony (nor so dumb as to try to enter an absolute-zero environment suit).
