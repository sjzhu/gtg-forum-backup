I'm amused how they say they went through so much trouble to make sure they never use names that other properties for similar heroes, but Tempest is their aquatic hero, and both Marvel and DC have aquatic heroes named Tempest!  To be fair, they did also suggest in passing that names that are so common as to have entered the public domain are also OK.

I'm also amused how they initially wanted to create something that came out of the early 00's, then got sidetracked and wanted to create a Dark Watch Zhu Long-based alchemist (blood mage, really, the way they were describing it), and then ended up with a monster and a bruiser instead.  I kept trying to second-guess where they'd end up and got it wrong every time.

That said, since they never quite finished, here's where I'd go with it:

--------------

In Zhu Long's earliest days, even he made mistakes.  His first attempts at the Oni masks involved simply killing people and performing the ritual on them.  Usually this worked--violent deaths at the hands of Zhu Long's assassins are usually sufficient cause for a spirit to become vengeful--but some of the masks simply failed to work when used.  Unfortunately, Zhu Long didn't keep close track of which of his masks were early efforts and which are later ones after he worked the kinks out, so every once in a while, one of his assassins tries to turn themselves into an Oni and the mask just doesn't do anything.

Until....

The comic opens from the perspective of an elderly Asian man wearing the outfit of a Zhu Long ninja.  He reminisces about his heyday as one of Zhu Long's most favored agents, first a simple foot soldier, then a poisoner and alchemist, later a competent blood mage.  But he's not immortal, and he lost his edge as he aged, eventually being demoted back down to assassin.  He was never really evil, but he had his own reasons for being loyal to Zhu Long and would stick to those reasons on principle.  This mission would be his last hurrah, and he's at peace with that.  He'll attack the heroes, put on the mask, and be at peace at last.

Or so he thinks.

The mask actually contains the spirit of an ancient Tibetan lama, who also died at peace with his beliefs and his death, violent as it was.  As this trapped spirit is compatible with the spirit and mindset of the elderly assassin, they merge in the same way as an Oni would, but the result, though monstrous (it still has an Oni's face, after all), is calm, wise, and learned.  He still has the assassin's willingness to let the ends justify the means, but he also has the lama's sense of goodness and justice.  A powerful magician and a skilled martial artist, he named himself Hakutaku, after a legendary beast that feeds on nightmares and bad memories, known for teaching heroes about the nature of monsters.

Rabit:

I have to add: I understand their point about a teleporter in SotM, but I still disagree. (And as a lover of teleporters, I created a home-brew hero to prove it to myself – as one does.

Yeah, I agree, I think there must be ways to handle positional stuff like a teleporter character. The first thing that comes to my mind is a hero with cards that do various special effects that change the game state based on positions of things on the table - like taking a target in play and putting it under the top card of the Environment deck, maybe, or Chastise-like things. And, of course, there's always reaction/redirection effects similar to the Synaptic Interruption. (I get the feeling that Stuntman maybe feels like this?)

But the more I think about it, the more I think this isn't going far enough. If I were to start building a portal character, I'd start from the idea that the hero's power is "Search your deck for a Portal card. Put it into play." and then fill the deck with these:

Portal

Ongoing

Play this card in front of a deck or target.

The first time each turn that deck plays a card or that target deals damage, you may use a power.

The rest of the hero's deck would consist of cards that do things between decks/targets tied together by portals. For example, selecting two targets with Portals in from of them, and redirect all their damage to each other. Or selecting two decks with Portals in front of them, and swapping their top cards. Or swapping the play areas of two Equipment or Device cards, and having them affect their new areas. Man, that would be fun! It would also capture some of the positional aspects of the character.

I could imagine redoing Mad Bomber Blade somewhat similarly…maybe something like shuffling his Device cards into the Environment and Hero decks, and having those "bombs" go off when they are played, revealed, or drawn. The heroes would have to actually find them all to win, through the normal game or some search mechanic on his rule card. That might capture the idea of searching the city better than the "bombs" just popping out of his deck and all cycling to the same place (under his card). That feels more like searching different locations, too. It might not be the most exciting game, though - it should be fun first of all!

I wonder how you did it, Rabit.
