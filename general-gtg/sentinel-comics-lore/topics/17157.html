Did anyone else get annoyed by the assertion that gravity goes up as you go closer to the core of the earth (in the discussion of Magmaria)? I did, and I looked it up, and it is actually true on the earth.

Inside a planet of uniform density, the gravity force is proportional to the distance from the center, as opposed to what happens outside, where it's inversely proportional to the distance squared. (That is, outside the planet, gravity goes up as you get closer to the center. Inside, gravity goes down as you get closer to the center.) This is a fun exercise I did in physics class at some point.

But the earth is not uniform: the core is much denser than the rest, and as a result the gravity still goes up underneath the earth's crust, until you get about halfway to the core. Apparently the maximum is 1.09 times the surface gravity, so it may well be true that Mag Man feels lighter on the surface.

I'm assuming that Christopher and Adam knew all this.

I wonder what Ray Manta knows, given his belief in a hollow flat earth. Inside a hollow earth, there would be no gravity (free fall). It seems that Ray Manta knows plenty of physics and would know this.

Reference: [1308.1342] The Gravity Tunnel in a Non-Uniform Earth
