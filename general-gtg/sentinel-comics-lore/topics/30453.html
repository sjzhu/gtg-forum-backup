Trajector:

With the idea in mind that GTG is a company trying to make money on consumer entertainment products, I’ve been wondering if all this meta-building is in service of a particular goal.

I do think that the biggest thing SCRPG, which is honestly not a very good RPG in any sense other than being really easy and fun for non-veteran and pressed-for-time players to just jump in and start rolling with it (which does count for a lot, but there’s an in-built tension between the improvvy “do anything with anything to solve anything” nature, and the fact that it’s a game book with definite rules that you paid money to buy)…anyway, I’d better back up. The biggest thing that could make a hypothetical second edition of SCRPG specifically (which is unlikely due to the >G situation), as opposed to the GYRO system (which may well see future products without FRG having to sign off on them). Argh, my fleaking sentence structure. Lemme try again.

The biggest thing, which SCRPG itself has going for it, is that it has the metafiction concept built in. You’re not just playing a superhero, you’re playing a writer of a book about a superhero, trying to create a comic which will sell. That’s not something I think any other RPG does or tries to do. And I think the best hope for a future SC RPG 2.0 to capture relevant market share, would be to lean into that meta nature and make THAT the more concrete aspect of the rules. Instead of saying that my hero fails his Overcome because of a bad roll and penalties and not having an Ability or Principle that applies to counteract it, tell me whether the story of my hero trying his best and failing speaks to the readers, in a way that either drives sales or generates fan interest. Put more game mechanics into that part of the game. Writers can justify anything within the fiction, but will the fiction be good in the metafiction’s opinion, that’s the real challenge to overcome with robust die-rolling and a minmaxed build.
