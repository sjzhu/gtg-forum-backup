Adam’s instinct for what would happen if the Moon turned to dust is really good.

For a non-pseudo-science picture of what would happen if the Moon got atomized:

  • First all the fragments and dust would spread out in the sky as a result of the initial impact energy. It would all end up in a ring around the Earth, following the Moon’s original orbit path.
  • The ring would probably re-accrete back into a moon, over millions of years. In the meantime, the Earth would have a ring. But over the long term, rings are not stable around the Earth (ironically, to get a stable ring, you’d need a moon!) and would either accrete into a moon or dissipate out into space.
  • If the Moon was close enough to the Earth and the pulverizing impact energy was high enough, you could probably get full-sky coverage of dust for a short (geologically speaking) time. In that case, we could estimate how much sunlight is blocked from reaching the Earth’s surface. It would be a general broadband reduction, like a neutral density filter rather than a UV filter.
  • If there’s enough dimming, the big problem is plants. With not enough sunlight, you’d see huge dieoffs with major crop yield reductions. Basically the Lady Astronaut scenario.
  • Yeah there are life forms that live without sunlight, but the thing they have is some other energy source. Plants on the surface would die faster than they could evolve to use some other energy.

Thunderbird:

I think we’re mixing up a Schmeese’s Cup of (Pseudo) Science and Magic. The werewolves’ curse is predicated on the full moon, right?

Lasers work by pumping energy into a material that is directly between two mirrors, one of which is partially transparent. The material, the “lasing medium,” has to emit light at some wavelength in response to the energy input. The other thing you need is to make sure that the mirror separation distance is an integer multiple of the wavelength. So, the lasing medium emits light, it bounces between the mirrors, and every time it goes through the gain medium there’s constructive interference, building a brighter and more coherent beam. (Plus stuff with Einstein coefficients.) You don’t have to build all the constructive interference, but it helps. There are variations with one mirror, for instance, or a fiber loop that just circles around. Eventually some of the light escapes out the partially transparent mirror, and that’s the beam that zaps out, pew pew.

So anyway, during a full Moon, the Earth and everyone on it are located right between the Sun, a source of energy, and the Moon, a mirror. So werewolves must be a lasing medium, and the rest of humans aren’t.

A lot to say on this episode as well, now that I’m caught up to it. I think a lot about, for lack of a better term, the science of supernatural creatures; it’s acceptable that C&A are loose with this kind of lore, since comics only try so hard to make sense or be realistic, but I do like to try and Justify things a little more.

From a scientific perspective, the problem with a large number of vampires is that, especially with much of the sun’s light being blocked out, the vampire population will rapidly deplete the accessible sources of useful energy. Plants would no longer grow, and anything not in a seed vault would be permanently extinct even if the cloud eventually cleared. Vampires would rapidly run out of people, animals, plants cultured to produce blood, combustible fuels to operate steampunk devices, and any electrical supply not tied to a geothermal or hydrodynamic stationary location. Technology would not be able to save the vampires for long, and most fictional magic systems come with some sort of cost for power, so it’s likely this couldn’t fix the problem either.
