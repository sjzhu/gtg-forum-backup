A thing to remember is that we're getting all the stories in abstract form -- we're getting the summaries and after-action reports that don't include the nuts and bolts.

A disclaimer here: The lore is one of my favorite things about SOTM, to the point where my game group is probably a little sick of me gushing about it. I love the world that Christopher and Adam have built and the way they've thought up not only the characters' backstories, but the backstories of the comic company itself. So I don't want anything I say to be taken as a slight against their writing (and drawing) abilities.

Now, let's say someone pitched to us a story about a noble hero, born to save the world with incredible power, but in trying to help the ones he loves, ends up dooming the very people he meant to save.

That sounds like an awesome, badass story, right? It's got all the right elements in the pitch for something that would be fantastically tragic to watch.

But then if you go and film it and call it Star Wars: Episode I, II and III, that's where you run into a problem.

See, we're all imagining idealized versions of the storylines they're describing -- dialog that really works, art that is visually stunning, storylines that are well-plotted and coherent. All things that are easy to describe in a podcast, but execution is much, much trickier.

I admire Christopher and Adam's storytelling ability and what they have shown and told us is fantastic, otherwise I wouldn't be listening to the podcast every week. But as a writer myself, I know that not everything you plot out ends up executing as well as you'd hoped, and sometimes it just plain flops.

So while I would certainly buy and probably enjoy comics with these heroes, I'm also perfectly fine with leaving them to the imagination.

"I should have known it was you when I received reports of disturbances in the airspace over the center of the Atlantic Ocean.  Though you should know that, no matter what terrible plot you are enacting, I will stop you."
