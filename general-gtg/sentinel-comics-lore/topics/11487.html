One "complaint" about SotM that you often here is that the choices are obvious. Well, I was curious to see if that was true, and if everyone plays the same way. Each week, I will post a random game, and I would like to see how everyone approached the battle given the same situation. Obviously, there is no way to make sure everyone gets the same cards at the same time, but let's keep the situation as close as possible, by keeping the same heroes in the same order. If you have less than five players, just don't use the heroes in extra slots.

For each game, start your post by saying whether you played on advance or not, and how many players you had. However, do not state the outcome in the beginning. If you can, explain how the gameplay went, and what strategy you were trying to use. At the end, put down whether you were victorious or not.

One thing that is really important to the argument and countering it is:

What were the cards in your hand?

The big argument is that there was no choice to be made.   That requires one card to be the clear better choice and X targets to be the clear better targets.  To be able to analyze this, we need to know what cards were in the player's hand when they made their decision so we can see if there really were other options that could be equally or differently effective.

Does knowing the villain and their deck alter this in a positive (more choices or more meaningful choices) or negative (less choices, or less meaningful choices) direction?  Does knowing that Citizen Dawn could have a Devestating Aurora waiting for you make your choices more or less important or have no change?

Does knowing your hero deck alter this in a positive or negative direction?

 

For my opinions on this there are plenty of choices to be made.  Especially if you talk with your teammates.  Should I take out the big guy or multiple little guys?  Should I deal a bit of damage this round because I can potentially do more next round, or do I go all-out now?  Do we destroy the current environment card or a villain ongoing?  Do you destroy an ongoing or do damage? 

If you talk with your teammates, the choices may boil down a bit because they can take care of something that allows you to make a different, more effective play.  And, if you go for the "optimum" play, that will reduce your options even more.  But this is true with any game, not just SotM.

Knowing the villain means you may want to hold a card back in case of emergencies, or avoid destroying one ongoing because it isn't as harmful as another potential ongoing.  So it seems to me this is adding more choices.

Knowing the hero means you can save up some cards for a strong combo later instead of using the simple "this looks best at this moment" play strategy.  Save up those "Eye on the Prize" cards for a round with "Hunter Hunted" instead of using them right away.  Again, more choices with more knowledge of your hero.

In the end, I suspect the people who feel there were no choices to be made didn't know their characters or the villains and went for the immediate best play for now.  Which means their judgement that the game didn't have choices was flawed by their lack of experience with the game.

The people that make the "no choices" comments often refer to damage as if it's always the best thing to do. They claim the options are "do 2 damage or 3 damage" and don't seem to acknowledge that damage is only part of the game.

If you're damage-fixated like that, many of the cards in your hand will be essentially dead, because you'll discount anything that doesn't just punch the villain in the face. And then you're much more likely to claim that the choices aren't meaningful if you're ignoring the "mess up the villain" and "help your teammates" cards in favour of "do 2 damage" and "do 3 damage" cards.

It would be fun to do an actual play video where players explain why they are doing what they're doing, to give insight into the decision-making process.

I personally spend what is probably an unnecessary amount of time agonising over decisions in pretty much every game (as the G+ players will tell you).
