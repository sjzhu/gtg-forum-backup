Slash fiction is fanfiction where two canon characters are involved in a relationship. The last syllable of that last word is where the "shipping" part comes from - if fans of something "ship" two characters, it means they're writing fanfics in which those two characters get together even though they didn't in the actual canon storyline. So it's "Character A/Character B" - the slash in the middle is why it's called "slash".

I don't read facfiction but I do spend quite a bit of time on TV Tropes ;).

awp832:

Slash & Ship is a thing?   I don't get it.    Is it not American?  =p.

"Shipping" (from "relationship") is about imagining fictional characters becoming romantically involved in ways that they aren't in the work itself (at least not at the time that you ship them; sometimes the author is way ahead of you, but other times s/he would think you're seriously wrong in the head for even suggesting such a pairing).  "Slash fiction" is the next step - erotic or semi-erotic fan-fiction based around such "unauthorized" couples (most frequently homosexual; it's not that a story about a boy and a girl isn't slash, just that if you say slash and don't explain any further, it will usually be taken as implying Kirk/Spock or the like.  That's where the name comes from, incidentally - Kirk-Slash-Spock stories, meaning stories in which Spock decides that Kirk is, and always has been, more than just his friend, or vice versa.  Other couplings quickly followed but that was pretty much THE trope definer.)
