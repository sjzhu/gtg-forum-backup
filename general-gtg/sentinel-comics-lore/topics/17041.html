I think this comes down to the different focuses of the "comic's writers" for both sections. Dreamer does have a ton of power, but look at what all that power gets her? Comas and dangerous mental projections that kill people, and look at the lives led by Supers in Tactics. They are hated by the general populace, disliked by the government, and they constantly are in fights for their lives.

 

Vanessa is barely a teenager, it isn't unreasonable to assume that she just decides to not take part in life and death struggles and have a large portion of the people who know her hate her for having powers she probably never wanted in the first place. We figure Visionary taught her safety measures so her power is under control, so you have a character who chooses to do nothing… and so nothing happens.

 

Now, I agree getting told that would have been helpful, and talking about that story and perhaps even taking elements of that story and working it into an interesting arc could all be done, but Tactics is a pretty dark and brutal world in the sentinels timeline and having a powerful character fade out because she chooses to not get involved kind of fits that style of world. Everything is hopeless and bad, the new generation isn't really stepping up, and the stories are more focused on how the constant battle is wearing on the heroes of old.

 

As to your second point, I disagree for a few reasons, but I recognize this is all opinion.

 

The biggest is that falling in battle has a different type of narrative structure. If she is killed in a fight, the focus might become the consequences within that fight. The question of "how do we defeat this villain" is still prominent. Instead, the approach they described goes for something a little different. The battle is won, but then she falls. They rush her to receive medical care, unaware of the state of her body and how truly too late it is. And she passes quietly in a hospital room, succumbing to the illnesses and stresses that have haunted her since her first appearance. It is a very somber and stark scene that I picture, one that focuses on her death and also, has echoes of the types of lives that are truly led by people.

 

She doesn't die in fire and fury on the battlefield. She doesn't die because of some laser blast from a villain or a new threat arising. She dies quietly from her disease, one she has fought longer and harder than any villain. And no villain can take the credit. No person can crow from the rooftops that they killed the Visionary, no villain was capable of it. It resonants with me, perhaps because of the recent loss of my grandmother, but I feel like that is a quiet and powerful moment, instead of something that instead tries to be epic and loud, and it seems to fit her.

 

Ra, Ra would never die quietly, but he also never had a serious medical problem. Him dying on the battlefield, facing a foe even the gods could not stand before matches his mythic roots, it fits him and the type of character he was. It could work for Visionary, but I think I like a quieter end to her.

 

Also, this makes the fifth known death of a major hero (Nightmist and Ra from the Card game. Tachyon, KNYFE and Visionary from Tactics) with a lot more hinted at being just utterly terrible. I'm glad things are happier in the RPG, Yikes.
