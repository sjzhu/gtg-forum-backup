When I first heard the name of the game Sentinel Tactics, I assumed that 'of the Multiverse' had been left off of the name simply for ease of use, that something like "Sentinels of the Multiverse: Tactics" was considered too unwieldy.

 

Recently, though, it was revealed (or at least implied) that 'of the Multiverse' was left off because by the time of Sentinel Tactics, the 'Multiverse' had been resolved.

 

This suggests to me that everything in SotM related to multiple timelines and multiple -verses (still not sure if that's two different things or the same thing) is tied together, and that all of the timelines/-verses collapse into a single one by the time the events of Oblivaeon are resolved.

 

This further suggests that the events of Oblivaeon happen, in part, _because_ the timelines have been shattered. 

 

What really blows my mind, though, is that if the above is true, then my assumptions about the name 'Sentinels of the Multiverse' have been wrong! I'd always assumed that from the start Christopher and Adam had conceived of a multiverse that had superheroes in it, but it seems instead that they had conceived of a _universe_ that had superheroes in it and then their imagined storylines included shattered timelines which eventually led to multiverses and a massive cross-universe event.

 

Recall that the name of the comics is 'Sentinel Comics', not 'Sentinels of the Multiverse Comics'. 

 

My perspective is shifting around. I used to think of 'Sentinel Tactics' as being a sort of awkward shortening of 'Sentinels of the Multiverse: Tactics', but I now realize that 'Sentinels of the Multiverse' and 'Sentinel Tactics' are just two ways to refer to Sentinel Comics. Probably what happened is that when Christopher and Adam appropriated the characters from Sentinel Comics into the card game and started thinking about what stories they wanted to tell in that game, they settled on the resolution of the multiverse arc as the climax of the game, and thus NAMED THE GAME AFTER THAT ARC OF THE STORY.

 

That's the thing that's blowing my mind. I think they named the game after the story arc in a way that I did not recognize until we were five years and _every_ expansion into the game. Maybe I'm just being slow about this and others saw this all along.

 

So, another things related to this. When the Sentinels hero deck came out, the naming seemed sloppy to me. Why would one relatively minor superhero group get the same name as the entire game?! Now we've seen that Chokepoint went to The Sentinels' training grounds, Ft. Adamant, with some sort of mission in mind, and it's all seemingly wrapped up in what's going on in Oblivaeon.

 

I'm beginning to think that The Sentinels have some crucial role to play in the events of Oblivaeon that I don't understand yet.

 

Assuming Adam and Christopher had a damn good reason to name The Sentinels the way they did (and why didn't I trust that was the case all along?), WHAT IS THAT REASON?!
