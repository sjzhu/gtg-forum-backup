Ordinarily I avoid spoilers, but the reason I do so is that I believe in experiencing "reveals" the way the author intended.

In the case of Tactics, there's no such thing.  There's a story, but no one's telling it--the only way to find it out is through the podcasts or by inferring it through game mechanics and flavor text.

There's a "telling" in the RPG, but the intended way to experience that is as a player, by having the GM buy the module and running you through it.  That's never going to happen for me--I'm always the GM in my groups, the rare times I can get one together, and I don't buy or play published modules anyway.  Having Christopher and Adam "spoil" the story in the podcasts doesn't make any less sense that learning about it any other way I'm liable to encounter it.

Also, the "spoilers" section seems to be getting more and more expansive.  It's pretty much the whole "Future" section now.  The Unity episode outright stated stuff that was in Benchmark's spoilers section.  The spoiler "tags" hardly seem necessary since if you're avoiding spoilers, you should just skip the whole Future section, and there's nothing after it.  I also don't know how you'd find the closing spoiler tag if you weren't listening.  Trevor should add in a voiceover, "Spoiler section ends at 1:54:11" or whatever.

I think "Interlude" just sounds less important.  Also, people who listen to other podcasts might be used to the "side" episodes as being something different.  For instance, some other podcasts I listen to are Wait Wait Don't Tell Me and Acquisitions Incorporated: The "C" Team.  In both of those, the bonus episodes consist of the staff/players just chatting or joking around, and you can totally skip them if you're just interested in the main show/story content.  Renaming the interludes as just regular episodes makes a lot of sense, and it might be worth actually stating in the beginning of the next two or three normal episodes, "Hey, what we've been calling Interludes are really full-length episodes, sorry for the confusion, you should go listen to any of the ones you skipped."

I think it's pretty obvious that I listen to everything.

I agree with Foote's definition that "an Interlude is anything that's not a Tuesday release" is an apt one, but the name implies that they're less important which might explain lower download rates.

For spoilers I think I've said before somewhere (maybe over on Reddit) that I think that spoilers only really make sense for story points that are important for a narrative game. In general, the card game doesn't qualify and neither does Tactics (sure there's a lot of story behind the scenes, but the act of discovery of the story isn't the point of the game). The RPG can make sense to mark spoilers, but mostly only for things that are important GM-only info. That is, If it's a big reveal late in an adventure that's meant to blow the players' minds, then it's appropriate for a spoiler tag. If it's part of the opening "tell the players this" exposition of a campaign, it's probably not a spoiler.

Since I listen to it all regardless, maybe my opinions on what's worth marking as a spoiler are less useful for the purposes of actually compartmentalising information that people care about, though.
