Well, first off, it's clear that this is a deliberate choice on their part to tell the story out of order (as opposed to just not getting around to it). Of course, something could be both a deliberate choice and a missed opportunity.

But I also don't know whether or not it was a good choice on their part. It's obvious looking at even the base set that there's a really rich story behind all the characters, that there's much more going on than is revealed on the surface. I feel like this adds a lot to the game, even for casual players. Of course there are different degrees of this.

Some of the out-of-order storytelling is quite unusual, but other bits are more ordinary serial storytelling conventions. For instance, it's quite typical in fiction series that we don't know what's going to be happening in the final showdown. It's unusual that we have seen little hints of what might be happening. I know it's one standard fiction-writing technique to write little back vignettes for your characters that you don't share with the readers.

In the specific case of Mr. Fixer, I think his story is revealed pretty straightforwardly in the Temple of Zhu Long deck.
