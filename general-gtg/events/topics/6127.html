    Arenson9 and I want to help motivate and mobilize this community to share Sentinels of the Multiverse with new players. To that end we've planned a series of challenges for the community as a whole, recognition for those people who teach the most new players, and an unfolding fan-written story that will be unlocked as each achievement is reached.

    The story is written by one of our youngest Sentinels of the Multiverse fans. The first chapter is below, along with a little information about her.

    For our first challenge, can we as a community teach twenty five new players? To help us meet the challenge, add to the count by posting to this thread with: New player count, who taught the game (forum user name), location (city, state/province,country) and date.

    If we meet this challenge, we will not only unlock the second chapter, but possibly also illustrations of that chapter as well.

    We are in need of multiple illustrators willing to lend their talent to bringing these words to life. If you have interest, send a private message to kirioni and arenson9, preferably with a link to your online art portfolio.

    Without further adieu here is the first chapter:

by Kiva Turner

Legacy was planning to go on a vacation at the beach.  Ra was just about to check the pyramids.         When Blade was out of sight the heroes looked around and saw at least twenty people wrapped up in toxic seaweed!  Ra reacted first, burning the toxic seaweed off of eight people, but the other twelve had seaweed too strong.  Wraith had brought her smoke bombs and razor blades to help. Character 1: Citizen of the M

By J.R. Burns

             I don’t believe in all this end of the world junk, but a lot of my friends, smart guys some of them, do. I mean, I don’t think they are crazy or anything. Life here… well that is crazy and it has only gotten more so in the past few years. I work construction; retrofit, string new lines, patch up buildings, build new buildings. A lot of grunt work, but it’s good honest work. This city here…? It needs a lot of good hard work with all the whackos running around. Just the other day, drivin’ into the M (Megalopolis is what the outta town folks call it) and hit traffic. Find out it’s not some big movie star or a run of the mill flat tire without the sense to pull over… instead it is a circus of loons running around throwing all sorts of crazy stuff at each other. This one chick came by my car with enough guns you think she was trying to take over a small country and the police are just standing by. Call that taxes wasted. And get this, get this, you will never believe what the paper writes… calls ‘em heroes, talks about some space man trying to enslave the world. I have seen a lot in my days; Wars, riots, stock markets crashing, the lot… but space men is… just, pardon the phrase, outta this world. What am I to believe next weird rips in space time and dinosaurs? I call shenanigans of the third degree. You’d think the tabloids would run better stuff. But I try to pay them no mind, as long as a clock in and fix whatever building they’ve knocked over in their vigilante turkey shoot and clock out at the end of the day it don’t matter none to me. I guess that what us normal folk do to make a living in the M.

 

About the Author

John first fell in love with games with Hungry Hungry Hippos along with scavenger hunts he and his three siblings would make for one another(usually leading to ice cream or popcorn).Speaking of; His favorite ice cream flavor is chocolate mouse tracks and his (current) favorite Sentinel is Unity.

Character 2 Dr. Paki Onuris : Fated Beginnings

By J.R. Burns

             I am but a grain of sand in the ever shifting desert. The more I witness the less I understand. This story began, as many days do, with the Sun climbing into the sky and the land awaking the fires within the countless waves of sand moving so slowly that their power is underestimated. Outsiders are not uncommon to our lands, and many come seeking treasure and meeting their end among the storms and silent deaths that are veiled in time lost. The leader of this group was not, however, common. This man had the zeal of ten and the thirst of knowledge many a fool knows not to crave. Upon our first meeting he offered words of honor upon me and my family, and I could do no other than to invite him to our meal as I would dishonor my family to be inhospitable when honored in this way. He did not take long, once the meal’s observances were finished, to get to what he sought. The manner of his questioning was rare even among my people, he questioned with piercing clarity as if he knew the answers and was testing my knowledge of the sands and the secrets they held in firm grasp. I sensed and opportunity and so struck with him a pact. I would serve as guide in exchange for education for myself and my sons. It seemed as though I had spoken not from my mind but his, as he held out his hand in bond. And so it was our first meeting and the beginning of such mysteries that will only reveal in time, for this man I would see become god, and in so witnessing would live into my given name. For I am called Paki or “Witness”, son of Onuris “Brings Back the Distant One”, and my story and his are now forever intertwined as sand slips over sand.

Character 3: Olivia Carter Dickson: Employee of the Month

By J.R. Burns

People call me a neat freak, well some just call me a freak. I prefer to be called Livy, though most call be OCD.

When I read the announcement of a new research facility being built, I knew this was my chance to finally work somewhere that made a difference. I had been in pharmaceuticals for years, it paid the bills, but there was little for me in the way of cutting edge research. I read the thorough application and filled it out with a glee that only true dorks have. I love forms, I like the look of a crisp sheet almost as much as one full of data, and that data was me. I got an interview and again had to fill out a lengthy non-disclosure agreement, which had some odd fine print. It had things such as a comprehensive list of “improbable unfortunate events” that they were not legally liable for. This was not even a form for employment, just the standard “tour the facility” paperwork. I guess, looking back these should have all been red flags, but I was young..(er) then and just excited to embrace a new facet of life.

I ended up taking the job and with all my education, all my years of experience... the position was hybrid between a rodent wrangler, glorified janitor and hazmat team. In the beginning most days were uneventful; my team would make the rounds; snag, bag and tag any stray rats. We’d check that gages on numerous vats were functioning properly. We were responsible for any chemical spills or hazardous materials disposal. Work was like clockwork until there was a major change in “management”. I clearly recall the day when the notice was handed down, most people were worried about their jobs, I had an arrogant feeling they would be hard pressed to replace me or my team, boy was I ever wrong.

After the initial personnel reshuffle I WAS the night team, shifts went from slow paced to non-stop. I was not the only area that can been “restructured” and we all had the sense we were working in a ticking time bomb. Too many gages, whole floors no one knew who or what was working on as well as odd cryptic overtones in memos that were hard to decipher their meaning, as if they were posted not to staff, but to someone, or something else entirely.

One day, purely by happenstance I ventured off an elevator which had shorted between floors. I discovered that there was, in fact a floor between floors. Overhead lighting was dim and flickered. The elevator issue seemed like a breaker malfunction that was effecting the entire level. A bioluminescence filled the space as the lights died and there were vats lining the walls which to my horror contained humanoid shaped creatures! I ran back to the elevator as the generator spun back up, the doors closed behind me as the white lights bust on. I continued to my floor still catching my breath. My head spun, and I knew I should report this, but who could I trust? Someone had to be reading those memos, someone checking on whatever “experiment” between the floors. After a sleepless night I received a visit, at home, by a stern looking man who said I had been awarded the employee of the month, along with a bonus and raise which he had brought, in cash.The hollowness in his voice matched the scalpel sharpness of his stare; I knew that this gesture was both one of intimidation, a call to silence. I also got the next day off of work, which I took as a “think it over” day. The news that day showed me, and the world, that the place I worked was far more than or anyone had known.
